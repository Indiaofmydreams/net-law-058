This set has 23 multiple-choice questions on Positivism and Post-Positivism from the UGC NET Paper 1 syllabus, unit 2, Research Aptitude. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key. This set is an answer-key drill: check each answer, then revisit the notes for any you miss.

23 practice questions with answers

1The founder of positivism is:

  1. Karl Popper
  2. Auguste Comte
  3. Thomas Kuhn
  4. Max Weber
Show answer

Answer: B. Auguste Comte

Report this question

2Comte's law of three stages, in order, is:

  1. Positive, Metaphysical, Theological
  2. Metaphysical, Positive, Theological
  3. Positive, Theological, Metaphysical
  4. Theological, Metaphysical, Positive
Show answer

Answer: D. Theological, Metaphysical, Positive

3Match the following:
List I: (i) Comte, (ii) Popper, (iii) Kuhn
List II: (P) falsification, (Q) paradigm shift, (R) positive philosophy

  1. i-R, ii-P, iii-Q
  2. i-P, ii-Q, iii-R
  3. i-Q, ii-R, iii-P
  4. i-R, ii-Q, iii-P
Show answer

Answer: A. i-R, ii-P, iii-Q

4Comte's term for the scientific study of society was:

  1. Anthropology
  2. Psychology
  3. Sociology

5Positivism draws heavily on:

  1. Mysticism
  2. Revelation

6The methodology most associated with positivism is:

  1. Ethnographic
  2. Narrative inquiry

7Which is NOT a positivist assumption?

  1. Reality is objective
  2. Research can be value-free
  3. Reality consists of multiple, socially constructed realities
  4. Knowledge is based on observation

8The positivist researcher's role is generally that of a:

  1. Participant and co-constructor

  4. Detached, neutral observer

  1. Narrative inquiry
  2. Autoethnography

  1. Emotionally appealing
  2. Empirically verifiable or analytically true

  1. Verification

14Popper wrote:

15Assertion (A): Popper rejected verification as the criterion for demarcating science.
Reason (R): A universal statement cannot be conclusively verified by a finite number of observations.

  1. Both true, and R explains A
  2. Both true, but R does not explain A
  3. A true, R false
  4. A false, R true
Show answer

Answer: A. Both true, and R explains A

Report this question

16Popper's demarcation criterion separates:

  1. Science from non-science or pseudo-science
  2. Qualitative from quantitative research
  3. Primary from secondary data
  4. Pure from applied research
Show answer

Answer: A. Science from non-science or pseudo-science

Report this question

17According to Popper, a scientific theory:

  1. Can be conclusively proved
  2. Is accepted on authority
  3. Needs no testing
  4. Can never be conclusively verified, only corroborated until falsified

19Kuhn's famous work is:

  1. The Logic of Scientific Discovery
  2. The Structure of Scientific Revolutions
  3. Novum Organum
  4. The Open Society and Its Enemies

20Match the following:
List I: (i) Lakatos, (ii) Feyerabend, (iii) Carnap
List II: (P) Against Method, (Q) research programmes, (R) Vienna Circle

  1. i-P, ii-Q, iii-R
  2. i-Q, ii-P, iii-R
  3. i-R, ii-P, iii-Q
  4. i-Q, ii-R, iii-P

Answer: B. i-Q, ii-P, iii-R

Report this question

21In Kuhn's view, "normal science" is:

  1. Puzzle-solving research within an accepted paradigm
  2. Revolutionary research
  3. Research without any theory
  4. Pure speculation
Show answer

Answer: A. Puzzle-solving research within an accepted paradigm

Report this question

22Kuhn's sequence of scientific change is:

  1. Normal science → revolution → anomaly → crisis
  2. Normal science → anomalies → crisis → revolution → new normal science
  3. Crisis → normal science → anomalies
  4. Revolution → crisis → normal science → anomaly
Show answer

Answer: B. Normal science → anomalies → crisis → revolution → new normal science

Report this question

23Post-positivism holds that:

  1. Absolute truth can be fully known
  2. Reality does not exist
  3. Knowledge is conjectural and absolute truth is unattainable
  4. Research needs no evidence
