This set has 20 multiple-choice questions on Historical Method from the UGC NET Paper 1 syllabus, unit 2, Research Aptitude. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key. This set is an answer-key drill: check each answer, then revisit the notes for any you miss.

20 practice questions with answers

1The historical method involves:

  1. Controlled experimentation
  2. Systematic collection and critical evaluation of evidence about the past
  3. Large-scale surveys
  4. Lab measurement
Show answer

Answer: B. Systematic collection and critical evaluation of evidence about the past

Report this question

2Heuristics in historical research means:

  1. Writing the final report
  2. Testing hypotheses statistically
  3. Locating and collecting sources
  4. Interviewing participants
Show answer

Answer: C. Locating and collecting sources

Report this question

3Chronology refers to:

  1. Statistical analysis
  2. Sampling of dates
  3. Arranging events in the order of time
  4. Controlling variables
Show answer

Answer: C. Arranging events in the order of time

Report this question

4A historical hypothesis is:

  1. A proven fact
  2. A statistical test
  3. A tentative explanation that guides the search for evidence
  4. A questionnaire item
Show answer

Answer: C. A tentative explanation that guides the search for evidence

Report this question

5Which of these is a typical historical research problem?

  1. The influence of Wood's Despatch (1854) on Indian education
  2. Effect of a drug on blood pressure
  3. Correlation of IQ and marks
  4. Opinion poll on current policy
Show answer

Answer: A. The influence of Wood's Despatch (1854) on Indian education

Report this question

6A primary source is:

  1. A later textbook
  2. An encyclopaedia entry
  3. A review article
  4. An eyewitness's diary
Show answer

Answer: D. An eyewitness's diary

7A secondary source is:

  1. An original manuscript
  2. A government order of that time
  3. An account written later by someone who did not witness the events
  4. An inscription
Show answer

Answer: C. An account written later by someone who did not witness the events

Report this question

8Oral history relies on:

  1. Recorded recollections of persons who witnessed or experienced events
  2. Statistical tables
  3. Laboratory notes
  4. Random samples
Show answer

Answer: A. Recorded recollections of persons who witnessed or experienced events

Report this question

9An archaeological inscription from the period under study is a:

  1. Primary source
  2. Secondary source
  3. Tertiary source
  4. Hypothesis
Show answer

Answer: A. Primary source

10Which of the following is NOT a primary source?

  1. A letter written at that time
  2. A biography written decades later by another author
  3. A contemporary newspaper report
  4. An official record of that time
Show answer

Answer: B. A biography written decades later by another author

11External criticism of a source is concerned with:

  1. Its authenticity, i.e. whether it is genuine
  2. Its meaning
  3. Its language style
  4. Its length
Show answer

Answer: A. Its authenticity, i.e. whether it is genuine

Report this question

12Internal criticism is concerned with:

  1. The paper and ink used
  2. The date of the binding
  3. Its price
  4. The credibility, accuracy and meaning of the content
Show answer

Answer: D. The credibility, accuracy and meaning of the content

13Detecting a forged document belongs to:

  1. Internal criticism
  2. External criticism
  3. Sampling
  4. Hypothesis testing

Answer: B. External criticism

14Corroboration in historical research means:

  1. Copying a source
  2. Ignoring contradictions
  3. Using a single source
  4. Confirming a claim through independent sources

15Historiography is:

  1. A laboratory method
  2. A sampling technique
  3. A type of experiment
  4. The study of how history has been written

16Leopold von Ranke is associated with:

  1. Positive psychology
  2. Source-based, critical historiography
  3. Experimental design
  4. Sociometry

  1. Father of Sociology
  2. Father of Statistics
  3. Father of History

  1. Variables can be fully controlled
  2. The data are always numerical
