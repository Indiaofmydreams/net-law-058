About this question set

This set has 25 multiple-choice questions on Ethical Principles in Research from the UGC NET Paper 1 syllabus, unit 2, Research Aptitude. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key. This set is an answer-key drill: check each answer, then revisit the notes for any you miss.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Research Aptitude notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

25 practice questions with answers

1Ethics in research refers to:

  1. Rules for formatting a thesis
  2. Statistical procedures for analysis
  3. Rules for obtaining funding
  4. Moral principles and standards that guide the conduct of research
Show answer

Answer: D. Moral principles and standards that guide the conduct of research

Report this question

2The primary purpose of research ethics is to:

  1. Increase the sample size
  2. Speed up publication
  3. Reduce the cost of research
  4. Protect participants and ensure the honesty and integrity of research
Show answer

Answer: D. Protect participants and ensure the honesty and integrity of research

Report this question

3Consider the following statements:
I. Research ethics apply only to medical research.
II. Social science research involving human participants also requires ethical safeguards.
III. Ethical clearance is needed only after data collection.

  1. I only
  2. I and III
  3. II only
  4. II and III
Show answer

Answer: C. II only

Report this question

4The Nuremberg Code (1947) arose out of:

  1. The Tuskegee study
  2. The Milgram experiment
  3. The trials of Nazi doctors for experiments on prisoners
  4. The thalidomide tragedy
Show answer

Answer: C. The trials of Nazi doctors for experiments on prisoners

Report this question

5The first principle of the Nuremberg Code is that:

  1. The voluntary consent of the human subject is absolutely essential
  2. Experiments must be profitable
  3. Authority approval alone is enough
  4. The researcher's convenience comes first
Show answer

Answer: A. The voluntary consent of the human subject is absolutely essential

Report this question

6The Declaration of Helsinki was issued by the:

  1. UNESCO
  2. WHO
  3. ICMR
  4. World Medical Association
Show answer

Answer: D. World Medical Association

Report this question

7The Declaration of Helsinki was first adopted in:

  1. 1947
  2. 1964
  3. 1979
  4. 2002
Show answer

Answer: B. 1964

Report this question

8The three basic principles of the Belmont Report are:

  1. Respect for persons, beneficence and justice
  2. Autonomy, beneficence and non-maleficence
  3. Honesty, objectivity and integrity
  4. Privacy, confidentiality and anonymity
Show answer

Answer: A. Respect for persons, beneficence and justice

Report this question

9The Belmont Report was published in the USA in:

  1. 1932
  2. 1947
  3. 1964
  4. 1979
Show answer

Answer: D. 1979

Report this question

10The Tuskegee syphilis study is considered unethical mainly because:

  1. Treatment was withheld from men with syphilis without their informed consent
  2. Participants were paid too much
  3. Animals were used
  4. The study was too short
Show answer

Answer: A. Treatment was withheld from men with syphilis without their informed consent

Report this question

11Match the following:
List I: (i) Nuremberg Code, (ii) Declaration of Helsinki, (iii) Belmont Report
List II: (P) 1979, (Q) 1947, (R) 1964

  1. i-R, ii-Q, iii-P
  2. i-Q, ii-R, iii-P
  3. i-P, ii-R, iii-Q
  4. i-Q, ii-P, iii-R
Show answer

Answer: B. i-Q, ii-R, iii-P

Report this question

12Beneficence in research means that the researcher should:

  1. Pay all participants equally
  2. Maximise possible benefits and minimise possible harms
  3. Keep all data anonymous
  4. Publish as quickly as possible
Show answer

Answer: B. Maximise possible benefits and minimise possible harms

Report this question

13Non-maleficence means:

  1. The obligation to avoid causing harm
  2. Maximising profit
  3. Sharing data openly
  4. Rewarding participants
Show answer

Answer: A. The obligation to avoid causing harm

Report this question

14The principle of justice requires that:

  1. Only educated persons are included
  2. Vulnerable groups are chosen for convenience
  3. Participants are selected fairly and the burdens and benefits of research are shared equitably
  4. Only volunteers from the researcher's institution are used
Show answer

Answer: C. Participants are selected fairly and the burdens and benefits of research are shared equitably

Report this question

15The four principles of biomedical ethics proposed by Beauchamp and Childress are:

  1. Validity, reliability, objectivity and generalisability
  2. Honesty, trust, fairness and respect
  3. Autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence and justice
  4. Privacy, consent, anonymity and confidentiality
Show answer

Answer: C. Autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence and justice

Report this question

16According to the risk-benefit principle:

  1. Risks may be of any size if the researcher benefits
  2. Risks to participants should be minimised and reasonable in relation to the anticipated benefits
  3. Benefits are irrelevant
  4. Only physical risks count
Show answer

Answer: B. Risks to participants should be minimised and reasonable in relation to the anticipated benefits

Report this question

17An Institutional Review Board (IRB) or Institutional Ethics Committee (IEC):

  1. Funds the research
  2. Publishes journals
  3. Checks plagiarism only
  4. Reviews research proposals involving human participants to protect their rights, safety and well-being
Show answer

Answer: D. Reviews research proposals involving human participants to protect their rights, safety and well-being

Report this question

18The ICMR guidelines issued in 2017 are titled:

  1. Indian Code for Academic Plagiarism
  2. National Policy on Journal Publishing
  3. Guidelines for Animal Housing
  4. National Ethical Guidelines for Biomedical and Health Research Involving Human Participants
Show answer

Answer: D. National Ethical Guidelines for Biomedical and Health Research Involving Human Participants

Report this question

19The ICMR 2017 guidelines list how many general ethical principles?

  1. 4
  2. 6
  3. 12
  4. 20
Show answer

Answer: C. 12

Report this question

20Assertion (A): Ethics committee approval is required before beginning a study with human participants.
Reason (R): It helps ensure that participants' rights, safety and well-being are protected.

  1. Both A and R are true, and R explains A
  2. Both are true, but R does not explain A
  3. A is true, R is false
  4. A is false, R is true
Show answer

Answer: A. Both A and R are true, and R explains A

Report this question

21Which group requires additional protection as vulnerable research participants?

  1. Children, prisoners and persons with cognitive impairment
  2. Highly educated adults
  3. Researchers
  4. Journal reviewers
Show answer

Answer: A. Children, prisoners and persons with cognitive impairment

Report this question

22Deception in research is considered acceptable only when:

  1. It is convenient
  2. No non-deceptive alternative exists, the risk is minimal and participants are debriefed afterwards
  3. It saves money
  4. Participants dislike the information
Show answer

Answer: B. No non-deceptive alternative exists, the risk is minimal and participants are debriefed afterwards

Report this question

23Debriefing means:

  1. Paying participants
  2. Destroying the data
  3. Reporting to the journal
  4. Informing participants after the study about its true nature and purpose and addressing their concerns
Show answer

Answer: D. Informing participants after the study about its true nature and purpose and addressing their concerns

Report this question

24The main ethical criticism of Milgram's obedience experiment concerned:

  1. Deception and severe psychological distress caused to participants
  2. Use of animals
  3. Fabrication of data
  4. Plagiarism
Show answer

Answer: A. Deception and severe psychological distress caused to participants

Report this question

25The Stanford Prison Experiment was conducted by:

  1. Stanley Milgram
  2. F. Skinner
  3. Ivan Pavlov
  4. Philip Zimbardo
Show answer

Answer: D. Philip Zimbardo
