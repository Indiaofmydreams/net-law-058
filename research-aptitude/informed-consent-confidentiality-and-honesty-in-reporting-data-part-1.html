About this question set

This set has 25 multiple-choice questions on Informed Consent, Confidentiality and Honesty in Reporting Data from the UGC NET Paper 1 syllabus, unit 2, Research Aptitude. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key. This set is an answer-key drill: check each answer, then revisit the notes for any you miss.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Research Aptitude notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

25 practice questions with answers

1Informed consent is:

  1. A signature taken without explanation
  2. Permission from the supervisor only
  3. A voluntary agreement to participate, given after adequate information about the study
  4. A payment receipt
Show answer

Answer: C. A voluntary agreement to participate, given after adequate information about the study

Report this question

2The essential elements of valid informed consent include:

  1. Cost, speed and convenience
  2. Anonymity only
  3. Disclosure, comprehension and voluntariness
  4. Payment only
Show answer

Answer: C. Disclosure, comprehension and voluntariness

Report this question

3A consent form should explain:

  1. The purpose, procedures, risks, benefits, confidentiality and the right to withdraw
  2. Only the title of the study
  3. Only the researcher's qualifications
  4. Only the funding details
Show answer

Answer: A. The purpose, procedures, risks, benefits, confidentiality and the right to withdraw

Report this question

4Voluntariness in consent means:

  1. Obtained only from relatives
  2. Free from coercion and undue influence
  3. Obtained in secret
  4. Obtained after the study
Show answer

Answer: B. Free from coercion and undue influence

Report this question

5Participants have the right to withdraw from a study:

  1. Only before signing
  2. Only with the researcher's permission
  3. At any time, without penalty or loss of benefits to which they are otherwise entitled
  4. Only after payment
Show answer

Answer: C. At any time, without penalty or loss of benefits to which they are otherwise entitled

Report this question

6The consent form should be written in:

  1. Technical jargon only
  2. A foreign language only
  3. Simple language the participant can understand, including the local language where needed
  4. Legal Latin
Show answer

Answer: C. Simple language the participant can understand, including the local language where needed

Report this question

7When a participant cannot read, the consent process usually involves:

  1. Skipping consent
  2. Obtaining it in the presence of an impartial witness
  3. Consent by the researcher
  4. Consent by a journal editor
Show answer

Answer: B. Obtaining it in the presence of an impartial witness

Report this question

8If a participant is harmed because of participation in a study, ethical guidelines require that:

  1. The participant bears the cost
  2. Medical care and appropriate compensation be provided as per applicable guidelines
  3. The harm be hidden
  4. The study continue unchanged
Show answer

Answer: B. Medical care and appropriate compensation be provided as per applicable guidelines

Report this question

9Assertion (A): Participants must be told that they may withdraw from the study at any time.
Reason (R): Voluntariness is a core requirement of informed consent.

  1. Both A and R are true, and R explains A
  2. Both are true, but R does not explain A
  3. A is true, R is false
  4. A is false, R is true
Show answer

Answer: A. Both A and R are true, and R explains A

Report this question

10"Assent" refers to:

  1. Payment to participants
  2. Approval by a journal
  3. A statistical decision
  4. Agreement by a child or other person unable to give full legal consent, obtained in addition to the permission of the parent or guardian
Show answer

Answer: D. Agreement by a child or other person unable to give full legal consent, obtained in addition to the permission of the parent or guardian

Report this question

11In India, the age at which a person can give legal consent as an adult is generally:

  1. 12 years
  2. 15 years
  3. 18 years
  4. 21 years
Show answer

Answer: C. 18 years

Report this question

12Where a person lacks the capacity to consent, consent is obtained from:

  1. The researcher
  2. The sponsor
  3. A legally authorised representative
  4. The reviewer
Show answer

Answer: C. A legally authorised representative

Report this question

13In research involving children, the researcher should:

  1. Skip consent
  2. Obtain only the child's consent
  3. Use deception freely
  4. Obtain parental or guardian permission and the child's assent, and minimise risk
Show answer

Answer: D. Obtain parental or guardian permission and the child's assent, and minimise risk

Report this question

14Community consent or engagement is especially important when research involves:

  1. Only laboratory animals
  2. Only secondary data
  3. Only theory
  4. Communities such as tribal groups, where individual consent alone may not be sufficient
Show answer

Answer: D. Communities such as tribal groups, where individual consent alone may not be sufficient

Report this question

15A waiver of informed consent may be considered by an ethics committee when:

  1. The study involves minimal risk and obtaining consent is impracticable, subject to committee approval
  2. The researcher is in a hurry
  3. The sample is large
  4. Participants refuse
Show answer

Answer: A. The study involves minimal risk and obtaining consent is impracticable, subject to committee approval

Report this question

16Undue inducement means:

  1. A modest refund of travel costs
  2. Offering excessive rewards that may compromise a person's voluntary decision
  3. Giving information
  4. Withdrawal of consent
Show answer

Answer: B. Offering excessive rewards that may compromise a person's voluntary decision

Report this question

17A teacher telling students that their grades will suffer unless they participate in the teacher's study is an example of:

  1. Informed consent
  2. Debriefing
  3. Assent
  4. Coercion
Show answer

Answer: D. Coercion

Report this question

18Therapeutic misconception occurs when participants:

  1. Withdraw from the study
  2. Sign a consent form
  3. Believe the research procedures are meant to treat them personally, rather than to generate generalisable knowledge
  4. Receive a placebo knowingly
Show answer

Answer: C. Believe the research procedures are meant to treat them personally, rather than to generate generalisable knowledge

Report this question

19Covert observation without consent can be considered only if:

  1. The researcher wishes
  2. Participants are children
  3. The data are sensitive
  4. The setting is public, risk is minimal and an ethics committee approves it
Show answer

Answer: D. The setting is public, risk is minimal and an ethics committee approves it

Report this question

20Anonymity means that:

  1. The researcher knows the identities but keeps them secret
  2. The identity of participants cannot be linked to their responses, even by the researcher
  3. Participants know each other
  4. Data are public
Show answer

Answer: B. The identity of participants cannot be linked to their responses, even by the researcher

Report this question

21Confidentiality means that:

  1. The researcher knows the identities but protects them from disclosure
  2. The researcher does not know who the participants are
  3. Data are published openly
  4. Participants are paid
Show answer

Answer: A. The researcher knows the identities but protects them from disclosure

Report this question

22Which practice best ensures anonymity?

  1. Collecting responses without any identifying information
  2. Recording names on every form
  3. Keeping a list of names with responses
  4. Taking photographs of participants
Show answer

Answer: A. Collecting responses without any identifying information

Report this question

23Using codes or pseudonyms instead of real names is called:

  1. Plagiarism
  2. Fabrication
  3. Coercion
  4. Pseudonymisation or de-identification
Show answer

Answer: D. Pseudonymisation or de-identification

Report this question

24Secure storage of research data should include:

  1. Leaving files on a public computer
  2. Sharing passwords
  3. Posting them on social media
  4. Restricted access, password protection and encryption where appropriate
Show answer

Answer: D. Restricted access, password protection and encryption where appropriate

Report this question

25The limits of confidentiality, for example the legal duty to report a serious risk of harm to someone, should be:

  1. Explained to participants in advance, in the consent process
  2. Hidden
  3. Kept for after publication
  4. Never mentioned
Show answer

Answer: A. Explained to participants in advance, in the consent process
