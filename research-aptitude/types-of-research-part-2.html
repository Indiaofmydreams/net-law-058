About this question set

This set has 20 multiple-choice questions on Types of Research from the UGC NET Paper 1 syllabus, unit 2, Research Aptitude. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key. This set is an answer-key drill: check each answer, then revisit the notes for any you miss.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Research Aptitude notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

20 practice questions with answers

1A feature of action research is that:

  1. The researcher is a detached observer
  2. The researcher is a participant in the situation
  3. It always uses a large random sample
  4. It aims at universal laws
Show answer

Answer: B. The researcher is a participant in the situation

Report this question

2A principal studies the causes of low enrolment in her own school and takes corrective steps. This is:

  1. Fundamental research
  2. Historical research
  3. Ex post facto research
  4. Action research
Show answer

Answer: D. Action research

Report this question

3The first step in action research is usually:

  1. Publishing the results
  2. Selecting a national sample
  3. Writing the thesis
  4. Identifying a practical problem in one's own setting
Show answer

Answer: D. Identifying a practical problem in one's own setting

Report this question

4Action research is best described as:

  1. A one-time linear survey
  2. A purely theoretical exercise
  3. A spiral of cycles of planning, acting, observing and reflecting
  4. A historical review
Show answer

Answer: C. A spiral of cycles of planning, acting, observing and reflecting

Report this question

5Kemmis and McTaggart are associated with:

  1. Participatory action research and the self-reflective spiral
  2. Experimental design
  3. Hypothetico-deductive testing
  4. Historical analysis
Show answer

Answer: A. Participatory action research and the self-reflective spiral

Report this question

6Match the following:
List I: (i) Fundamental, (ii) Applied, (iii) Action
List II: (P) theory development, (Q) practical use in wider settings, (R) solving a local classroom problem

  1. i-Q, ii-P, iii-R
  2. i-P, ii-Q, iii-R
  3. i-R, ii-P, iii-Q
  4. i-P, ii-R, iii-Q
Show answer

Answer: B. i-P, ii-Q, iii-R

Report this question

7Which type of research has the most immediate local application?

  1. Fundamental
  2. Action
  3. Historical
  4. Conceptual
Show answer

Answer: B. Action

Report this question

8Consider the following statements:
I. Fundamental research is concerned with generalisations.
II. Applied research is concerned with practical problems.
III. Action research aims at solving a problem in a specific situation.

  1. I, II and III
  2. I and II only
  3. II and III only
  4. I and III only
Show answer

Answer: A. I, II and III

Report this question

9Which pair is correctly matched?

  1. Applied research: practical problem-solving
  2. Fundamental research: immediate local solution
  3. Action research: grand theory building
  4. Historical research: experimentation
Show answer

Answer: A. Applied research: practical problem-solving

Report this question

10The main difference between fundamental and applied research is that:

  1. Fundamental research is always local
  2. Both are identical
  3. Neither uses data
  4. Fundamental research is driven by curiosity and theory, while applied research is driven by practical need
Show answer

Answer: D. Fundamental research is driven by curiosity and theory, while applied research is driven by practical need

Report this question

11Research to gain familiarity with a new phenomenon and generate ideas is:

  1. Evaluation research
  2. Experimental research
  3. Exploratory research
  4. Ex post facto research
Show answer

Answer: C. Exploratory research

Report this question

12Explanatory research mainly seeks to:

  1. List features of a population
  2. Collect opinions
  3. Record history
  4. Establish why something happens, i.e. cause-effect relationships
Show answer

Answer: D. Establish why something happens, i.e. cause-effect relationships

Report this question

13A descriptive survey mainly:

  1. Describes the existing status of a phenomenon
  2. Manipulates variables
  3. Tests drugs
  4. Interprets ancient texts
Show answer

Answer: A. Describes the existing status of a phenomenon

Report this question

14Descriptive research:

  1. Explains causes through experiments
  2. Describes characteristics of a population or phenomenon as it exists
  3. Interprets ancient texts
  4. Builds a grand theory
Show answer

Answer: B. Describes characteristics of a population or phenomenon as it exists

Report this question

15Correlational research examines:

  1. The effect of a manipulated independent variable
  2. Historical documents
  3. A single case
  4. The degree of relationship between variables without manipulating them
Show answer

Answer: D. The degree of relationship between variables without manipulating them

Report this question

16Survey research mainly involves:

  1. Controlled experiments
  2. Collecting data from a sample through questionnaires or interviews to learn about opinions or status
  3. Archival analysis only
  4. The case history of one person
Show answer

Answer: B. Collecting data from a sample through questionnaires or interviews to learn about opinions or status

Report this question

17A study of the same group over a long period is:

  1. Cross-sectional
  2. Case study
  3. Historical
  4. Longitudinal
Show answer

Answer: D. Longitudinal

Report this question

18A study collecting data at a single point in time is:

  1. Longitudinal
  2. Cross-sectional
  3. Cohort
  4. Panel
Show answer

Answer: B. Cross-sectional

Report this question

19Which is a type of longitudinal study?

  1. Case study
  2. Cohort study
  3. Content analysis
  4. Pilot study
Show answer

Answer: B. Cohort study

Report this question

20A trend study:

  1. Examines changes in a population over time using different samples drawn at different times
  2. Follows the same panel for years
  3. Collects data at only one point in time
  4. Studies a single case
Show answer

Answer: A. Examines changes in a population over time using different samples drawn at different times
