About this question set

This set has 15 multiple-choice questions on UK from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 10, Comparative Public Law and Systems of Governance. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key. This set is an answer-key drill: check each answer, then revisit the notes for any you miss.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Comparative Public Law notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

15 practice questions with answers

1"Parliamentary sovereignty" and the "rule of law" were classically expounded by:

  1. Bagehot
  2. Jennings
  3. A.V. Dicey
  4. Wade
Show answer

Answer: C. A.V. Dicey

Report this question

2The UK Supreme Court was established by the:

  1. Constitutional Reform Act 2005
  2. Human Rights Act 1998
  3. Supreme Court Act 1981
  4. Judicature Act 1873
Show answer

Answer: A. Constitutional Reform Act 2005

Report this question

3In the Miller/Cherry case (2019), the UK Supreme Court held that:

  1. Brexit required a fresh referendum
  2. Parliament could not be dissolved
  3. The Queen's assent was invalid
  4. The advice to prorogue Parliament was unlawful
Show answer

Answer: D. The advice to prorogue Parliament was unlawful

Report this question

4Under Section 4 of the Human Rights Act 1998, courts can:

  1. Strike down Acts of Parliament
  2. Issue a declaration of incompatibility
  3. Repeal conflicting legislation
  4. Suspend the Act
Show answer

Answer: B. Issue a declaration of incompatibility

Report this question

5Under the Parliament Act 1911, the House of Lords can delay a Money Bill for a maximum of:

  1. One year
  2. Two years
  3. One month
  4. Six months
Show answer

Answer: C. One month

Report this question

6Entick v. Carrington (1765) established that:

  1. Parliament is supreme
  2. The Crown may search at will
  3. Executive action requires legal authority
  4. Judges may legislate
Show answer

Answer: C. Executive action requires legal authority

Report this question

7In Factortame (No. 2) (1991), UK courts:

  1. Struck down an Act of Parliament
  2. Rejected EU law
  3. Abolished the Crown's prerogative
  4. Disapplied an Act of Parliament conflicting with directly effective EU law
Show answer

Answer: D. Disapplied an Act of Parliament conflicting with directly effective EU law

Report this question

8R (Miller) v. Secretary of State for Exiting the EU (2017) held that:

  1. Parliament's authorisation was required to trigger Article 50
  2. The Prime Minister alone could trigger Article 50
  3. A referendum was binding in law
  4. Scotland had a veto
Show answer

Answer: A. Parliament's authorisation was required to trigger Article 50

Report this question

9The Fixed-term Parliaments Act 2011 was repealed by the:

  1. Constitutional Reform Act 2005
  2. Dissolution and Calling of Parliament Act 2022
  3. Parliament Act 1949
  4. Human Rights Act 1998
Show answer

Answer: B. Dissolution and Calling of Parliament Act 2022

Report this question

10The Salisbury Convention provides that the House of Lords will not:

  1. Amend any bill
  2. Debate money bills
  3. Oppose at second reading a government bill implementing a manifesto commitment
  4. Sit during recess
Show answer

Answer: C. Oppose at second reading a government bill implementing a manifesto commitment

Report this question

11R (Jackson) v. Attorney General (2005) upheld the validity of the:

  1. Human Rights Act
  2. European Communities Act
  3. Parliament Act 1949
  4. Scotland Act
Show answer

Answer: C. Parliament Act 1949

Report this question

12Pickin v. British Railways Board (1974) affirms that courts cannot:

  1. Question the internal parliamentary process leading to an Act
  2. Interpret statutes
  3. Review executive action
  4. Apply common law
Show answer

Answer: A. Question the internal parliamentary process leading to an Act

Report this question

13In the GCHQ case (1985), Lord Diplock identified three grounds of judicial review: illegality, irrationality, and:

  1. Proportionality
  2. Procedural impropriety
  3. Legitimate expectation
  4. Bias
Show answer

Answer: B. Procedural impropriety

Report this question

14The Bill of Rights 1689 established the supremacy of:

  1. The Crown
  2. The Church
  3. The judiciary
  4. Parliament over the Crown
Show answer

Answer: D. Parliament over the Crown

15The Sewel Convention provides that Westminster will not normally legislate on:

  1. Foreign affairs
  2. Defence
  3. Devolved matters without the consent of the devolved legislature
  4. Taxation
