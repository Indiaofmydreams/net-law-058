This set has 20 multiple-choice questions on Constitutional & Administrative Law from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 2, Constitutional and Administrative Law. 20 practice questions with answers

1Separation of powers in India is:

  1. Strictly enforced as in the USA
  2. Applicable only to States
  3. Not rigid but recognised as part of the basic structure
  4. Absent
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Not rigid but recognised as part of the basic structure

Ram Jawaya Kapur and Indira Gandhi v. Raj Narain recognise functional separation.

Report this question

2The doctrine of pith and substance is used to determine:

  1. Quorum of the House
  2. Existence of emergency
  3. Which legislature is competent over a law
  4. Validity of ordinances
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Which legislature is competent over a law

It identifies the true nature of legislation.

Report this question

3The doctrine of colourable legislation rests on the idea that:

  1. What cannot be done directly cannot be done indirectly
  2. Law operates prospectively
  3. Courts cannot review laws
  4. Hear both sides
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. What cannot be done directly cannot be done indirectly

A legislature cannot transgress its limits by disguise.

Report this question

4The doctrine of severability means:

  1. Only the invalid separable part is struck down
  2. Statute is suspended
  3. Retrospective validation
  4. The whole statute falls if part is void
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Only the invalid separable part is struck down

Valid portions survive if independent.

Report this question

5Assertion (A): Articles 20 and 21 cannot be suspended during an Emergency.
Reason (R): The 44th Amendment bars suspension of these Articles under Article 359.

  1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true, but R is false
  4. A is false, but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A

R states the constitutional basis of A.

Report this question

6Assertion (A): The President may promulgate ordinances when Parliament is not in session.
Reason (R): Article 123 permits ordinances only when both Houses are in session.

  1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true, but R is false
  4. A is false, but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A is true, but R is false

R is false: ordinances are promulgated when Parliament is not in session.

Report this question

7Assertion (A): Decisions of Administrative Tribunals are subject to High Court review.
Reason (R): L. Chandra Kumar held judicial review is part of the basic structure.

  1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true, but R is false
  4. A is false, but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A

R explains A.

Report this question

8Consider the statements: 1. The President can be impeached for violation of the Constitution. 2. Impeachment is provided in Article 61.

  1. Only statement 1 is correct
  2. Only statement 2 is correct
  3. Both statements are correct
  4. Neither statement is correct
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Both statements are correct

Both are correct.

Report this question

9Consider the statements: 1. The Governor holds office at the pleasure of the President. 2. A Governor's five-year term is guaranteed in all circumstances.

  1. Only statement 1 is correct
  2. Only statement 2 is correct
  3. Both statements are correct
  4. Neither statement is correct
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Only statement 1 is correct

The term is subject to presidential pleasure.

Report this question

10Consider the statements: 1. The Election Commission comprises the CEC and other Election Commissioners fixed by the President. 2. The CEC is removable in the manner of a Supreme Court judge.

  1. Only statement 1 is correct
  2. Only statement 2 is correct
  3. Both statements are correct
  4. Neither statement is correct
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Both statements are correct

Both are correct under Article 324.

11Match writs: A Habeas corpus, B Certiorari, C Quo warranto, D Prohibition with (1) Forbid excess of jurisdiction (2) By what authority (3) Quash order (4) Produce the body

  1. A-3, B-4, C-1, D-2
  2. A-4, B-2, C-3, D-1
  3. A-4, B-3, C-2, D-1
  4. A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A-4, B-3, C-2, D-1

Standard meanings of the writs.

  1. A-3, B-4, C-1, D-2
  2. A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4
  3. A-4, B-3, C-2, D-1

  1. A-2, B-3, C-4, D-1
  2. A-1, B-4, C-3, D-2
  3. A-4, B-1, C-2, D-3

14A State law on 'police' incidentally touches a Union subject. Courts uphold it by applying:

  1. Double jeopardy
  2. Pith and substance
  3. Res judicata
  4. Ultra vires
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Pith and substance

Incidental encroachment is tolerated.

Report this question

15A government servant is dismissed without any hearing. Which principle is violated?

  1. Audi alteram partem
  2. Res ipsa loquitur
  3. Ejusdem generis
  4. Nemo judex
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Audi alteram partem

Right to be heard.

Report this question

16A statute is partly invalid but the valid part stands independently. The court will:

  1. Strike the whole Act
  2. Refer to the President
  3. Suspend the Act
  4. Apply severability and strike only the invalid part
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Apply severability and strike only the invalid part

Doctrine of severability.

Report this question

17An authority announces a scheme, people act on it, and it is later withdrawn arbitrarily. Which doctrine may enforce the promise?

  1. Promissory estoppel / legitimate expectation
  2. Colourable legislation
  3. Pith and substance
  4. Eclipse
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Promissory estoppel / legitimate expectation

Public law fairness doctrines.

Report this question

18Laws placed in the Ninth Schedule after 24 April 1973 can be reviewed on the basis of basic structure per:

  1. Waman Rao
  2. Ashoka Kumar Thakur
  3. Kihoto Hollohan
  4. I.R. Coelho v. State of Tamil Nadu
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. I.R. Coelho v. State of Tamil Nadu

Decided in 2007.

19The Tenth Schedule decisions of the Speaker are subject to judicial review per:

  1. Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu
  2. Minerva Mills
  3. Indira Sawhney
  4. L. Chandra Kumar
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu

Decided in 1992.

20Indira Sawhney v. Union of India is known for:

  1. Privy purses
  2. 50% reservation ceiling and creamy layer for OBCs
  3. Right to privacy
  4. Press freedom
