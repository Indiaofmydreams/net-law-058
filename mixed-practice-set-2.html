20 practice questions with answers

1Legal positivism's 'separation thesis' holds that:

  1. Law and morality are necessarily connected
  2. Law is whatever courts will do
  3. Law derives from divine will
  4. There is no necessary connection between a law's validity and its moral merit
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. There is no necessary connection between a law's validity and its moral merit

Positivists such as Austin and Hart separate what law is from what it ought to be.

Report this question

2In Hart's analysis, primary rules:

  1. Impose duties on individuals
  2. Identify valid law
  3. Regulate adjudication
  4. Confer power to change rules
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Impose duties on individuals

Secondary rules (recognition, change, adjudication) deal with the primary rules themselves.

Report this question

3Ownership differs from possession mainly because ownership is:

  1. A right of ultimate title and residual control
  2. Mere physical control
  3. Always temporary
  4. Never transferable
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A right of ultimate title and residual control

Possession is factual control; ownership is the most extensive right over a thing.

Report this question

4A company's legal personality is an example of a:

  1. Natural person
  2. Chattel
  3. Juristic (artificial) person
  4. Unincorporated body
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Juristic (artificial) person

Law creates artificial persons by fiction.

Report this question

5Assertion (A): Liability may be civil or criminal.
Reason (R): Civil liability aims at compensation while criminal liability aims at punishment.

  1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true, but R is false
  4. A is false, but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A

Remedial versus penal purposes explain the distinction.

Report this question

6Assertion (A): According to Austin, international law is positive law proper.
Reason (R): International law lacks a determinate sovereign's sanction.

  1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true, but R is false
  4. A is false, but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A is false, but R is true

Austin called it positive morality, so A is false while R is true.

Report this question

7Assertion (A): Kelsen's theory is called 'pure'.
Reason (R): It seeks to exclude sociological, political and moral elements from legal science.

  1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true, but R is false
  4. A is false, but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A

R correctly explains why the theory is termed pure.

Report this question

8Consider the statements: 1. Legal realism studies how courts actually decide cases. 2. American realists treat law as a closed system of logically deduced rules.

  1. Only statement 1 is correct
  2. Only statement 2 is correct
  3. Both statements are correct
  4. Neither statement is correct
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Only statement 1 is correct

Realists reject the closed logical system view.

Report this question

9Consider the statements: 1. A right in rem is available against the world at large. 2. A right in personam is available only against determinate persons.

  1. Only statement 1 is correct
  2. Only statement 2 is correct
  3. Both statements are correct
  4. Neither statement is correct
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Both statements are correct

Both statements correctly state the distinction.

Report this question

10Consider the statements: 1. Post-modernism in law accepts grand narratives and objective truth. 2. Post-modernists are sceptical of universal claims.

  1. Only statement 1 is correct
  2. Only statement 2 is correct
  3. Both statements are correct
  4. Neither statement is correct
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Only statement 2 is correct

Post-modernism is marked by incredulity towards grand narratives.

Report this question

11Match: A Kelsen, B Hart, C Savigny, D Pound with (1) Social engineering (2) Volksgeist (3) Grundnorm (4) Rule of recognition

  1. A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4
  2. A-3, B-4, C-1, D-2
  3. A-3, B-4, C-2, D-1
  4. A-4, B-3, C-1, D-2
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A-3, B-4, C-2, D-1

Kelsen–Grundnorm, Hart–rule of recognition, Savigny–Volksgeist, Pound–social engineering.

Report this question

12Match: A Aquinas, B Bentham, C Holmes, D Rawls with (1) Felicific calculus (2) Eternal law (3) Bad man's prediction (4) Veil of ignorance

  1. A-4, B-1, C-3, D-2
  2. A-2, B-1, C-3, D-4
  3. A-2, B-3, C-1, D-4
  4. A-1, B-2, C-4, D-3
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A-2, B-1, C-3, D-4

Each thinker is matched with a signature concept.

Report this question

13Hohfeld: match A Claim, B Privilege, C Power, D Immunity with (1) Liability (2) No-right (3) Duty (4) Disability

  1. A-1, B-4, C-3, D-2
  2. A-3, B-1, C-2, D-4
  3. A-2, B-3, C-4, D-1
  4. A-3, B-2, C-1, D-4
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A-3, B-2, C-1, D-4

Claim–duty, privilege–no-right, power–liability, immunity–disability.

Report this question

14Every law's validity is traced back through a chain to a presupposed historically first constitution. This ultimate norm is the:

  1. Sovereign command
  2. Volksgeist
  3. Social fact
  4. Grundnorm
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Grundnorm

Kelsen's Grundnorm is the presupposed basic norm.

Report this question

15An idol owns land and its shebait sues to protect it. Which concept explains the idol's standing?

  1. Juristic personality
  2. Adverse possession
  3. Vicarious liability
  4. Easement
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Juristic personality

The idol is treated as a juristic person.

Report this question

16A court balances competing interests such as security and liberty to decide a case. This reflects:

  1. Pure theory
  2. Command theory
  3. Sociological jurisprudence
  4. Historical school
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Sociological jurisprudence

Pound's theory of balancing interests.

Report this question

17A validly enacted statute is morally objectionable. A legal positivist would say it is:

  1. Invalid for immorality
  2. Valid law though possibly unjust
  3. Void ab initio
  4. Mere custom
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Valid law though possibly unjust

Validity depends on source, not on moral merit.

Report this question

18'Law as integrity' is associated with:

  1. H.L.A. Hart
  2. Ronald Dworkin
  3. John Finnis
  4. Joseph Raz
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Ronald Dworkin

Dworkin's interpretive theory rejects strict positivism.

Report this question

19'Natural Law and Natural Rights' (1980) is by:

  1. John Finnis
  2. Robert Nozick
  3. Lon Fuller
  4. John Rawls
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. John Finnis

Finnis revived natural law through basic goods.

Report this question

20The 'entitlement theory' of justice appears in 'Anarchy, State, and Utopia' by:

  1. Ronald Dworkin
  2. Amartya Sen
  3. Robert Nozick
  4. John Rawls
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Robert Nozick

Nozick defends a minimal state and just acquisition and transfer.
