9 practice questions with answers

1Which Article empowers the Parliament to legislate on State List subjects in the national interest?

  1. Article 252
  2. Article 253
  3. Article 249
  4. Article 250
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Article 249

Article 249 empowers Parliament to legislate on State List subjects if the Rajya Sabha passes a resolution by 2/3 majority declaring it is necessary or expedient in the national interest.

2'Colorable Legislation' refers to a situation where:

  1. The legislature tries to do indirectly what it cannot do directly
  2. Legislation is unconstitutional due to mala fide intent
  3. Legislation uses colorful/ambiguous language
  4. Both A and B
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The legislature tries to do indirectly what it cannot do directly

Colorable legislation means the legislature, though apparently acting within its powers, in substance encroaches upon the field reserved for the other legislature. Leading case: K.C. Gajapati Narayan Deo v. State of Orissa.

3The Union List, State List, and Concurrent List are contained in which Schedule of the Constitution?

  1. Fifth Schedule
  2. Seventh Schedule
  3. Sixth Schedule
  4. Eighth Schedule
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Seventh Schedule

The Seventh Schedule contains three lists: List I (Union List — 97 entries), List II (State List — 66 entries), and List III (Concurrent List — 47 entries). Currently List I has 100 entries.

4In case of repugnancy between a Union law and a State law on a Concurrent List subject, which prevails under Article 254?

  1. President decides
  2. State law always prevails
  3. The later law prevails regardless of maker
  4. Union law prevails
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Union law prevails

Under Article 254(1), if there is a repugnancy between Union and State laws on a Concurrent List subject, the Union law prevails and the State law is void to the extent of repugnancy. Exception under Article 254(2): if the State law has received Presidential assent, it prevails in that State.

5The concept of 'Implied Powers' in constitutional interpretation means:

  1. Powers not mentioned anywhere in the Constitution
  2. Emergency powers
  3. Residuary powers
  4. Powers that can be inferred from express provisions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Powers that can be inferred from express provisions

Implied powers are those which are not expressly mentioned but are necessary to carry out the express powers. The doctrine is used to fill gaps in legislative competence.

Report this question

6Residuary powers under Article 248 vest in:

  1. President
  2. Union Parliament
  3. Concurrent List
  4. States
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Union Parliament

Article 248 gives Parliament exclusive power to make any law with respect to any matter not enumerated in the Concurrent or State List (residuary powers). Entry 97 of List I also confirms residuary powers.

Report this question

7Which of the following is in the Union List (List I, Seventh Schedule)?

  1. Public order
  2. Police
  3. Defence of India
  4. Agriculture
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Defence of India

Defence of India is Entry 1 of the Union List. Public order and Police are in the State List (List II). Agriculture is in the State List (Entry 14). Education is in the Concurrent List (Entry 25, after 42nd Amendment).

Report this question

8'Education' was originally in which List? After which Amendment was it moved to the Concurrent List?

  1. State List; moved to Concurrent List by 42nd Amendment, 1976
  2. Always in Concurrent List
  3. Union List; moved by 44th Amendment
  4. State List; moved by 86th Amendment
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. State List; moved to Concurrent List by 42nd Amendment, 1976

Originally, 'Education' was in the State List (List II). The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 shifted 'Education' including universities to Entry 25 of the Concurrent List, enabling both Parliament and State Legislatures to legislate on it.

9The 'Three Lists' system of legislative distribution in the Indian Constitution was immediately borrowed from:

  1. USA
  2. Australia
  3. Canada
  4. Government of India Act, 1935
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Government of India Act, 1935

The three-list scheme (Federal List, Provincial List, Concurrent List) was first introduced in the Government of India Act, 1935. The Constitution carried this forward as Union List, State List, and Concurrent List in the Seventh Schedule. Canada's Constitution had two lists; the US has enumerated + reserved powers.
