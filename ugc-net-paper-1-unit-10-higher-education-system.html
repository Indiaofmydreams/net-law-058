UGC NET Paper 1 Unit 10: Higher Education System Notes

This unit covers how Indian higher education grew from Nalanda to NEP 2020 and which bodies now regulate it. Questions are mainly about names, years and the role of each body. These notes give the timeline and regulators in a form that is quick to revise for candidates from any subject.

What Unit 10 covers

Higher Education System questions are about names and years, so a clean timeline and regulator table will carry most of the unit. The official syllabus lists 6 topics for this unit. Each is explained below with the points examiners most often use.

1Institutions of higher learning and education in ancient India

  • Takshashila (Taxila) was in present-day Pakistan, near Rawalpindi. Nalanda Mahavihara and Vikramashila were in present-day Bihar.
  • Vallabhi in Gujarat and Odantapuri in Bihar were other known centres. Nalanda drew students from several countries.
  • Education was oral at first, and there were Gurukulas and Buddhist viharas. Debate, discussion and memorisation were common methods.
  • Subjects included the Vedas, grammar, logic, medicine, mathematics, astronomy and philosophy.

2Evolution of higher learning and research in post-independence India

  • Macaulay's Minute (1835) favoured English education. Wood's Despatch (1854) is called the Magna Carta of English education in India and led to the universities of Calcutta, Bombay and Madras in 1857.
  • University Education Commission or Radhakrishnan Commission (1948-49) reviewed university education. The University Grants Commission (UGC) was set up on the basis of its advice.
  • Kothari Commission (1964-66) linked education to national development and recommended the 10+2+3 structure.
  • National Policy on Education 1986 (revised 1992) was replaced by the National Education Policy 2020.

3Oriental, conventional and non-conventional learning programmes

  • Oriental learning refers to traditional Indian systems such as Sanskrit, Persian and Arabic studies and Ayurveda.
  • Conventional programmes are classroom-based degrees in colleges and universities.
  • Non-conventional programmes include open and distance learning, online and blended learning, such as IGNOU (established 1985), state open universities, SWAYAM and MOOCs.
  • The Distance Education Bureau under UGC and the open universities support quality in distance learning.

4Professional, technical and skill-based education

  • Technical education is regulated by the All India Council for Technical Education (AICTE), which includes engineering, management and pharmacy. IITs and NITs are institutions of national importance.
  • Professional education is regulated by bodies such as the Bar Council of India (legal), the National Medical Commission (medical) and the National Council for Teacher Education (teacher education).
  • Skill education is promoted through the National Skills Qualifications Framework and schemes such as Skill India.
  • NEP 2020 aims to integrate vocational education into mainstream education.

5Value education and environmental education

  • Value education develops ethical, moral and social values such as honesty, empathy, tolerance and responsibility.
  • Education Commissions and NEP 2020 stress constitutional values, ethics and service to society as part of the curriculum.
  • Environmental education is compulsory at school and college level under a Supreme Court direction (2003).
  • Teachers act as role models, so value education works best through example as well as lessons.

6Policies, governance and administration: structure of higher education and regulatory bodies

  • Higher education is on the Concurrent List. Central universities are established by an Act of Parliament, and state universities by an Act of the State legislature. Deemed and private universities follow separate rules.
  • UGC (statutory body in 1956) coordinates and maintains standards and gives grants to universities. AICTE (statutory in 1987) regulates technical education. NCTE (statutory in 1995) regulates teacher education.
  • NAAC (1994) assesses and accredits institutions. NIRF (2015) is the framework for ranking. AISHE is the annual All India Survey on Higher Education.
  • NEP 2020 proposes a single regulator, the Higher Education Commission of India (HECI), a flexible four-year undergraduate programme with multiple entry and exit, and the Academic Bank of Credits.

How to study this unit

  • Make one timeline from Wood's Despatch to NEP 2020 and add each commission and Act to it. Questions on years become easy.
  • For regulators, learn the pair body and field, for example AICTE and technical, NCTE and teacher, BCI and law.
  • Read the main headings of NEP 2020, such as HECI, multiple entry and exit and Academic Bank of Credits, as they are likely to be asked.
