20 practice questions with answers

1The rule in Rylands v. Fletcher (1868) established:

  1. Absolute liability
  2. Strict liability
  3. Vicarious liability
  4. Fault-based liability
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Strict liability

Rylands v. Fletcher established strict liability for non-natural use of land — with exceptions (act of God, consent, etc.).

Report this question

2The Supreme Court of India laid down the principle of 'Absolute Liability' (no exceptions) in:

  1. Rylands v. Fletcher
  2. Donoghue v. Stevenson
  3. Salomon v. Salomon
  4. MC Mehta v. Union of India (Oleum Gas Leak case)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. MC Mehta v. Union of India (Oleum Gas Leak case)

In MC Mehta v. Union of India (1987), the Supreme Court evolved absolute liability — stricter than Rylands v. Fletcher with no exceptions.

Report this question

3'Damnum sine injuria' means:

  1. Injury without damage
  2. Legal injury with damage
  3. Mental injury without physical harm
  4. Damage without legal injury
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Damage without legal injury

Damnum sine injuria = damage without legal injury — not actionable in law.

Report this question

4'Injuria sine damnum' means:

  1. Damage without legal injury
  2. Legal injury without actual damage
  3. Self-inflicted damage
  4. Damage to property only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Legal injury without actual damage

Injuria sine damnum = legal right violated without actual damage — actionable (e.g., Ashby v. White).

Report this question

5The case of Ashby v. White (1703) illustrates which principle?

  1. Damnum sine injuria
  2. Injuria sine damnum
  3. Volenti non fit injuria
  4. Res ipsa loquitur
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Injuria sine damnum

Ashby v. White: A voter was prevented from voting (legal right violated) though no actual damage was proved — actionable.

Report this question

6'Actus non facit reum nisi mens sit rea' means:

  1. Every act is criminal
  2. An act does not make a person guilty unless there is a guilty mind
  3. Strict liability applies to all acts
  4. Only acts cause liability, not omissions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. An act does not make a person guilty unless there is a guilty mind

This fundamental maxim establishes that criminal liability requires both act (actus reus) and guilty mind (mens rea).

Report this question

7Vicarious liability in tort is based on the principle of:

  1. 'Volenti non fit injuria' — consent negates liability
  2. 'Damnum sine injuria'
  3. 'Respondeat superior' — let the master answer
  4. 'Novus actus interveniens'
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 'Respondeat superior' — let the master answer

Respondeat superior: an employer is vicariously liable for torts committed by employees in course of employment.

Report this question

8Which of the following is NOT an exception to the rule of strict liability under Rylands v. Fletcher?

  1. Ignorance of the defendant
  2. Act of God
  3. Consent of the plaintiff
  4. Act of a stranger/third party
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Ignorance of the defendant

Ignorance of the defendant is not a recognised exception to strict or absolute liability.

Report this question

9'Volenti non fit injuria' means:

  1. Voluntary acts are always lawful
  2. Damage must be proved for liability
  3. Employer is liable for employee's acts
  4. No legal injury is done to a person who consents
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. No legal injury is done to a person who consents

Volenti non fit injuria: consent of the plaintiff is a complete defence to tortious liability.

Report this question

10The distinction between 'civil liability' and 'criminal liability' is that:

  1. Civil liability involves only fines
  2. Criminal liability is enforced by the victim
  3. Civil liability is remedial; criminal liability is penal/punitive
  4. There is no distinction in modern law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Civil liability is remedial; criminal liability is penal/punitive

Civil liability aims to compensate; criminal liability aims to punish and deter.

Report this question

11The maxim 'Actori incumbit onus probandi' relates to:

  1. Legal personality of minors
  2. Burden of proof lies on the person who makes an assertion
  3. Presumption of innocence
  4. Corporate liability
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Burden of proof lies on the person who makes an assertion

This maxim means the burden of proof lies on the actor/claimant who makes the assertion.

Report this question

12The principle 'res ipsa loquitur' means:

  1. The defendant is always liable
  2. The thing speaks for itself — negligence is inferred from the very nature of the accident
  3. Strict liability applies without exception
  4. The plaintiff must always prove fault
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The thing speaks for itself — negligence is inferred from the very nature of the accident

Res ipsa loquitur: the accident speaks for itself — negligence is inferred without specific proof.

Report this question

13'Novus actus interveniens' means:

  1. A new act by the plaintiff breaks the chain of causation
  2. A new intervening act by a third party breaks the chain of causation, relieving the defendant
  3. The defendant's act was the sole cause
  4. Contributory negligence by plaintiff
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A new intervening act by a third party breaks the chain of causation, relieving the defendant

Novus actus interveniens: a new intervening act severs the causal link between defendant's act and plaintiff's damage.

Report this question

14'Contributory negligence' as a defence means:

  1. The plaintiff was entirely at fault
  2. The plaintiff's own negligence contributed to the damage
  3. The defendant had no duty of care
  4. The damage was unforeseeable
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The plaintiff's own negligence contributed to the damage

Contributory negligence: plaintiff's own fault contributed to the damage — reduces or negates defendant's liability.

Report this question

15'Mens rea' is NOT required in which of the following?

  1. Offences of strict liability
  2. Murder
  3. Theft
  4. Cheating
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Offences of strict liability

Strict liability offences do not require proof of mental element — the act alone is sufficient.

Report this question

16Criminal liability requires which of the following elements?

  1. Actus reus only
  2. Mens rea only
  3. Both actus reus and mens rea (generally)
  4. Neither actus reus nor mens rea
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Both actus reus and mens rea (generally)

Generally, criminal liability requires both actus reus (guilty act) and mens rea (guilty mind).

Report this question

17The concept of 'vicarious criminal liability' of a master for servant's acts is:

  1. The general rule in criminal law
  2. Not recognised in any jurisdiction
  3. An exception, applied narrowly (e.g., licensing offences, public nuisance)
  4. Same as civil vicarious liability
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. An exception, applied narrowly (e.g., licensing offences, public nuisance)

Vicarious criminal liability is an exception — applied in licensing and regulatory offences.

Report this question

18'Strict liability' in criminal law means:

  1. The accused must have intended the crime
  2. Negligence must be proved
  3. The act alone is sufficient for liability — no mens rea is required
  4. Liability is imposed only by a court of appeal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The act alone is sufficient for liability — no mens rea is required

Strict liability: the act itself makes a person liable without proof of any mental element.

Report this question

19The 'neighbour principle' in the law of negligence was laid down in:

  1. Donoghue v. Stevenson (1932)
  2. Rylands v. Fletcher
  3. Ashby v. White
  4. Salomon v. Salomon
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Donoghue v. Stevenson (1932)

Lord Atkin in Donoghue v. Stevenson (1932): you owe a duty of care to your 'neighbour' — those reasonably foreseeably affected by your acts.

Report this question

20Which of the following is NOT a defence to tortious liability?

  1. Volenti non fit injuria
  2. Act of God (Vis major)
  3. Statutory authority
  4. Ignorance of law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Ignorance of law

Ignorantia juris non excusat — ignorance of law is no excuse and is not a defence in tort.
