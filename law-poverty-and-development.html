About this question set

This set has 9 multiple-choice questions on Law, Poverty and Development from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 1, Jurisprudence. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 9 of the 9 questions carry a written explanation.

9 practice questions with answers

1The concept of 'Law and Development' movement emerged primarily in:

  1. The 1940s
  2. The 1960s–1970s
  3. The 1990s
  4. The 2000s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The 1960s–1970s

The Law and Development movement emerged in the 1960s-70s, focused on using law to transplant development models.

Report this question

2The 'rule of law' as a tool for development requires which of the following?

  1. Executive supremacy
  2. Predictable, accessible, and equally applied legal norms
  3. Restriction of judicial review
  4. Sovereign immunity from law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Predictable, accessible, and equally applied legal norms

Rule of law as a development tool requires predictability, access, and equal application of norms.

Report this question

3Amartya Sen's 'capability approach' in relation to poverty and law argues that:

  1. Development is the expansion of real freedoms/capabilities that people enjoy
  2. GDP growth alone measures development
  3. Law is irrelevant to poverty reduction
  4. Poverty is purely an economic phenomenon
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Development is the expansion of real freedoms/capabilities that people enjoy

Sen's capability approach: development = expansion of substantive freedoms and capabilities.

Report this question

4The concept of 'access to justice' as a tool against poverty includes:

  1. Abolishing courts
  2. Privatising the legal system
  3. Making courts faster and cheaper for the poor
  4. Limiting PIL jurisdiction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Making courts faster and cheaper for the poor

Access to justice means making legal remedies available, affordable and effective for the poor.

Report this question

5Legal Aid to the poor in India is a constitutional mandate under Article:

  1. 14
  2. 21
  3. 39A
  4. 32
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 39A

Article 39A DPSP mandates equal justice and free legal aid; implemented through the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987.

Report this question

6The 'Law and Development' movement's first wave (1960s) primarily aimed at:

  1. Using law as an instrument to transplant Western legal institutions in developing countries to promote economic growth
  2. Codifying customary law
  3. Abolishing colonial law
  4. Establishing international courts
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Using law as an instrument to transplant Western legal institutions in developing countries to promote economic growth

The first wave of Law and Development sought to use law to transplant Western liberal institutions to promote development.

Report this question

7'Access to justice' as a movement was theorised by Mauro Cappelletti and Bryant Garth through their famous:

  1. Florence Project
  2. Harvard Project
  3. Yale Project
  4. Oxford Project
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Florence Project

The Florence Access-to-Justice Project (1970s) identified three 'waves' of access to justice reform.

Report this question

8PIL (Public Interest Litigation) in India is primarily a tool for:

  1. Giving access to justice to the poor and marginalised
  2. Corporate litigation
  3. Resolving commercial disputes
  4. Challenging international law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Giving access to justice to the poor and marginalised

PIL in India was developed to give access to justice to those unable to approach courts — a judge-made tool for the poor.

Report this question

9'Human Development Index' was developed by:

  1. World Bank
  2. UNDP (United Nations Development Programme)
  3. IMF
  4. WTO
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. UNDP (United Nations Development Programme)

HDI was developed by UNDP, conceptualised by Mahbub ul Haq with input from Amartya Sen.
