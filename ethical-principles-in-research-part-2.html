About this question set

This set has 25 multiple-choice questions on Ethical Principles in Research from the UGC NET Paper 1 syllabus, unit 2, Research Aptitude. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key. This set is an answer-key drill: check each answer, then revisit the notes for any you miss.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Research Aptitude notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

25 practice questions with answers

1"Minimal risk" means:

  1. No risk whatsoever
  2. Risk of death
  3. Risk no greater than that ordinarily encountered in daily life or routine examinations
  4. Risk that is high but accepted
Show answer

Answer: C. Risk no greater than that ordinarily encountered in daily life or routine examinations

Report this question

2A conflict of interest in research is:

  1. A situation in which personal or financial interests may influence professional judgement
  2. A disagreement between two researchers
  3. A funding mismatch
  4. Failure of equipment
Show answer

Answer: A. A situation in which personal or financial interests may influence professional judgement

Report this question

3Researchers should:

  1. Hide conflicts of interest
  2. Disclose conflicts of interest to ethics committees, sponsors and journals
  3. Disclose them only to friends
  4. Never think about them
Show answer

Answer: B. Disclose conflicts of interest to ethics committees, sponsors and journals

Report this question

4Fabrication in research means:

  1. Making up data or results and recording or reporting them
  2. Altering equipment
  3. Copying text
  4. Citing wrongly
Show answer

Answer: A. Making up data or results and recording or reporting them

Report this question

5Falsification means:

  1. Inventing data
  2. Copying words
  3. Publishing twice
  4. Manipulating research materials, processes or data, or omitting data, so that the research is not accurately represented
Show answer

Answer: D. Manipulating research materials, processes or data, or omitting data, so that the research is not accurately represented

Report this question

6FFP in research misconduct stands for:

  1. Funding, Fairness and Privacy
  2. Findings, Facts and Proof
  3. Fraud, Fees and Piracy
  4. Fabrication, Falsification and Plagiarism
Show answer

Answer: D. Fabrication, Falsification and Plagiarism

Report this question

7Which of the following is NOT regarded as research misconduct?

  1. Fabrication
  2. Falsification
  3. Honest error or a genuine difference of opinion
  4. Plagiarism
Show answer

Answer: C. Honest error or a genuine difference of opinion

Report this question

8The ICMJE recommends that authorship be based on how many criteria, all of which must be met?

  1. 2
  2. 3
  3. 4
  4. 5
Show answer

Answer: C. 4

Report this question

9Ghost authorship refers to:

  1. A person who made a substantial contribution but is not named as an author
  2. An author who has died
  3. An honorary author with no contribution
  4. An anonymous reviewer
Show answer

Answer: A. A person who made a substantial contribution but is not named as an author

Report this question

10Gift or honorary authorship means:

  1. Naming all contributors
  2. Naming only the guide
  3. Using a pseudonym
  4. Naming someone as author who made no substantial contribution
Show answer

Answer: D. Naming someone as author who made no substantial contribution

Report this question

11Duplicate (redundant) publication is:

  1. Translating a paper with the journal's permission
  2. Citing a paper twice
  3. Publishing substantially the same work in more than one journal without disclosure
  4. Reviewing a paper twice
Show answer

Answer: C. Publishing substantially the same work in more than one journal without disclosure

Report this question

12"Salami slicing" in publishing means:

  1. Dividing one study into several thin publications to inflate the publication count
  2. Cutting samples into slices
  3. Combining many studies into one
  4. Slicing data for analysis
Show answer

Answer: A. Dividing one study into several thin publications to inflate the publication count

Report this question

13A retraction is:

  1. Publication of a revised edition
  2. Withdrawal of funding
  3. Another term for peer review
  4. The formal withdrawal of a published article because of serious errors or misconduct
Show answer

Answer: D. The formal withdrawal of a published article because of serious errors or misconduct

Report this question

14COPE stands for:

  1. Council of Publishing Experts
  2. Centre for Original Publications and Education
  3. Committee on Publication Ethics
  4. Committee of Peer Editors
Show answer

Answer: C. Committee on Publication Ethics

15A peer reviewer's ethical duty includes:

  1. Sharing the manuscript with colleagues
  2. Using the author's ideas in own work
  3. Delaying the review deliberately
  4. Keeping the manuscript confidential and declaring conflicts of interest
Show answer

Answer: D. Keeping the manuscript confidential and declaring conflicts of interest

16A whistleblower is a person who:

  1. Plagiarises
  2. Edits a journal
  3. Funds a study
  4. Reports suspected misconduct in good faith

Answer: D. Reports suspected misconduct in good faith

17The "3Rs" in animal research stand for:

  1. Record, Report, Review
  2. Replacement, Reduction, Refinement
  3. Rights, Rules, Regulations
  4. Read, Recite, Revise
Show answer

Answer: B. Replacement, Reduction, Refinement

18In India, experiments on animals are regulated by the:

  1. Committee for Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals (CCSEA)
  2. Central Drugs Authority
  3. UGC
  4. Press Council
Show answer

Answer: A. Committee for Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals (CCSEA)

19Which set lists the core values of research integrity?

  1. Speed, profit and fame
  2. Secrecy, loyalty and rank
  3. Popularity, citations and funding
  4. Honesty, rigour, transparency, accountability and respect
Show answer

Answer: D. Honesty, rigour, transparency, accountability and respect

20The Singapore Statement (2010) is concerned with:

  1. A citation style
  2. Principles and responsibilities for research integrity
  3. Plagiarism software
  4. Statistical tests
Show answer

Answer: B. Principles and responsibilities for research integrity

Report this question

21Open science and data sharing mainly promote:

  1. Transparency, reproducibility and scrutiny
  2. Secrecy
  3. Plagiarism
  4. Higher publication fees
Show answer

Answer: A. Transparency, reproducibility and scrutiny

Report this question

22HARKing means:

  1. Harvesting data from the internet
  2. Hiring assistants
  3. Hypothesising after the results are known and presenting the hypothesis as if it was made in advance
  4. Harmonising references
Show answer

Answer: C. Hypothesising after the results are known and presenting the hypothesis as if it was made in advance

Report this question

23p-hacking refers to:

  1. Manipulating analyses or data choices until a statistically significant result appears
  2. Using more references
  3. Protecting participant data
  4. A peer review method
Show answer

Answer: A. Manipulating analyses or data choices until a statistically significant result appears

Report this question

24Cherry-picking data means:

  1. Selecting the best participants ethically
  2. Reporting only the data that support the hypothesis and ignoring the rest
  3. Randomising participants
  4. Drawing a stratified sample
Show answer

Answer: B. Reporting only the data that support the hypothesis and ignoring the rest

Report this question

25The "replication crisis" is ethically significant because:

  1. It proves all research is fraud
  2. It ends the need for peer review
  3. It concerns only chemistry
  4. It shows that many published findings could not be reproduced, stressing the need for rigour and transparency
Show answer

Answer: D. It shows that many published findings could not be reproduced, stressing the need for rigour and transparency
