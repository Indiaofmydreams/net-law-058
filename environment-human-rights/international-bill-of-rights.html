18 practice questions with answers

1The International Bill of Rights consists of:

  1. UDHR, ICCPR, and ICESCR only
  2. UDHR, ICCPR, ICESCR, and the Optional Protocol to ICCPR
  3. UN Charter, UDHR, and ICCPR
  4. ICCPR, ICESCR, and their Optional Protocols only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. UDHR, ICCPR, ICESCR, and the Optional Protocol to ICCPR

The International Bill of Human Rights collectively refers to: UDHR (1948), ICCPR (1966, in force 1976) with its First Optional Protocol (individual complaints) and Second Optional Protocol (death penalty abolition), and ICESCR (1966, in force 1976).

2The Human Rights Committee (HRC) was established under:

  1. UN Charter
  2. ICESCR
  3. ICCPR Article 28
  4. Vienna Declaration 1993
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. ICCPR Article 28

Article 28 of the ICCPR established the Human Rights Committee - a body of 18 independent experts monitoring implementation of the ICCPR. Under the First Optional Protocol, individuals can submit complaints to the HRC.

3Article 4 of ICCPR allows derogation from rights only:

  1. In all circumstances when the state so decides
  2. Only during natural disasters
  3. During times of public emergency threatening the life of the nation, with notification to UN Secretary-General
  4. During times of economic crisis
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. During times of public emergency threatening the life of the nation, with notification to UN Secretary-General

ICCPR Article 4: States may derogate during public emergency threatening the life of the nation, with notification to UN Secretary-General. Non-derogable rights include Articles 6, 7, 8, 11, 15, 16, 18.

4ICESCR rights are subject to 'progressive realization', which means:

  1. States must immediately implement all rights fully
  2. Rights are merely aspirational and have no binding force
  3. Rights apply only in economically developed countries
  4. States must take steps to the maximum of available resources to achieve rights progressively
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. States must take steps to the maximum of available resources to achieve rights progressively

ICESCR Article 2(1): Each state party undertakes to take steps, to the maximum of its available resources, with a view to achieving progressively the full realization of rights. States must not deliberately retrogress.

5The Second Optional Protocol to ICCPR aims at:

  1. Protecting women from discrimination
  2. Rights of indigenous peoples
  3. Individual complaint mechanism
  4. Abolition of the death penalty
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Abolition of the death penalty

Second Optional Protocol to the ICCPR (1989) aims at abolition of the death penalty. States party to it may not execute any person. India has NOT ratified this Protocol and retains capital punishment for 'rarest of rare' cases.

6The right of self-determination appears in both ICCPR and ICESCR in:

  1. Article 2 of both
  2. Article 27 of ICCPR and Article 15 of ICESCR
  3. Article 1 of both
  4. Preamble only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Article 1 of both

Both ICCPR Article 1 and ICESCR Article 1 identically state: 'All peoples have the right of self-determination.' This is the one right common to both Covenants.

7Which body monitors the implementation of the ICESCR?

  1. Committee on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (CESCR)
  2. Human Rights Committee
  3. ECOSOC directly
  4. UN High Commissioner for Human Rights
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Committee on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (CESCR)

The Committee on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (CESCR) - 18 independent experts - monitors implementation of the ICESCR. Established by ECOSOC Resolution 1985/17. Under Optional Protocol to ICESCR (2008), individuals can submit communications.

8The UDHR was adopted without a single vote against it. How many abstentions were there?

  1. 0
  2. 5
  3. 8
  4. 12
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 8

The UDHR was adopted on 10 December 1948 with 48 votes in favour, 0 against, and 8 abstentions. Abstaining countries: Saudi Arabia, South Africa, and 6 Soviet bloc countries (USSR, Ukrainian SSR, Byelorussian SSR, Poland, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia).

9The Optional Protocol to the ICESCR (2008) allows:

  1. States to opt out of ICESCR obligations
  2. Only inter-state complaints
  3. Inquiry procedure only for torture
  4. Individual and group communications to the CESCR for violations
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Individual and group communications to the CESCR for violations

The Optional Protocol to the ICESCR (adopted 2008, in force 2013) allows individuals and groups to submit communications to the CESCR alleging violations of their ICESCR rights. It also provides for an inquiry procedure and inter-state complaints mechanism.

Report this question

10Article 18 of the ICCPR protects freedom of thought, conscience and religion. This right is:

  1. Non-derogable even in times of public emergency
  2. Fully derogable in emergencies
  3. Derogable with Security Council permission
  4. Only applicable to majority religions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Non-derogable even in times of public emergency

Article 18 ICCPR (freedom of thought, conscience and religion, including forum internum) is listed in Article 4(2) as a non-derogable right. The internal dimension (forum internum) is absolute. External manifestation (forum externum) may be restricted on grounds of public safety, order, health, morals or rights of others.

11The 'Limburg Principles' (1986) and 'Maastricht Guidelines' (1997) relate to:

  1. Interpretation and application of the ICCPR
  2. Interpretation of the UN Charter
  3. Refugee law principles
  4. Nature and scope of state obligations under the ICESCR
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Nature and scope of state obligations under the ICESCR

The Limburg Principles (1986) and Maastricht Guidelines (1997) are expert guidelines clarifying state obligations under ICESCR - including the 'minimum core obligations' concept and what constitutes a violation of ICESCR.

Report this question

12Article 2(1) of the ICCPR requires each state party to:

  1. Adopt a Bill of Rights in its constitution
  2. Report to the Human Rights Committee every 2 years
  3. Respect and ensure ICCPR rights to all individuals within its territory and subject to its jurisdiction
  4. Ratify all Optional Protocols to ICCPR
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Respect and ensure ICCPR rights to all individuals within its territory and subject to its jurisdiction

Article 2(1) ICCPR: Each State Party undertakes to respect and to ensure to all individuals within its territory and subject to its jurisdiction the rights recognized in the Covenant, without distinction of any kind. The 'ensure' obligation requires positive steps, not just refraining from violations.

13The minimum core obligations under ICESCR means:

  1. States must fully realize all socioeconomic rights immediately
  2. Only developed states have binding ICESCR obligations
  3. Obligations are limited to non-discrimination only
  4. States must satisfy at least a minimum essential level of each right regardless of available resources
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. States must satisfy at least a minimum essential level of each right regardless of available resources

'Minimum core obligations' was developed by CESCR in General Comment No. 3 (1990): even where resources are severely limited, a State Party must ensure minimum essential levels of each right - minimum essential food, primary healthcare, basic shelter. Failure is prima facie a violation.

Report this question

14The ICCPR protects the rights of minorities under:

  1. Article 27
  2. Article 18
  3. Article 20
  4. Article 25
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Article 27

Article 27 ICCPR: In those States in which ethnic, religious or linguistic minorities exist, persons belonging to such minorities shall not be denied the right, in community with other members of their group, to enjoy their own culture, to profess and practise their own religion, or to use their own language.

15The 'travaux preparatoires' of the UDHR show that the drafting committee included:

  1. Only Western nations drafted the UDHR
  2. All 58 UN member states at the time actively participated in drafting
  3. The drafting took 10 years
  4. The drafting committee had diverse representation - China, Lebanon, France, USA among others including India's Hansa Mehta
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The drafting committee had diverse representation - China, Lebanon, France, USA among others including India's Hansa Mehta

The UDHR drafting committee had diverse membership including: Eleanor Roosevelt (USA), Rene Cassin (France), Peng Chun Chang (China), Charles Malik (Lebanon), Hansa Mehta (India - who ensured 'all human beings' instead of 'all men'). This diversity counters the claim that UDHR is purely a Western document.

16Under the ICCPR, the right to life (Article 6) is interpreted to impose on states:

  1. Only negative obligation not to take life arbitrarily
  2. Both negative (not to kill arbitrarily) and positive (to take measures to protect life against threats including environmental hazards) obligations
  3. Only obligation to abolish the death penalty
  4. Only obligation to prevent war
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Both negative (not to kill arbitrarily) and positive (to take measures to protect life against threats including environmental hazards) obligations

The Human Rights Committee's General Comment 36 on Article 6 (2018) gave the right to life its broadest interpretation: states must not arbitrarily deprive of life AND must take positive measures to protect life - including against threats from private actors, environmental degradation, climate change, and industrial pollution.

Report this question

17A 'Covenant' was chosen as the form for ICCPR and ICESCR (rather than a declaration) because:

  1. A Covenant is a legally binding treaty - unlike a declaration, it creates binding obligations on ratifying states
  2. Declarations are not recognized in international law
  3. Declarations cannot be adopted by the General Assembly
  4. The term Covenant has religious significance appropriate for rights instruments
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A Covenant is a legally binding treaty - unlike a declaration, it creates binding obligations on ratifying states

Both ICCPR and ICESCR are in the form of international treaties (Covenants) that create binding legal obligations on state parties who ratify them. The UDHR is a declaration - a statement of aspirations. Choosing the Covenant form gave the rights legally enforceable character with monitoring mechanisms.

Report this question

18Which of the following is a non-treaty-based (Charter-based) human rights body?

  1. Human Rights Committee (HRC)
  2. CEDAW Committee
  3. Committee on the Rights of the Child
  4. UN Human Rights Council
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. UN Human Rights Council

The UN Human Rights Council is a Charter-based body - it derives its authority from the UN Charter, not from a specific human rights treaty. Treaty-based bodies (Human Rights Committee, CESCR, CEDAW Committee etc.) derive their authority from their respective treaties.
