15 practice questions with answers

1Which article imposes a Fundamental Duty on citizens to protect the natural environment?

  1. Article 48A
  2. Article 51A(g)
  3. Article 21
  4. Article 19(1)(g)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Article 51A(g)

Article 51A(g): 'It shall be the duty of every citizen of India to protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers and wildlife and to have compassion for living creatures.' Article 48A is the corresponding Directive Principle.

2The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act was enacted in:

  1. 1972
  2. 1974
  3. 1981
  4. 1986
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1974

Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act 1974 was India's first comprehensive environmental protection legislation. It established the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) and State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs).

Report this question

3The 42nd Amendment 1976 inserted which entries in the Concurrent List relating to environment?

  1. Entry 17 - Forests
  2. Entry 20 - Economic and Social Planning
  3. Entry 17A - Forests and Entry 17B - Protection of wild animals and birds
  4. Entry 13 - Pilgrimages to places outside India
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Entry 17A - Forests and Entry 17B - Protection of wild animals and birds

The 42nd Amendment 1976 inserted Entry 17A (Forests) and Entry 17B (Protection of wild animals and birds) in the Concurrent List (List III), enabling both Parliament and State Legislatures to legislate on these subjects.

4The doctrine of 'Absolute Liability' was established in:

  1. Rylands v. Fletcher, 1868
  2. Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum v. UOI, 1996
  3. Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action v. UOI, 1996
  4. M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Oleum Gas Leak), 1987
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Oleum Gas Leak), 1987

In the Oleum Gas Leak case (M.C. Mehta v. UOI, 1987), the Supreme Court laid down the doctrine of Absolute Liability - departing from Rylands v. Fletcher - no exceptions whatsoever apply.

5The Public Trust Doctrine was applied by the Indian Supreme Court in:

  1. Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum v. UOI, 1996
  2. Rural Litigation Kendra v. State of UP, 1985
  3. Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action, 1996
  4. M.C. Mehta v. Kamal Nath, 1997
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. M.C. Mehta v. Kamal Nath, 1997

In M.C. Mehta v. Kamal Nath (1997), the SC applied the Public Trust Doctrine to hold that the State Government had committed a patent breach of public trust by leasing the bank of River Beas to a private resort.

Report this question

6The Forest Conservation Act 1980 requires prior approval of whom for diversion of forest land?

  1. State Government
  2. Supreme Court
  3. National Green Tribunal
  4. Central Government
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Central Government

Forest Conservation Act 1980, Section 2: No State Government or other authority shall make, except with prior approval of the Central Government, any order directing that any forest land shall be used for any non-forest purpose.

Report this question

7In Rural Litigation and Entitlement Kendra v. State of UP (1985), the Supreme Court ordered:

  1. Compensation for air pollution victims
  2. Closure of limestone quarries in the Mussoorie/Doon Valley hills
  3. Mandatory EIA before any mining project
  4. Relocation of industries from Delhi
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Closure of limestone quarries in the Mussoorie/Doon Valley hills

This was India's first PIL on environmental matters. The SC ordered closure of limestone quarries in the Doon Valley affecting the Himalayan ecology, holding the right to wholesome environment is part of Article 21.

Report this question

8In Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum v. Union of India (1996), the Supreme Court dealt with:

  1. Closure of Taj Mahal area industries
  2. Industrial pollution of River Ganga
  3. Oleum gas leakage from a factory
  4. Pollution by tanneries in Tamil Nadu
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Pollution by tanneries in Tamil Nadu

The Vellore case dealt with pollution caused by tanneries in Tamil Nadu discharging untreated effluents into rivers. The SC explicitly recognized the Precautionary Principle and Polluter Pays Principle as part of Indian environmental law.

Report this question

9Which case involved the Bichhri village industrial pollution in Rajasthan?

  1. Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action v. Union of India, 1996
  2. M.C. Mehta v. Kamal Nath, 1997
  3. AP Pollution Control Board v. Prof. M.V. Nayudu, 1999
  4. Subhash Kumar v. State of Bihar, 1991
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action v. Union of India, 1996

Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action v. UOI (1996) - the 'Bichhri case' - involved industries in Bichhri village, Rajasthan discharging highly toxic sludge. The SC applied the Polluter Pays Principle and directed industries to pay remediation costs.

10The Wildlife Protection Act was enacted in:

  1. 1972
  2. 1974
  3. 1980
  4. 1986
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 1972

Wildlife Protection Act 1972 provides for protection of wild animals, birds and plants. It establishes National Parks, Wildlife Sanctuaries, and Tiger Reserves. Amended significantly in 2002 and 2022.

Report this question

11A.P. Pollution Control Board v. Prof. M.V. Nayudu (1999) is significant because:

  1. It established the Public Trust Doctrine
  2. It created the doctrine of Absolute Liability
  3. It ordered closure of all tanneries
  4. It applied the Precautionary Principle to siting of an industry near drinking water sources
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. It applied the Precautionary Principle to siting of an industry near drinking water sources

In this case, the SC applied the Precautionary Principle to uphold rejection of consent to an industry near drinking water reservoirs, holding that scientific uncertainty cannot be an excuse for inaction where serious damage is threatened.

Report this question

12The Taj Trapezium case (M.C. Mehta v. UOI, 1996) related to:

  1. Industrial pollution damaging the Taj Mahal by acid rain
  2. Vehicular pollution in Delhi
  3. Slaughter houses near Taj Mahal
  4. Encroachment on Yamuna riverbed
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Industrial pollution damaging the Taj Mahal by acid rain

The Taj Trapezium case (1996) involved industries in the Taj Trapezium Zone emitting sulphur dioxide causing acid rain that was yellowing and corroding the Taj Mahal. The SC ordered relocation of 292 industries.

Report this question

13The National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) was established under:

  1. Wildlife Protection Act 1972
  2. Environment Protection Act 1986
  3. Forest Conservation Act 1980
  4. Biological Diversity Act 2002
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Biological Diversity Act 2002

The Biological Diversity Act 2002 established the National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) at national level, State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs) at state level, and Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs) at local level. It implements India's CBD 1992 obligations.

Report this question

14In M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (CNG case, 2002), the Supreme Court directed:

  1. Closure of all diesel vehicles in India
  2. Conversion of Delhi's public transport fleet to CNG
  3. Relocation of industries from Delhi
  4. Introduction of emission standards for vehicles
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Conversion of Delhi's public transport fleet to CNG

In the CNG case (continuing M.C. Mehta v. UOI vehicular pollution litigation), the SC directed conversion of Delhi's entire public transport fleet (buses, three-wheelers, taxis) to Compressed Natural Gas (CNG) by 2002.

Report this question

15The Environment (Protection) Act 1986 was enacted within how many years of the Bhopal Gas Tragedy?

  1. 6 months
  2. 1 year
  3. 2 years
  4. 5 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 2 years

The Bhopal Gas Tragedy occurred on 2-3 December 1984. The Environment Protection Act was enacted in 1986 - approximately 2 years later - as a direct legislative response to the tragedy which exposed the absence of comprehensive environmental protection law in India.
