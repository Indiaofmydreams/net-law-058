8 practice questions with answers

1Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) in India is mandated under:

  1. Environment (Protection) Act 1986 read with EIA Notification 2006
  2. Forest Conservation Act 1980
  3. Water Act 1974
  4. Companies Act 2013
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Environment (Protection) Act 1986 read with EIA Notification 2006

EIA is mandated under EPA 1986 and the EIA Notification 2006 (which replaced the 1994 notification). Projects are classified as Category A (Central-level) and Category B (State-level appraisal).

2EIA Notification 2006 categorizes projects into:

  1. Category I and Category II
  2. Red, Orange and Green categories
  3. Schedule I, II and III
  4. Category A and Category B (further sub-divided into B1 and B2)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Category A and Category B (further sub-divided into B1 and B2)

EIA Notification 2006 classifies projects into Category A (national-level appraisal by EAC under MoEFCC) and Category B. Category B is further divided into B1 (requiring full EIA) and B2 (requiring only EMP).

3Public hearing in the EIA process is conducted by:

  1. State Pollution Control Board or UTPCC
  2. Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change
  3. National Green Tribunal
  4. District Collector
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. State Pollution Control Board or UTPCC

Under EIA Notification 2006, public hearing is mandatory for Category A and B1 projects, and is conducted by the State Pollution Control Board (SPCB) or Union Territory Pollution Control Committee (UTPCC).

4The Bhopal Gas Tragedy of 1984 was caused by leakage of:

  1. Methyl Isocyanate (MIC)
  2. Chlorine gas
  3. Sulphur dioxide
  4. Carbon monoxide
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Methyl Isocyanate (MIC)

The Bhopal Gas Tragedy (2-3 December 1984) at the Union Carbide India Ltd. plant was caused by leakage of Methyl Isocyanate (MIC) gas. It is the world's worst industrial disaster and directly led to enactment of EPA 1986.

5The Hazardous Wastes (Management, Handling and Transboundary Movement) Rules are framed under:

  1. Basel Convention directly
  2. Water Act 1974
  3. Environment Protection Act 1986
  4. Indian Forest Act 1927
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Environment Protection Act 1986

Hazardous Wastes Rules 2008 (as amended) are framed under EPA 1986. They regulate generation, storage, transportation, treatment and disposal of hazardous wastes and implement India's Basel Convention obligations.

Report this question

6The Stockholm Convention (2001) deals with:

  1. Climate change
  2. Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs)
  3. Ozone-depleting substances
  4. Biodiversity conservation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs)

Stockholm Convention on Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs) 2001 aims to eliminate or restrict the 'dirty dozen' chemicals including DDT, PCBs, dioxins, and furans. India ratified it in 2006.

Report this question

7Ramsar Convention (1971) deals with:

  1. Conservation and wise use of wetlands
  2. Protection of coral reefs
  3. Marine biodiversity
  4. Migratory birds
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Conservation and wise use of wetlands

Ramsar Convention on Wetlands of International Importance especially as Waterfowl Habitat (1971, in force 1975) promotes the 'wise use' of wetlands. India has 75+ Ramsar sites.

Report this question

8In the context of EIA, 'scoping' means:

  1. Conducting public hearings
  2. Post-project monitoring
  3. Granting environmental clearance
  4. Identifying the key issues and impacts to be studied in the EIA
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Identifying the key issues and impacts to be studied in the EIA

Scoping is the process of identifying significant issues an EIA should address - determining the Terms of Reference (ToR) for the study. In India, ToR is issued by the Expert Appraisal Committee (EAC) or SEAC.
