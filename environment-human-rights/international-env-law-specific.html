16 practice questions with answers

1The 'Polluter Pays Principle' as applied to ship-source marine pollution is primarily embodied in:

  1. UNCLOS 1982
  2. International Convention on Civil Liability for Oil Pollution Damage (CLC) 1969 and Fund Convention
  3. MARPOL 73/78 Convention
  4. London Dumping Convention 1972
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. International Convention on Civil Liability for Oil Pollution Damage (CLC) 1969 and Fund Convention

The Civil Liability Convention (CLC) 1969 (and its 1992 Protocol) imposes strict liability on shipowners for oil pollution damage from tankers and requires compulsory insurance. The International Oil Pollution Compensation (IOPC) Fund provides additional compensation - implementing Polluter Pays Principle in the maritime context.

2The Biodiversity Beyond National Jurisdiction (BBNJ) Agreement (2023) deals with:

  1. Conservation and sustainable use of marine biological diversity of areas beyond national jurisdiction (high seas)
  2. Biodiversity in outer space
  3. Biodiversity conservation in Antarctic regions only
  4. Genetic resources in indigenous territories
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Conservation and sustainable use of marine biological diversity of areas beyond national jurisdiction (high seas)

The BBNJ Agreement (also called the 'High Seas Treaty') was adopted in June 2023 under UNCLOS. It is the first international treaty to protect marine biodiversity in the high seas (beyond national jurisdiction, covering 64% of the ocean). It covers marine genetic resources, area-based management tools, EIA in high seas.

3The 'equitable utilization' principle in international water law means:

  1. Each riparian state is entitled to a reasonable and equitable share of the waters of an international watercourse, considering all relevant factors
  2. All riparian states get equal shares of shared rivers
  3. Upstream states have priority over downstream states
  4. Water resources are distributed based on population
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Each riparian state is entitled to a reasonable and equitable share of the waters of an international watercourse, considering all relevant factors

The principle of equitable utilization is the cornerstone of international water law, codified in the UN Convention on the Law of Non-Navigational Uses of International Watercourses 1997. No single factor determines 'equitable' share - relevant factors include geographic position, climate, past utilization, population needs, and conservation measures.

4The 'non-binding' nature of the Rio Declaration 1992 means:

  1. It does not create binding treaty obligations but its principles have been widely recognized as reflecting or contributing to customary international environmental law
  2. Its principles have no relevance in international or domestic law
  3. States can completely ignore the Declaration
  4. Only states that voted for it are bound by it
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It does not create binding treaty obligations but its principles have been widely recognized as reflecting or contributing to customary international environmental law

The Rio Declaration is soft law - not a binding treaty. However, its 27 principles have significantly influenced international environmental law: several have been recognized as reflecting customary international law, incorporated into binding treaties, and applied by domestic courts including Indian courts in Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum 1996.

5The 'Kigali Amendment' to the Montreal Protocol (2016) deals with:

  1. Phasing down hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs) which are potent greenhouse gases used as substitutes for CFCs
  2. Extending the Protocol to developing countries
  3. Strengthening penalties for ODS violations
  4. Creating a new fund for ozone layer restoration
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Phasing down hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs) which are potent greenhouse gases used as substitutes for CFCs

The Kigali Amendment (2016) to the Montreal Protocol requires parties to phase down production and consumption of hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs). While HFCs do not deplete the ozone layer, they are powerful greenhouse gases. The Amendment is expected to prevent up to 0.5 degrees C of global warming by 2100. India ratified it in 2021.

6The Convention on Long-Range Transboundary Air Pollution (CLRTAP, 1979) was adopted under the auspices of:

  1. UNEP
  2. WHO
  3. WMO
  4. UN Economic Commission for Europe (UNECE)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. UN Economic Commission for Europe (UNECE)

CLRTAP (Geneva, 1979) was adopted under the UN Economic Commission for Europe (UNECE). It was the first international legally binding instrument on air pollution, dealing with acid rain and long-range air pollutants. It has eight protocols covering specific pollutants. India is not a party.

7The principle of 'State Responsibility' in international environmental law means:

  1. States are responsible only for their own direct pollution
  2. States are responsible for environmental damage caused by their nationals abroad
  3. Responsibility of states to fund international environmental bodies
  4. A state is internationally responsible for acts or omissions attributable to it that cause significant transboundary environmental harm in violation of international law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A state is internationally responsible for acts or omissions attributable to it that cause significant transboundary environmental harm in violation of international law

State responsibility in international environmental law (based on ILC Articles on State Responsibility 2001) means a state that fails to prevent significant transboundary environmental harm through inadequate regulation, enforcement, or notification incurs international responsibility. The Trail Smelter principle is the foundational case.

8The 'Corfu Channel case' (1949) is relevant to environmental law because:

  1. It established maritime pollution standards
  2. It established UNCLOS principles
  3. It dealt with nuclear testing in the ocean
  4. The ICJ held that states have an obligation not to knowingly allow their territory to be used for acts contrary to the rights of other states
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The ICJ held that states have an obligation not to knowingly allow their territory to be used for acts contrary to the rights of other states

ICJ in the Corfu Channel case (UK v. Albania, 1949) held Albania responsible for damages to British ships from mines in Albanian territorial waters, establishing that states must not knowingly allow their territory to be used in ways that damage other states. This principle is now applied to environmental harm.

9The 'Nuclear Test Cases' (1974) before the ICJ involved applications by:

  1. Australia and New Zealand against France regarding atmospheric nuclear testing in the Pacific
  2. USA and Russia against each other
  3. India against Pakistan regarding nuclear tests
  4. Non-nuclear states against P5 nations
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Australia and New Zealand against France regarding atmospheric nuclear testing in the Pacific

Australia v. France and New Zealand v. France (1974) - both challenged French atmospheric nuclear testing in the South Pacific. France announced it would cease atmospheric testing and the ICJ held the cases had become moot. These cases raised important issues about transboundary environmental harm and erga omnes obligations.

10The 'soft law' instruments in international environmental law include:

  1. Declarations, guidelines, resolutions and action plans that are not legally binding but influence state behaviour
  2. Only the UNFCCC and its Protocols
  3. Only domestic environmental regulations
  4. Bilateral environmental treaties
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Declarations, guidelines, resolutions and action plans that are not legally binding but influence state behaviour

Soft law - non-binding instruments that nonetheless shape state behaviour and may eventually crystallize into customary international law or binding treaties. Key environmental soft law instruments: Stockholm Declaration 1972, Rio Declaration 1992, Agenda 21, UNEP guidelines, OECD Recommendations on Polluter Pays.

11The ICJ's advisory opinion on 'Legality of the Threat or Use of Nuclear Weapons' (1996) recognized:

  1. Nuclear weapons are legal in all circumstances
  2. The environment represents the living space and quality of life of future generations - states must take account of environmental considerations in using nuclear weapons
  3. Nuclear weapons cannot be used even in self-defence
  4. Environmental law does not apply to armed conflict
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The environment represents the living space and quality of life of future generations - states must take account of environmental considerations in using nuclear weapons

ICJ Advisory Opinion 1996 on Nuclear Weapons: The Court recognized that the environment is not an abstraction but represents the living space, quality of life and health of human beings, including future generations. States must take account of environmental considerations when assessing whether use of nuclear weapons is compatible with international humanitarian law.

12The 'Principle of Good Neighbourliness' (sic utere tuo) in environmental law means:

  1. States must always cooperate on environmental projects
  2. Neighbouring states must share natural resources equally
  3. Use your own territory in a way that does not harm your neighbours - a state must not allow activities causing transboundary harm
  4. Border states must jointly manage environmental resources
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Use your own territory in a way that does not harm your neighbours - a state must not allow activities causing transboundary harm

Sic utere tuo ut alienum non laedas (use your own property so as not to injure that of another) - the ancient maxim that is the root of the 'no harm' rule and trail smelter principle. It is the international law equivalent of the neighbourhood/nuisance principle in domestic tort law.

13The 'loss and damage' mechanism established at COP27 (Sharm el-Sheikh, 2022) is significant because:

  1. It established binding emission reduction targets for all countries
  2. It abolished the Kyoto Protocol mechanisms
  3. It established a new climate court
  4. It created a formal fund to help developing countries for irreversible climate impacts that cannot be adapted to
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. It created a formal fund to help developing countries for irreversible climate impacts that cannot be adapted to

COP27 (Sharm el-Sheikh, 2022) established a formal Loss and Damage Fund - a landmark development for vulnerable developing nations and small island states facing permanent loss of territory, cultural heritage and livelihoods due to climate change impacts that cannot be adapted to. This had been demanded by developing nations for decades.

14The Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD) has how many objectives?

  1. 1
  2. 2
  3. 3
  4. 5
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 3

The CBD 1992 has three objectives: (1) Conservation of biological diversity; (2) Sustainable use of its components; and (3) Fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising out of the utilization of genetic resources. The Nagoya Protocol (2010) specifically implements the third objective - access and benefit sharing (ABS).

15The principle of 'common but differentiated responsibilities' (CBDR) was first given formal legal status in:

  1. Stockholm Declaration 1972
  2. UNFCCC 1992 (Article 3)
  3. Kyoto Protocol 1997 (Annex I)
  4. Rio Declaration Principle 7 (1992)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Rio Declaration Principle 7 (1992)

While CBDR as a concept was discussed at Stockholm (1972), it was first given formal recognition as a stated principle of international environmental law in the Rio Declaration Principle 7 (1992). It was subsequently incorporated into the UNFCCC (1992) as a guiding principle and operationalized through the Kyoto Protocol's Annex I/non-Annex I distinction.

16The 'concept of carbon neutrality' or 'net zero' was pledged by India to be achieved by:

  1. 2050
  2. 2060
  3. 2070
  4. 2080
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 2070

India pledged to achieve net zero carbon emissions by 2070 at COP26 (Glasgow, November 2021). This is later than the 2050 target of most developed countries. India also pledged: 50% of cumulative electric power from non-fossil fuels by 2030; reduce emissions intensity of GDP by 45% by 2030; and increase non-fossil fuel based energy capacity to 500 GW by 2030.
