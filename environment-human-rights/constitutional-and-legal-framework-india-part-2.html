15 practice questions with answers

1The 'precautionary principle' shifts the burden of proof to:

  1. The developer/industry to demonstrate that proposed activity is safe
  2. The affected community to prove harm
  3. The state to prove no harm will occur
  4. The court to conduct independent investigation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The developer/industry to demonstrate that proposed activity is safe

A key consequence of the Precautionary Principle is reversal of burden of proof - it is for the developer or industrialist to show their action is environmentally benign. Affirmed by SC in Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum (1996) and A.P. Pollution Control Board v. Nayudu (1999).

2The 'precautionary principle' was first mentioned in an international instrument in:

  1. Stockholm Declaration 1972
  2. Vienna Convention on Ozone Layer 1985
  3. World Charter for Nature 1982
  4. Rio Declaration 1992
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. World Charter for Nature 1982

While often associated with Rio Principle 15 (1992), the precautionary principle was first mentioned in an international instrument in the World Charter for Nature adopted by UNGA in 1982. It was also present in the Vienna Convention for the Protection of the Ozone Layer 1985.

3Which article enables the State to protect laws enacted for environmental purposes from challenge under Articles 14 and 19?

  1. Article 19(1)(f) - now deleted
  2. Article 300A read with Articles 48A and 51A(g)
  3. Article 31C read with Article 48A
  4. Article 32
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Article 31C read with Article 48A

Article 31C (inserted by 42nd Amendment) protects laws giving effect to Directive Principles in Articles 39(b) and 39(c) from challenge on grounds of Articles 14 and 19. Article 48A (environment protection) being a DPSP, legislation made in furtherance of it may receive some protection.

4The Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules were framed in:

  1. 1986 under EPA
  2. 2000 under EPA 1986
  3. 1994 under Air Act 1981
  4. 2006 under EPA 1986
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 2000 under EPA 1986

Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules 2000 were framed under the Environment Protection Act 1986. They prescribe ambient noise standards for different zones (industrial, commercial, residential, silence zones) for day and night time separately.

5In Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corporation (1985), the Supreme Court held:

  1. Slum dwellers have an absolute right to remain on public land
  2. Municipal corporations cannot evict encroachers
  3. Right to livelihood is part of right to life under Article 21
  4. Pavement dwellers are entitled to rehabilitation under DPSPs
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Right to livelihood is part of right to life under Article 21

Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corporation (1985) - 'Pavement Dwellers case' - the SC held that the right to livelihood is an integral component of the right to life under Article 21. Eviction without notice would destroy means of livelihood, violating Article 21.

6The concept of 'environmental democracy' in India is primarily reflected through:

  1. Right to vote in elections
  2. PILs, Right to Information Act 2005, and public hearings in EIA
  3. Parliamentary debates on environment
  4. Environmental courts established in all districts
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. PILs, Right to Information Act 2005, and public hearings in EIA

Environmental democracy is reflected in India through: PIL (liberalized locus standi); Right to Information Act 2005 (access to environmental information); mandatory public hearings in EIA process; and gram sabha consent under Forest Rights Act 2006.

7The Biological Diversity Act 2002 regulation of access to biological resources is NOT applicable to:

  1. Vaids, hakims, and local communities using biological resources for personal consumption
  2. Foreign nationals or companies seeking access to biological resources
  3. Collaborative research with foreign institutions
  4. Indian companies using biological resources for commercial purposes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Vaids, hakims, and local communities using biological resources for personal consumption

Section 7 of the Biological Diversity Act 2002 provides that local communities, growers, vaids, hakims and indigenous/local communities are exempt from the requirement to take approval from the NBA for personal/non-commercial use, cultivation, breeding or treatment.

8'Ecocide' in environmental and international law refers to:

  1. Large-scale destruction of ecosystems that may constitute a crime under international law
  2. Killing of a particular animal species
  3. Industrial accidents causing pollution
  4. Legal mechanism for environmental compensation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Large-scale destruction of ecosystems that may constitute a crime under international law

Ecocide refers to large-scale damage or destruction to ecosystems. There is a growing international movement to recognize ecocide as the fifth international crime under the Rome Statute, alongside genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes, and the crime of aggression.

9Which case established that environmental clearance obtained by fraud or misrepresentation can be cancelled?

  1. Common Cause v. Union of India (coal blocks case, 2014)
  2. Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum v. UOI (1996)
  3. M.C. Mehta v. UOI (Taj Trapezium)
  4. RLEK v. State of UP (1985)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Common Cause v. Union of India (coal blocks case, 2014)

In Common Cause v. Union of India (2014) - the coal blocks allocation case - the SC held that allocation of coal blocks through an arbitrary and non-transparent process was illegal and cancelled 214 coal block allocations, reinforcing the Public Trust Doctrine.

10The Supreme Court in M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Ganga Pollution case) directed:

  1. Closure of all industries near River Ganga
  2. Tanneries and other polluting industries discharging into the Ganga to set up effluent treatment plants or face closure
  3. Nationalisation of all Ganga-bank industries
  4. Construction of a dam to control Ganga flooding
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Tanneries and other polluting industries discharging into the Ganga to set up effluent treatment plants or face closure

M.C. Mehta v. UOI (Ganga Pollution cases, 1987-88 onwards) - a series of orders directing tanneries, municipalities, and industries discharging untreated effluents into the River Ganga to install effluent treatment plants (ETPs) or face closure.

11The concept of 'extended producer responsibility' (EPR) in waste management means:

  1. Producers are responsible for the entire lifecycle of their products including post-consumer waste management
  2. Producers are responsible only for the quality of their products
  3. Responsibility of large producers to support small producers
  4. Government's extended responsibility for industrial waste
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Producers are responsible for the entire lifecycle of their products including post-consumer waste management

EPR - manufacturers/importers/brand owners are responsible for their products' environmental impact throughout the product lifecycle, including collection, recycling or disposal of post-consumer waste. EPR is incorporated in India's E-Waste Rules, Plastic Waste Management Rules, and Battery Waste Management Rules under EPA 1986.

12Under the Forest Conservation Act 1980, 'non-forest purpose' explicitly excludes:

  1. Mining in forest areas
  2. Reforestation or afforestation - which is considered a forest purpose
  3. Construction of hydel projects
  4. Construction of roads through forests
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Reforestation or afforestation - which is considered a forest purpose

Forest Conservation Act 1980, Section 2 Explanation: 'Non-forest purpose' does not include reforestation or afforestation. Planting trees on forest land is a forest purpose and does not require Central Government approval.

Report this question

13The Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers Act 2006 vests rights in forest dwellers as of:

  1. Date of enactment of the Act
  2. 13 December 2005 - the cut-off date for establishing occupation
  3. Date of application for rights
  4. Date of grant of patta by State Government
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 13 December 2005 - the cut-off date for establishing occupation

The Forest Rights Act 2006 recognizes rights of those who have been occupying forest land for cultivation or habitation as of 13 December 2005 (the cut-off date). The individual has to prove occupation through at least two of the three specified periods. This cut-off date is the most commonly tested factual detail from this Act.

14The Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ) Notification 2019 classifies coastal areas into how many categories?

  1. 2
  2. 3
  3. 4
  4. 6
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 4

CRZ Notification 2019 classifies coastal areas into 4 zones: CRZ-I (ecologically sensitive, intertidal zone - most restricted); CRZ-II (urban areas); CRZ-III (rural coastal areas, divided into III-A and III-B); CRZ-IV (water area up to 12 nautical miles including islands). This replaced the CRZ Notification 2011.

15The 'Doctrine of Public Trust' applies in India to which categories of resources?

  1. Agricultural land and urban property
  2. Only rivers and seas - not forests
  3. Air, sea, water, forests and other natural resources held by the State in trust for the public
  4. Only resources specifically enumerated in the Environment Protection Act
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Air, sea, water, forests and other natural resources held by the State in trust for the public

As articulated in M.C. Mehta v. Kamal Nath (1997) and subsequent cases, the Public Trust Doctrine in India applies to air, sea, water, forests, seashores, and natural resources generally - held by the State in trust for the public and cannot be alienated for private commercial purposes.
