1The Supreme Court in S. Jagannath v. Union of India (1997) - the Shrimp Culture case - applied which principle?

  1. Absolute liability for shrimp farms
  2. Public Trust Doctrine to privatize beaches
  3. Precautionary principle and Polluter Pays principle to order closure of shrimp farms in coastal ecologically sensitive areas
  4. Sustainable development to permit shrimp farming with conditions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Precautionary principle and Polluter Pays principle to order closure of shrimp farms in coastal ecologically sensitive areas

S. Jagannath v. UOI (1997) - Shrimp Culture case: The SC applied the Precautionary Principle and Polluter Pays Principle to order closure of shrimp (prawn) farms within 500 metres of the High Tide Line in coastal states, holding they were causing irreversible damage to mangroves and coastal ecology.

2Under the Forest Rights Act 2006, the authority empowered to receive, verify and approve/reject forest rights claims is:

  1. Three-tier structure - Gram Sabha (initial recognition), Sub-Divisional Level Committee, District Level Committee
  2. District Collector exclusively
  3. State Forest Department
  4. National Biodiversity Authority
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Three-tier structure - Gram Sabha (initial recognition), Sub-Divisional Level Committee, District Level Committee

Forest Rights Act 2006 provides a three-tier adjudication: Gram Sabha (initiates the process, receives claims, prepares maps); Sub-Divisional Level Committee (SDLC - examines and forwards); District Level Committee (DLC - final authority to approve or reject). The Gram Sabha is the entry point for all claims.

3The 'Doctrine of Parens Patriae' has been applied in environmental law in India to mean:

  1. The State as guardian of the environment and natural resources on behalf of the public
  2. Parents' right to a clean environment for their children
  3. A doctrine imposing personal liability on company directors for environmental damage
  4. Power of the court to appoint environmental commissioners
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The State as guardian of the environment and natural resources on behalf of the public

Parens patriae (parent of the nation) - in environmental law, Indian courts have used it to hold that the State is the guardian/trustee of natural resources on behalf of the public (linked to the Public Trust Doctrine). Courts can direct the State to perform its parens patriae duties when it fails to protect environmental resources.

4The 'National Action Plan on Climate Change' (NAPCC) was launched in India in:

  1. 2005
  2. 2008
  3. 2012
  4. 2015
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 2008

India's National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC) was launched in June 2008. It has eight national missions including: National Solar Mission; National Mission for Enhanced Energy Efficiency; National Water Mission; National Mission for Sustaining the Himalayan Ecosystem; National Mission for a Green India; National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture.

5The 'Doctrine of res judicata' in environmental litigation has been modified by the Supreme Court to allow:

  1. No relaxation - res judicata applies fully in environmental cases
  2. Only NGT can reopen settled environmental cases
  3. Reopening of settled environmental matters through continuing mandamus - the court exercises ongoing judicial supervision
  4. Res judicata does not apply to PIL cases at all
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Reopening of settled environmental matters through continuing mandamus - the court exercises ongoing judicial supervision

In environmental PILs, the SC has developed the concept of 'continuing mandamus' - ongoing judicial supervision of environmental compliance even after initial orders. The SC can reopen matters, modify orders, and issue further directions as environmental conditions change or new evidence emerges.

6The Wild Life (Protection) Act 1972 was amended significantly in which years?

  1. 2002 and 2022
  2. 1980 and 1991
  3. 1991 and 1999
  4. 2006 and 2016
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 2002 and 2022

The Wild Life (Protection) Act 1972 has been amended several times. The two most significant amendments were: the Wild Life (Protection) Amendment Act 2002 (which strengthened penalties, added new offences, and created the Wildlife Crime Control Bureau) and the Wild Life (Protection) Amendment Act 2022 (which implemented CITES obligations and simplified schedules).

7The Environment Relief Fund (ERF) under the Public Liability Insurance Act 1991 is managed by:

  1. Central Government
  2. National Green Tribunal
  3. CPCB
  4. State Pollution Control Boards
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Central Government

Under the Public Liability Insurance Act 1991, every owner handling hazardous substances is required to contribute to the Environment Relief Fund (ERF). The ERF is managed by the Central Government and is used to provide relief to victims of accidents when the owner's insurance is insufficient.

8The 'Ganges River Dolphin' was declared India's National Aquatic Animal in:

  1. 2006
  2. 2009
  3. 2012
  4. 2015
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 2009

The Ganges River Dolphin (Platanista gangetica) was declared India's National Aquatic Animal in 2009. It is a Schedule I species under the Wild Life (Protection) Act 1972 - given the highest level of protection. Its conservation is closely linked to Ganga river ecology and pollution cases before the SC.

9The 'green GDP' or 'natural capital accounting' concept has been explored by India through:

  1. Mandatory provision in EPA 1986
  2. NITI Aayog formal mandate
  3. Ministry of Finance annual exercise
  4. National Statistical Office pilot studies and ecosystem accounting
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. National Statistical Office pilot studies and ecosystem accounting

Green GDP or natural capital accounting adjusts traditional GDP calculations to account for the depletion of natural resources and environmental degradation. India has been exploring Natural Capital Accounting through National Statistical Office pilot studies and the SEEA (System of Environmental-Economic Accounting) framework under UNSD guidance.

10The case of Sterlite Industries/Vedanta Copper smelter in Tuticorin (Thoothukudi) resulted in:

  1. The plant being allowed to operate with more stringent conditions
  2. Relocation of the plant to an industrial zone
  3. Payment of compensation only without plant closure
  4. The plant being ordered closed in 2018 following environmental violations and deaths in protests
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The plant being ordered closed in 2018 following environmental violations and deaths in protests

Sterlite Copper smelter (Vedanta Group) in Tuticorin/Thoothukudi has been subject to prolonged litigation over SO2 emissions and environmental damage. The Tamil Nadu government ordered the plant closed in 2018 following deaths in protests against pollution. The plant closure has been upheld in subsequent legal proceedings before the NGT and courts.

11The 'Environmental Information System' (ENVIS) in India was established in:

  1. 1982 by the Ministry of Environment
  2. 1972 following Stockholm Conference
  3. 1992 following Rio Earth Summit
  4. 2002 under the Biological Diversity Act
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 1982 by the Ministry of Environment

ENVIS (Environmental Information System) was established in 1982 by the Ministry of Environment (now MoEFCC) as part of a global network under UNEP. It is a network of subject-specific and state-based centres that collect, store and disseminate environmental information to support environmental decision-making.

12The 'Special Marriage Act 1954' is relevant to human rights because:

  1. It recognizes same-sex marriages
  2. It provides a secular, civil marriage framework allowing inter-religious marriages without conversion, protecting freedom of conscience and equality
  3. It is the basis for divorce rights of Muslim women
  4. It provides for compulsory registration of all marriages
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. It provides a secular, civil marriage framework allowing inter-religious marriages without conversion, protecting freedom of conscience and equality

The Special Marriage Act 1954 provides a civil, secular marriage framework enabling individuals of different religions or faiths to marry without religious conversion. It reflects human rights principles of freedom of conscience, religion, and equality before law (Articles 14, 15, 25). It is relevant to discussions on a Uniform Civil Code in India.
