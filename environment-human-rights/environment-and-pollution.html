24 practice questions with answers

1Which Act in India provides the most comprehensive definition of 'environment'?

  1. Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974
  2. Environment (Protection) Act, 1986
  3. Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981
  4. Wildlife Protection Act, 1972
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Environment (Protection) Act, 1986

Section 2(a) of the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 defines 'environment' to include water, air, land and the interrelationships which exist among and between them and human beings, other living creatures, plants, microorganisms and property.

2Which Constitutional Amendment inserted Articles 48A and 51A(g) relating to environment?

  1. 42nd Amendment, 1976
  2. 40th Amendment
  3. 44th Amendment
  4. 86th Amendment
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 42nd Amendment, 1976

The 42nd Constitutional Amendment 1976 inserted Article 48A (DPSP - State shall protect and improve the environment) and Article 51A(g) (Fundamental Duty - every citizen to protect natural environment).

3Under the Environment Protection Act 1986, 'environmental pollution' means:

  1. Presence of any pollutant in the environment
  2. Presence of pollutant exceeding prescribed standards only
  3. Pollution of water and air only
  4. Presence of radioactive substances only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Presence of any pollutant in the environment

Section 2(b) of EPA 1986 defines environmental pollution as the presence in the environment of any environmental pollutant. There is no threshold of exceeding standards in the definition itself.

4In which case did the Supreme Court explicitly hold that right to a clean environment is part of Article 21?

  1. Rural Litigation and Entitlement Kendra v. State of UP, 1985
  2. M.C. Mehta v. Union of India, 1987
  3. Subhash Kumar v. State of Bihar, 1991
  4. Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum v. Union of India, 1996
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Subhash Kumar v. State of Bihar, 1991

In Subhash Kumar v. State of Bihar (1991), the Supreme Court explicitly held that the right to live under Article 21 includes the right to enjoyment of pollution-free water and air.

5'Intergenerational equity' in environmental law means:

  1. Equal rights of men and women to environment
  2. Obligation of the present generation to preserve natural resources for future generations
  3. Present generation's right to develop freely without restriction
  4. Equal sharing of environmental burdens among nations
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Obligation of the present generation to preserve natural resources for future generations

Intergenerational equity (developed by Edith Brown Weiss) means the present generation holds the natural environment in trust for future generations. It is the basis of sustainable development (Brundtland Report 1987).

6The Environment Protection Act 1986 was enacted by Parliament under which article of the Constitution?

  1. Article 246
  2. Article 252
  3. Article 248
  4. Article 253
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Article 253

EPA 1986 was enacted under Article 253, which empowers Parliament to make laws for implementing international treaties and agreements. It was enacted following India's obligations after the Stockholm Conference 1972 and Bhopal Gas Tragedy 1984.

7The Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act was enacted in:

  1. 1974
  2. 1978
  3. 1981
  4. 1986
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1981

Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act 1981 was enacted to provide for prevention, control and abatement of air pollution, pursuant to decisions taken at the Stockholm Conference 1972. It uses the same Central and State Boards as the Water Act 1974.

8The National Environment Policy of India was adopted in:

  1. 1992
  2. 2000
  3. 2006
  4. 2010
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 2006

India's National Environment Policy was adopted in 2006 by the Ministry of Environment and Forests. It is a policy framework (not legislation) articulating principles like precaution, polluter pays, and intergenerational equity.

9Which of the following is NOT a schedule under the Environment Protection Act 1986?

  1. Standards for emission or discharge of environmental pollutants
  2. List of hazardous microorganisms
  3. List of protected wildlife species
  4. Standards for noise pollution
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. List of protected wildlife species

The list of protected wildlife species is under the Wildlife Protection Act 1972, not EPA 1986. EPA 1986 schedules deal with: emission/discharge standards, prohibited industrial locations, hazardous chemicals/microorganisms, and environmental laboratories.

10Under the Environment Protection Act 1986, what is the minimum imprisonment for a first conviction for violation?

  1. 1 year
  2. 6 months
  3. 3 years
  4. 5 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 1 year

Section 15 of EPA 1986: Whoever fails to comply with or contravenes any provision shall be punishable with imprisonment which may extend to 5 years or with fine up to Rs. 1 lakh, or both. After 5 years of continuing failure, imprisonment may extend to 7 years.

11The 'Coastal Regulation Zone' (CRZ) notifications are issued under:

  1. Environment Protection Act 1986
  2. Coastal Zone Management Act
  3. Maritime Zones Act 1976
  4. Water Act 1974
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Environment Protection Act 1986

CRZ Notifications (currently CRZ Notification 2019) are issued under Section 3 of the Environment Protection Act 1986. They regulate development activities along the coastline within specified distances from the High Tide Line.

12The term 'hazardous substance' under the Environment Protection Act 1986 means:

  1. Only radioactive material
  2. Only substances listed under the Basel Convention
  3. Substances which cause air pollution only
  4. Any substance which by reason of its chemical or physico-chemical properties or handling is liable to cause harm to human beings, other living creatures, plants, microorganisms, property or environment
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Any substance which by reason of its chemical or physico-chemical properties or handling is liable to cause harm to human beings, other living creatures, plants, microorganisms, property or environment

Section 2(e) of EPA 1986 defines hazardous substance comprehensively - not limited to radioactive material or any single category. This definition is the basis for framing Hazardous Waste Rules, MSIHC Rules 1989, and other delegated legislation under EPA 1986.

13The Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) was constituted under:

  1. Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act 1974
  2. Environment Protection Act 1986
  3. Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act 1981
  4. National Green Tribunal Act 2010
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act 1974

CPCB was constituted under Section 3 of the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act 1974. Subsequently, under Section 4 of the Air Act 1981, the same CPCB was also entrusted with powers and functions under the Air Act.

Report this question

14The doctrine of strict liability in Rylands v. Fletcher (1868) requires:

  1. Non-natural use of land and escape of a dangerous thing causing damage
  2. Proof of negligence by the defendant
  3. Only proof of causation without any other element
  4. Criminal intent of the polluter
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Non-natural use of land and escape of a dangerous thing causing damage

Rylands v. Fletcher (1868 - House of Lords): A person who brings on his land anything likely to do mischief if it escapes must keep it at his peril. Two key elements: non-natural use of land and escape. India's Absolute Liability rule (Oleum Gas case 1987) dispensed with the exceptions to this rule.

15The 'polluter pays principle' as applied in India extends to:

  1. Only prevention costs before damage occurs
  2. Only fines imposed by courts
  3. Only costs of installing pollution control equipment
  4. Both remediation of past damage and prevention of future damage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Both remediation of past damage and prevention of future damage

The Indian SC in Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action v. UOI (1996 - Bichhri case) held that the Polluter Pays Principle means the polluter must bear both the cost of remedying environmental damage already caused AND the cost of preventing future damage.

16Under the EPA 1986, cognizance of offences can be taken by a court only upon:

  1. A complaint by any citizen
  2. FIR filed by police
  3. A complaint made by the Central Government, State Government, or any authorized officer or person who has given notice of 60 days
  4. Application made to the NGT
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A complaint made by the Central Government, State Government, or any authorized officer or person who has given notice of 60 days

Section 19 of EPA 1986: No court shall take cognizance of any offence under the Act except on a complaint made by the Central Government or any authority or officer authorized, or any person who has given notice of not less than sixty days to the Central Government of the alleged offence.

17Under the Environment Protection Act 1986, Section 5 directions to any person, officer or authority include:

  1. Closure, prohibition or regulation of any industry; stoppage or regulation of supply of electricity, water or any other service
  2. Only closure orders
  3. Only directions regarding emission standards
  4. Only directions to State Pollution Control Boards
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Closure, prohibition or regulation of any industry; stoppage or regulation of supply of electricity, water or any other service

Section 5 of EPA 1986: The Central Government may issue written directions including directions regarding: closure, prohibition or regulation of any industry, operation or process; stoppage or regulation of supply of electricity, water or any other service.

18The term 'environment' in its legal sense was first defined in Indian law under:

  1. Water Act 1974
  2. Forest Conservation Act 1980
  3. Environment Protection Act 1986
  4. Air Act 1981
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Environment Protection Act 1986

The first comprehensive statutory definition of 'environment' in Indian law appears in Section 2(a) of the Environment Protection Act 1986. Earlier legislations like the Water Act 1974 and Air Act 1981 dealt with specific media but did not define 'environment' as a whole.

19The 'neighbour principle' in environmental liability originated from:

  1. Rylands v. Fletcher, 1868
  2. Cambridge Water Co. v. Eastern Counties Leather, 1994
  3. Overseas Tankship v. Morts Dock, 1961
  4. Donoghue v. Stevenson, 1932
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Donoghue v. Stevenson, 1932

The neighbour principle from Donoghue v. Stevenson (1932 - Lord Atkin) - you must take reasonable care to avoid acts or omissions which you can reasonably foresee would likely injure your neighbour - is the foundational principle of negligence liability applied in tort-based environmental compensation claims.

20'Ecocentrism' differs from 'anthropocentrism' in environmental law in that:

  1. Ecocentrism places human interests at the centre of environmental decision-making
  2. Anthropocentrism prohibits all human use of natural resources
  3. They are essentially the same concept with different names
  4. Ecocentrism treats the entire ecosystem as having intrinsic value, not merely instrumental value for humans
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Ecocentrism treats the entire ecosystem as having intrinsic value, not merely instrumental value for humans

Anthropocentrism (human-centred) protects environment for human benefit. Ecocentrism (ecosystem-centred) treats ecosystems, species, and nature as having intrinsic worth independent of human use. The Rights of Nature movement (Ecuador's constitution, 2008) reflects ecocentrism.

21In which case did the Supreme Court apply the doctrine of 'sustainable development' to order balance between environment and development?

  1. T.N. Godavarman Thirumulpad v. Union of India (Forest case, 1995 - ongoing)
  2. M.C. Mehta v. UOI (Oleum, 1987)
  3. Rural Litigation Kendra v. State of UP (1985)
  4. Subhash Kumar v. State of Bihar (1991)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. T.N. Godavarman Thirumulpad v. Union of India (Forest case, 1995 - ongoing)

T.N. Godavarman Thirumulpad v. UOI (1995 onwards) - the landmark ongoing Forest case in which the SC has been monitoring forest protection across India. The court applied sustainable development to hold that while development is necessary, it cannot destroy forests; non-forest use requires prior Central Government approval.

22The concept of 'joint and several liability' in environmental law means:

  1. All polluters are equally liable regardless of their contribution
  2. Each polluter can be held fully liable for the total environmental damage even if multiple parties contributed
  3. Liability is divided proportionally among all polluters
  4. Only the largest polluter bears the full liability
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Each polluter can be held fully liable for the total environmental damage even if multiple parties contributed

Joint and several liability means each liable party can be held responsible for the full amount of environmental damage, regardless of their proportionate contribution. This is significant in cases of multiple polluters - the victim does not have to sue each polluter separately for their share.

23'Biopiracy' in the context of environmental and IPR law refers to:

  1. Piracy of biological research publications
  2. Illegal hunting and poaching of wildlife
  3. Unauthorized appropriation of biological resources and traditional knowledge of indigenous communities by commercial entities without fair compensation
  4. Smuggling of hazardous biological materials
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Unauthorized appropriation of biological resources and traditional knowledge of indigenous communities by commercial entities without fair compensation

Biopiracy - the appropriation of traditional biological resources and associated knowledge (of tribal/indigenous communities) by corporations, often through patents, without consent or benefit-sharing. The Neem patent controversy, Turmeric patent, and Basmati case are classic Indian examples. Addressed by Nagoya Protocol 2010 and Biological Diversity Act 2002.

24The term 'environmental refugee' refers to:

  1. A person who has committed environmental crimes and fled the country
  2. A refugee who is granted asylum on environmental grounds
  3. A person displaced from their usual place of residence due to environmental disruption - rising seas, drought, floods etc.
  4. A person who voluntarily migrates to environmentally pristine areas
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A person displaced from their usual place of residence due to environmental disruption - rising seas, drought, floods etc.

Environmental/climate refugees are persons displaced by environmental changes - rising sea levels, desertification, floods, droughts. They are not recognized under the 1951 Refugee Convention (which covers only persecution-based displacement). This is an evolving area of international law.
