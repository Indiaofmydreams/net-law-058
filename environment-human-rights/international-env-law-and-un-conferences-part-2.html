14 practice questions with answers

1The Espoo Convention (1991) deals with:

  1. Protection of endangered species in Europe
  2. Environmental impact assessment in a transboundary context
  3. Transboundary movement of hazardous waste
  4. Nuclear safety
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Environmental impact assessment in a transboundary context

The Espoo (EIA) Convention (1991) on Environmental Impact Assessment in a Transboundary Context requires states to assess environmental impact of certain activities and notify/consult each other on major projects likely to have significant adverse environmental impact across boundaries.

2The United Nations Environment Assembly (UNEA) was established in:

  1. 1992 after Rio Summit
  2. 1972 along with UNEP
  3. 2015 along with the SDGs
  4. 2012 at Rio+20 to replace UNEP Governing Council
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 2012 at Rio+20 to replace UNEP Governing Council

UNEA was established in 2012 following Rio+20 to replace the UNEP Governing Council. It is the world's highest-level decision-making body on environment, with universal membership of all 193 UN member states. It meets biennially in Nairobi.

3The United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS) was adopted in:

  1. 1972
  2. 1978
  3. 1982
  4. 1994
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1982

UNCLOS was adopted in 1982 at Montego Bay, Jamaica and entered into force in 1994. Called the 'Constitution of the Oceans,' it established the EEZ, continental shelf regime, the International Seabed Authority, and Part XII on protection of marine environment. India ratified in 1995.

4Principle 10 of the Rio Declaration 1992 deals with:

  1. Precautionary principle
  2. Access to information, public participation, and access to justice in environmental matters
  3. Polluter pays principle
  4. Common but differentiated responsibilities
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Access to information, public participation, and access to justice in environmental matters

Rio Principle 10: Environmental issues are best handled with participation of all concerned citizens. Each individual shall have appropriate access to information concerning the environment, opportunity to participate in decision-making processes, and effective access to judicial and administrative proceedings.

5The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) was established by:

  1. UN General Assembly in 1992
  2. UNFCCC secretariat in 1994
  3. UNEP and WMO jointly in 1988
  4. Kyoto Protocol in 1997
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. UNEP and WMO jointly in 1988

The IPCC was established jointly by the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) and the World Meteorological Organization (WMO) in 1988. It does not conduct original research but assesses the state of scientific knowledge on climate change.

6The concept of 'Exclusive Economic Zone' (EEZ) under UNCLOS extends up to:

  1. 200 nautical miles from baseline
  2. 12 nautical miles from baseline
  3. 24 nautical miles from baseline
  4. 350 nautical miles from baseline
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 200 nautical miles from baseline

Under UNCLOS 1982: Territorial Sea = 12 nautical miles; Contiguous Zone = 24 nautical miles; Exclusive Economic Zone = 200 nautical miles. In the EEZ, the coastal state has sovereign rights for exploiting natural resources but other states retain freedoms of navigation and overflight.

7The Vienna Convention for the Protection of the Ozone Layer was adopted in:

  1. 1981
  2. 1985
  3. 1987
  4. 1990
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1985

Vienna Convention for the Protection of the Ozone Layer was adopted in 1985. It is the framework convention - a general commitment to protect the ozone layer. The specific obligations for phasing out ozone-depleting substances came through the Montreal Protocol 1987.

8The Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) were adopted in:

  1. 2015 at the UN Sustainable Development Summit
  2. 2012 at Rio+20
  3. 2013 at the UN General Assembly
  4. 2016 alongside the Paris Agreement
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 2015 at the UN Sustainable Development Summit

The 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development with its 17 SDGs and 169 targets was adopted at the UN Sustainable Development Summit on 25 September 2015 in New York. The SDGs replaced the Millennium Development Goals (MDGs). SDG 13 = climate action; SDG 16 = peace, justice, institutions.

9The 'no harm' rule in international environmental law means:

  1. States must not cause any environmental harm within their territory
  2. No state can harm the global commons
  3. A state must not allow activities within its territory to cause significant transboundary environmental harm to other states
  4. Nuclear testing is prohibited in all circumstances
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A state must not allow activities within its territory to cause significant transboundary environmental harm to other states

The 'no harm' principle - originating in the Trail Smelter Arbitration (USA v. Canada, 1938-41) - holds that no state has the right to use or permit use of its territory in such a manner as to cause injury to the territory of another state. Codified in Stockholm Principle 21 and Rio Principle 2.

10The Trail Smelter Arbitration (1941) is significant because:

  1. It was the first international adjudication holding a state responsible for transboundary air pollution
  2. It established the principle of common heritage of mankind
  3. It established the precautionary principle
  4. It created the first international environmental court
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It was the first international adjudication holding a state responsible for transboundary air pollution

Trail Smelter Case (USA v. Canada, 1941) - fumes from a Canadian smelter caused damage to crops and forests in Washington state, USA. The arbitral tribunal held Canada responsible and directed cessation of harmful emissions. This is the foundational case for state responsibility for transboundary environmental harm.

11The Gabcikovo-Nagymaros Project case (Hungary v. Slovakia, ICJ 1997) dealt with:

  1. Nuclear waste in the Danube River
  2. Oil pollution of the Black Sea
  3. Air pollution from Slovak factories
  4. Suspension of a joint dam project on the Danube - ICJ applied sustainable development and EIA principles
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Suspension of a joint dam project on the Danube - ICJ applied sustainable development and EIA principles

Gabcikovo-Nagymaros (1997) - Hungary unilaterally suspended a joint dam project with Slovakia citing environmental concerns. ICJ held both parties had violated treaty obligations and notably applied the principle of sustainable development and recognized evolving environmental norms.

12The 'Polluter Pays Principle' in the Rio Declaration is contained in:

  1. Principle 16
  2. Principle 7
  3. Principle 10
  4. Principle 15
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Principle 16

Rio Declaration Principle 16: National authorities should endeavour to promote the internalization of environmental costs, taking into account the approach that the polluter should, in principle, bear the cost of pollution. Principle 15 = Precautionary; Principle 7 = CBDR; Principle 10 = Access to information.

13The Kyoto Protocol's 'Clean Development Mechanism' (CDM) allows:

  1. Developed countries to buy emission credits from projects in developing countries
  2. Developing countries to impose emission caps on developed countries
  3. Countries to avoid emission reductions by paying a fine
  4. Carbon trading among developing countries only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Developed countries to buy emission credits from projects in developing countries

CDM under the Kyoto Protocol (Article 12): Allows developed countries (Annex I parties) to implement emission-reduction projects in developing countries and earn Certified Emission Reduction (CER) credits, allowing them to partially meet their Kyoto targets.

14The concept of 'environmental constitutionalism' refers to:

  1. Environmental laws enacted by Parliament only
  2. Constitutional review of all environmental legislation
  3. Parliamentary supremacy over environmental treaties
  4. Inclusion of environmental rights and duties in national constitutions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Inclusion of environmental rights and duties in national constitutions

Environmental constitutionalism - the incorporation of environmental rights, duties or principles in national constitutions. India (Articles 48A and 51A(g)), Ecuador (Rights of Nature), South Africa (Section 24 - right to environment) are leading examples. Over 150 countries now have some form of constitutional environmental provision.
