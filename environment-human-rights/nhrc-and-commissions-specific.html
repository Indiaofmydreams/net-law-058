12 practice questions with answers

1The NHRC can, under Section 18 of the PHR Act 1993, approach the Supreme Court or High Court for:

  1. Issuing binding directions to the government
  2. Seeking directions or orders where it considers it necessary to do so for enforcement of its recommendations
  3. Filing criminal complaints against human rights violators
  4. Seeking enhancement of its powers beyond the Act
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Seeking directions or orders where it considers it necessary to do so for enforcement of its recommendations

Section 18(b) of PHR Act 1993: If the Commission considers it necessary, it may approach the Supreme Court or the concerned High Court for the issue of such directions, orders or writs as that Court may deem necessary. This gives the NHRC a route to convert its non-binding recommendations into court-enforceable orders.

2The National Commission for Minorities Act 1992 defines 'minority' to mean:

  1. Any community that forms less than 50% of the national population
  2. A community notified as a minority by the Central Government
  3. Religious minorities only as defined by the Constitution
  4. Linguistic minorities only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A community notified as a minority by the Central Government

The NCM Act 1992 does not define 'minority' by any fixed criterion - Section 2(c) defines 'minority' as a community notified as such by the Central Government. This is why the power to notify minorities is entirely with the Central Government.

3The National Commission for Scheduled Castes under Article 338 submits its annual report to:

  1. The Parliament directly
  2. The President of India who causes it to be laid before Parliament along with a memorandum of action taken
  3. The Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment
  4. The Supreme Court
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The President of India who causes it to be laid before Parliament along with a memorandum of action taken

Article 338(5) and (6): The NCSC shall present annually to the President a report on the working of its safeguards. The President shall cause all such reports to be laid before each House of Parliament, together with a memorandum explaining action taken or proposed to be taken on the recommendations.

4The term of office of the Chairperson and Members of the NHRC under the PHR Act 1993 (as amended in 2019) is:

  1. 5 years or age of 75 years, whichever is earlier
  2. 5 years with possibility of reappointment
  3. 3 years or age of 70 years, whichever is earlier
  4. At the pleasure of the President
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 3 years or age of 70 years, whichever is earlier

PHR Act 1993 as amended in 2019: The Chairperson and every Member shall hold office for a term of three years from the date on which they enter upon their office or until they attain the age of seventy years, whichever is earlier. They are not eligible for reappointment. Previously the term was 5 years.

5Which of the following statements about the NHRC is CORRECT?

  1. NHRC can suo motu initiate inquiry only if a court directs it to do so
  2. NHRC has both civil and criminal jurisdiction
  3. NHRC can visit and inspect jails and other places of detention under intimation to state government
  4. NHRC can inquire into violations committed more than 2 years before the complaint
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. NHRC can visit and inspect jails and other places of detention under intimation to state government

Section 12(c) and Section 14 of PHR Act 1993: The NHRC may visit, under intimation to the State Government, any institution under the control of the State Government where persons are detained or lodged for purposes of treatment, reformation or protection. The words 'under intimation' means it informs but need not seek prior permission.

6The State Human Rights Commission under the PHR Act 1993 can transfer a complaint to the NHRC if:

  1. It cannot understand the complaint
  2. The complaint is more than 6 months old
  3. The State Government requests such transfer
  4. The complaint involves a matter within the jurisdiction of the NHRC or another SHRC
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The complaint involves a matter within the jurisdiction of the NHRC or another SHRC

Section 36A of the PHR Act 1993 (inserted by amendment): Where a complaint is made to a State Commission, and the Commission is of the opinion that the complaint involves a matter which is within the jurisdiction of the National Commission, or the subject matter is common with matters pending before the National Commission, it may transfer the complaint.

7The National Commission for Scheduled Castes under Article 338 has the powers of a civil court in respect of:

  1. Criminal trials against atrocity perpetrators
  2. Awarding compensation directly to victims
  3. Summoning and examining witnesses, requiring production of documents, and receiving evidence on affidavit
  4. Ordering arrest of accused persons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Summoning and examining witnesses, requiring production of documents, and receiving evidence on affidavit

Article 338(8) of the Constitution: The Commission shall, while investigating any matter, have all the powers of a civil court trying a suit including summoning and enforcing attendance of any person, requiring discovery and production of documents, receiving evidence on affidavits. Same power is given to NCST (Art 338A) and NCBC (Art 338B).

8The NHRC can award interim relief under Section 18 by:

  1. Directly ordering payment of compensation
  2. Ordering attachment of property of the violator
  3. Recommending payment of immediate interim relief to the complainant
  4. Directing arrest of the person responsible
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Recommending payment of immediate interim relief to the complainant

Section 18(3) of PHR Act 1993: The Commission may recommend to the concerned Government or authority the payment of immediate interim relief to the victim or members of victim's family. This is in addition to recommending final compensation. The government must implement the recommendation or give reasons for non-compliance.

9The Protection of Human Rights Act 1993 specifically empowers the NHRC to inquire into:

  1. Only matters referred to it by the Supreme Court
  2. Matters referred by State Governments only
  3. Complaints by any person or on its own motion (suo motu) relating to violation or abetment of violation of human rights, or negligence by a public servant
  4. International human rights violations affecting Indian citizens abroad
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Complaints by any person or on its own motion (suo motu) relating to violation or abetment of violation of human rights, or negligence by a public servant

Section 12 of the PHR Act 1993: The Commission shall perform the following functions: inquire, suo motu or on a petition presented to it by a victim or any person on his behalf, into complaint of violation of human rights or abetment thereof, or negligence in the prevention of such violation by a public servant.

10The NHRC's recommendations under the PHR Act 1993 include the power to recommend:

  1. Only payment of compensation
  2. Compensation, initiation of proceedings before High Court/Supreme Court, prosecution of the concerned person, or approach any court for orders
  3. Only institutional reforms
  4. Only training of police and security forces
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Compensation, initiation of proceedings before High Court/Supreme Court, prosecution of the concerned person, or approach any court for orders

Section 18 of PHR Act 1993: The Commission may approach the Supreme Court or concerned High Court; recommend payment of compensation or damages; recommend prosecution; recommend that the concerned Government or authority take further remedial action; or approach any court for appropriate orders.

11The National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC) under Article 338B was given constitutional status by the:

  1. 99th Constitutional Amendment
  2. 100th Constitutional Amendment
  3. 102nd Constitutional Amendment 2018
  4. 105th Constitutional Amendment 2021
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 102nd Constitutional Amendment 2018

The 102nd Constitutional Amendment Act 2018 inserted Article 338B giving constitutional status to the National Commission for Backward Classes. It also inserted Article 342A empowering the President to specify Socially and Educationally Backward Classes (SEBCs) for central purposes. The 105th Amendment 2021 restored states' power to identify OBCs.

12The National Human Rights Commission has recommended which approach to be adopted in custodial death cases?

  1. Cases should be investigated by the same police station
  2. All custodial deaths to be tried by military courts
  3. NHRC should conduct the inquiry itself in all custodial death cases
  4. Immediate reporting of custodial deaths to NHRC; magisterial inquiry; NHRC should be sent post-mortem report within 24 hours
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Immediate reporting of custodial deaths to NHRC; magisterial inquiry; NHRC should be sent post-mortem report within 24 hours

NHRC has established a mandatory protocol on custodial deaths: immediate intimation to NHRC (within 24 hours); videographed post-mortem by a board of three doctors; post-mortem report to be sent to NHRC within 24 hours; and magisterial inquiry. Failure to report is itself treated as a violation.
