10 practice questions with answers

1The 'Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety' is a protocol to which Convention?

  1. Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD)
  2. UNFCCC
  3. Stockholm Convention on POPs
  4. Basel Convention
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD)

The Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety (2000) is a protocol supplementing the Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD) 1992. It governs the transboundary movement of living modified organisms (LMOs). Named after Cartagena, Colombia, though the Protocol itself was adopted in Montreal. India ratified it in 2003.

2The full name of the 'Ramsar Convention' is:

  1. Convention on the Conservation of Migratory Species of Wild Animals
  2. Convention on the Conservation of Nature and Natural Resources
  3. Convention on Biological Diversity of Wetland Ecosystems
  4. Convention on Wetlands of International Importance especially as Waterfowl Habitat
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Convention on Wetlands of International Importance especially as Waterfowl Habitat

The full official name is the 'Convention on Wetlands of International Importance especially as Waterfowl Habitat' - adopted in Ramsar, Iran in 1971. It is the only global environmental convention focused on a specific ecosystem type. India joined Ramsar in 1982 and has over 75 Ramsar sites.

3The 'Bonn Convention' (1979) deals with:

  1. Transboundary air pollution
  2. Hazardous waste movement
  3. Nuclear liability
  4. Conservation of migratory species of wild animals
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Conservation of migratory species of wild animals

The Convention on the Conservation of Migratory Species of Wild Animals (CMS) - known as the Bonn Convention - was adopted in Bonn, Germany in 1979. It aims to conserve terrestrial, marine and avian migratory species throughout their range. India is a party.

4The 'London Convention' (1972) deals with:

  1. Marine pollution from ships (MARPOL)
  2. Nuclear testing in the ocean
  3. Dumping of wastes and other matter at sea
  4. Liability for offshore oil spills
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Dumping of wastes and other matter at sea

The Convention on the Prevention of Marine Pollution by Dumping of Wastes and Other Matter (London Convention) 1972 prohibits dumping of certain hazardous materials at sea and regulates dumping of other materials. Its 1996 Protocol converted it to a precautionary approach.

5The full form of MARPOL 73/78 is:

  1. International Convention for the Prevention of Pollution from Ships
  2. Marine Pollution Prevention and Limitation Convention
  3. Maritime Pollution Abatement and Regulation of Land-based sources
  4. Marine Protocol Against Releases and Liberation of Oil
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. International Convention for the Prevention of Pollution from Ships

MARPOL (International Convention for the Prevention of Pollution from Ships) 1973/1978 is the main international convention covering prevention of pollution from ships - oil, chemicals, harmful substances in packaged form, sewage, garbage and air pollution. India is a party to MARPOL through its six Annexes.

6The 'Rotterdam Convention' (1998) deals with:

  1. Trade in endangered species
  2. Marine pollution from Rotterdam port
  3. Prior Informed Consent (PIC) procedure for certain hazardous chemicals and pesticides in international trade
  4. Nuclear waste transport
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Prior Informed Consent (PIC) procedure for certain hazardous chemicals and pesticides in international trade

Rotterdam Convention on the Prior Informed Consent Procedure for Certain Hazardous Chemicals and Pesticides in International Trade (1998, in force 2004) - requires exporters of listed hazardous chemicals to obtain Prior Informed Consent (PIC) of importing countries before shipment.

7The 'Aarhus Convention' was named after:

  1. The country where it was negotiated - Austria
  2. Its principal drafter, Henrik Aarhus
  3. The Danish city where it was adopted - Aarhus, Denmark
  4. The Aarhus Protocol under CLRTAP
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The Danish city where it was adopted - Aarhus, Denmark

The Convention on Access to Information, Public Participation in Decision-Making and Access to Justice in Environmental Matters was adopted on 25 June 1998 in Aarhus (Arhus), Denmark at the Fourth Ministerial Conference of the UN Economic Commission for Europe.

8The 'Minamata Convention' is named after:

  1. A Japanese word meaning 'clean water'
  2. Its principal negotiator, Dr. Minamata
  3. The city in Japan where mass mercury poisoning occurred in the 1950s-60s
  4. A Japanese environmental activist
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The city in Japan where mass mercury poisoning occurred in the 1950s-60s

Minamata disease was a severe neurological syndrome caused by mercury poisoning in Minamata city, Japan, from the 1950s. The Chisso Corporation discharged methylmercury into Minamata Bay, contaminating fish and shellfish - causing thousands of deaths and severe birth defects.

9The 'Nagoya Protocol on Access and Benefit Sharing' was adopted at which city?

  1. Tokyo
  2. Kyoto
  3. Nagoya
  4. Osaka
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Nagoya

The Nagoya Protocol on Access to Genetic Resources and Fair and Equitable Sharing of Benefits Arising from their Utilization was adopted in Nagoya, Japan at COP10 of the CBD in October 2010. It supplements the CBD and provides a transparent framework for implementing the CBD's third objective.

10The Convention on Cluster Munitions (2008) is relevant to human rights because:

  1. It prohibits use, production, transfer and stockpiling of cluster munitions that cause indiscriminate harm to civilians and create long-term humanitarian problems
  2. It regulates manufacture of explosives globally
  3. It creates liability for environmental damage from warfare
  4. It governs compensation for victims of nuclear weapons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It prohibits use, production, transfer and stockpiling of cluster munitions that cause indiscriminate harm to civilians and create long-term humanitarian problems

Convention on Cluster Munitions (2008) - prohibits cluster bombs which scatter submunitions over wide areas, killing and maiming civilians long after conflicts end (unexploded submunitions act like landmines). It is a humanitarian disarmament treaty with direct human rights implications. India has not signed or ratified it.
