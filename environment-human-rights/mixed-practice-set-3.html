5The Prohibition of Child Marriage Act 2006 declares a child marriage:

  1. Void ab initio in all cases
  2. A criminal offence but valid marriage
  3. Voidable at the option of the contracting party who was a child at the time of marriage
  4. Void only if solemnized under Hindu personal law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Voidable at the option of the contracting party who was a child at the time of marriage

Prohibition of Child Marriage Act 2006: A child marriage is voidable (not void ab initio) at the option of the party who was a child - the child can file a petition for annulment before the District Court within two years of attaining majority. Certain child marriages can be declared void when the minor is taken by fraud, force, or allurement.

6The Domestic Violence Act 2005 defines 'domestic relationship' to include:

  1. Only husband and wife in a legally registered marriage
  2. Only blood relatives living together
  3. Persons who live or have lived together in a shared household - including marriage, relationship in nature of marriage, family, and adoption
  4. Only persons related by marriage under personal laws
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Persons who live or have lived together in a shared household - including marriage, relationship in nature of marriage, family, and adoption

Section 2(f) of the DV Act 2005 defines 'domestic relationship' broadly: a relationship between two persons who live or have lived together at any point in time in a shared household, when they are related by consanguinity, marriage, a relationship in the nature of marriage, adoption, or are family members living as a joint family.

7The Pre-Conception and Pre-Natal Diagnostic Techniques (PCPNDT) Act 1994 was enacted to:

  1. Regulate IVF procedures in India
  2. Regulate prenatal care for pregnant women
  3. Prevent birth of children with genetic disorders
  4. Prohibit sex determination of foetus before or after conception and prevent female foeticide
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Prohibit sex determination of foetus before or after conception and prevent female foeticide

PCPNDT Act 1994 prohibits: use of any technique for sex determination before or after conception; advertisement of such techniques; sex-selective abortion. It mandates registration of all ultrasound clinics and genetic counselling centres. The Act was amended in 2003 to cover pre-conception techniques.

8The 'Optional Protocol to the CRC on the Sale of Children, Child Prostitution and Child Pornography' (2000) was adopted to:

  1. Define children's economic rights
  2. Regulate child labour in hazardous industries
  3. Criminalise the sale of children, child prostitution and child pornography with binding obligations on states
  4. Protect children in armed conflict
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Criminalise the sale of children, child prostitution and child pornography with binding obligations on states

The Optional Protocol to CRC on Sale of Children (2000) requires states to: criminalize the sale of children, child prostitution and child pornography; establish universal jurisdiction over these offences; protect child victims; and cooperate internationally on prosecution. India ratified this protocol in 2005.

9The National Commission for Women Act 1990 empowers the NCW to take up cases of:

  1. Deprivation of women's rights, non-implementation of laws enacted for protection of women, and non-compliance of policy decisions to their detriment
  2. Only criminal offences against women
  3. Only matrimonial disputes
  4. Employment discrimination in private sector exclusively
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Deprivation of women's rights, non-implementation of laws enacted for protection of women, and non-compliance of policy decisions to their detriment

Section 10 of the NCW Act 1990 specifies that one of NCW's functions is to look into complaints relating to: deprivation of women's rights; non-implementation of laws enacted by Parliament and State Legislatures for women; non-compliance of policy decisions meant for women's welfare. The NCW can summon witnesses and call for documents like a civil court.

10The 'Optional Protocol to the CRC on the Involvement of Children in Armed Conflict' (2000) raises the minimum age for:

  1. Military recruitment and direct participation in hostilities - to 18 years for direct participation
  2. All child labour to 18 years
  3. School leaving age to 18 years
  4. Criminal responsibility to 18 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Military recruitment and direct participation in hostilities - to 18 years for direct participation

Optional Protocol to the CRC on the Involvement of Children in Armed Conflict (2000): States must take all feasible measures to ensure that persons below 18 years do not take direct part in hostilities; prohibits compulsory recruitment below 18; voluntary recruitment below 18 requires safeguards. India ratified in 2005.

11The 'Commissions for Protection of Child Rights Act 2005' requires each state to establish a State Commission within:

  1. 6 months of the Act's commencement
  2. 1 year of the Act's commencement
  3. As decided by each state government
  4. 3 years of the Act's commencement
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 3 years of the Act's commencement

The CPCR Act 2005 established the NCPCR and required states to establish State Commissions for Protection of Child Rights (SCPCRs) within three years of the Act's commencement. These commissions examine and review safeguards for protection of child rights.

12The case that first applied the 'Doctrine of Absolute Liability' in India was decided by a Constitution Bench of how many judges?

  1. 3
  2. 5
  3. 7
  4. 9
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 5

M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Oleum Gas Leak case, 1987) was decided by a Constitution Bench of 5 judges of the Supreme Court. The judgment was authored by Chief Justice P.N. Bhagwati. The Constitution Bench was necessary because the case involved interpretation of Articles 21 and 32 of the Constitution.

13The Bhopal Gas Tragedy case in the Supreme Court resulted in a settlement of how much compensation?

  1. USD 300 million
  2. USD 470 million
  3. USD 750 million
  4. USD 1 billion
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. USD 470 million

In Union Carbide Corporation v. Union of India (1989), the Supreme Court approved a settlement of USD 470 million (approximately Rs. 715 crore at the time) to be paid by Union Carbide Corporation. This settlement was widely criticized as grossly inadequate given the scale of the disaster.

14In Narmada Bachao Andolan v. Union of India (2000), the Supreme Court ruled:

  1. Construction of the dam could proceed subject to proper rehabilitation of displaced persons
  2. The Sardar Sarovar Dam should be demolished
  3. The dam violated the Forest Conservation Act and must be stopped
  4. Environmental clearance for the dam was illegal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Construction of the dam could proceed subject to proper rehabilitation of displaced persons

In Narmada Bachao Andolan v. UOI (2000), a 3-judge bench (2:1 majority) allowed construction of the Sardar Sarovar Dam to proceed, subject to the condition that resettlement and rehabilitation of displaced persons must be completed before submergence. Justice B.N. Kirpal wrote the majority judgment.

15The case of M.C. Mehta v. Union of India relating to child labour in hazardous industries (1996) directed:

  1. Complete ban on all child labour immediately
  2. Criminal prosecution of all employers of child labour
  3. Creation of a Child Labour Rehabilitation-cum-Welfare Fund and payment of Rs. 20,000 per child employed in hazardous industries
  4. Relocation of all hazardous industries outside city limits
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Creation of a Child Labour Rehabilitation-cum-Welfare Fund and payment of Rs. 20,000 per child employed in hazardous industries

M.C. Mehta v. UOI (1996 - Child Labour case): SC directed that employers of child labour in hazardous industries must pay Rs. 20,000 per child into a Child Labour Rehabilitation-cum-Welfare Fund; the State must provide an adult family member with a job; and non-hazardous child labour must be regulated with compulsory education.

16The case of 'T.N. Godavarman Thirumulpad v. Union of India' has been pending in the Supreme Court continuously since:

  1. 1990
  2. 1995
  3. 1998
  4. 2000
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1995

T.N. Godavarman Thirumulpad v. UOI was filed in 1995 and has been continuously pending - making it one of the longest-running cases in Indian judicial history. The SC has been monitoring forest protection, encroachments, compensatory afforestation, and implementation of the Forest Conservation Act across all states. It is the most comprehensive exercise of continuing mandamus in Indian environmental law.
