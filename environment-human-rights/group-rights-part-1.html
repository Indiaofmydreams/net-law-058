14 practice questions with answers

1CEDAW (1979) is called the 'international bill of rights for women' because:

  1. It creates a Women's World Court
  2. It comprehensively addresses discrimination against women in all spheres of life
  3. It grants women equal voting rights
  4. It establishes maternity leave standards globally
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. It comprehensively addresses discrimination against women in all spheres of life

CEDAW (Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women) adopted 1979, in force 1981 - covers discrimination in political/public life, education, employment, health, economic/social life, and marriage/family. India ratified in 1993 with reservations.

2The UN Convention on the Rights of the Child was adopted in:

  1. 1979
  2. 1989
  3. 1990
  4. 1995
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1989

UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC) was adopted on 20 November 1989 (Universal Children's Day) and entered into force in 1990. It is the most widely ratified human rights treaty in history. India ratified it in 1992.

3The four guiding principles of the CRC are:

  1. Education, health, shelter, food
  2. Non-discrimination; best interests of the child; right to life/survival/development; right to participation
  3. Freedom, equality, justice, dignity
  4. Civil, political, economic, social rights for children
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Non-discrimination; best interests of the child; right to life/survival/development; right to participation

CRC's four guiding principles: Non-discrimination (Art 2); Best interests of the child (Art 3); Right to life, survival and development (Art 6); Respect for the views of the child/participation (Art 12).

4The UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) was adopted in:

  1. 2000
  2. 2004
  3. 2006
  4. 2010
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 2006

CRPD was adopted by UNGA on 13 December 2006, entered into force May 2008. India ratified in 2007. It shifts from the medical/charity model to a rights-based model of disability. Domestic implementation: Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act 2016.

5The Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act 2016 (India) increased specified disabilities to:

  1. 7
  2. 14
  3. 21
  4. 35
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 21

The RPwD Act 2016 replaced the Persons with Disabilities Act 1995, increasing specified disabilities from 7 to 21. New additions include acid attack victims, Parkinson's disease, sickle cell disease. It mandates 5% reservation in government jobs.

Report this question

6Articles 29 and 30 of the Indian Constitution protect:

  1. Rights of women
  2. Cultural and educational rights of minorities
  3. Rights of Scheduled Castes
  4. Rights of persons with disabilities
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Cultural and educational rights of minorities

Article 29 protects the right of any section of citizens to conserve its distinct language, script or culture. Article 30 gives linguistic and religious minorities the right to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice.

7The Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act was enacted in:

  1. 1998
  2. 2001
  3. 2005
  4. 2010
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 2005

Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act 2005 (came into force 26 October 2006) provides civil remedies for domestic violence - protection orders, residence orders, monetary relief, custody orders. Covers married women, women in live-in relationships, and women in other domestic relationships.

8The UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples was adopted in:

  1. 1993
  2. 2000
  3. 2007
  4. 2012
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 2007

UNDRIP was adopted by UNGA on 13 September 2007 (India voted in favour). It recognizes indigenous peoples' rights to self-determination, lands, territories and resources, culture, and traditional knowledge.

9The Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act 2007 provides:

  1. Pension rights for all senior citizens
  2. Maintenance of parents/senior citizens by children, speedy Maintenance Tribunals, and old age homes
  3. Only criminal liability for abandoning parents
  4. Free medical care for all persons above 60 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Maintenance of parents/senior citizens by children, speedy Maintenance Tribunals, and old age homes

The Act 2007 provides for: maintenance of parents and senior citizens by children/relatives through Maintenance Tribunals; establishment of old age homes by states; protection for property transfers made under threat or coercion.

10The Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act 2006 recognizes:

  1. Only ownership rights over forest land
  2. Rights of forest departments over tribal lands
  3. Rights of states to claim forest revenue from tribals
  4. Individual and community forest rights including rights to collect and use minor forest produce
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Individual and community forest rights including rights to collect and use minor forest produce

Forest Rights Act 2006 recognizes: Individual rights (to live on and cultivate forest land) and community rights (community tenures, rights to collect/use/dispose of minor forest produce, developmental rights). Gram Sabha is the key authority.

11The Optional Protocol to CEDAW allows:

  1. Individual women to submit communications to the CEDAW Committee
  2. States to add reservations to CEDAW
  3. Only states to file inter-state complaints
  4. CEDAW Committee to impose sanctions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Individual women to submit communications to the CEDAW Committee

The Optional Protocol to CEDAW (1999, in force 2000) provides two procedures: the communications procedure (individual women or groups can submit complaints to the CEDAW Committee) and the inquiry procedure (Committee can investigate grave or systematic violations). India has not ratified the Optional Protocol.

Report this question

12The Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Amendment Act 2016 prohibits employment of children below:

  1. 14 years in all occupations
  2. 16 years in all occupations
  3. 14 years in all occupations and adolescents (14-18 years) in hazardous occupations
  4. 18 years in any paid employment
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 14 years in all occupations and adolescents (14-18 years) in hazardous occupations

The Child Labour Amendment Act 2016 prohibits: employment of children below 14 years in all occupations (with limited exceptions); employment of adolescents (14-18 years) in hazardous processes. This aligns with Article 21A (Right to Education) and Article 24.

Report this question

13The National Commission for Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR) was established under:

  1. CRC 1989
  2. Juvenile Justice Act 2000
  3. Commissions for Protection of Child Rights Act 2005
  4. Constitution of India, Article 24
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Commissions for Protection of Child Rights Act 2005

NCPCR was established under the Commissions for Protection of Child Rights (CPCR) Act 2005. It monitors implementation of laws relating to child rights and reviews child-related policies. State Commissions for Protection of Child Rights (SCPCRs) are established at state level under the same Act.

Report this question

14The Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act was enacted in:

  1. 2005
  2. 2010
  3. 2012
  4. 2015
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 2012

POCSO Act 2012 was enacted to protect children from sexual assault, harassment and pornography. It defines a child as any person below 18 years. It was amended in 2019 to introduce the death penalty for aggravated penetrative assault on children below 12 years.
