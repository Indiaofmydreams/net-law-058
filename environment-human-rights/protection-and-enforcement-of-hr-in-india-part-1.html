17 practice questions with answers

1The Protection of Human Rights Act was enacted in:

  1. 1991
  2. 1993
  3. 1995
  4. 1997
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1993

The Protection of Human Rights Act 1993 established the National Human Rights Commission, State Human Rights Commissions, and Human Rights Courts. It was enacted following India's ratification of the ICCPR and ICESCR.

2The Chairperson of the NHRC must be:

  1. A retired Chief Justice of India or a retired Judge of the Supreme Court
  2. A retired Chief Justice of a High Court
  3. Any retired Supreme Court judge
  4. A person with 20 years of human rights work
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A retired Chief Justice of India or a retired Judge of the Supreme Court

As amended by the Protection of Human Rights (Amendment) Act 2019, the Chairperson of the NHRC shall be a retired Chief Justice of India or a retired Judge of the Supreme Court of India.

Report this question

3The NHRC cannot inquire into a complaint if the alleged violation occurred more than:

  1. 1 year ago
  2. 6 months ago
  3. 2 years ago
  4. 3 years ago
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 1 year ago

Section 36(2) of the PHR Act 1993: The Commission shall not inquire into any matter if the complaint is made after ONE YEAR from the date on which the act constituting violation is alleged to have been committed.

Report this question

4The NHRC cannot directly investigate complaints against the armed forces. This is governed by:

  1. Article 33 of the Constitution
  2. Armed Forces (Special Powers) Act 1958
  3. National Security Act 1980
  4. Section 19 of the Protection of Human Rights Act 1993
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Section 19 of the Protection of Human Rights Act 1993

Section 19 of the PHR Act 1993: The Commission shall not inquire into complaints against the armed forces. It may seek a report from the Central Government on such matters and make recommendations thereon.

Report this question

5The recommendations of the NHRC are:

  1. Binding on the government
  2. Not binding but the government must consider them and report compliance
  3. Binding only if approved by the Supreme Court
  4. Without any legal force whatsoever
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Not binding but the government must consider them and report compliance

NHRC recommendations under Section 18 are not binding - the Commission can recommend compensation, approach courts, recommend prosecution. The government must consider the recommendations and report what action it has taken.

Report this question

6Who was the first Chairperson of the NHRC?

  1. Justice A.S. Anand
  2. Justice Ranganath Misra
  3. Justice M.N. Venkatachaliah
  4. Justice J.S. Verma
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Justice Ranganath Misra

Justice Ranganath Misra (former Chief Justice of India) was the first Chairperson of the NHRC when it was constituted on 12 October 1993. This is a frequently tested factual detail.

Report this question

7The National Commission for Minorities was established under:

  1. The Constitution of India (Article 338)
  2. Protection of Human Rights Act 1993
  3. Minorities Safeguard Act 1990
  4. National Commission for Minorities Act 1992
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. National Commission for Minorities Act 1992

National Commission for Minorities Act 1992 established the NCM. It evaluates the working of safeguards for minorities under the Constitution and laws, makes recommendations to Central/State governments.

Report this question

8Which communities are officially notified as minorities by the Central Government under the NCM Act?

  1. Muslims, Sikhs, Buddhists, Jains only
  2. Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists, Jains, Zoroastrians (Parsis)
  3. Any community that is less than 50% of a state's population
  4. All non-Hindu communities
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists, Jains, Zoroastrians (Parsis)

Six communities have been notified as minorities: Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists, Zoroastrians (Parsis), and Jains (Jains notified in 2014). The power to notify minorities vests in the Central Government under the NCM Act.

Report this question

9The National Commission for Women was established in:

  1. 1987
  2. 1990
  3. January 1992
  4. 1995
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. January 1992

National Commission for Women was established in January 1992 under the National Commission for Women Act 1990. Jayanti Patnaik was its first Chairperson. It reviews constitutional and legal safeguards for women.

Report this question

10The National Commission for Scheduled Castes derives its constitutional basis from:

  1. Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955
  2. Article 338 of the Constitution
  3. SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act 1989
  4. Untouchability (Offences) Act 1955
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Article 338 of the Constitution

Article 338 of the Constitution establishes the National Commission for Scheduled Castes. The 89th Constitutional Amendment Act 2003 bifurcated the earlier combined commission into NCSC (Article 338) and NCST (Article 338A).

Report this question

11The National Commission for Scheduled Tribes was created by:

  1. 89th Constitutional Amendment 2003 - Article 338A
  2. 65th Constitutional Amendment
  3. SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act
  4. An executive order of the President
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 89th Constitutional Amendment 2003 - Article 338A

The 89th Constitutional Amendment Act 2003 inserted Article 338A creating a separate National Commission for Scheduled Tribes. Before this amendment, both SCs and STs were covered under a single commission under Article 338.

Report this question

12The National Commission for Backward Classes received constitutional status through:

  1. National Commission for Backward Classes Act 1993
  2. Article 340 read with executive order
  3. 102nd Constitutional Amendment Act 2018 - Article 338B
  4. Mandal Commission Report implementation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 102nd Constitutional Amendment Act 2018 - Article 338B

The 102nd Constitutional Amendment Act 2018 inserted Article 338B giving constitutional status to the National Commission for Backward Classes. It also inserted Article 342A empowering the President to specify SEBCs.

Report this question

13Human Rights Courts under the PHR Act 1993 are established at:

  1. Supreme Court level
  2. High Court level
  3. District level (one Court of Session per district)
  4. Taluk/Sub-district level
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. District level (one Court of Session per district)

Section 30 of the PHR Act 1993: The State Government shall specify for each district a Court of Session to be a Human Rights Court for trial of offences arising out of violations of human rights. A Special Public Prosecutor must be appointed for each such court.

Report this question

14The NHRC can visit any institution under the control of the State Government for the purpose of:

  1. Conducting elections
  2. Studying the conditions of inmates - jails, reformatories, psychiatric hospitals etc.
  3. Auditing government accounts
  4. Inspecting environmental compliance
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Studying the conditions of inmates - jails, reformatories, psychiatric hospitals etc.

Section 12(c) of the PHR Act 1993: The NHRC may visit, under intimation to the State Government, any institution where persons are detained or lodged - including jails, juvenile homes, psychiatric hospitals - to study living conditions of inmates and make recommendations.

Report this question

15Which case established that the right to health is a fundamental right under Article 21?

  1. Bandhua Mukti Morcha v. UOI (1984)
  2. Paschim Banga Khet Mazdoor Samity v. State of WB (1996)
  3. Consumer Education & Research Centre v. UOI (1995)
  4. Both (B) and (C)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Both (B) and (C)

Both cases are significant: Consumer Education & Research Centre v. UOI (1995) held right to health is a fundamental right under Article 21. Paschim Banga Khet Mazdoor Samity (1996) held State's failure to provide emergency medical treatment violates Article 21.

Report this question

16The annual report of the NHRC is laid before:

  1. Each House of Parliament
  2. The Supreme Court
  3. The President of India only
  4. The concerned State Legislative Assemblies
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Each House of Parliament

Section 20 of the PHR Act 1993: The Commission shall submit an annual report to the Central Government. The Central Government shall cause the annual report and special reports to be laid before each House of Parliament along with a memorandum of action taken.

Report this question

17Which case held that 'bonded labour' violates Articles 21 and 23 of the Constitution?

  1. Bandhua Mukti Morcha v. Union of India (1984)
  2. Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan (1997)
  3. Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corporation (1985)
  4. D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal (1997)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Bandhua Mukti Morcha v. Union of India (1984)

Bandhua Mukti Morcha v. Union of India (1984) - the SC held that bonded labour violates Article 21 (right to life and dignity) and Article 23 (prohibition of forced labour). Justice Bhagwati ordered the release of bonded labourers in stone quarries.
