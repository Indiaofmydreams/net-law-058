8 practice questions with answers

1The 'Four Freedoms' speech by President Franklin D. Roosevelt (1941) articulated:

  1. Freedom of speech, freedom of press, freedom of assembly, freedom of petition
  2. Freedom of speech, freedom of religion, freedom from want, freedom from fear
  3. Freedom, equality, justice and dignity
  4. Life, liberty, property and pursuit of happiness
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Freedom of speech, freedom of religion, freedom from want, freedom from fear

FDR's Four Freedoms (January 1941 State of the Union address): Freedom of speech and expression; Freedom of worship (religion); Freedom from want (economic security); Freedom from fear (global reduction of armaments). These articulations directly influenced the drafting of the UN Charter and UDHR.

2The 'Declaration on the Granting of Independence to Colonial Countries and Peoples' (UN General Assembly Resolution 1514, 1960) recognized:

  1. Right to economic development
  2. Right of all peoples to self-determination and immediate independence from colonialism
  3. Right to participate in democratic elections
  4. Rights of minority communities within colonies
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Right of all peoples to self-determination and immediate independence from colonialism

UNGA Resolution 1514 (XV) 1960: proclaimed that all peoples have the right to self-determination and that alien subjugation, domination and exploitation constitute a denial of fundamental human rights. It accelerated the decolonization process and established self-determination as a binding norm.

3The 'International Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination' (ICERD) was adopted in:

  1. 1963
  2. 1965
  3. 1969
  4. 1973
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1965

ICERD was adopted by UNGA on 21 December 1965 and entered into force in 1969. It is the first of the core UN human rights treaties to be adopted after the UDHR. The Committee on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination (CERD) monitors its implementation. India ratified ICERD in 1968.

4The 'Convention Against Torture and Other Cruel, Inhuman or Degrading Treatment or Punishment' (CAT) was adopted in:

  1. 1975
  2. 1979
  3. 1984
  4. 1989
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1984

CAT (Convention Against Torture) was adopted by UNGA on 10 December 1984 and entered into force in 1987. It defines torture, prohibits it absolutely, and requires criminalization, universal jurisdiction, non-refoulement in torture cases, and the right to rehabilitation for victims. India signed in 1997 but has NOT ratified CAT.

5The UN Declaration on the Rights of Persons Belonging to National or Ethnic, Religious and Linguistic Minorities was adopted in:

  1. 1981
  2. 1986
  3. 1992
  4. 1998
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1992

UNGA Resolution 47/135 adopted the Declaration on Minorities on 18 December 1992. It builds on ICCPR Article 27 and provides more detailed protection - states shall protect the existence and national/ethnic/cultural/religious/linguistic identity of minorities and adopt measures to that end.

6The 'International Convention for the Protection of All Persons from Enforced Disappearance' was adopted in:

  1. 1998
  2. 2002
  3. 2006
  4. 2010
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 2006

The International Convention for the Protection of All Persons from Enforced Disappearance was adopted by UNGA on 20 December 2006 and entered into force in 2010. It defines enforced disappearance as a crime, prohibits it absolutely, and establishes the Committee on Enforced Disappearances. India has not ratified this Convention.

7'Minorities' in international human rights law are primarily identified based on:

  1. Ethnic, religious or linguistic characteristics, combined with non-dominant position in society and often numerical inferiority
  2. Economic status alone
  3. Only religious characteristics
  4. Being less than 15% of total population
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Ethnic, religious or linguistic characteristics, combined with non-dominant position in society and often numerical inferiority

Francesco Capotorti's definition (UN Special Rapporteur, 1979) - a minority is a group numerically inferior to the rest of the population, in a non-dominant position, whose members possess ethnic, religious or linguistic characteristics differing from the rest of the population. Numerical inferiority alone is not sufficient - non-dominance is equally important.

8The concept of 'human dignity' as the foundation of human rights is most explicitly enshrined in which instrument?

  1. ICCPR
  2. Universal Declaration of Human Rights (Preamble and Article 1)
  3. African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights
  4. American Convention on Human Rights
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Universal Declaration of Human Rights (Preamble and Article 1)

The UDHR Preamble explicitly recognizes 'the inherent dignity and of the equal and inalienable rights of all members of the human family' as the foundation of freedom, justice and peace. Article 1 states 'All human beings are born free and equal in dignity and rights.' The concept of dignity is most elaborately developed in the UDHR.
