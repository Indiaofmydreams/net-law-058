16 practice questions with answers

1The Principal Bench of the NGT is located at:

  1. New Delhi
  2. Mumbai
  3. Chennai
  4. Bhopal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. New Delhi

The Principal Bench of the NGT is at New Delhi. Zonal circuit benches are at Bhopal (Central Zone), Pune (Western Zone), Kolkata (Eastern Zone), and Chennai (Southern Zone).

2The NGT does NOT have jurisdiction over matters relating to:

  1. Air and water pollution
  2. Environmental impact assessment
  3. Hazardous substances
  4. Wildlife and biodiversity - the Wildlife Protection Act 1972
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Wildlife and biodiversity - the Wildlife Protection Act 1972

Section 14 read with Schedule I of the NGT Act 2010 excludes matters covered under the Wildlife Protection Act 1972 from NGT's jurisdiction. Forest Conservation Act and Biological Diversity Act matters are also excluded.

Report this question

3The limitation period to file an application before the NGT is:

  1. 1 year from the cause of action
  2. 3 months from the cause of action
  3. 6 months from the cause of action
  4. 2 years from the cause of action
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 6 months from the cause of action

Section 16 of the NGT Act: Every application for grant of relief or compensation shall be made within 6 months of the occurrence of the cause of action. This period can be extended by the NGT on showing sufficient cause.

4The Chairperson of the NGT must be:

  1. A retired Chief Justice of a High Court
  2. An IFS officer with 25 years of experience
  3. A retired Chief Justice of India or a retired Judge of the Supreme Court
  4. Any expert with 20 years of environmental experience
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A retired Chief Justice of India or a retired Judge of the Supreme Court

Section 5 of the NGT Act: The Chairperson shall be a retired Chief Justice of India or a Judge of the Supreme Court. Expert Members must have 15 years of administrative experience at senior levels in environment-related matters.

Report this question

5Appeals from NGT orders lie to:

  1. High Court under Article 226
  2. National Environmental Appellate Authority
  3. Supreme Court under Article 136
  4. Union Cabinet
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Supreme Court under Article 136

Appeals from NGT orders lie to the Supreme Court of India under Article 136 (Special Leave Petition). The NGT Act specifically bars jurisdiction of civil courts over NGT matters.

Report this question

6Every bench of the NGT must consist of at least:

  1. 3 judicial members
  2. 2 judicial members and 1 expert member
  3. 1 judicial member and 1 expert member
  4. 1 judicial member only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1 judicial member and 1 expert member

The NGT Act requires that every bench shall consist of at least one Judicial Member and one Expert Member sitting together. This dual composition ensures both legal and scientific expertise in adjudication.

Report this question

7The NGT is described as the 'first of its kind in Asia' because:

  1. It has the power to impose criminal sentences
  2. It is the first specialized environmental court/tribunal in Asia
  3. It can override Supreme Court orders on environment
  4. It has international jurisdiction over transboundary pollution
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. It is the first specialized environmental court/tribunal in Asia

The NGT is described as the first dedicated, specialized environmental court/tribunal in Asia. Australia's Land and Environment Court (1979) and New Zealand's Environment Court predate it globally.

Report this question

8The NGT replaced which earlier body?

  1. Central Pollution Control Board
  2. Ministry of Environment and Forests Tribunal
  3. High Court Green Benches
  4. National Environment Appellate Authority (NEAA)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. National Environment Appellate Authority (NEAA)

The NGT replaced the National Environment Appellate Authority (NEAA), established under the National Environment Appellate Authority Act 1997. The NEAA had very limited jurisdiction (only appeals against environmental clearances) making it largely ineffective. NGT has far wider jurisdiction.

Report this question

9The NGT Act 2010 provides for no-fault liability under:

  1. Section 14
  2. Section 17
  3. Section 19
  4. Section 25
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Section 17

The NGT Act 2010, Section 17, provides for no-fault liability - any person who has suffered substantial injury due to an accident or occurrence of any hazardous substance is entitled to claim relief and compensation without proof of negligence. This is consistent with the absolute liability doctrine.

10Can the NGT suo motu take cognizance of environmental violations?

  1. No, it can only act on applications filed by affected parties
  2. Yes, the NGT has exercised suo motu powers in several cases
  3. Only if directed by the Supreme Court
  4. Only in cases involving national security
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Yes, the NGT has exercised suo motu powers in several cases

Although the NGT Act does not explicitly provide for suo motu powers, the NGT has in practice taken suo motu cognizance of environmental violations based on newspaper reports and other sources, following the precedent of constitutional courts.

11The NGT Act 2010 defines 'substantial question relating to environment' to include:

  1. A question where there is a direct violation of a specific statutory environmental obligation or where the community at large is affected
  2. Any question relating to environment no matter how minor
  3. Only questions involving international environmental law
  4. Only questions arising from EIA violations
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A question where there is a direct violation of a specific statutory environmental obligation or where the community at large is affected

Section 2(m) of the NGT Act 2010 defines 'substantial question relating to environment' to mean a question where there is: a direct violation of a specific statutory environmental obligation; the community at large is affected; damage to environment is broad in scope; and harm to environment is irreversible.

12The NGT derives its power to enforce its orders from:

  1. Section 26 of the NGT Act 2010 - which gives it powers of a civil court
  2. The Code of Civil Procedure 1908
  3. The Contempt of Courts Act 1971
  4. Article 215 of the Constitution
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Section 26 of the NGT Act 2010 - which gives it powers of a civil court

Section 26 of the NGT Act 2010: Every order made by the Tribunal shall be executable by the Tribunal as a decree of a civil court. Section 25 provides that failure to comply with any NGT order may result in imprisonment up to 3 years or fine up to Rs. 10 crore, or both.

13The Expert Members of the NGT must have experience in:

  1. Environmental science or technology only
  2. Administrative experience of at least 15 years in dealing with environmental matters at the level of Additional Secretary to Government of India or above
  3. Any science subject for 10 years
  4. Legal experience of 15 years in environmental cases
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Administrative experience of at least 15 years in dealing with environmental matters at the level of Additional Secretary to Government of India or above

Section 6 of the NGT Act 2010: Expert Members must have experience and knowledge of not less than 15 years in the field of environment and forests. They must have held the position of Additional Secretary to GoI or above, or equivalent.

Report this question

14The NGT in Almitra H. Patel v. Union of India dealt with:

  1. Solid waste management and garbage dumping in cities
  2. Ganga river pollution
  3. Coastal erosion in Mumbai
  4. Air pollution from Delhi's landfills
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Solid waste management and garbage dumping in cities

Almitra H. Patel v. UOI is the landmark case on municipal solid waste management in India. The SC/NGT issued extensive directions for implementation of Municipal Solid Waste Rules and subsequently Solid Waste Management Rules 2016.

Report this question

15Which of the following matters falls WITHIN the NGT's jurisdiction under Schedule I of the NGT Act 2010?

  1. Matters under the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act 1974
  2. Matters under the Wildlife Protection Act 1972
  3. Matters under the Biological Diversity Act 2002 (excluded from Schedule I)
  4. Matters under the Forest (Conservation) Act 1980 (excluded)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Matters under the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act 1974

Schedule I of the NGT Act 2010 lists environmental laws within NGT's jurisdiction including: Water Act 1974, Water Cess Act 1977, Air Act 1981, EPA 1986, Public Liability Insurance Act 1991. The Wildlife Protection Act 1972 is excluded from Schedule I.

Report this question

16The Public Liability Insurance Act 1991 was enacted to:

  1. Provide mandatory insurance for immediate relief to victims of accidents involving hazardous substances, on a no-fault basis
  2. Provide insurance to public sector undertakings
  3. Create a government fund for environmental disasters
  4. Provide life insurance for workers in hazardous industries
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Provide mandatory insurance for immediate relief to victims of accidents involving hazardous substances, on a no-fault basis

Public Liability Insurance Act 1991 requires every owner handling hazardous substances to take out an insurance policy to meet liability for giving immediate relief to victims of accidents. Relief is on a no-fault basis. The Act was a legislative response to the Bhopal Gas Tragedy.
