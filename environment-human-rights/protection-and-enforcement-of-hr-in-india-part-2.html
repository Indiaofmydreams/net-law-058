16 practice questions with answers

1D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal (1997) laid down guidelines on:

  1. Prevention of custodial torture and deaths, and arrest procedures
  2. Environmental protection
  3. Sexual harassment at workplace
  4. Rights of bonded labourers
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Prevention of custodial torture and deaths, and arrest procedures

D.K. Basu v. State of WB (1997) - the SC laid down detailed guidelines to prevent custodial torture, deaths and illegal detention including: visible identification of police; memo of arrest; medical examination every 48 hours; informing relative; right to legal aid. Violation is contempt of court.

2The Domestic Violence Act 2005 provides which type of order for the immediate safety of the aggrieved?

  1. Mandatory Injunction
  2. Maintenance Order only
  3. Custody Order only
  4. Protection Order under Section 18
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Protection Order under Section 18

The DV Act 2005 provides for: Protection Orders (Section 18 - prohibiting the respondent from committing domestic violence); Residence Orders (Section 19); Monetary Relief (Section 20); Custody Orders (Section 21); and Compensation Orders (Section 22).

3The SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Amendment Act 2015 added which significant provision?

  1. Added new offences including social boycott and garlanding with footwear
  2. Increased imprisonment for offences
  3. Established special courts in every taluk
  4. Extended the Act to cover OBCs
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Added new offences including social boycott and garlanding with footwear

The SC/ST (POA) Amendment Act 2015 added new offences: social or economic boycott; dedicating SC/ST women as devadasi; forcing to dispose of human/animal carcasses; garlanding with footwear; imposing social disabilities; and manual scavenging. Also strengthened special courts.

4The Protection of Human Rights Act 1993 defines 'human rights' to mean rights relating to:

  1. Only rights enumerated in the Constitution of India
  2. Life, liberty, equality and dignity guaranteed by the Constitution or embodied in International Covenants and enforceable by courts in India
  3. Rights under UDHR 1948 only
  4. Rights under ICCPR and ICESCR only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Life, liberty, equality and dignity guaranteed by the Constitution or embodied in International Covenants and enforceable by courts in India

Section 2(1)(d) of the PHR Act 1993: 'Human rights' means the rights relating to life, liberty, equality and dignity of the individual guaranteed by the Constitution or embodied in the International Covenants and enforceable by courts in India.

5The State Human Rights Commission under the PHR Act 1993 can inquire into:

  1. Any human rights violation by anyone
  2. Complaints of violations of human rights only in respect of matters relatable to entries in List II and List III of the Seventh Schedule
  3. Violations by the Central Government
  4. Violations relating to armed forces in the state
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Complaints of violations of human rights only in respect of matters relatable to entries in List II and List III of the Seventh Schedule

Section 29 of PHR Act 1993: The State Human Rights Commission may inquire into complaints of violations in respect of matters relatable to any of the entries in List II (State List) and List III (Concurrent List). It cannot inquire into matters under List I (Union List).

6Under the PHR Act 1993, the NHRC consists of:

  1. Chairperson and 2 members only
  2. Chairperson and not more than 5 members (after 2019 amendment)
  3. Chairperson and 7 members
  4. Chairperson, 3 judicial members and 2 expert members
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Chairperson and not more than 5 members (after 2019 amendment)

The Protection of Human Rights (Amendment) Act 2019 amended the composition: NHRC consists of a Chairperson (retired CJI or SC judge) and not more than 5 members. Ex-officio members include Chairpersons of NCM, NCSC, NCST, NCW, and NCBC.

7The National Commission for Women Act 1990 empowers the NCW to:

  1. Review constitutional and legal safeguards, take up cases of violations, and make recommendations for remedial legislation
  2. Prosecute offenders directly in criminal courts
  3. Grant compensation to women victims
  4. Appoint special public prosecutors in all women-related cases
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Review constitutional and legal safeguards, take up cases of violations, and make recommendations for remedial legislation

Under the NCW Act 1990, the NCW's functions include: reviewing constitutional and legal safeguards for women; recommending remedial legislative measures; facilitating redressal of grievances; and advising the Central Government on policies affecting women.

8The SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act 1989 established which specialized courts?

  1. Human Rights Courts
  2. Special Courts and Exclusive Special Courts
  3. Fast Track Courts
  4. Lok Adalats
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Special Courts and Exclusive Special Courts

The SC/ST (POA) Act 1989 provides for: Special Courts (designated Sessions Courts) for trial of offences under the Act; and after the 2015 Amendment, Exclusive Special Courts (dedicated courts dealing exclusively with atrocity cases) in districts with higher incidence. A Special Public Prosecutor must be appointed.

9The Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955 was enacted to:

  1. Protect civil rights of all citizens
  2. Give effect to Article 17 - abolition of untouchability - and prescribe punishment for practising untouchability
  3. Protect religious minority rights
  4. Implement ICCPR obligations
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Give effect to Article 17 - abolition of untouchability - and prescribe punishment for practising untouchability

The Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955 (originally the Untouchability (Offences) Act 1955) gives effect to Article 17 of the Constitution which abolishes untouchability. It prescribes punishment for enforcing disabilities arising from untouchability.

10The National Commission for Backward Classes Act 1993 provided for examination of complaints relating to:

  1. Inclusion in and exclusion from the lists of backward classes and conditions of backwardness
  2. Economic backwardness of all communities
  3. Reservation in private sector employment
  4. Educational reservations only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Inclusion in and exclusion from the lists of backward classes and conditions of backwardness

Under the NCBC Act 1993, the Commission examined requests for inclusion of communities in central lists of backward classes and complaints of over-inclusion or under-inclusion. After the 102nd Constitutional Amendment 2018, the power to identify SEBCs for central purposes vests with the President on Commission's advice.

11The NHRC cannot inquire into a matter if it is already pending before:

  1. Any lower court
  2. Any NGO
  3. A State Human Rights Commission or any other Commission constituted under any law
  4. Ministry of Home Affairs
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A State Human Rights Commission or any other Commission constituted under any law

Section 36(1) of PHR Act 1993: The Commission shall not inquire into any matter which is pending before a State Commission or any other Commission duly constituted under any law for the time being in force. This prevents overlapping jurisdiction between NHRC and SHRCs or specialized commissions.

12The Protection of Human Rights (Amendment) Act 2019 made which significant change regarding ex-officio members of NHRC?

  1. Removed all ex-officio members
  2. Replaced ex-officio members with government nominees
  3. Added Chairperson of National Commission for Backward Classes as an ex-officio member
  4. Made ex-officio membership mandatory for CBI Director
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Added Chairperson of National Commission for Backward Classes as an ex-officio member

The 2019 Amendment to PHR Act 1993 added the Chairperson of the National Commission for Backward Classes as an ex-officio member of the NHRC. Other ex-officio members remain: Chairpersons of NCM, NCSC, NCST, and NCW.

13Section 12 of the PHR Act 1993 specifies the functions of the NHRC. Which of the following is NOT a function of the NHRC?

  1. Inquiring into complaints of human rights violations
  2. Reviewing constitutional and legal safeguards for human rights
  3. Directly prosecuting persons who commit human rights violations
  4. Spreading human rights literacy among the masses
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Directly prosecuting persons who commit human rights violations

The NHRC does not have the power to directly prosecute - it can recommend prosecution (Section 18) to the concerned government. Its functions include: inquiry into complaints; reviewing safeguards; promoting research and education in human rights; visiting institutions where persons are detained.

14The 'Special Rapporteur' system under the UN Human Rights Council is:

  1. A treaty-based mechanism requiring ratification
  2. A Charter-based mechanism - independent experts appointed to examine, monitor, report and advise on human rights situations
  3. An enforcement mechanism with binding powers
  4. Part of the Universal Periodic Review process
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A Charter-based mechanism - independent experts appointed to examine, monitor, report and advise on human rights situations

Special Rapporteurs are Charter-based (not treaty-based) - they do not require state ratification to function. They are independent experts appointed by the Human Rights Council to examine specific country situations or thematic issues (torture, violence against women, right to food, right to a clean environment).

15The 'Paris Principles' (1991) relate to:

  1. Environmental standards agreed at Paris
  2. Principles of international climate law
  3. Principles governing diplomatic immunity
  4. Principles relating to the status and functioning of national institutions for the promotion and protection of human rights
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Principles relating to the status and functioning of national institutions for the promotion and protection of human rights

The Paris Principles were adopted at the First International Workshop of National Institutions for the Promotion and Protection of Human Rights (Paris, 1991) and endorsed by UNGA Resolution 48/134 (1993). They set minimum standards for national human rights institutions (NHRIs) including independence, broad mandate, composition.

16The appointment of the NHRC Chairperson and Members is made by the:

  1. Chief Justice of India
  2. Parliament by resolution
  3. President of India on the recommendation of a Committee headed by the Prime Minister
  4. President on advice of UPSC
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. President of India on the recommendation of a Committee headed by the Prime Minister

Section 4 of the PHR Act 1993: The Chairperson and Members are appointed by the President on the recommendation of a Committee consisting of: the Prime Minister (Chairperson of Committee); Speaker of Lok Sabha; Home Minister; Leader of Opposition in Lok Sabha; Leader of Opposition in Rajya Sabha; and Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha.
