19 practice questions with answers

1Who is known as the 'Father of International Environmental Law'?

  1. Mostafa Tolba
  2. Gunnar Myrdal
  3. Philippe Sands
  4. Maurice Strong
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Maurice Strong

Maurice Strong (Canadian diplomat) is widely regarded as the Father of International Environmental Law. He was the Secretary-General of both the Stockholm Conference 1972 and the Rio Earth Summit 1992. He also served as the first Executive Director of UNEP.

2Who chaired the Brundtland Commission that produced the report 'Our Common Future' (1987)?

  1. Indira Gandhi
  2. Maurice Strong
  3. Mostafa Tolba
  4. Gro Harlem Brundtland
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Gro Harlem Brundtland

Gro Harlem Brundtland - former Prime Minister of Norway and later Director-General of WHO - chaired the World Commission on Environment and Development (1983-1987). The Commission's report 'Our Common Future' gave the most widely cited definition of sustainable development.

3The lawyer who filed the maximum number of landmark environmental PILs in India's Supreme Court is:

  1. Ram Jethmalani
  2. Harish Salve
  3. M.C. Mehta
  4. Prashant Bhushan
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. M.C. Mehta

M.C. Mehta (Mahesh Chandra Mehta) is an Indian environmental lawyer who filed the largest number of landmark environmental PILs in the Supreme Court - the Ganga pollution case, Oleum gas case, Taj Trapezium case, vehicular pollution case etc. He received the Goldman Environmental Prize in 1996 and Ramon Magsaysay Award in 1997.

4The Chipko Movement (1973) in India was associated with which personalities?

  1. Sunderlal Bahuguna and Chandi Prasad Bhatt
  2. Medha Patkar
  3. Vandana Shiva
  4. Baba Amte
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Sunderlal Bahuguna and Chandi Prasad Bhatt

The Chipko Movement (1973, Uttarakhand) - women hugging trees to prevent felling - was associated with Chandi Prasad Bhatt (who organized the movement in Gopeshwar) and Sunderlal Bahuguna (who gave it national prominence through his padyatras). 'Chipko' means to embrace/hug.

5The Narmada Bachao Andolan against the Sardar Sarovar Dam is associated with:

  1. Vandana Shiva
  2. Medha Patkar
  3. Sunderlal Bahuguna
  4. Arundhati Roy
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Medha Patkar

Medha Patkar is the founder and leader of the Narmada Bachao Andolan (NBA) - the movement against the Sardar Sarovar Dam on River Narmada, raising issues of displacement of tribal and rural communities, lack of rehabilitation, and submergence of forest and agricultural land.

6'Silent Spring' (1962) - the book credited with launching the modern environmental movement globally - was written by:

  1. Aldo Leopold
  2. Barry Commoner
  3. Paul Ehrlich
  4. Rachel Carson
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Rachel Carson

Rachel Carson's 'Silent Spring' (1962) documented the harmful effects of pesticides (especially DDT) on the environment, particularly birds. It led to the ban on DDT in the USA in 1972 and is credited with launching the modern environmental movement, inspiring the first Earth Day (1970).

7The concept of 'intergenerational equity' in environmental law was developed by:

  1. Gro Harlem Brundtland
  2. Philippe Sands
  3. Patricia Birnie
  4. Edith Brown Weiss
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Edith Brown Weiss

Edith Brown Weiss (Georgetown University) developed the concept of intergenerational equity in her book 'In Fairness to Future Generations: International Law, Common Patrimony and Intergenerational Equity' (1989). She proposed three principles: conservation of options, conservation of quality, and conservation of access.

8Eleanor Roosevelt described the UDHR as:

  1. 'The foundation of international law'
  2. 'The constitution of the world'
  3. 'An international Magna Carta for all mankind'
  4. 'A beacon of hope for all nations'
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 'An international Magna Carta for all mankind'

Eleanor Roosevelt described the Universal Declaration of Human Rights as 'an international Magna Carta for all mankind' - drawing a parallel with the Magna Carta 1215 as a foundational document limiting arbitrary power.

9The Indian delegate to the UN Commission on Human Rights who ensured that UDHR Article 1 reads 'all human beings' instead of 'all men' was:

  1. Vijayalakshmi Pandit
  2. Sarojini Naidu
  3. Hansa Mehta
  4. Lakshmi Sahgal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Hansa Mehta

Hansa Mehta - Indian delegate - successfully argued that Article 1 of the UDHR should read 'All human beings are born free and equal in dignity and rights' instead of the original 'All men are born free...' This change gave the UDHR its explicitly gender-neutral and inclusive character.

10Rene Cassin - who won the Nobel Peace Prize 1968 - is associated with:

  1. Drafting the European Convention on Human Rights
  2. Establishing the International Court of Justice
  3. Creating the UN refugee system
  4. Being the principal drafter of the UDHR
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Being the principal drafter of the UDHR

Rene Cassin (French jurist and judge) was the principal drafter of the UDHR and played a central role in the UN Commission on Human Rights under Eleanor Roosevelt's chairmanship. He later served as President of the European Court of Human Rights. He received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1968.

11The term 'genocide' was coined by:

  1. Hersch Lauterpacht
  2. Hans Kelsen
  3. Hugo Grotius
  4. Raphael Lemkin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Raphael Lemkin

Raphael Lemkin (Polish-Jewish lawyer) coined the term 'genocide' in his book 'Axis Rule in Occupied Europe' (1944) - combining the Greek word 'genos' (race/people) and the Latin suffix '-cide' (killing). He campaigned for the Genocide Convention 1948, adopted one day before the UDHR.

12The 'Three Pillars of Sustainable Development' as articulated at the World Summit on Sustainable Development (Johannesburg, 2002) are:

  1. Economic development, Social development, Environmental protection
  2. Economy, Society, Law
  3. People, Planet, Prosperity
  4. Reduce, Reuse, Recycle
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Economic development, Social development, Environmental protection

The Johannesburg Declaration (2002) reaffirmed the three pillars of sustainable development: Economic development, Social development, and Environmental protection. These three pillars are interdependent and mutually reinforcing.

13'Small is Beautiful' (1973) - an influential book on economics and environment - was written by:

  1. E.F. Schumacher
  2. Herman Daly
  3. Nicholas Stern
  4. Amartya Sen
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. E.F. Schumacher

E.F. Schumacher's 'Small is Beautiful: Economics As If People Mattered' (1973) argued against the obsession with GDP growth and large-scale industrialization, advocating for human-scale, sustainable economic systems. It influenced the concept of appropriate technology and sustainable development discourse.

14The first Executive Director of UNEP was:

  1. Gro Harlem Brundtland
  2. Mostafa Tolba
  3. Klaus Topfer
  4. Maurice Strong
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Maurice Strong

Maurice Strong served as the first Executive Director of UNEP when it was established in 1972 following the Stockholm Conference. He served until 1975. The UNEP headquarters is in Nairobi, Kenya - the only UN body headquartered in a developing country.

15Vandana Shiva - physicist turned environmental activist - is known for work on:

  1. Coastal pollution control
  2. Industrial waste management
  3. Climate finance for developing countries
  4. Biodiversity, biopiracy, organic farming, and the rights of farmers - seed sovereignty
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Biodiversity, biopiracy, organic farming, and the rights of farmers - seed sovereignty

Vandana Shiva is known for her work on biodiversity, biopiracy, organic farming, and the rights of farmers (seed sovereignty). She founded the Research Foundation for Science, Technology and Ecology. She is particularly associated with the Chipko movement connections and work on seed sovereignty.

16The 'Preamble' to the UDHR begins with the recognition of the inherent dignity of all members of the human family as the foundation of:

  1. International peace only
  2. Economic development and social progress
  3. Freedom, justice and peace in the world
  4. Democratic governance globally
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Freedom, justice and peace in the world

The UDHR Preamble begins: 'Whereas recognition of the inherent dignity and of the equal and inalienable rights of all members of the human family is the foundation of freedom, justice and peace in the world...' This opening statement establishes dignity as the foundational concept.

17The 'small is beautiful' economist E.F. Schumacher advocated for which concept in environmental economics?

  1. GDP maximization through industrialization
  2. Free trade as the path to environmental sustainability
  3. Green GDP as the primary economic measure
  4. Appropriate technology - human-scale, sustainable, locally controlled technology
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Appropriate technology - human-scale, sustainable, locally controlled technology

E.F. Schumacher advocated for 'appropriate technology' - technology that is small-scale, energy-efficient, environmentally sound, and controlled by local communities rather than large corporations or states. This contrasts with the massive capital-intensive technology of industrial economies and influenced the sustainable development discourse.

18Who is associated with the development of the 'deep ecology' philosophy in environmental ethics?

  1. Aldo Leopold
  2. Rachel Carson
  3. Barry Commoner
  4. Arne Naess
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Arne Naess

Arne Naess (Norwegian philosopher, 1912-2009) coined the term 'deep ecology' in 1973 - distinguishing it from 'shallow ecology' (anthropocentric environmental protection for human benefit). Deep ecology holds that all living beings have inherent value regardless of their utility to humans. This influences ecocentric approaches to environmental law.

19The 'Goldman Environmental Prize' - often called the Green Nobel Prize - was won by which Indian lawyer for his environmental PIL work?

  1. Harish Salve
  2. Prashant Bhushan
  3. Colin Gonsalves
  4. M.C. Mehta (1996)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. M.C. Mehta (1996)

M.C. Mehta won the Goldman Environmental Prize in 1996 for his pioneering work in filing environmental PILs in the Supreme Court of India. He also won the Ramon Magsaysay Award in 1997. The Goldman Environmental Prize is awarded annually to grassroots environmental heroes from each of the world's six geographic regions.
