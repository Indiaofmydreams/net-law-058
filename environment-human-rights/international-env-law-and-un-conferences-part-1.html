14 practice questions with answers

1The first UN Conference on Human Environment was held at:

  1. Rio de Janeiro, 1992
  2. Stockholm, 1972
  3. Nairobi, 1982
  4. Johannesburg, 2002
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Stockholm, 1972

The Stockholm Conference (UN Conference on the Human Environment) 1972 was the first major international environmental conference. It produced the Stockholm Declaration with 26 principles and led to the creation of UNEP headquartered in Nairobi, Kenya.

2Agenda 21 is a product of which international conference?

  1. Stockholm Conference, 1972
  2. Kyoto Conference, 1997
  3. Copenhagen Summit, 2009
  4. Rio Earth Summit (UNCED), 1992
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Rio Earth Summit (UNCED), 1992

Agenda 21 is a comprehensive, non-binding action plan for sustainable development adopted at the Rio Earth Summit (UNCED) 1992. It covers social, economic, and environmental dimensions of sustainable development.

Report this question

3The Brundtland Commission Report 'Our Common Future' was published in:

  1. 1972
  2. 1983
  3. 1987
  4. 1992
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1987

The World Commission on Environment and Development (Brundtland Commission) published 'Our Common Future' in 1987. It coined the most widely cited definition of sustainable development.

Report this question

4Principle 21 of the Stockholm Declaration recognizes:

  1. Right to development
  2. States' sovereign right to exploit resources and responsibility not to cause transboundary harm
  3. Common but differentiated responsibilities
  4. Precautionary approach
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. States' sovereign right to exploit resources and responsibility not to cause transboundary harm

Stockholm Principle 21 (replicated as Rio Principle 2): States have sovereign rights to exploit their own resources AND the responsibility to ensure activities within their jurisdiction do not cause damage to the environment of other States. This dual character is frequently tested.

5The concept of 'Common But Differentiated Responsibilities' (CBDR) was articulated in:

  1. Stockholm Declaration, 1972
  2. Rio Declaration, 1992 - Principle 7
  3. Kyoto Protocol, 1997
  4. Paris Agreement, 2015
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Rio Declaration, 1992 - Principle 7

CBDR is enshrined in Rio Principle 7 (1992): Developed countries acknowledge the responsibility they bear given the pressures their societies place on the global environment. It is the cornerstone of international climate law.

Report this question

6The Kyoto Protocol (1997) relates to:

  1. Biodiversity conservation
  2. Reduction of greenhouse gas emissions
  3. Control of hazardous waste
  4. Protection of the ozone layer
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Reduction of greenhouse gas emissions

The Kyoto Protocol (1997, in force 2005) set binding emission reduction targets for developed countries (Annex I parties). It introduced the Clean Development Mechanism (CDM), Emissions Trading, and Joint Implementation.

Report this question

7The Montreal Protocol (1987) deals with:

  1. Climate change mitigation
  2. Protection of the ozone layer
  3. Marine pollution
  4. Hazardous waste management
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Protection of the ozone layer

Montreal Protocol (1987) on Substances that Deplete the Ozone Layer requires phasing out production and use of ozone-depleting substances. It is considered the most successful multilateral environmental agreement.

Report this question

8The Basel Convention (1989) regulates:

  1. International trade in endangered species
  2. Depletion of ozone layer
  3. Marine pollution from ships
  4. Transboundary movement of hazardous wastes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Transboundary movement of hazardous wastes

Basel Convention on the Control of Transboundary Movements of Hazardous Wastes and Their Disposal (1989) regulates movement of hazardous waste between nations. The 1995 Basel Ban Amendment prohibits export from developed to developing countries.

Report this question

9The 'Rio+20' conference (2012) produced which outcome document?

  1. Johannesburg Plan of Implementation
  2. Paris Agreement
  3. Nairobi Outcome
  4. The Future We Want
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The Future We Want

'The Future We Want' was the outcome document of Rio+20 (UN Conference on Sustainable Development, Rio 2012). It led to the establishment of the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) framework.

10CITES (1973) regulates:

  1. International trade in endangered species of wild fauna and flora
  2. Climate finance for developing countries
  3. Protection of marine environment
  4. Transboundary air pollution
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. International trade in endangered species of wild fauna and flora

CITES (Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora) 1973 regulates international trade in over 38,000 species through a permit system with three Appendices.

Report this question

11The UNFCCC (United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change) was adopted in:

  1. 1992 at Rio de Janeiro
  2. 1990 at Geneva
  3. 1997 at Kyoto
  4. 2015 at Paris
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 1992 at Rio de Janeiro

UNFCCC was adopted at the Rio Earth Summit 1992 and entered into force in 1994. It is the parent treaty of both the Kyoto Protocol (1997) and the Paris Agreement (2015). India ratified it in 1993.

Report this question

12'Common Heritage of Mankind' in the law of the sea refers to:

  1. Exclusive Economic Zones of all nations combined
  2. The high seas fishing rights
  3. The deep seabed and its mineral resources beyond national jurisdiction
  4. Territorial sea resources
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The deep seabed and its mineral resources beyond national jurisdiction

Common Heritage of Mankind (CHM) in UNCLOS 1982 (Part XI) declares that resources of the international seabed Area beyond national jurisdiction are CHM - cannot be appropriated, must be managed by the International Seabed Authority, with benefits shared with all states.

Report this question

13The Minamata Convention (2013) deals with:

  1. Carbon emissions
  2. Mercury pollution
  3. Nuclear waste
  4. Plastic pollution
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Mercury pollution

The Minamata Convention on Mercury (2013, in force 2017) is named after Minamata disease in Japan - mass mercury poisoning from industrial wastewater (1950s-60s). It aims to protect human health and environment from anthropogenic emissions of mercury. India ratified it in 2018.

Report this question

14The 'polluter pays principle' in international environmental law was first formally adopted by:

  1. OECD in 1972 as a policy guideline
  2. Stockholm Declaration 1972
  3. Rio Declaration 1992
  4. Kyoto Protocol 1997
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. OECD in 1972 as a policy guideline

The Polluter Pays Principle was first formally adopted by the OECD in 1972 as an economic policy guideline. It was subsequently incorporated into international environmental law through Rio Principle 16 (1992) and is now considered customary international environmental law.
