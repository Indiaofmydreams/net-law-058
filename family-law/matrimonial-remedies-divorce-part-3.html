23 practice questions with answers

1In Samar Ghosh v. Jaya Ghosh (2007), the Supreme Court held which conduct amounts to mental cruelty?

  1. Wife visiting her parents frequently
  2. Husband's failure to buy gifts
  3. Unilateral decision by the wife not to have a child without informing the husband, coupled with abortion
  4. Husband working long hours
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Unilateral decision by the wife not to have a child without informing the husband, coupled with abortion

Samar Ghosh v. Jaya Ghosh (2007) — a comprehensive judgment giving 12+ illustrative examples of mental cruelty, including unilateral decision not to have children and secret abortions.

2Under Section 23 of the HMA, 1955, a court shall not pass a decree for divorce if:

  1. The petitioner has connived at or condoned the act complained of
  2. The petitioner took advantage of own wrong, connived, condoned matrimonial offence or there is collusion or unnecessary delay
  3. The petitioner has condoned the act and there has been unnecessary delay in presenting the petition
  4. The parties have no children
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The petitioner took advantage of own wrong, connived, condoned matrimonial offence or there is collusion or unnecessary delay

Section 23 bars relief if: (a) petitioner took advantage of own wrong, (b) accessory to or connived at respondent's offence, (c) condoned, (d) collusion, (e) unnecessary delay in institution of proceedings.

3In K. Srinivas Rao v. D.A. Deepa (2013), the Supreme Court held that filing a large number of criminal cases against the husband and his family amounts to:

  1. Legitimate exercise of legal rights
  2. No matrimonial relief
  3. Mental cruelty justifying divorce
  4. Grounds for judicial separation only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Mental cruelty justifying divorce

K. Srinivas Rao v. D.A. Deepa (2013) — filing multiple false or exaggerated criminal cases against the spouse and family amounts to mental cruelty and is a ground for divorce.

4In Smt. Chand Dhawan v. Jawaharlal Dhawan (1993), the Supreme Court held that for desertion to be proved:

  1. Only physical separation is sufficient
  2. Both factum of separation AND animus deserendi must be proved; absence of just cause also required
  3. Only animus deserendi is required without physical separation
  4. A police report of absence must be filed
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Both factum of separation AND animus deserendi must be proved; absence of just cause also required

Chand Dhawan (1993) — desertion requires: (1) actual separation, (2) intention to desert permanently (animus deserendi), (3) without reasonable cause, (4) without consent of the other party.

5In V. Bhagat v. D. Bhagat (1994), the Supreme Court held that levelling false and grave allegations of adultery against the spouse amounts to:

  1. Refusal to cook food
  2. Grounds for judicial separation only
  3. No matrimonial consequence
  4. Mental cruelty sufficient to grant divorce
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Mental cruelty sufficient to grant divorce

V. Bhagat v. D. Bhagat (1994) — levelling false and grave allegations of adultery or unchastity against the spouse constitutes mental cruelty sufficient to grant divorce.

6Under the Shayara Bano judgment (2017), which judges in the majority held Triple Talaq violates Article 14?

  1. Chief Justice Khehar
  2. Justice Nariman and Justice Lalit
  3. Justice Kurian Joseph
  4. All five judges
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Justice Nariman and Justice Lalit

3:2 majority: Justices R.F. Nariman and U.U. Lalit held Triple Talaq manifestly arbitrary under Article 14; Justice Kurian Joseph held it violates the Quran itself; CJI Khehar and Justice Nazeer dissented.

7The Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Marriage) Act, 2019 provides that pronouncement of instant triple talaq is:

  1. A cognisable and non-bailable offence punishable with imprisonment up to 3 years and fine
  2. A civil wrong attracting damages
  3. A compoundable offence only
  4. Only grounds for divorce — not an offence
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A cognisable and non-bailable offence punishable with imprisonment up to 3 years and fine

Section 4 of the Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Marriage) Act, 2019 — instant triple talaq is a cognisable, non-bailable offence; imprisonment up to 3 years plus fine.

8Under the Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Marriage) Act, 2019, a complaint can be filed by:

  1. Any person
  2. The husband only
  3. Only the Magistrate suo motu
  4. The wife alone, or any person related to her by blood or marriage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The wife alone, or any person related to her by blood or marriage

Section 7(a) of the 2019 Act — the offence is cognisable only on information given to the police by the married Muslim woman or any person related to her by blood or marriage.

9The offence under the Triple Talaq Act 2019 may be compounded by:

  1. The husband's family
  2. The Qazi
  3. The Magistrate, on the application of the wife, on terms and conditions determined by the Magistrate
  4. Any court
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The Magistrate, on the application of the wife, on terms and conditions determined by the Magistrate

Section 7(b) of the 2019 Act — the offence is compoundable at the instance of the wife, with the permission of the Magistrate, on such terms and conditions as the Magistrate determines.

10In Danial Latifi v. Union of India (2001), the constitutional bench upheld the Muslim Women Act 1986 by reading it to mean:

  1. Maintenance beyond iddat is unconstitutional
  2. The husband must make fair and reasonable provision for the wife's entire future life during the iddat period itself
  3. Only the Waqf Board is responsible for maintenance
  4. Maintenance is only during iddat period
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The husband must make fair and reasonable provision for the wife's entire future life during the iddat period itself

Danial Latifi (2001) — the SC gave an expansive interpretation: the husband must make reasonable and fair provision and maintenance for the entire future life of the divorced wife, to be discharged within the iddat period.

11In Shamim Ara v. State of UP (2002), the Supreme Court held that a valid talaq requires:

  1. Only witnesses
  2. Triple pronouncement in one sitting
  3. Court approval
  4. Reasonable cause, attempts at reconciliation, and proper communication — talaq mentioned only in a written statement is not valid
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Reasonable cause, attempts at reconciliation, and proper communication — talaq mentioned only in a written statement is not valid

Shamim Ara (2002) — SC held: valid talaq requires (1) reasonable cause, (2) attempts at reconciliation, (3) proper communication. Talaq mentioned in a written pleading without prior communication is invalid.

12Under Muslim Law, 'Talaq-ul-Ahsan' is called the most proper form because:

  1. It is pronounced three times in one sitting
  2. It consists of a single pronouncement during tuhr followed by abstinence during iddat — revocable throughout iddat
  3. It requires court approval
  4. It requires both parties' consent
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. It consists of a single pronouncement during tuhr followed by abstinence during iddat — revocable throughout iddat

Talaq-ul-Ahsan: single pronouncement in tuhr (period of purity) + abstinence during entire iddat; most proper as it is revocable throughout and becomes final only on expiry of iddat.

13The key difference between Talaq-ul-Ahsan and Talaq-ul-Hasan is:

  1. Talaq-ul-Hasan is only for Shia Muslims
  2. There is no practical difference
  3. Talaq-ul-Ahsan = one pronouncement during tuhr + full iddat abstinence; Talaq-ul-Hasan = three pronouncements in three successive tuhrs — irrevocable after third
  4. Talaq-ul-Hasan requires a court; Talaq-ul-Ahsan does not
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Talaq-ul-Ahsan = one pronouncement during tuhr + full iddat abstinence; Talaq-ul-Hasan = three pronouncements in three successive tuhrs — irrevocable after third

Talaq-ul-Ahsan = 1 pronouncement + iddat abstinence; Talaq-ul-Hasan = 3 pronouncements in 3 successive tuhrs (monthly periods); irrevocable after the third pronouncement. Both are valid approved forms.

14Under Sunni Muslim Law, a talaq pronounced during the wife's menstrual period (haid) is:

  1. Irregular (biddat form) — takes effect under Hanafi law but is considered sinful
  2. Valid and the most preferred form
  3. Absolutely void
  4. Void only under Shia law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Irregular (biddat form) — takes effect under Hanafi law but is considered sinful

Talaq during menstruation is an irregular (biddat) talaq — it takes effect under Sunni Hanafi law but is considered sinful; under Shia law it is void.

15The concept of 'proper dower' (Mahr-ul-Misl) under Muslim Law applies when:

  1. No dower is specified or the amount specified is inadequate — determined by reference to the dower of similar women in the wife's family
  2. The wife demands more dower after marriage
  3. The parties specify an amount in the nikah deed
  4. The dower is paid by the wife's father
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. No dower is specified or the amount specified is inadequate — determined by reference to the dower of similar women in the wife's family

Mahr-ul-Misl = proper dower determined by looking at the dower of similar women (status, beauty, age, education) on the father's side; applies where no dower was specified.

16Under Muslim Law, a minor's marriage contracted by the father or paternal grandfather — can the minor repudiate it on attaining puberty?

  1. Yes — Khyar-ul-Bulugh always applies
  2. No — contracts of father/paternal grandfather are binding; Khyar-ul-Bulugh does not apply
  3. Yes — but only for girls
  4. Only if there was no consummation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. No — contracts of father/paternal grandfather are binding; Khyar-ul-Bulugh does not apply

Khyar-ul-Bulugh (option of puberty) does NOT apply if the marriage was contracted by the father or paternal grandfather, whose authority is of the highest degree.

17Under the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939, Section 4 provides that a Muslim wife's apostasy (renunciation of Islam):

  1. Does NOT by itself dissolve the marriage
  2. Automatically dissolves the marriage
  3. Entitles the husband to talaq immediately
  4. Makes the marriage irregular
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Does NOT by itself dissolve the marriage

Section 4 of the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939 — the renunciation of Islam by a Muslim wife or her conversion to another faith shall NOT by itself operate to dissolve her marriage.

18Under Muslim Law, a husband in 'Marz-ul-Maut' (death illness) who divorces his wife — what is the effect on her inheritance rights?

  1. She loses all inheritance rights
  2. She retains inheritance rights from the husband if he dies of that illness — preventing deathbed disinheritance
  3. She retains only dower rights
  4. The divorce is void
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. She retains inheritance rights from the husband if he dies of that illness — preventing deathbed disinheritance

Divorce during marz-ul-maut is valid but the wife retains inheritance rights if the husband dies of that illness, as long as she has not remarried. This prevents a husband from depriving the wife of inheritance through a deathbed divorce.

19Under Muslim Law, a husband can revoke a talaq during the iddat period:

  1. Only before a Qazi
  2. By express words or by conduct implying resumption of conjugal relationship — mere intention alone is insufficient
  3. Only in writing
  4. Only with the wife's consent
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. By express words or by conduct implying resumption of conjugal relationship — mere intention alone is insufficient

Revocation of talaq can be express (by words) or implied (by conduct such as resuming conjugal relations during iddat) — no particular form required, but mere intention without expression or conduct is insufficient.

20Which of the following is a ground on which a Muslim wife may seek dissolution of marriage under Section 2 of the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939?

  1. Husband's failure to provide maintenance for two years
  2. Husband's refusal to accept her relatives as guests
  3. Husband's refusal to change residence
  4. Husband's lower income than her family
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Husband's failure to provide maintenance for two years

Section 2(ii) DMMA 1939 — failure of the husband to provide maintenance for two years is a ground. Others include whereabouts unknown for 4 years, imprisonment for 7 years, cruelty and unequal treatment of co-wives (Section 2(viii)(f)).

21Under Muslim Law, 'Khyar-ul-Bulugh' (option of puberty) applies when:

  1. The woman refuses consummation
  2. The wife wants to choose the dower amount
  3. A minor married before puberty by a guardian other than father/grandfather may repudiate the marriage on attaining puberty
  4. The husband wants to repudiate an arranged marriage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A minor married before puberty by a guardian other than father/grandfather may repudiate the marriage on attaining puberty

Khyar-ul-Bulugh = option of puberty; applies where a minor was married by a guardian other than father/grandfather — on attaining puberty the minor can repudiate the marriage before consummation.

22Section 10A of the Indian Divorce Act, 1869 (added by the 2001 Amendment) provides divorce by mutual consent for Christians requiring parties to have lived separately for:

  1. 2 years
  2. 1 year
  3. 6 months
  4. 3 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 2 years

Section 10A of the Indian Divorce Act, 1869 — mutual consent divorce requires 2 years of separation, unlike the 1 year requirement under the HMA.

23In Joseph Shine v. Union of India (2018), the Supreme Court:

  1. Recognised live-in relationships
  2. Declared triple talaq unconstitutional
  3. Decriminalised homosexuality
  4. Struck down Section 497 IPC (adultery) as unconstitutional — held it treated women as property of their husbands
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Struck down Section 497 IPC (adultery) as unconstitutional — held it treated women as property of their husbands

Joseph Shine v. Union of India (2018) — constitutional bench struck down Section 497 IPC (adultery) as unconstitutional, holding it violated Articles 14, 15, and 21 by treating women as property.
