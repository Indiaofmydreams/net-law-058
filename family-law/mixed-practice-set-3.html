17 practice questions with answers

1A couple married under the SMA seek mutual divorce. Which Act applies?

  1. Special Marriage Act
  2. Divorce Act 1869
  3. Muslim Marriages Act
  4. Hindu Marriage Act
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Special Marriage Act

SMA contains its own provisions.

Report this question

2Sarla Mudgal v. Union of India concerns:

  1. Bigamy by conversion and Article 44
  2. Guardianship
  3. Maintenance
  4. Adoption
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Bigamy by conversion and Article 44

Decided in 1995.

Report this question

3Danial Latifi v. Union of India upheld:

  1. Adoption
  2. Adultery
  3. The 1986 Muslim Women Act with reasonable and fair provision
  4. Triple talaq
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The 1986 Muslim Women Act with reasonable and fair provision

Read the Act in line with Article 21.

Report this question

4Joseph Shine v. Union of India struck down:

  1. Section 377
  2. Triple talaq
  3. Sedition
  4. Adultery as a crime
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Adultery as a crime

Decided in 2018.

Report this question

5Foreign judgments are recognised in India under which provision of the Civil Procedure Code?

  1. Section 44A
  2. Section 14
  3. Section 11
  4. Section 13
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Section 13

Section 13 CPC provides that a foreign judgment shall be conclusive as to any matter directly adjudicated upon, subject to certain exceptions.

Report this question

6Under Section 13 CPC, a foreign judgment is NOT conclusive when:

  1. It is pronounced by a court of competent jurisdiction
  2. It has been given on the merits of the case
  3. It is founded on a refusal to recognise Indian law where such law is applicable
  4. Both parties had opportunity to be heard
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. It is founded on a refusal to recognise Indian law where such law is applicable

Section 13(c) CPC provides that a foreign judgment is not conclusive if it is founded on a refusal to recognise the law of India in cases in which such law is applicable.

Report this question

7Section 14 CPC deals with:

  1. Presumption as to foreign judgments
  2. Enforcement of foreign decrees
  3. Foreign arbitral awards
  4. Res judicata
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Presumption as to foreign judgments

Section 14 CPC provides for presumption as to foreign judgments — the court shall presume that a certified copy of a foreign judgment was pronounced by a court of competent jurisdiction.

Report this question

8A NRI Hindu husband obtains a divorce decree in a US court without the wife's appearance. Under Indian law, this decree is:

  1. Not conclusive as it was not obtained on merits and violates natural justice
  2. Fully valid and binding
  3. Valid under comity of nations
  4. Valid if ratified by Indian court
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Not conclusive as it was not obtained on merits and violates natural justice

Under Section 13(d) and 13(e) CPC, a foreign decree is not conclusive if obtained without giving the other party a reasonable opportunity of being heard, or if it was obtained by fraud.

Report this question

9In Y. Narasimha Rao v. Y. Venkata Lakshmi (1991), the Supreme Court held that a foreign divorce decree is valid in India only if:

  1. Both parties are Indian citizens
  2. The forum court applied the law under which the marriage was solemnised OR both parties genuinely submit to that jurisdiction
  3. The decree is registered in India
  4. It is granted by a Commonwealth court
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The forum court applied the law under which the marriage was solemnised OR both parties genuinely submit to that jurisdiction

The Supreme Court held that for a foreign divorce decree to be recognised in India, either the law of the forum must be the same as that under which the marriage was solemnised, or both parties must have genuinely and voluntarily submitted to that jurisdiction.

Report this question

10Section 44A CPC provides for the execution of decrees of courts in:

  1. Reciprocating territories as notified by Central Government
  2. Countries having bilateral treaties with India
  3. Only Commonwealth countries
  4. All foreign countries
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Reciprocating territories as notified by Central Government

Section 44A CPC provides for execution of decrees passed by courts in reciprocating territories as notified by the Central Government in the Official Gazette.

Report this question

11The Hague Convention on Private International Law is relevant to family law in the context of:

  1. International child abduction (Hague Convention, 1980)
  2. Recognition of foreign divorces
  3. International adoptions (Hague Convention, 1993)
  4. All of the above
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. All of the above

The Hague Conventions cover international child abduction (1980), inter-country adoption (1993), and the recognition of divorces (1970), all relevant to family law.

Report this question

12The Family Courts Act was enacted in:

  1. 1990
  2. 1984
  3. 1986
  4. 1980
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1984

The Family Courts Act, 1984 was enacted to establish Family Courts for the promotion of conciliation and speedy settlement of family disputes.

Report this question

13Family Courts under the Family Courts Act, 1984 have jurisdiction over:

  1. Criminal domestic violence cases only
  2. Only matrimonial disputes
  3. Matrimonial disputes, custody, maintenance, property disputes within a family, declaration of legitimacy, guardianship
  4. Only divorce petitions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Matrimonial disputes, custody, maintenance, property disputes within a family, declaration of legitimacy, guardianship

Section 7 of the Family Courts Act, 1984 confers jurisdiction on Family Courts over a wide range of matters including matrimonial causes, custody, maintenance, legitimacy, guardianship, and family property.

Report this question

14Under the Family Courts Act, 1984, engagement of a lawyer by a party:

  1. Is not allowed at all
  2. Is mandatory
  3. Requires permission of the court
  4. Is an absolute right
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Requires permission of the court

Section 13 of the Family Courts Act provides that no party to a suit shall be entitled, as of right, to be represented by a legal practitioner. The court has discretion to allow representation.

Report this question

15The primary objective of establishing Family Courts under the Family Courts Act, 1984 was:

  1. Reduce burden on High Courts
  2. Introduce Islamic divorce procedures
  3. Handle criminal domestic violence cases
  4. Promote reconciliation and speedy settlement of family disputes with a conciliatory, non-adversarial approach
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Promote reconciliation and speedy settlement of family disputes with a conciliatory, non-adversarial approach

The Preamble to the Family Courts Act, 1984 states the objective as promoting conciliation in, and securing speedy settlement of, disputes relating to marriage and family affairs.

Report this question

16Appeals from the orders of Family Courts under the Family Courts Act, 1984 lie to:

  1. Supreme Court directly
  2. Sessions Court
  3. District Court
  4. High Court
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. High Court

Section 19 of the Family Courts Act, 1984 provides that appeals from judgments, decrees, orders or sentences of a Family Court shall lie to the High Court.

Report this question

17Under Section 9 of the Family Courts Act, 1984, the Family Court shall:

  1. Refer all cases to mediation centre
  2. Immediately adjudicate all disputes
  3. Endeavour to assist and persuade parties to arrive at a settlement before adjudicating — mandatory conciliation attempt
  4. Call police immediately in domestic disputes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Endeavour to assist and persuade parties to arrive at a settlement before adjudicating — mandatory conciliation attempt

Section 9 Family Courts Act, 1984 — the Family Court shall endeavour to assist and persuade the parties to arrive at a settlement in respect of the subject matter before proceeding to adjudicate.
