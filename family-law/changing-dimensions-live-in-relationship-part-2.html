16 practice questions with answers

1In Chanmuniya v. Virendra Kumar Singh Kushwaha (2010), the Supreme Court recommended:

  1. A broad interpretation of 'wife' under Section 125 CrPC to include women in long-term live-in relationships
  2. Live-in relationships are illegal
  3. Section 125 CrPC cannot apply to live-in partners
  4. Live-in partners must register their relationship
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A broad interpretation of 'wife' under Section 125 CrPC to include women in long-term live-in relationships

Chanmuniya (2010) — SC recommended a broad interpretation of 'wife' in Section 125 CrPC to include women in live-in relationships to prevent injustice; noted this requires legislative amendment.

2In Indra Sarma v. V.K.V. Sarma (2013), the Supreme Court held that where one party to a live-in relationship is already married:

  1. The relationship is void and unprotected
  2. The woman may get PWDVA protection against domestic violence but NOT the status of wife or matrimonial rights
  3. It is a fully protected domestic relationship under PWDVA
  4. Protected only if there are children
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The woman may get PWDVA protection against domestic violence but NOT the status of wife or matrimonial rights

Indra Sarma (2013) — SC noted that where one party is already married, the live-in relationship may attract PWDVA protection for domestic violence but the woman cannot claim the status of a wife or matrimonial rights.

3The presumption of marriage arising from long cohabitation is:

  1. Irrebuttable — cannot be challenged
  2. A rebuttable presumption — can be rebutted by evidence that parties knew they were not married or were incapable of marriage
  3. Available only after 10 years of cohabitation
  4. Available only if there are children
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A rebuttable presumption — can be rebutted by evidence that parties knew they were not married or were incapable of marriage

The presumption of marriage from prolonged cohabitation (omnia praesumuntur pro matrimonio) is rebuttable — it can be disproved if circumstances show parties did not intend marriage or a legal marriage was impossible.

4The Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 was enacted to give effect to India's obligations under:

  1. CEDAW (Convention on Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women, 1979)
  2. ICCPR
  3. Geneva Convention
  4. UDHR, 1948
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. CEDAW (Convention on Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women, 1979)

The Statement of Objects and Reasons of the PWDVA 2005 refers to India's obligations under CEDAW, which India ratified in 1993.

Report this question

5Under Section 2(s) of the PWDVA, 2005, 'shared household' means:

  1. A household jointly owned by both parties
  2. A household where the aggrieved person lives or has at any point lived in a domestic relationship with the respondent — regardless of ownership
  3. Only a house owned by the husband
  4. A rented accommodation only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A household where the aggrieved person lives or has at any point lived in a domestic relationship with the respondent — regardless of ownership

Section 2(s) PWDVA — shared household = where the aggrieved person lives or has at any point lived with the respondent in a domestic relationship — ownership is irrelevant.

6In Satish Chander Ahuja v. Sneha Ahuja (2020), the Supreme Court held that the right to reside in a shared household under Section 17 PWDVA:

  1. Is only available during pendency of proceedings
  2. Is only available to women who own the property
  3. Extends to a wife's right to reside in her matrimonial home even if it belongs to her mother-in-law or father-in-law
  4. Lapses on separation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Extends to a wife's right to reside in her matrimonial home even if it belongs to her mother-in-law or father-in-law

Satish Chander Ahuja v. Sneha Ahuja (2020) — a three-judge bench overruled S.R. Batra and held 'shared household' is not limited to property owned by the husband; it includes the household where the woman lived in a domestic relationship, even if owned by in-laws.

7The Uttarakhand UCC, 2024 makes registration of live-in relationships mandatory. Failure to register is punishable with:

  1. Fine of Rs. 500 only
  2. Imprisonment up to 3 months or fine up to Rs. 10,000 or both
  3. Civil penalty only
  4. Cancellation of ration card
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Imprisonment up to 3 months or fine up to Rs. 10,000 or both

Uttarakhand UCC 2024 — penalty for non-registration of live-in relationship: imprisonment up to 3 months or fine up to Rs. 10,000 or both.

8In Hiral P. Harsora v. Kusum Narottamdas Harsora (2016), the Supreme Court held regarding PWDVA that:

  1. It applies against any adult person in a domestic relationship — including female relatives (struck down 'adult male' from Section 2(q))
  2. It applies only in cases of physical violence
  3. It does not apply to Hindu families
  4. It applies only against husbands
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It applies against any adult person in a domestic relationship — including female relatives (struck down 'adult male' from Section 2(q))

Hiral P. Harsora (2016) — SC struck down the words 'adult male' from Section 2(q) PWDVA, making the Act applicable against any person in a domestic relationship, including female relatives.

9Which Indian states have a Uniform Civil Code in force (with Uttarakhand's exempting Scheduled Tribes)?

  1. Uttarakhand only
  2. Goa only
  3. Himachal Pradesh
  4. Both Goa and Uttarakhand
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Both Goa and Uttarakhand

Goa has had the Portuguese Civil Code since 1961; Uttarakhand enacted its own UCC in 2024. Both have functioning UCCs though different in origin and scope.

10In Lata Singh v. State of UP (2006), the Supreme Court directed that:

  1. Live-in relationships should be registered
  2. Police must protect inter-caste couples and persons threatening such couples would be liable for contempt of court
  3. Inter-caste marriages require parental approval
  4. Adults above 21 years do not need parental consent
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Police must protect inter-caste couples and persons threatening such couples would be liable for contempt of court

Lata Singh (2006) — SC directed police to protect inter-caste couples and warned that persons threatening such couples would be liable for contempt of court.

11The most accurate statement about children born in live-in relationships under Hindu Law is:

  1. They inherit both parents' self-acquired and joint family property equally
  2. Under Tulsa (2008) they inherit parents' self-acquired property; rights in joint family/ancestral property require establishing parentage within a valid marriage or void/voidable marriage under Section 16 HMA
  3. They have no inheritance rights
  4. They inherit only from mother
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Under Tulsa (2008) they inherit parents' self-acquired property; rights in joint family/ancestral property require establishing parentage within a valid marriage or void/voidable marriage under Section 16 HMA

Tulsa v. Durghatiya (2008) + Revanasiddappa (2011) — children of live-in relationships inherit self-acquired property of parents; Section 16 HMA applies to void/voidable marriages; rights in ancestral property remain limited.

12In Nandakumar v. State of Kerala (2018), the Supreme Court held that:

  1. Cohabiting couples must register their relationship within 30 days
  2. Live-in relationships are equivalent to marriage
  3. Live-in relationships are criminal under obscenity laws
  4. Two consenting adults may live together in a live-in relationship, which is not illegal, even if the man has not reached the marriageable age of 21
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Two consenting adults may live together in a live-in relationship, which is not illegal, even if the man has not reached the marriageable age of 21

Nandakumar v. State of Kerala (2018) — the Supreme Court held that two adults can live together outside marriage and that a live-in relationship is not an offence; it set aside the High Court's order.

13Under the PWDVA 2005, Section 2(f) definition of 'domestic relationship' includes persons who are related through:

  1. Blood relations only
  2. Only Hindu couples
  3. Consanguinity, marriage, relationship in nature of marriage, adoption, or as family members living together
  4. Marriage only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Consanguinity, marriage, relationship in nature of marriage, adoption, or as family members living together

Section 2(f) PWDVA — domestic relationship = those who live or have lived together in a shared household, related by consanguinity, marriage, through a relationship in the nature of marriage, adoption, or as family members.

14In Madan Mohan Singh v. Rajni Kant (2010), the Supreme Court held that prolonged cohabitation raises a presumption of:

  1. Marriage — a rebuttable presumption in favour of the legality of the relationship
  2. Concubinage only
  3. No legal status
  4. Domestic partnership under PWDVA only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Marriage — a rebuttable presumption in favour of the legality of the relationship

Madan Mohan Singh v. Rajni Kant (2010) — prolonged cohabitation raises a presumption of marriage, which is rebuttable.

Report this question

15A woman who has been in a live-in relationship for 10 years with a man who was already married throughout that period and has children with him — on his death, can she inherit his property?

  1. She inherits the entire estate
  2. She cannot claim as 'wife' since the man had a legal wife; children may inherit as his legitimate children
  3. Yes — entitled as 'wife' since the relationship was long
  4. She has no rights and neither do the children
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. She cannot claim as 'wife' since the man had a legal wife; children may inherit as his legitimate children

Indra Sarma (2013) — a woman in a relationship with a man who has a legal wife cannot claim the status of wife. Children of long cohabitation are treated as legitimate (Tulsa v. Durghatiya; Bharatha Matha) and can inherit his property.

16In D. Velusamy (2010), the Supreme Court described a 'walk-in walk-out' relationship as:

  1. Protected under Article 21
  2. Entitled to all matrimonial remedies
  3. Equivalent to marriage under PWDVA
  4. Not a 'relationship in the nature of marriage' under Section 2(f) of the PWDVA, 2005
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Not a 'relationship in the nature of marriage' under Section 2(f) of the PWDVA, 2005

Velusamy (2010) — a casual 'walk-in walk-out' relationship without public acknowledgment and commitment is not a relationship in the nature of marriage under the PWDVA.
