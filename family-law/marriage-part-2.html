21 practice questions with answers

1A Muslim male marrying his fifth wife simultaneously — the status of that fifth marriage under Sunni law is:

  1. Valid with a penalty
  2. Irregular (fasid) — can be regularised by divorcing one of the four wives
  3. Voidable
  4. Void
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Irregular (fasid) — can be regularised by divorcing one of the four wives

Marriage with a fifth wife is irregular (fasid) under Sunni Hanafi law, not void. It can be regularised by divorcing one of the existing four wives.

2The Prohibition of Child Marriage (Amendment) Bill, 2021 proposed to make child marriages:

  1. Punishable only for the solemnising party
  2. Valid if registered
  3. Punishable with higher fines only
  4. Void ab initio (absolutely void) instead of voidable
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Void ab initio (absolutely void) instead of voidable

The 2021 Bill proposed to make all child marriages void ab initio. Under the current 2006 Act, child marriages are voidable at the option of the contracting child.

3Under the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, which of the following marriages is void?

  1. Marriage between first cousins where custom permits
  2. Marriage where the bridegroom is 20 years old
  3. Marriage where a party has a living spouse (bigamy)
  4. Marriage where Saptapadi was performed by proxy
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Marriage where a party has a living spouse (bigamy)

Section 11 HMA renders a marriage void on three grounds: bigamy (living spouse), prohibited degree of relationship, and sapinda relationship.

4Section 29(2) of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 saves:

  1. Marriages solemnised before the Act
  2. All customs relating to marriage
  3. Rights under any other existing law or custom
  4. Religious ceremonies
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Marriages solemnised before the Act

Section 29(2) is a savings clause that preserves the validity of marriages solemnised before the commencement of the HMA, 1955.

5The Special Marriage Act, 1954 applies to:

  1. Hindus only
  2. All citizens of India regardless of religion, and Indian nationals abroad
  3. Only non-Hindus
  4. Only inter-religion couples
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. All citizens of India regardless of religion, and Indian nationals abroad

The Special Marriage Act, 1954 is a secular law applicable to all citizens of India irrespective of religion and to Indian nationals abroad.

Report this question

6Under Section 27 of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, at the time of passing a decree, the court may make orders with respect to:

  1. Custody of children only
  2. Property presented to the parties at or about the time of marriage jointly belonging to both
  3. Ancestral property only
  4. Maintenance permanently
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Property presented to the parties at or about the time of marriage jointly belonging to both

Section 27 HMA allows the court to make such provisions in the decree as it deems just and proper with respect to any property presented at or about the time of marriage which may belong jointly to both husband and wife.

7Assertion (A): The Special Marriage Act, 1954 allows inter-religion marriages without requiring either party to convert. Reason (R): The Special Marriage Act is a secular law providing a civil form of marriage applicable to all citizens irrespective of religion.

  1. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are correct but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is correct but R is incorrect
  4. A is incorrect but R is correct
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A

Both the assertion and reason are correct. The SMA 1954 is a secular law enabling civil marriages without conversion, and this explains why inter-religion marriages are possible under it.

Report this question

8Under the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006, a child marriage can be declared voidable. The child must seek such a declaration:

  1. Before attaining 18 years only
  2. Within 2 years of attaining majority (18 years)
  3. Before attaining 21 years
  4. At any time
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Within 2 years of attaining majority (18 years)

Section 3 of the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006 provides that a child marriage is voidable at the option of the contracting party who was a child, and the petition must be filed within 2 years of attaining majority.

9Under the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006, which statement is correct?

  1. A child marriage is valid and cannot be challenged
  2. Only the guardian can challenge a child marriage
  3. A child marriage is voidable at the option of the contracting child party; a marriage in contravention of a court injunction is void
  4. A child marriage is void ab initio
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A child marriage is voidable at the option of the contracting child party; a marriage in contravention of a court injunction is void

Section 3 makes child marriages voidable at the option of the child party. Section 12 makes a marriage void if solemnised in contravention of an injunction issued under Section 13.

10Under Section 5(ii) of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, which mental condition renders a marriage voidable?

  1. A party is incapable of giving valid consent due to unsoundness of mind
  2. Mere depression or anxiety disorder
  3. A party has a mild intellectual disability with no impact on consent
  4. A party is hard of hearing
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A party is incapable of giving valid consent due to unsoundness of mind

Section 5(ii)(a) — incapacity to give valid consent due to unsoundness of mind makes the marriage voidable under Section 12, not void under Section 11.

11Which of the following correctly distinguishes a void marriage from a voidable marriage under the HMA, 1955?

  1. A void marriage requires a court decree to be ineffective; a voidable marriage is automatically void
  2. A void marriage is absolutely invalid from inception and no decree is needed; a voidable marriage is valid until annulled by a court decree
  3. Both require court decrees to become invalid
  4. Only a voidable marriage requires a court decree; both can be treated as non-existent without a decree
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A void marriage is absolutely invalid from inception and no decree is needed; a voidable marriage is valid until annulled by a court decree

Section 11 — void marriages have no legal effect from the beginning; Section 12 — voidable marriages subsist and are valid until a court grants annulment.

Report this question

12A Hindu male marries a second time while the first wife is alive. The second wife files a petition for annulment. Under the HMA, 1955:

  1. She cannot file as the marriage is void and she has no status
  2. The second marriage is void under Section 11 and either party can seek a declaration
  3. Only the first wife can challenge the marriage
  4. Only the husband can seek annulment
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The second marriage is void under Section 11 and either party can seek a declaration

Section 11 — bigamous marriage is void; even parties to a void marriage can seek a formal declaration. The second wife is not precluded from petitioning.

Report this question

13In ancient Hindu Law, 'Anuloma' and 'Pratiloma' marriages refer to:

  1. Marriage within same gotra and different gotra
  2. Anuloma — higher caste male with lower caste female (approved); Pratiloma — lower caste male with higher caste female (disapproved)
  3. Registered and unregistered marriages
  4. First and second marriages
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Anuloma — higher caste male with lower caste female (approved); Pratiloma — lower caste male with higher caste female (disapproved)

Classical Hindu Law: Anuloma = 'with the grain' = approved hypergamous union; Pratiloma = 'against the grain' = disapproved hypogamous union.

14Under Section 7(2) of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, where the ceremony of Saptapadi is observed, the marriage becomes complete and binding when:

  1. On registration
  2. At the time of Kanyadan
  3. Upon exchange of garlands
  4. When the seventh step is taken
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. When the seventh step is taken

Section 7(2) — the marriage becomes complete and binding when the seventh step is taken before the sacred fire.

15Grounds for a decree of nullity for a voidable marriage under Section 12 of the HMA, 1955 include:

  1. Bigamy, prohibited degrees, sapinda relationship
  2. Adultery, cruelty, desertion
  3. Impotence, unsound mind, consent by force or fraud, pre-marriage pregnancy by another
  4. Conversion, leprosy, venereal disease
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Impotence, unsound mind, consent by force or fraud, pre-marriage pregnancy by another

Section 12 — voidable marriages: impotence, unsoundness of mind, consent obtained by force/fraud, respondent pregnant by another person at time of marriage.

16Under Section 16 of the HMA, 1955, children of void and voidable marriages are:

  1. Legitimate only if the parents later solemnise a valid marriage
  2. Legitimate for all purposes, including rights over the property of the parents' relatives
  3. Legitimate; their rights extend to the property of their parents, not to the property of other relatives
  4. Illegitimate with no rights
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Legitimate; their rights extend to the property of their parents, not to the property of other relatives

Section 16 HMA makes children of void and voidable marriages legitimate, with rights in their parents' property only (Revanasiddappa v. Mallikarjun, 2011, extends this to the parents' share even in joint family property).

17A marriage solemnised between two Hindus within prohibited degrees of relationship is:

  1. Valid if performed with Vedic rites
  2. Valid if registered
  3. Voidable at the option of either party
  4. Void under Section 11 read with Section 5(iv) — unless custom permits
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Void under Section 11 read with Section 5(iv) — unless custom permits

Section 11 read with Section 5(iv) — marriage within prohibited degrees is void unless there is a custom or usage governing each party which permits such marriage.

Report this question

18Under the Special Marriage Act, 1954, if both parties were Hindus before the marriage, succession to their property is governed by:

  1. The Hindu Succession Act continues to apply for succession under Section 21A
  2. No personal law — only the SMA
  3. Muslim Personal Law
  4. The Indian Succession Act only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The Hindu Succession Act continues to apply for succession under Section 21A

Section 21A SMA — if both parties were Hindus before the marriage, the Hindu Succession Act continues to apply to succession; for matrimonial causes, the SMA governs.

19Under the Special Marriage Act, 1954, objections to a marriage can be filed within how many days of publication of the notice?

  1. No provision for objection
  2. 60 days
  3. 7 days
  4. 30 days
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 30 days

Section 7 SMA — any person may object to the intended marriage within 30 days of the notice being published by the Marriage Officer.

20Section 9 of the HMA (Restitution of conjugal rights) was struck down as unconstitutional by which High Court before the Supreme Court upheld it?

  1. Bombay High Court
  2. Madras High Court
  3. Delhi High Court
  4. Andhra Pradesh High Court in T. Sareetha v. T. Venkatasubbaiah (1983)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Andhra Pradesh High Court in T. Sareetha v. T. Venkatasubbaiah (1983)

The Andhra Pradesh HC in T. Sareetha v. T. Venkatasubbaiah (1983) struck down Section 9 HMA as violating Articles 14, 19, and 21; the Supreme Court overruled this in Saroj Rani v. Sudarshan Kumar Chadha (1984).

21Under the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006, a child marriage is:

  1. Void if either party is a girl
  2. Void ab initio
  3. Always valid if customary
  4. Voidable at the option of the contracting child party within 2 years of attaining majority — void only if done in contravention of an injunction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Voidable at the option of the contracting child party within 2 years of attaining majority — void only if done in contravention of an injunction

Section 3 PCMA 2006 — child marriage is voidable at the option of the child party, petition to be filed within 2 years of attaining majority. Section 12 — marriage in breach of an injunction is void.
