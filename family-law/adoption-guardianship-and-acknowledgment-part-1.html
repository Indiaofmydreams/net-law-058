20 practice questions with answers

1Under the Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956 (HAMA), after the 2010 Amendment, who has the capacity to give a child in adoption?

  1. Father only
  2. Mother only
  3. Father and, in his absence, mother
  4. Both father and mother have equal right to give in adoption
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Both father and mother have equal right to give in adoption

After the Personal Laws (Amendment) Act, 2010, Section 9 HAMA was amended to give both father and mother equal right to give a child in adoption.

Report this question

2Under HAMA, 1956, the maximum age of a Hindu child who can be adopted is:

  1. 12 years
  2. 18 years
  3. 15 years
  4. There is no age limit
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 15 years

Section 10 of HAMA, 1956 provides that a child must not have completed 15 years of age at the time of adoption, unless there is a custom or usage permitting the adoption of persons above 15 years.

Report this question

3Under Muslim Personal Law, adoption is:

  1. Fully recognised
  2. Recognised only for daughters
  3. Recognised under the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act
  4. Not recognised; only acknowledgment of paternity is recognised
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Not recognised; only acknowledgment of paternity is recognised

Adoption is not recognised under Muslim Personal Law as it creates a legal fiction of parenthood which conflicts with the Islamic principle that every person must know their true lineage.

Report this question

4In Shabnam Hashmi v. Union of India (2014), the Supreme Court held that:

  1. Non-Hindus can adopt under the Juvenile Justice Act and CARA guidelines
  2. Muslim women can adopt daughters only
  3. Adoption is only for Hindus
  4. Muslims cannot adopt
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Non-Hindus can adopt under the Juvenile Justice Act and CARA guidelines

The Supreme Court held that the right to adopt is available to all citizens under the Juvenile Justice Act, 2000 (now 2015) and CARA guidelines, irrespective of religion.

Report this question

5'Acknowledgment of legitimacy' under Muslim Law is valid if:

  1. The child is known to be illegitimate
  2. The acknowledged child could be the acknowledger's legitimate child and the acknowledger was of lawful age
  3. The child is above 18 years
  4. There are two witnesses
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The acknowledged child could be the acknowledger's legitimate child and the acknowledger was of lawful age

Acknowledgment is valid only if: (1) the acknowledger was of sufficient age, (2) the child could possibly be the legitimate child of the acknowledger, and (3) the child was not known to be illegitimate.

Report this question

6Under the Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act, 1956, the natural guardian of a minor Hindu boy is:

  1. Father
  2. Mother
  3. Court-appointed guardian
  4. Paternal grandfather
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Father

Section 6(a) of HMGA, 1956 provides that the natural guardian of a minor Hindu boy is the father, and after him, the mother.

Report this question

7The welfare of the minor is the paramount consideration under which of the following?

  1. Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act, 1956
  2. Guardians and Wards Act, 1890
  3. Juvenile Justice Act, 2015
  4. All of the above
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. All of the above

The welfare of the minor is the paramount consideration under all three statutes governing guardianship and custody.

Report this question

8Under HAMA, 1956, which of the following can adopt a child?

  1. A Hindu male with wife's consent
  2. A Hindu female who is unmarried, widow or divorcee
  3. A Hindu male who is a widower or divorced
  4. All of the above
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. All of the above

Sections 7 and 8 of HAMA, 1956 permit any Hindu male (with wife's consent) or Hindu female (if unmarried, widow, divorcee, or whose husband is incompetent) to adopt.

Report this question

9Once an adoption under HAMA is complete:

  1. It can be cancelled by the biological parents
  2. The child retains ties with the birth family for inheritance
  3. The child can be sent back within 6 months
  4. It is irrevocable and the child is deemed born in the adoptive family
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. It is irrevocable and the child is deemed born in the adoptive family

Section 12 of HAMA, 1956 provides that an adopted child shall be deemed to be the child of his adoptive father or mother for all purposes. The adoption is irrevocable.

Report this question

10CARA (Central Adoption Resource Authority) operates under:

  1. The Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956
  2. The Guardians and Wards Act, 1890
  3. The Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015
  4. The Special Marriage Act, 1954
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015

CARA was given statutory status under the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015 as the nodal body for adoption in India.

Report this question

11Under Section 7 of HAMA, a Hindu male wishing to adopt must:

  1. Obtain court approval
  2. Register the adoption
  3. Obtain consent of his wife (or wives)
  4. Ensure the child is below 12 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Obtain consent of his wife (or wives)

Section 7 of HAMA, 1956 requires a Hindu male to obtain the consent of his wife or wives before effecting an adoption.

Report this question

12The 'Doctrine of Relation Back' in adoption under Hindu Law means:

  1. The adopted child relates back to the birth family
  2. The adopted child is deemed to have been born to the adoptive family at the time of birth
  3. No rights are conferred until the child attains majority
  4. Adoption is retroactively valid from birth of the child
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The adopted child is deemed to have been born to the adoptive family at the time of birth

Under old Hindu Law, an adopted son was deemed to have been born in the adoptive family from birth. HAMA 1956 Section 12 modified this — vested interests prior to adoption are not divested.

Report this question

13Laxmikant Pandey v. Union of India (1984) is a landmark case related to:

  1. Guardianship of orphans
  2. Domestic adoption procedures
  3. Guidelines for inter-country adoption
  4. Adoption by Muslims
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Guidelines for inter-country adoption

In Laxmikant Pandey v. Union of India (1984), the Supreme Court laid down comprehensive guidelines for inter-country adoption to prevent trafficking and exploitation of children.

Report this question

14Under Muslim Law, the concept similar to adoption is called:

  1. Nicah
  2. Iddat
  3. Zihar
  4. Kafala (foster care/acknowledgment)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Kafala (foster care/acknowledgment)

Kafala is the Islamic concept of foster care and acknowledgment. It does not create the legal parent-child relationship that adoption does.

Report this question

15Assertion (A): Muslim law does not recognise adoption. Reason (R): The basis is that under Islam, every person must know their true lineage and a legal fiction of parenthood would disturb this.

  1. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are correct but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is correct but R is wrong
  4. A is wrong but R is correct
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A

Both the assertion and reason are correct. Islam prohibits adoption because it creates a legal fiction conflicting with the Quranic requirement of knowing true lineage.

Report this question

16CARA (Central Adoption Resource Authority) was given statutory status under:

  1. HAMA 1956
  2. Juvenile Justice Act 2015
  3. Guardian and Wards Act 1890
  4. POSCO Act 2012
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Juvenile Justice Act 2015

CARA was given statutory status under the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015.

Report this question

17Under HAMA, 1956, a Hindu male can adopt a son only if he has no:

  1. Living father
  2. Property
  3. Hindu son, son's son (grandson) or son's son's son (great-grandson) — whether by birth or adoption
  4. Living brother
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Hindu son, son's son (grandson) or son's son's son (great-grandson) — whether by birth or adoption

Section 11(i) of HAMA, 1956 prohibits a Hindu male from adopting a son if he already has a Hindu son, son's son, or son's son's son living (whether by birth or adoption).

Report this question

18The only essential act required for completing adoption under HAMA, 1956 is:

  1. Written agreement
  2. Actual giving and taking of the child — physical transfer with intention to adopt
  3. Registration
  4. Court decree
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Actual giving and taking of the child — physical transfer with intention to adopt

Section 11(vi) of HAMA provides that the child is actually given and taken in adoption is an essential condition — the Datta Homam ceremony is no longer required.

Report this question

19Under HAMA, 1956, when a married Hindu female adopts, the child is considered to be the child of:

  1. The adoptive mother alone
  2. The adoptive mother's parents
  3. The court-appointed guardian
  4. The adoptive mother and her husband (if she is married)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The adoptive mother and her husband (if she is married)

Section 14(2) of HAMA provides that if the adoption is made by a female who is married, the child shall be deemed to be the child of the adoptive mother and her husband.

Report this question

20Under Section 16 of HAMA, 1956, a registered document of adoption creates:

  1. No legal presumption
  2. A presumption that adoption was made in compliance with the Act — burden shifts to challenger
  3. Conclusive proof of adoption
  4. A rebuttable presumption requiring no evidence to challenge
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A presumption that adoption was made in compliance with the Act — burden shifts to challenger

Section 16 creates a presumption that a duly registered deed of adoption was made in compliance with the provisions of HAMA. The burden of proof lies on the person challenging the adoption.
