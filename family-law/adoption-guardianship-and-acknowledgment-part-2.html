20 practice questions with answers

1Under Section 12 of HAMA, 1956, adopted child's ties with birth family are severed EXCEPT:

  1. Property vested before adoption
  2. The child cannot marry anyone from the birth family within prohibited degrees
  3. Right to marry a person from the birth family who would have been prohibited
  4. All ties are completely severed
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The child cannot marry anyone from the birth family within prohibited degrees

Section 12 proviso preserves marriage prohibitions — the adopted child cannot marry anyone from the birth family with whom they would have been in a prohibited degree of relationship.

2Under Guardians and Wards Act, 1890, the court while appointing a guardian is primarily guided by:

  1. Preference of the father
  2. Religion of the child
  3. Wealth of the proposed guardian
  4. Welfare of the minor
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Welfare of the minor

Section 17 of the Guardians and Wards Act, 1890 provides that the court shall be guided by the welfare of the minor as the paramount consideration in appointing a guardian.

Report this question

3Under HMGA, 1956, a natural guardian cannot alienate immovable property of a minor without prior court permission. Any such unauthorised alienation is:

  1. Voidable at the option of the minor on attaining majority
  2. Valid if in writing
  3. Punishable with fine only
  4. Void ab initio
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Voidable at the option of the minor on attaining majority

Section 8(3) of HMGA, 1956 provides that any disposal of immovable property by a natural guardian without prior court permission is voidable at the instance of the minor or any person claiming under them.

4In Githa Hariharan v. Reserve Bank of India (1999), the Supreme Court interpreted the phrase 'after him' in Section 6(a) of HMGA, 1956 to mean:

  1. After the father's death only
  2. After the father in preference — the mother can act as natural guardian when the father is absent, indifferent, or incapacitated
  3. After the father reaches old age
  4. After both parents agree
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. After the father in preference — the mother can act as natural guardian when the father is absent, indifferent, or incapacitated

The Supreme Court held that 'after him' in Section 6(a) means 'in the absence of the father' (not just after death). Absence includes situations of incapacity, indifference, or unavailability.

5Statement I: A stepmother (childless) is entitled to claim maintenance under HAMA, 1956. Statement II: Under HAMA, 1956, the same child cannot be adopted simultaneously by two or more persons.

  1. Both Statement I and Statement II are correct
  2. Statement I is correct but Statement II is incorrect
  3. Statement I is incorrect but Statement II is correct
  4. Both are incorrect
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both Statement I and Statement II are correct

Section 21 HAMA lists stepmother among dependants entitled to maintenance from the husband's estate. Section 11(iv) prohibits simultaneous adoption of the same child by two or more persons.

Report this question

6The old Datta Homam ceremony for adoption under Hindu Law:

  1. Remains compulsory for Hindus
  2. Can be substituted by a court order only
  3. Is required only for male children
  4. Is no longer required — actual physical giving and taking of the child with intent to adopt is sufficient under HAMA 1956
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Is no longer required — actual physical giving and taking of the child with intent to adopt is sufficient under HAMA 1956

Under HAMA, 1956 (Section 11(vi)), the Datta Homam ceremony is not required. The only essential act is the actual giving and taking of the child with the intention to adopt.

Report this question

7Under HAMA, 1956, when an adoption is made, the adopted child shall be deemed to be the child of the adoptive parents:

  1. From the date of court approval
  2. From birth — retroactively
  3. From the date of adoption onwards — not retroactively from birth
  4. From majority
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. From the date of adoption onwards — not retroactively from birth

Section 12 HAMA — the child is deemed to be the child of adoptive parents 'for all purposes with effect from the date of the adoption' — NOT from birth, unlike the old Doctrine of Relation Back.

Report this question

8Under HAMA, 1956, a Hindu male aged 24 wishes to adopt a girl child aged 2. The adoption is:

  1. Permitted — the adopter must be at least 21 years older than the female child; 24-2=22 years, so this IS permitted
  2. Not permitted as the adopter is too young
  3. Freely permitted without any age gap condition
  4. Permitted only with court approval
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Permitted — the adopter must be at least 21 years older than the female child; 24-2=22 years, so this IS permitted

Section 11(iv) — where the adoption is of a female, the adoptive father must be at least 21 years older than the child. Here 24-2=22 years difference, so the adoption is valid.

9Under Section 13 of the HMGA, 1956, the paramount consideration in all guardianship proceedings is:

  1. Welfare of the minor
  2. Wishes of the paternal family
  3. Religion of the child
  4. Financial capacity of the guardian
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Welfare of the minor

Section 13 HMGA — in the appointment or declaration of any person as guardian, the welfare of the minor shall be the paramount consideration.

Report this question

10The Guardians and Wards Act, 1890 is:

  1. A Muslim personal law statute
  2. A Hindu personal law statute
  3. A statute applicable only to orphans
  4. A secular general statute applicable to all persons in India regardless of religion
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A secular general statute applicable to all persons in India regardless of religion

The Guardians and Wards Act, 1890 is a secular, general statute applicable to all persons in India regardless of religion — it fills gaps in personal law regarding guardianship.

Report this question

11Under Section 8 of HMGA 1956, alienation of a minor's immovable property by a natural guardian without court permission results in:

  1. A transaction valid for 7 years unless challenged
  2. A void transaction
  3. A voidable transaction — can be avoided by the minor or anyone claiming through them on attaining majority
  4. A valid transaction if small in value
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A voidable transaction — can be avoided by the minor or anyone claiming through them on attaining majority

Section 8(3) HMGA — any disposal of a minor's immovable property by a natural guardian without prior permission of the court is voidable at the instance of the minor.

Report this question

12Under Section 11(i) of HAMA, 1956, a Hindu male cannot adopt a son if he has a living:

  1. Living father
  2. Hindu son, son's son or son's son's son — whether by birth or adoption
  3. Living brother
  4. Stepson
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Hindu son, son's son or son's son's son — whether by birth or adoption

Section 11(i) HAMA — a person shall not adopt a son if they already have a Hindu son, son's son, or son's son's son living, whether by birth or prior adoption.

Report this question

13Under Section 10(ii) of HAMA, 1956, a child who has already been adopted:

  1. Can be adopted again after 5 years
  2. Cannot be adopted again — a child can be adopted only once
  3. Can be adopted if the first adoption failed
  4. Can be adopted if both adoptive parents consent
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Cannot be adopted again — a child can be adopted only once

Section 10(ii) HAMA — the person to be adopted must not have already been adopted. A child cannot be adopted twice.

Report this question

14Under Section 15 of HAMA, 1956, a valid adoption:

  1. Cannot be cancelled or set aside by either the adoptive or natural parents after it is complete
  2. Can be cancelled by the biological parents within 5 years
  3. Can be cancelled by court if in the best interest of the child
  4. Can be cancelled by the adoptive parents if the child is unsatisfactory
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Cannot be cancelled or set aside by either the adoptive or natural parents after it is complete

Section 15 HAMA — a valid adoption cannot be cancelled by either the adoptive father or mother or by the natural parent, nor can the adopted child renounce the adoption.

Report this question

15In ABC v. State (NCT of Delhi) (2015), the Supreme Court held that an unmarried Christian mother:

  1. Is the sole natural guardian of her illegitimate child and does not need the father's consent or involvement for guardianship
  2. Cannot be the natural guardian of her illegitimate child
  3. Must marry the father within 6 months
  4. Must put the child in state care
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Is the sole natural guardian of her illegitimate child and does not need the father's consent or involvement for guardianship

ABC v. State NCT Delhi (2015) — SC held that an unmarried mother is the sole natural guardian of her illegitimate child; compelling disclosure of the father's identity violates the mother's right to privacy under Article 21.

Report this question

16Under Section 9(2) of HAMA, 1956 (as amended in 2010), where both parents are alive, the right to give a child in adoption belongs to:

  1. The father alone
  2. The mother alone
  3. Either father or mother with the consent of the other
  4. Only jointly by both parents together
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Either father or mother with the consent of the other

Section 9(2) HAMA as amended by the Personal Laws (Amendment) Act, 2010 — father or mother, if alive, have equal right to give a child in adoption, but it cannot be exercised without the consent of the other (unless the other has renounced the world, ceased to be a Hindu or been declared of unsound mind).

Report this question

17Under Section 8 HAMA, 1956, a married Hindu female (whose marriage is subsisting) can adopt in her own right only if her husband has:

  1. Filed a petition in family court
  2. Completely and finally renounced the world, OR ceased to be a Hindu, OR been declared of unsound mind by a court
  3. Given written consent
  4. No biological children
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Completely and finally renounced the world, OR ceased to be a Hindu, OR been declared of unsound mind by a court

Section 8 HAMA — a married Hindu female may adopt in her own right if her marriage has been dissolved, her husband is dead, or he has completely and finally renounced the world, ceased to be a Hindu or been declared of unsound mind by a court.

Report this question

18Under Section 12 of HAMA, 1956, the adopted child does NOT divest property already vested before adoption. This means:

  1. The child gets rights in the adoptive family's property from the date of adoption but pre-adoption vested interests are retained
  2. The child loses all property from birth family
  3. The child retains full rights in both families permanently
  4. The child's pre-adoption property vests in the adoptive family
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The child gets rights in the adoptive family's property from the date of adoption but pre-adoption vested interests are retained

Section 12 HAMA — the adopted child does NOT divest any property already vested before adoption; they take rights in the adoptive family from the date of adoption onward.

Report this question

19The Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015, Section 2(2) defines adoption as:

  1. A temporary custody arrangement
  2. A guardianship arrangement
  3. The process through which the adopted child is permanently separated from biological parents and becomes the lawful child of adoptive parents with all rights
  4. Fostering a child temporarily
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The process through which the adopted child is permanently separated from biological parents and becomes the lawful child of adoptive parents with all rights

Section 2(2) JJ Act, 2015 — adoption = process of permanent separation from biological parents and becoming the lawful child of adoptive parents with all rights and responsibilities.

Report this question

20Under the JJ Act, 2015, CARA (Central Adoption Resource Authority) functions under the:

  1. Supreme Court
  2. Ministry of Women and Child Development
  3. Ministry of Home Affairs
  4. High Court of each state
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Ministry of Women and Child Development

CARA functions under the Ministry of Women and Child Development as the nodal body for adoption in India, including inter-country adoptions.
