18 practice questions with answers

1In Lakshmi Kant Pandey v. Union of India (1984), the Supreme Court's guidelines for inter-country adoption primarily aimed to prevent:

  1. Trafficking, exploitation and abuse of children through improperly regulated inter-country adoptions
  2. Foreign nationals from adopting Indian children
  3. Children from being adopted by non-Hindus
  4. Children from going abroad for education
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Trafficking, exploitation and abuse of children through improperly regulated inter-country adoptions

Lakshmi Kant Pandey (1984) — SC's primary concern was preventing trafficking and exploitation through unregulated inter-country adoptions; laid down elaborate procedural safeguards.

2The Hague Convention on Inter-Country Adoption (1993) establishes the principle of subsidiarity meaning:

  1. Inter-country adoption is preferred over domestic adoption
  2. Domestic adoption must be exhausted before considering inter-country adoption
  3. Children should never be adopted across national boundaries
  4. Only biological parents can decide on adoption
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Domestic adoption must be exhausted before considering inter-country adoption

Hague Convention on Inter-Country Adoption, 1993 — Article 4 establishes the subsidiarity principle: inter-country adoption is considered only after domestic options are exhausted.

Report this question

3Under Hanafi Muslim law, the mother's right of hizanat (custody) over a son continues until he reaches the age of:

  1. 5 years
  2. 15 years
  3. 7 years
  4. 12 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 7 years

Under Hanafi law, the mother is entitled to custody of a son until the age of 7 and of a daughter until puberty (Gohar Begum v. Suggi, 1960 and later cases). The child's welfare remains paramount.

Report this question

4Under Muslim Law, the mother loses her right of hizanat (custody) if she:

  1. Works outside the home
  2. Falls temporarily ill
  3. Lives in another city
  4. Remarries a stranger (a person not related to the child within prohibited degrees) — but not if she marries a close relative of the child
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Remarries a stranger (a person not related to the child within prohibited degrees) — but not if she marries a close relative of the child

Under Muslim Law, mother's hizanat is forfeited on remarriage to a stranger — a person not related within prohibited degrees to the child; remarriage to a mahram (e.g., child's paternal uncle) does not forfeit custody.

Report this question

5The constitutional basis for the welfare of the child as the paramount consideration in custody and guardianship is:

  1. Article 25 — religious freedom
  2. Article 14 only
  3. Article 32 only
  4. Articles 21 and 39(f) — right to life with dignity and State's duty to protect children's development
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Articles 21 and 39(f) — right to life with dignity and State's duty to protect children's development

Article 21 + Article 39(f) (DPSP — tender age of children shall not be abused; children shall be given opportunities to develop in a healthy manner) — constitutional foundation for welfare-based custody decisions.

Report this question

6In Rosy Jacob v. Jacob A. Chakramakkal (1973), the Supreme Court held that in custody matters:

  1. Father always has a superior right
  2. The court is not bound by the legal rights of the parents — the paramount and sole consideration is the welfare of the child
  3. Economic capacity of the parent is the only factor
  4. Religious upbringing is the primary consideration
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The court is not bound by the legal rights of the parents — the paramount and sole consideration is the welfare of the child

Rosy Jacob (1973) — SC held that the court's duty is to promote the welfare of the child; the rights of parents are subordinate to and must yield to the welfare of the child.

7Under Section 17 of the Guardians and Wards Act, 1890, in appointing a guardian, the court shall be guided primarily by:

  1. The wishes of the father only
  2. Age, sex, religion of the minor; character/capacity of proposed guardian; and most importantly, the welfare of the minor
  3. Economic considerations only
  4. The religion of both parents equally
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Age, sex, religion of the minor; character/capacity of proposed guardian; and most importantly, the welfare of the minor

Section 17 GWA — the court considers: age, sex, religion of minor; character/capacity of proposed guardian; preference of minor (if old enough); welfare is the paramount consideration.

Report this question

8In Nil Ratan Kundu v. Abhijit Kundu (2008), the Supreme Court held that in custody disputes the court has:

  1. No power to interview the child
  2. Inherent power and duty to act as parens patriae — can override both parents' wishes in the interest of the child
  3. Power only to enforce court orders
  4. Power only to appoint a guardian ad litem
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Inherent power and duty to act as parens patriae — can override both parents' wishes in the interest of the child

Nil Ratan Kundu (2008) — SC affirmed the court's role as parens patriae in custody matters; the court's paramount duty is the welfare of the child, not enforcing parental rights.

Report this question

9'Parens Patriae' jurisdiction in guardianship matters means:

  1. The father's rights are always preferred
  2. Courts can transfer custody to the government
  3. The state can adopt children directly
  4. The state acts as the parent of last resort for children and incompetent persons who lack adequate protection
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The state acts as the parent of last resort for children and incompetent persons who lack adequate protection

Parens patriae = 'parent of the nation'; this jurisdiction allows courts to protect those — including minors — who cannot protect themselves. It is the foundation of all guardianship and custody decisions.

Report this question

10Under the JJ Act, 2015, a single male can adopt:

  1. Any child regardless of gender
  2. Only a male child — a single male cannot adopt a female child
  3. Only if he is above 45 years
  4. Only with special court permission
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Only a male child — a single male cannot adopt a female child

Section 57(2) JJ Act 2015 and the CARA Adoption Regulations, 2022 — single male can adopt only a male child; single female can adopt a child of any gender; single male cannot adopt a female child.

11The CARA Adoption Regulations, 2022 mandate that prospective adoptive parents must register on:

  1. The State Government's adoption website
  2. The local Magistrate's office
  3. CARINGS (Child Adoption Resource Information and Guidance System) — CARA's online portal
  4. The court's e-filing portal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. CARINGS (Child Adoption Resource Information and Guidance System) — CARA's online portal

CARINGS is the online portal maintained by CARA where prospective adoptive parents must register; it facilitates a transparent, child-matching process for adoption in India.

Report this question

12Under Muslim Law, legal guardianship (wilayat) of the person and property of a minor vests primarily in:

  1. The court always
  2. The father first, then father's father — the paternal line; mother has hizanat (physical custody) but not legal guardianship
  3. The Qazi
  4. The mother always
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The father first, then father's father — the paternal line; mother has hizanat (physical custody) but not legal guardianship

Under Muslim Law, legal guardianship (wilayat) of person and property vests in the father, then father's father (paternal side); the mother has hizanat (physical custody) but not legal guardianship.

Report this question

13Under the JJ Act, 2015, the body that has power to declare a person or facility as 'fit' for placement of a child in need of care and protection is:

  1. The District Magistrate
  2. The Family Court
  3. The Child Welfare Committee (CWC)
  4. The Juvenile Justice Board
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The Child Welfare Committee (CWC)

The Child Welfare Committee (CWC) under the JJ Act, 2015 has the power to declare a person or facility as 'fit' for placement of a child in need of care and protection.

Report this question

14Under Section 11(vi) of HAMA, 1956, the only essential act required for completing adoption is:

  1. Actual giving and taking of the child with intention to adopt — the Datta Homam ceremony is NOT required
  2. Court decree
  3. Registration
  4. Written agreement between the parties
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Actual giving and taking of the child with intention to adopt — the Datta Homam ceremony is NOT required

Section 11(vi) HAMA — the child must actually be given and taken in adoption; this physical transfer with the intention to adopt is the sole essential act. The Datta Homam ceremony is not legally required.

Report this question

15Under HMGA 1956, Section 8, a natural guardian needs prior court permission to:

  1. Educate the child
  2. Represent the minor in civil proceedings
  3. Mortgage, sell, gift, exchange or lease (for more than 5 years or extending 1 year beyond minority) the minor's immovable property
  4. Open a bank account for the minor
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Mortgage, sell, gift, exchange or lease (for more than 5 years or extending 1 year beyond minority) the minor's immovable property

Section 8 HMGA — a natural guardian cannot, without prior permission of the court, mortgage, charge, transfer by sale, gift, exchange, or lease (for more than 5 years or extending 1 year beyond minority) any part of the immovable property of the minor.

Report this question

16Under Section 8 of the Guardians and Wards Act, 1890, who can apply for the appointment of a guardian?

  1. Only the father
  2. Any person desirous of being appointed, OR any relative or friend of the minor, OR the Collector of the district
  3. Only blood relatives
  4. Only the court suo motu
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Any person desirous of being appointed, OR any relative or friend of the minor, OR the Collector of the district

Section 8 GWA — the application may be made by the person desirous of being appointed guardian, any relative or friend of the minor, or the Collector of the district or other officer having authority in the place.

Report this question

17Under Muslim Law of guardianship, the property guardian (wali-ul-mal) of a minor can deal with the minor's property:

  1. Only for the minor's benefit or legal necessity — major transactions require court approval
  2. Only with co-heirs' consent
  3. Only for educational purposes
  4. Freely without restrictions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Only for the minor's benefit or legal necessity — major transactions require court approval

Under Muslim Law, the property guardian can manage the minor's property prudently and deal with it only for the minor's benefit or out of legal necessity; major transactions (sale, mortgage) require court approval.

Report this question

18Which of the following correctly distinguishes guardianship under the Guardians and Wards Act, 1890 from guardianship under the Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act, 1956?

  1. GWA applies only to Christians; HMGA applies only to Hindus
  2. HMGA recognises natural guardianship by operation of law; GWA requires a court order; GWA is the residual secular law applicable when no personal law governs; both subject to welfare principle
  3. GWA is superior to HMGA in all respects
  4. HMGA applies only for property matters; GWA only for custody
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. HMGA recognises natural guardianship by operation of law; GWA requires a court order; GWA is the residual secular law applicable when no personal law governs; both subject to welfare principle

HMGA 1956 = personal law of Hindus; creates natural guardianship by operation of law. GWA 1890 = secular general law; requires court appointment. GWA fills gaps where personal law doesn't apply. Both subject to paramount welfare of minor.
