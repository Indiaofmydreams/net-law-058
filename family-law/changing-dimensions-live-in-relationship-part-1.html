17 practice questions with answers

1The Supreme Court in which case first recognised that a long-standing live-in relationship can be presumed to be a valid marriage?

  1. S. Khushboo v. Kanniammal (2010)
  2. Indra Sarma v. V.K.V. Sarma (2013)
  3. Badri Prasad v. Dy. Director of Consolidation (1978)
  4. Velusamy v. D. Patchaiammal (2010)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Badri Prasad v. Dy. Director of Consolidation (1978)

Badri Prasad v. Dy. Director of Consolidation (1978) was the first case to raise the presumption of marriage from prolonged cohabitation. D. Velusamy later laid conditions for 'relationship in nature of marriage'.

2In D. Velusamy v. D. Patchaiammal (2010), the Supreme Court held that a woman in a live-in relationship can claim maintenance under Section 125 CrPC only if:

  1. She has a child from the relationship
  2. The relationship has lasted more than 5 years
  3. There is a written agreement
  4. The relationship is 'in the nature of marriage' — parties must be of marriageable age, unmarried, and live together publicly
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The relationship is 'in the nature of marriage' — parties must be of marriageable age, unmarried, and live together publicly

The Supreme Court laid down that a 'relationship in the nature of marriage' requires: legal age to marry, no legal bar to marry, and must have voluntarily lived together as husband and wife for a significant period.

Report this question

3The Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 provides protection to women in live-in relationships under the definition of:

  1. Wife
  2. Shared household
  3. Cohabitant
  4. Domestic relationship
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Domestic relationship

Section 2(f) of the PWDVA, 2005 defines 'domestic relationship' to include relationships in the nature of marriage, thereby covering live-in relationships.

Report this question

4Which was the FIRST Indian state to provide statutory recognition to live-in relationships through legislation?

  1. Himachal Pradesh
  2. Uttarakhand
  3. Goa
  4. Gujarat
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Uttarakhand

Uttarakhand's Uniform Civil Code, 2024 was the first Indian state legislation to mandatorily require registration of live-in relationships.

Report this question

5In Indra Sarma v. V.K.V. Sarma (2013), the Supreme Court categorised live-in relationships into how many types?

  1. 3
  2. 4
  3. 5
  4. 2
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 5

The Supreme Court in Indra Sarma categorised live-in relationships into 5 types, including domestic cohabitation between adult heterosexual unmarried couples, between a married man and an unmarried woman, etc.

Report this question

6Children born out of live-in relationships are treated as:

  1. Illegitimate with no rights
  2. Their status is not determined by any law
  3. Fully legitimate with all rights
  4. Legitimate for purpose of inheritance only from parents' property
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Legitimate for purpose of inheritance only from parents' property

In Tulsa v. Durghatiya (2008), the Supreme Court held children born from live-in relationships are legitimate for the purpose of inheriting their parents' property, but not joint family/ancestral property.

Report this question

7S. Khushboo v. Kanniammal (2010) is significant in the context of live-in relationships because:

  1. It made registration of live-in relationships mandatory
  2. It granted inheritance rights to live-in partners
  3. The Supreme Court held that live-in relationships between consenting adults are not illegal and are protected under Article 21
  4. It granted maintenance rights to live-in partners
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The Supreme Court held that live-in relationships between consenting adults are not illegal and are protected under Article 21

The Supreme Court in S. Khushboo held that live-in relationships between consenting adults do not violate any law and are protected under the right to life and personal liberty under Article 21.

Report this question

8Which Section of the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 defines 'aggrieved person'?

  1. Section 2(f)
  2. Section 3
  3. Section 2(a)
  4. Section 2(q)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Section 2(a)

Section 2(a) defines 'aggrieved person' as any woman in a domestic relationship with the respondent. Section 2(f) defines 'domestic relationship' to include live-in relationships.

Report this question

9In Madan Mohan Singh v. Rajni Kant (2010), the Supreme Court held regarding children from live-in relationships that:

  1. They are illegitimate
  2. They can inherit from mother only
  3. A presumption of marriage arises from prolonged cohabitation and children are legitimate
  4. Their legitimacy depends on court declaration
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A presumption of marriage arises from prolonged cohabitation and children are legitimate

The Court held that prolonged cohabitation raises a presumption of marriage, making the children legitimate with full inheritance rights.

10The Uttarakhand UCC 2024 makes registration of live-in relationships:

  1. Mandatory, with penalties for non-compliance
  2. Required only if either party is a non-resident Indian
  3. Required only if a child is born
  4. Optional
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Mandatory, with penalties for non-compliance

The Uttarakhand UCC 2024 mandates registration of live-in relationships and prescribes penalties for non-compliance.

Report this question

11Tulsa v. Durghatiya (2008) is a landmark case on:

  1. Adoption by Muslims
  2. Legitimacy of children born from long-standing live-in relationships
  3. Maintenance of divorced Muslim women
  4. Daughter's right to partition
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Legitimacy of children born from long-standing live-in relationships

In Tulsa v. Durghatiya, the Supreme Court held that children born from a long-cohabitation live-in relationship are legitimate and have the right to inherit ancestral property.

Report this question

12In Shafin Jahan v. Asokan K.M. (2018) (Hadiya case), the Supreme Court held:

  1. Conversion for marriage is fraudulent
  2. The right to choose one's religion and life partner are core fundamental rights under Article 21 — courts cannot override an adult's choice
  3. Interfaith marriages are void
  4. NIA can investigate all inter-faith marriages
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The right to choose one's religion and life partner are core fundamental rights under Article 21 — courts cannot override an adult's choice

Shafin Jahan (Hadiya case, 2018) — SC held that an adult's choice of religion and life partner is a fundamental right under Article 21; marriage could not be annulled merely because of religious conversion.

Report this question

13The Supreme Court in Shakti Vahini v. Union of India (2018) held that:

  1. Inter-caste marriages require parental approval
  2. Caste panchayats have authority over marriage choices
  3. The right to choose a life partner is an inextricable part of Articles 19 and 21 — khap panchayat interference is unconstitutional
  4. Honour killings are justified under custom
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The right to choose a life partner is an inextricable part of Articles 19 and 21 — khap panchayat interference is unconstitutional

Shakti Vahini (2018) — SC upheld the absolute right to choose a partner; directed states to establish protective homes; held khap/caste panchayat interference in marriages is unconstitutional.

Report this question

14In Tulsa v. Durghatiya (2008), the Supreme Court held that a child born of long and continuous cohabitation of a man and woman as husband and wife is:

  1. Illegitimate
  2. Legitimate only if the parents later register the marriage
  3. Legitimate, as a presumption of valid marriage arises from long cohabitation
  4. Entitled to maintenance only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Legitimate, as a presumption of valid marriage arises from long cohabitation

Tulsa v. Durghatiya (2008) — long cohabitation raises a presumption of marriage; the child is legitimate. Bharatha Matha (2010) and Revanasiddappa (2011) deal with the extent of property rights.

Report this question

15The legal basis for protecting live-in relationships in India primarily flows from:

  1. Article 21 — right to life and personal liberty including right to choose one's partner
  2. Article 14 — right to equality
  3. Article 25 — freedom of religion
  4. Article 19(1)(a) — freedom of speech
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Article 21 — right to life and personal liberty including right to choose one's partner

Article 21 — the SC has grounded the protection of live-in relationships in the right to life and personal liberty, which includes the right to choose a partner without State interference.

Report this question

16In S. Khushboo v. Kanniammal (2010), the Supreme Court quashed criminal cases and held:

  1. Such statements violate public morality
  2. Live-in relationships are void under Hindu law
  3. Pre-marital sex is a criminal offence
  4. Live-in relationships and pre-marital sex between consenting adults are not illegal and cannot be penalised
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Live-in relationships and pre-marital sex between consenting adults are not illegal and cannot be penalised

Khushboo (2010) — SC quashed FIRs against the actress; held consensual adult relationships are not illegal; distinguished social morality from legal standards.

Report this question

17In D. Velusamy v. D. Patchaiammal (2010), the Supreme Court held that for a 'relationship in the nature of marriage' under PWDVA, there is NO requirement of:

  1. Legal age to marry
  2. Voluntary cohabitation
  3. A minimum fixed cohabitation period of 5 years
  4. Holding themselves out as spouses
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A minimum fixed cohabitation period of 5 years

Velusamy — there is NO minimum fixed period of 5 years; the SC laid down qualitative conditions: legal age, no legal bar to marry, public acknowledgment as spouses — no fixed duration requirement.
