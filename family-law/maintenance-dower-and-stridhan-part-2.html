16 practice questions with answers

1The Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act was enacted in:

  1. 2005
  2. 2007
  3. 2010
  4. 1999
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 2007

The Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007 provides for maintenance of senior citizens and parents through Maintenance Tribunals.

Report this question

2Rajnesh v. Neha (2020) is an important Supreme Court judgment relating to:

  1. Daughter's coparcenary rights
  2. Inter-country adoption guidelines
  3. Comprehensive guidelines for maintenance proceedings — avoiding multiplicity, disclosure of assets, interim maintenance
  4. Triple talaq
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Comprehensive guidelines for maintenance proceedings — avoiding multiplicity, disclosure of assets, interim maintenance

In Rajnesh v. Neha (2020), the Supreme Court issued comprehensive guidelines for maintenance proceedings to avoid multiple proceedings, ensure disclosure of assets, and grant interim maintenance.

Report this question

3Under the PWDVA 2005, monetary relief under Section 20 is available to women in:

  1. Matrimonial homes only
  2. Domestic relationships including live-in relationships and relationships in the nature of marriage
  3. Registered marriages only
  4. Hindu marriages only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Domestic relationships including live-in relationships and relationships in the nature of marriage

The PWDVA, 2005 applies to women in any 'domestic relationship' including relationships in the nature of marriage (live-in relationships), as defined in Section 2(f).

Report this question

4Danial Latifi v. Union of India (2001) upheld the constitutional validity of the Muslim Women Act, 1986 by:

  1. Holding it fully consistent with the Shah Bano judgment
  2. Holding that Muslim women have no right beyond the iddat period
  3. Declaring it partially unconstitutional
  4. Giving it a broad interpretation — that the entire future maintenance must be computed and paid within the iddat period
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Giving it a broad interpretation — that the entire future maintenance must be computed and paid within the iddat period

The Supreme Court gave an expansive interpretation to the 1986 Act by reading it broadly — the husband must make a reasonable and fair provision for the entire future life of the divorced wife within the iddat period.

5The Dowry Prohibition Act was enacted in:

  1. 1961
  2. 1955
  3. 1983
  4. 1975
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 1961

The Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961 prohibits the giving and taking of dowry, with penalties for violation.

Report this question

6Under Section 6 of the Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961, dowry received by any person other than the woman must be transferred to her within:

  1. 1 year of marriage
  2. As directed by the court
  3. 3 months of marriage or within 3 months of attaining majority if she was a minor
  4. 1 month of marriage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 3 months of marriage or within 3 months of attaining majority if she was a minor

Section 6 provides that any dowry received by any person other than the woman must be transferred to her within 3 months of marriage, or 3 months of her attaining majority if she was a minor.

Report this question

7Section 498A of the IPC (now BNS Section 85) deals with:

  1. Bigamy
  2. Cruelty by husband or his relatives to a woman (including demands of dowry)
  3. Dowry death
  4. Kidnapping for marriage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Cruelty by husband or his relatives to a woman (including demands of dowry)

Section 498A IPC (now BNS Section 85) makes cruelty by the husband or his relatives (including wilful conduct and harassment for dowry) a cognisable, non-bailable, non-compoundable offence.

Report this question

8Section 304B IPC (now BNS Section 80) deals with:

  1. Dowry death — where a woman dies within 7 years of marriage under suspicious circumstances and there was cruelty or dowry harassment
  2. Domestic violence
  3. Forced marriage
  4. Cruelty to woman
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Dowry death — where a woman dies within 7 years of marriage under suspicious circumstances and there was cruelty or dowry harassment

Section 304B IPC (now BNS Section 80) defines dowry death: if a woman dies within 7 years of marriage and it is shown there was cruelty or harassment for dowry soon before her death, the husband or relatives are deemed to have caused her death.

9A Hindu wife loses her right to maintenance under Section 18(3) of HAMA, 1956 if she:

  1. Visits her parents for more than 3 months
  2. Lives unchastely or ceases to be a Hindu by conversion
  3. Starts working
  4. Files a complaint against the husband
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Lives unchastely or ceases to be a Hindu by conversion

Section 18(3) HAMA — a Hindu wife is not entitled to separate residence and maintenance if she is unchaste or converts to another religion.

Report this question

10Section 24 of the HMA, 1955 provides for:

  1. Interim custody of children
  2. Maintenance of children only
  3. Maintenance pendente lite and expenses of proceedings — payable to either spouse
  4. Permanent alimony after decree
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Maintenance pendente lite and expenses of proceedings — payable to either spouse

Section 24 — maintenance pendente lite (during pendency of proceedings) and expenses of proceedings available to either spouse; it is gender-neutral.

Report this question

11The key difference between Section 24 and Section 25 of the HMA, 1955 is:

  1. Section 24 is for wife only; Section 25 is for husband only
  2. Section 24 provides interim maintenance during proceedings; Section 25 provides permanent alimony after the decree — both are gender-neutral
  3. Section 24 is for divorce proceedings only; Section 25 is for annulment only
  4. There is no difference; both provide the same relief
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Section 24 provides interim maintenance during proceedings; Section 25 provides permanent alimony after the decree — both are gender-neutral

Section 24 = maintenance pendente lite (during proceedings); Section 25 = permanent alimony post-decree. Both are gender-neutral and available to either spouse.

Report this question

12Under Section 26 of the HMA, 1955, the court may make orders regarding:

  1. Division of stridhan
  2. Custody, maintenance and education of minor children
  3. Maintenance of parents
  4. Matrimonial property
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Custody, maintenance and education of minor children

Section 26 — the court may make provisions for custody, maintenance and education of minor children in any proceedings under the Act.

Report this question

13Section 21 of HAMA, 1956 lists dependants entitled to maintenance from the estate of a deceased Hindu. Which of the following is NOT listed as a dependant?

  1. His father
  2. His minor illegitimate son
  3. His unmarried daughter
  4. His brother
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. His brother

Section 21 HAMA — the list of dependants includes father, mother, widow, minor sons, unmarried daughters, widowed daughters, minor illegitimate children, step-mother etc. Brother is NOT listed.

Report this question

14Under HAMA 1956, Section 20 imposes an obligation on a Hindu to maintain:

  1. All family members
  2. Aged or infirm parents, minor legitimate and illegitimate children, and unmarried daughters
  3. Parents and children only
  4. Spouse and children only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Aged or infirm parents, minor legitimate and illegitimate children, and unmarried daughters

Section 20 HAMA — a Hindu is obligated to maintain aged or infirm parents, legitimate and illegitimate minor children, and unmarried daughters.

Report this question

15Both Iqbal Bano v. State of UP (2007) and Shabana Bano v. Imran Khan (2009) confirmed that:

  1. Muslim women cannot claim under Section 125 CrPC after the 1986 Act
  2. A Muslim divorced wife can choose to proceed under Section 125 CrPC unless she has opted for the Muslim Women Act 1986
  3. The Muslim Women Act 1986 completely replaces Section 125 CrPC
  4. Muslim women can claim under both Acts simultaneously
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A Muslim divorced wife can choose to proceed under Section 125 CrPC unless she has opted for the Muslim Women Act 1986

Both cases confirmed that Section 125 CrPC remains available to Muslim divorced women as an alternative; the 1986 Act does not compulsorily take away CrPC rights.

Report this question

16In Rajnesh v. Neha (2020), the Supreme Court issued comprehensive guidelines for maintenance proceedings to prevent:

  1. Child custody disputes
  2. Triple talaq
  3. Multiplicity of proceedings, require disclosure of assets, ensure grant of interim maintenance
  4. Inter-country adoption
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Multiplicity of proceedings, require disclosure of assets, ensure grant of interim maintenance

Rajnesh v. Neha (2021) — SC issued guidelines to avoid multiple overlapping maintenance proceedings, mandated disclosure of assets/liabilities by both parties, and directed grant of interim maintenance within 60 days.
