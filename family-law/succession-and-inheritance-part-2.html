18 practice questions with answers

1Under the Hindu Succession Act, 1956, which Section deals with disqualification of heirs on grounds of murder?

  1. Section 25
  2. Section 24
  3. Section 26
  4. Section 23
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Section 25

Section 25 of the HSA, 1956 provides that a person who commits murder or abets the commission of murder of the deceased shall be disqualified from succeeding to the property of the deceased.

2Under Section 14 of the Hindu Succession Act, 1956, a Hindu female who held any property before the Act's commencement:

  1. Continues to hold it as a limited estate
  2. Must surrender it to the Joint Family
  3. Inherits it equally with male heirs
  4. Acquires it as absolute owner — all limitations and restrictions cease
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Acquires it as absolute owner — all limitations and restrictions cease

Section 14(1) provides that any property possessed by a Hindu female, whether acquired before or after commencement of the Act, shall be held by her as full owner and not as a limited owner.

Report this question

3Under the Muslim Law of Inheritance, which relation is an 'Asaba' (residuary heir)?

  1. Daughter
  2. Mother
  3. Son
  4. Son's daughter
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Son

The son is the primary residuary heir (Asaba) in Muslim Law. He takes what remains after the sharers (Zawil Furud) have taken their fixed shares.

Report this question

4Under Sunni Law of Inheritance, the share of a daughter when she is the ONLY child is:

  1. 1/8
  2. 1/2
  3. 1/6
  4. 2/3
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1/2

Under Sunni Muslim Law (Quran 4:11), if there is only one daughter and no son, she takes 1/2 of the estate. If there are two or more daughters (and no son), they together take 2/3.

Report this question

5Under Sunni Law, the share of the husband in his wife's estate when she dies leaving children is:

  1. 1/4
  2. 1/3
  3. 1/6
  4. 1/2
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 1/4

Under Quran 4:12, the husband takes 1/2 of his wife's estate if she leaves no children, and 1/4 if she leaves children.

Report this question

6Under Sunni Law, the share of the wife in her husband's estate when he dies leaving children is:

  1. 1/3
  2. 1/4
  3. 1/8
  4. 1/6
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1/8

Under Quran 4:12, the wife (or wives collectively) takes 1/4 of the husband's estate if he leaves no children, and 1/8 if he leaves children.

Report this question

7Under Section 30 of the Hindu Succession Act, 1956, a Hindu can dispose of by will:

  1. Only separate property
  2. Only ancestral property
  3. Separate/self-acquired property AND his undivided interest in a Mitakshara joint family property
  4. Coparcenary property only (under Mitakshara)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Separate/self-acquired property AND his undivided interest in a Mitakshara joint family property

Section 30 HSA allows a Hindu to dispose of by will or other testamentary disposition any property which is capable of being so disposed of by them, including their undivided interest in a Mitakshara joint family property.

Report this question

8Under the Indian Succession Act, 1925, which of the following communities is NOT governed by it for intestate succession?

  1. Jews
  2. Muslims
  3. Parsis
  4. Christians
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Muslims

Muslims are governed by their personal law (Muslim Law of Inheritance) for succession, not the Indian Succession Act, 1925.

9The principle governing intestate succession under Parsi law follows which system?

  1. Only through male heirs
  2. Per stirpes — distribution by family branches
  3. Only through testamentary succession
  4. Per capita equally regardless of branch
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Per stirpes — distribution by family branches

Parsi succession under the Indian Succession Act, 1925 (Sections 50–56) follows the per stirpes principle — distribution is through family branches.

10The correct sequence of priority of heirs under Muslim Law (Sunni) is:

  1. Dhawul Arham → Dhawul Furud → Asabat → State
  2. State → Dhawul Arham → Dhawul Furud → Asabat
  3. Asabat → Dhawul Furud → Dhawul Arham → State
  4. Dhawul Furud → Asabat → Dhawul Arham → State
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Dhawul Furud → Asabat → Dhawul Arham → State

The order of priority in Sunni Muslim Inheritance: (1) Dhawul Furud/Zawil Furud (Quranic sharers), (2) Asabat (residuaries), (3) Dhawul Arham (distant kindred), (4) State.

11John Vallamattom v. Union of India (2003) struck down:

  1. Section 125 CrPC as applied to Muslims
  2. Section 118 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925 — which restricted Christians from making bequests to religious/charitable purposes within one year of death
  3. Section 9 of the HMA
  4. Triple Talaq
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Section 118 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925 — which restricted Christians from making bequests to religious/charitable purposes within one year of death

The Supreme Court struck down Section 118 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925 as unconstitutional. Section 118 had discriminated against Christians by restricting their right to make bequests for religious/charitable purposes within 12 months of death.

12In Revanasiddappa v. Mallikarjun (2011), the Supreme Court held that children born from void or voidable marriages:

  1. Are legitimate and have right to the parents' property (not joint family property)
  2. Have no right to inheritance
  3. Can only inherit from the mother
  4. Are completely illegitimate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Are legitimate and have right to the parents' property (not joint family property)

Section 16 HMA confers legitimacy on children of void and voidable marriages. In Revanasiddappa, the SC held they can inherit from their parents' property but not from joint family/ancestral property.

13Assertion (A): Under the Hindu Succession Act, 1956, daughters now have equal coparcenary rights as sons by virtue of the 2005 Amendment. Reason (R): The Supreme Court in Vineeta Sharma (2020) held that this right is available even if the father died before September 9, 2005.

  1. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are correct but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is correct but R is incorrect
  4. A is incorrect but R is correct
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A

Both A and R are correct. The 2005 Amendment grants daughters equal coparcenary rights and Vineeta Sharma (2020) confirmed that this right is not contingent on the father being alive on the date of the amendment.

14The most accurate distinction between Mitakshara and Dayabhaga schools on a widow's right to partition is:

  1. Under both schools, a widow has no right to demand partition
  2. Under Mitakshara, a widow can demand partition after husband's death; under Dayabhaga, a widow is a full coparcener
  3. Under Mitakshara, traditionally no coparcenary right for widow but she inherits husband's share; under Dayabhaga, widow directly inherits husband's interest and can demand partition
  4. Both schools give equal partition rights to widows
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Under Mitakshara, traditionally no coparcenary right for widow but she inherits husband's share; under Dayabhaga, widow directly inherits husband's interest and can demand partition

Under Mitakshara, a widow was traditionally not a coparcener but inherited her husband's interest on his death and could demand partition. Under Dayabhaga, the widow directly inherits the husband's share and can demand partition as his heir.

15Which of the following is correct about disqualifications under Muslim Law of Inheritance? I. A murderer is disqualified. II. An apostate is disqualified. III. A non-Muslim cannot inherit from a Muslim. IV. A child in the womb can inherit.

  1. I, II, III and IV
  2. II and IV only
  3. I and II only
  4. I, II and III only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. I, II, III and IV

All four are correct: murder, apostasy, and difference of religion are bars to inheritance; a child in the womb is treated as born for inheritance purposes.

Report this question

16The 'Doctrine of Umar' (Umariyatain) in Muslim inheritance applies when the deceased leaves:

  1. Only a spouse and daughters
  2. Only daughters
  3. Only agnate relatives
  4. Mother, father and a spouse — mother's share becomes 1/3 of the residue after the spouse's share, not 1/3 of the whole estate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Mother, father and a spouse — mother's share becomes 1/3 of the residue after the spouse's share, not 1/3 of the whole estate

The Umariyatain cases: where deceased leaves mother, father, and a spouse, the mother's share is 1/3 of the residue (after spouse's share) rather than 1/3 of the whole estate.

Report this question

17Under Schedule Class I of the HSA, 1956 (after the 2005 Amendment), which of the following is a Class I heir?

  1. Uncle
  2. Widow of a predeceased son's son
  3. Father
  4. Brother
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Widow of a predeceased son's son

The widow of a predeceased son's son is a Class I heir under the Schedule. Father, brother, and uncle are NOT Class I heirs.

Report this question

18Under Section 10 of the HSA, 1956, the property of a Hindu male dying intestate among Class I heirs is divided such that:

  1. The eldest son takes a larger share
  2. All Class I heirs take equally
  3. The widow takes one share, each surviving son/daughter takes one share, and the mother takes one share — simultaneously
  4. Sons take double the share of daughters
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The widow takes one share, each surviving son/daughter takes one share, and the mother takes one share — simultaneously

Section 10, Rule 1 — the widow takes one share, each surviving son and each surviving daughter takes one share, and the mother takes one share; all simultaneously.
