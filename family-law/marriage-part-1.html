22 practice questions with answers

1Under Section 5 of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, which of the following is NOT a condition for a valid Hindu marriage?

  1. Neither party has a living spouse
  2. Neither party is an idiot or lunatic
  3. Bride must be 18 years, bridegroom must be 21 years
  4. Both parties must belong to the same caste
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Both parties must belong to the same caste

The HMA, 1955 does not require parties to belong to the same caste. Section 5 conditions are: no living spouse, mental capacity, age (18/21), and no prohibited degree or sapinda relationship.

2A Hindu marriage is void under Section 11 of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 when:

  1. Consent was obtained by fraud
  2. The bridegroom is below 21 years
  3. One of the parties has a living spouse
  4. There is no proper ceremony performed
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. One of the parties has a living spouse

Section 11 HMA lists void marriages: bigamy (living spouse), prohibited degree of relationship, and sapinda relationship.

Report this question

3A Hindu marriage is voidable under Section 12 of the HMA, 1955 on the ground of:

  1. Impotency of the respondent
  2. Prohibited degree of relationship
  3. Bigamy
  4. Sapinda relationship
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Impotency of the respondent

Section 12 HMA makes a marriage voidable on grounds of: impotency, insanity (unsound mind), consent by force or fraud, and pre-marriage pregnancy by another person.

Report this question

4Under Muslim Law, Nikah (marriage) is essentially:

  1. A civil contract
  2. An administrative formality
  3. A sacrament
  4. A religious duty
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A civil contract

Under Muslim Law, marriage (Nikah) is a civil contract requiring offer (Ijab), acceptance (Qabool), competent parties, and dower (Mehr). It is not a sacrament.

Report this question

5The minimum number of witnesses required for a valid Muslim marriage (Nikah) is:

  1. Four
  2. One
  3. Three
  4. Two
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Two

Two witnesses are required for a valid Nikah — two competent male witnesses, or one male and two female witnesses under Sunni law.

Report this question

6Under Muslim Law, which of the following marriages is irregular (fasid) and NOT void (batil)?

  1. Marriage with own mother
  2. Marriage with foster mother
  3. Marriage with a woman in iddat
  4. Marriage with a fifth wife
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Marriage with a fifth wife

Marriage with a fifth wife simultaneously is irregular (fasid) under Sunni law — it can be regularised by divorcing one of the four existing wives. Marriages with mother or foster mother are absolutely void (batil).

Report this question

7Under the Special Marriage Act, 1954, notice of intended marriage is required to be given how many days before the marriage?

  1. 30 days
  2. 60 days
  3. 15 days
  4. 90 days
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 30 days

Under Section 5 of the Special Marriage Act, 1954, a notice of intended marriage must be given 30 days before the date of marriage.

Report this question

8A Muslim man can legally marry up to how many wives simultaneously under Muslim personal law?

  1. 2
  2. 3
  3. 4
  4. Unlimited
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 4

Under Muslim Personal Law, a Muslim male may marry up to four wives simultaneously, subject to the condition of equal treatment (Quran 4:3).

Report this question

9Under the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, which ceremony is considered essential for a valid Hindu marriage?

  1. Mangalsutra tying
  2. Exchange of garlands
  3. Kanyadan only
  4. Saptapadi (seven steps)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Saptapadi (seven steps)

Under Section 7 HMA, the marriage is complete and binding when the seventh step in Saptapadi is taken before the sacred fire.

Report this question

10The concept of 'prohibited degrees of relationship' under the HMA, 1955 is contained in:

  1. Section 13
  2. Section 3(g)
  3. Section 11
  4. Section 5(iv) and (v)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Section 5(iv) and (v)

Section 5(iv) prohibits marriage within prohibited degrees and Section 5(v) prohibits sapinda relationships, unless custom permits.

Report this question

11Under Muslim Law, a marriage with a woman undergoing iddat is:

  1. Void (batil)
  2. Voidable
  3. Irregular (fasid)
  4. Valid
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Irregular (fasid)

Under Sunni law, marriage with a woman in iddat is irregular (fasid), not void. It can be regularised once the iddat period expires.

Report this question

12Under Christian personal law, marriage in India is governed by:

  1. Indian Christian Marriage Act, 1872
  2. Indian Divorce Act, 1869
  3. Special Marriage Act, 1954
  4. Both (a) and (b)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Indian Christian Marriage Act, 1872

The Indian Christian Marriage Act, 1872 governs solemnisation of Christian marriages in India.

Report this question

13Which of the following is NOT a condition of a valid Nikah under Muslim Law?

  1. Presence of Qazi
  2. Offer (Ijab)
  3. Acceptance (Qabool)
  4. Dower (Mehr)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Presence of Qazi

Presence of a Qazi is not legally essential for a valid Nikah. The essentials are: offer, acceptance, competent parties, witnesses, and absence of legal disability.

Report this question

14'Muta' marriage is recognized under:

  1. Sunni Law
  2. Shia Law
  3. Both Sunni and Shia Law
  4. Neither
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Shia Law

Muta marriage is a temporary marriage recognised only under Shia (Ithna Ashari) Law. It is not recognised under Sunni law.

Report this question

15Under the HMA, 1955, the age of marriage for a bride and bridegroom respectively is:

  1. 18 and 21
  2. 21 and 21
  3. 16 and 18
  4. 18 and 18
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 18 and 21

Section 5(iii) of the HMA, 1955 requires the bride to be at least 18 years and the bridegroom at least 21 years of age.

Report this question

16The Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006 replaced:

  1. The Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act, 1956
  2. The Special Marriage Act, 1954
  3. The Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939
  4. The Child Marriage Restraint Act, 1929
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The Child Marriage Restraint Act, 1929

The Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006 replaced the Child Marriage Restraint Act, 1929 (Sharda Act), providing stronger penalties and making child marriages voidable.

Report this question

17'Muharramat' under Muslim Law refers to:

  1. Rules of inheritance
  2. Prohibited degrees of relationship for marriage
  3. Conditions of valid divorce
  4. Obligatory prayers
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Prohibited degrees of relationship for marriage

Muharramat refers to the prohibited degrees of relationship within which a Muslim cannot marry — these include relations by blood, foster relations, and relations by marriage (affinity).

Report this question

18Under the Special Marriage Act, 1954, if an objection is filed to a notice of intended marriage, the Marriage Officer must:

  1. Refer the matter to the court immediately
  2. Refuse the marriage
  3. Cancel the notice
  4. Inquire into the objection and decide within 30 days
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Inquire into the objection and decide within 30 days

Under Section 8 of the SMA, 1954, the Marriage Officer shall inquire into the objection and decide within 30 days. If the objection is upheld, a party can appeal to the district court.

Report this question

19A Hindu marriage where neither Saptapadi nor any form of ceremony is performed is:

  1. Irregular
  2. Valid if witnessed
  3. Void
  4. Voidable
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Void

Section 7 HMA mandates customary rites and ceremonies including Saptapadi where applicable. A marriage without any valid ceremony under Section 7 is void.

Report this question

20Under the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, registration of marriage is:

  1. Compulsory for validity
  2. Required only for inter-caste marriages
  3. Not provided for at all
  4. Provided under Section 8 but non-registration does not invalidate the marriage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Provided under Section 8 but non-registration does not invalidate the marriage

Section 8 of the HMA provides for registration of marriages but non-registration does not affect the validity of the marriage.

Report this question

21In Seema v. Ashwani Kumar (2006), the Supreme Court directed:

  1. Compulsory registration of marriages irrespective of religion
  2. Setting up family courts in every district
  3. Dissolution of unregistered marriages
  4. Mandatory DNA tests in matrimonial disputes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Compulsory registration of marriages irrespective of religion

The Supreme Court directed all states to make rules for compulsory registration of marriages within three months, applicable to all marriages irrespective of religion.

Report this question

22Under Muslim Law, a marriage with a non-Muslim woman (Kitabia — People of the Book) by a Sunni male is:

  1. Valid
  2. Voidable
  3. Irregular (fasid)
  4. Void (batil)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Valid

Under Sunni Hanafi law, a Muslim male can validly marry a Kitabia (Christian or Jewish) woman. Marriage with a fire-worshipper or idolater is irregular.
