23 practice questions with answers

1The iddat period for a divorced Muslim woman (non-pregnant) is:

  1. 2 menstrual cycles
  2. 4 months and 10 days
  3. 6 months
  4. 3 menstrual cycles
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 3 menstrual cycles

The iddat period for a divorced Muslim woman (non-pregnant) is 3 menstrual cycles (or 3 lunar months if she does not menstruate).

2In Naveen Kohli v. Neelu Kohli (2006), the Supreme Court granted divorce and recommended incorporation of irretrievable breakdown theory by exercising power under:

  1. Article 226 — writ jurisdiction
  2. Article 136 — Special Leave Petition
  3. Article 32 — constitutional remedies
  4. Article 142 — power to do complete justice
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Article 142 — power to do complete justice

The Supreme Court exercised its extraordinary jurisdiction under Article 142 to grant divorce on the ground of irretrievable breakdown and recommended Parliament to incorporate this theory.

3Under the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939, a Muslim wife can seek dissolution if her husband's whereabouts are unknown for:

  1. 2 years
  2. 7 years
  3. 3 years
  4. 4 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 4 years

Section 2(i) of the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939 provides that a wife can seek dissolution if the husband's whereabouts have been unknown for 4 years.

Report this question

4Under the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939, failure of the husband to provide maintenance entitles the wife to seek dissolution after non-payment for:

  1. 1 year
  2. 6 months
  3. 3 years
  4. 2 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 2 years

Section 2(ii) of the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939 provides that if the husband has neglected or failed to provide maintenance for 2 years, the wife can seek dissolution.

Report this question

5'Lian' under Muslim Law refers to:

  1. Divorce by mutual agreement
  2. Husband's abstinence oath
  3. Divorce through court
  4. A form of divorce where husband swears wife was unfaithful and wife takes counter-oath
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A form of divorce where husband swears wife was unfaithful and wife takes counter-oath

Lian is a form of divorce by mutual imprecation. The husband accuses the wife of adultery by oath; the wife denies it by oath. This results in permanent dissolution with prohibition on remarriage.

Report this question

6The 'Halala' doctrine in Muslim law means that a thrice-divorced wife can remarry her former husband only if:

  1. 3 years have elapsed
  2. She first marries another man, that marriage is consummated, and then that husband dies or divorces her
  3. She obtains a court certificate
  4. Both families consent
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. She first marries another man, that marriage is consummated, and then that husband dies or divorces her

Halala (Tahleel marriage) requires the thrice-divorced woman to first marry another man (nikah halala), the marriage must be consummated, and then after its dissolution (by death or divorce), she may remarry her first husband.

Report this question

7Under the Indian Divorce Act, 1869 (as amended in 2001), Christian spouses have:

  1. Adultery as the only ground (as under the old law for a wife)
  2. Equal grounds for divorce — both husband and wife can seek divorce on the same grounds
  3. Only mutual consent as an option
  4. Only cruelty
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Equal grounds for divorce — both husband and wife can seek divorce on the same grounds

The Indian Divorce (Amendment) Act, 2001 removed gender inequality. Now both spouses have equal grounds for divorce including adultery, cruelty, desertion, conversion, and others.

Report this question

8Section 10A of the Indian Divorce Act, 1869 (added by the 2001 Amendment) provides for:

  1. Divorce by mutual consent for Christians
  2. Annulment of marriage
  3. Judicial separation
  4. Restitution of conjugal rights
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Divorce by mutual consent for Christians

Section 10A was inserted by the 2001 Amendment to provide divorce by mutual consent for Christians, requiring 2 years of separation.

Report this question

9The concept of 'constructive desertion' under Hindu Law means:

  1. Physical absence from marital home
  2. When one spouse's conduct forces the other to leave — the forcing spouse is treated as the deserter
  3. Desertion for less than statutory period
  4. Desertion with valid reason
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. When one spouse's conduct forces the other to leave — the forcing spouse is treated as the deserter

Constructive desertion arises when one spouse's conduct is so unreasonable or intolerable that the other is compelled to leave. The spouse who causes the desertion is deemed the deserter.

10The Shia Muslim sect of Ithna Ashari (Twelvers) differs from Sunni law primarily in that it recognises:

  1. Muta (temporary) marriage
  2. Marriage without dower
  3. Marriage with five wives
  4. Triple talaq in one sitting
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Muta (temporary) marriage

Muta (temporary marriage for a fixed period) is a distinctive feature of Shia (Ithna Ashari) law and is not recognised under Sunni law.

11Under Section 13(2) of HMA, a wife can seek divorce if she was married before 15 years of age and repudiates the marriage. This must be done:

  1. Before attaining 21 years of age
  2. Before attaining 18 years of age
  3. At any time
  4. Within 2 years of attaining 18 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Before attaining 18 years of age

Section 13(2)(iv) provides that a wife married before the age of 15 may repudiate the marriage after attaining 15 but before attaining 18 years of age.

12Sarla Mudgal v. Union of India (1995) held that a second marriage after conversion to Islam by a Hindu male is:

  1. Valid under Muslim Law and not an offence
  2. Void and amounts to bigamy under Section 494 IPC — the first Hindu marriage continues to subsist
  3. Valid if the first wife consents
  4. Valid if registered under the Special Marriage Act
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Void and amounts to bigamy under Section 494 IPC — the first Hindu marriage continues to subsist

The Supreme Court held that conversion to Islam does not dissolve a Hindu marriage. A second marriage contracted after conversion constitutes bigamy under Section 494 IPC (now BNS Section 82).

Report this question

13In Lily Thomas v. Union of India (2000), the Supreme Court:

  1. Referred the matter to a constitutional bench
  2. Held that conversion automatically dissolves the first marriage
  3. Reversed the Sarla Mudgal judgment
  4. Confirmed the Sarla Mudgal ruling — conversion for purpose of second marriage does not dissolve the first
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Confirmed the Sarla Mudgal ruling — conversion for purpose of second marriage does not dissolve the first

In Lily Thomas, the Supreme Court reiterated and confirmed the Sarla Mudgal ruling. It held that using conversion to Islam as a device to circumvent the law against bigamy is illegal.

14In Shayara Bano v. Union of India (2017), the Supreme Court struck down Triple Talaq (Talaq-ul-Biddat) by a majority of:

  1. 3:2
  2. Unanimous
  3. 5:0
  4. 4:1
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 3:2

The 5-judge bench in Shayara Bano struck down Talaq-ul-Biddat by a 3:2 majority. Three judges held it unconstitutional; two held it valid but recommended legislation.

15In Smt. Savitri Pandey v. Prem Chandra Pandey (2002), the Supreme Court held that cruelty as a ground for divorce under the HMA includes:

  1. Only physical violence
  2. Both physical and mental cruelty — mental cruelty can be inferred from conduct including false allegations of adultery
  3. Only cruelty resulting in medical evidence
  4. Only cruelty witnessed by third parties
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Both physical and mental cruelty — mental cruelty can be inferred from conduct including false allegations of adultery

The Supreme Court held that cruelty in matrimonial law includes both physical and mental cruelty. Mental cruelty can be established by conduct, including levelling false and grave allegations of unchastity.

Report this question

16In V. Bhagat v. D. Bhagat (1994), the Supreme Court held that which conduct amounts to mental cruelty under HMA?

  1. Refusal to cook food
  2. Frequent arguments over finances
  3. Levelling false and grave allegations of adultery against the spouse
  4. Absence from matrimonial home for work
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Levelling false and grave allegations of adultery against the spouse

In V. Bhagat v. D. Bhagat (1994), the Supreme Court held that levelling false and grave allegations of adultery or unchastity against the spouse constitutes mental cruelty sufficient to grant a divorce.

Report this question

17In Dastane v. Dastane (1975), the Supreme Court defined 'cruelty' under the Hindu Marriage Act to mean:

  1. Financial harm only
  2. Conduct causing reasonable apprehension in the petitioner's mind that it will be harmful or injurious to live with the respondent
  3. Public humiliation only
  4. Only physical injury
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Conduct causing reasonable apprehension in the petitioner's mind that it will be harmful or injurious to live with the respondent

In Dastane v. Dastane (1975), the Supreme Court held that cruelty under HMA means conduct of such character as to cause danger to life, limb or health (physical or mental), or to give rise to reasonable apprehension of such danger.

18The Personal Laws (Amendment) Act, 2019 removed which of the following as a ground for divorce under the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955?

  1. Desertion
  2. Venereal disease
  3. Leprosy
  4. Insanity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Leprosy

The Personal Laws (Amendment) Act, 2019 omitted leprosy (Section 13(1)(iv) HMA) as a ground for divorce, to remove stigma. Venereal disease (Section 13(1)(v)), unsound mind and desertion remain grounds.

Report this question

19Under Section 14 of the HMA, 1955, no petition for divorce can be presented within how long after the solemnisation of marriage?

  1. 3 years
  2. 6 months
  3. 2 years
  4. 1 year
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 1 year

Section 14 — no petition for divorce shall be presented within one year of marriage. Exception: court may grant leave in cases of exceptional hardship or exceptional depravity.

20The bar under Section 14 HMA (one-year bar on divorce petition) does not apply when:

  1. Petitions for judicial separation are filed
  2. Court grants leave in cases of exceptional hardship or exceptional depravity
  3. Mutual consent divorce petitions are filed
  4. Petitions for restitution of conjugal rights are filed
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Court grants leave in cases of exceptional hardship or exceptional depravity

Section 14 — the court may grant leave to present a petition before one year if the case involves exceptional hardship suffered by the petitioner or exceptional depravity of the respondent.

Report this question

21In which landmark case did the Supreme Court strongly recommend incorporation of 'irretrievable breakdown of marriage' as a ground for divorce in the HMA?

  1. Savitri Pandey v. Prem Chandra Pandey (2002)
  2. Dastane v. Dastane (1975)
  3. Naveen Kohli v. Neelu Kohli (2006)
  4. Saroj Rani v. Sudarshan Kumar Chadha (1984)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Naveen Kohli v. Neelu Kohli (2006)

Naveen Kohli v. Neelu Kohli (2006) — the Supreme Court exercised Article 142 jurisdiction and strongly recommended Parliament to incorporate irretrievable breakdown as a ground for divorce.

Report this question

22'Animus deserendi' in the context of desertion under the Hindu Marriage Act means:

  1. The intention to permanently forsake and abandon the spouse
  2. The intention to seek divorce
  3. Physical absence from the matrimonial home
  4. Absence due to employment
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The intention to permanently forsake and abandon the spouse

Desertion has two elements: (1) factum of separation (actual separation) and (2) animus deserendi (intention to desert permanently). Both must coexist.

Report this question

23After the Supreme Court ruling in Amardeep Singh v. Harveen Kaur (2017), the 6-month cooling-off period under Section 13B(2) HMA is:

  1. Directory — courts can waive it if the marriage has irretrievably broken down and waiting serves no purpose
  2. Applicable only if parties have children
  3. Mandatory and cannot be waived under any circumstances
  4. Reduced to 3 months by Parliament
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Directory — courts can waive it if the marriage has irretrievably broken down and waiting serves no purpose

Amardeep Singh v. Harveen Kaur (2017) — SC held the 6-month period under Section 13B(2) is directory not mandatory; courts can waive it in appropriate cases.
