23 practice questions with answers

1The 'Fault Theory' of divorce is also known as:

  1. Breakdown Theory
  2. Mixed Theory
  3. Consent Theory
  4. Offence Theory / Guilt Theory
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Offence Theory / Guilt Theory

The Fault/Offence/Guilt Theory requires one party to be innocent and one guilty. If both are guilty, neither can seek divorce.

Report this question

2Under the Fault Theory, if both parties are guilty of matrimonial offences:

  1. Both can seek divorce
  2. Neither can seek divorce
  3. The husband can seek divorce
  4. The wife can seek divorce
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Neither can seek divorce

The Fault Theory requires one innocent and one guilty party. If both are at fault, the doctrine of 'recrimination' bars both from seeking divorce.

Report this question

3Mutual consent divorce under the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 is governed by:

  1. Section 13
  2. Section 13A
  3. Section 13B
  4. Section 14
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Section 13B

Section 13B of the HMA, 1955 provides for divorce by mutual consent, requiring parties to have lived separately for at least 1 year.

Report this question

4The minimum period of separation required before filing for mutual consent divorce under HMA, 1955 is:

  1. 6 months
  2. 2 years
  3. 1 year
  4. 3 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1 year

Section 13B(1) requires the parties to have been living separately for at least one year before filing a petition for divorce by mutual consent.

Report this question

5The 'Irretrievable Breakdown Theory' of divorce is recognised in which Indian legislation?

  1. Hindu Marriage Act, 1955
  2. Not explicitly recognised in any Indian statute, but recognised judicially by Supreme Court
  3. Indian Divorce Act, 1869
  4. Special Marriage Act, 1954
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Not explicitly recognised in any Indian statute, but recognised judicially by Supreme Court

The Irretrievable Breakdown Theory is not codified in any Indian statute. It has been judicially applied by the Supreme Court under Article 142. The Law Commission (71st Report, 1978) recommended its incorporation.

Report this question

6Under the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, desertion as a ground of divorce requires continuous desertion for how many years?

  1. 5 years
  2. 2 years
  3. 1 year
  4. 3 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 2 years

Section 13(1)(ib) of the HMA requires continuous desertion for not less than two years immediately preceding the petition.

Report this question

7The Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act was enacted in:

  1. 1929
  2. 1937
  3. 1939
  4. 1935
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1939

The Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act was enacted in 1939, providing Muslim wives with 9 grounds to seek judicial dissolution of marriage.

Report this question

8'Triple Talaq' (Talaq-ul-Biddat) was declared unconstitutional by the Supreme Court in:

  1. Shayara Bano v. Union of India (2017)
  2. Danial Latifi v. Union of India (2001)
  3. Shah Bano Begum case (1985)
  4. Sarla Mudgal case (1995)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Shayara Bano v. Union of India (2017)

In Shayara Bano v. Union of India (2017), the Supreme Court by a 3:2 majority declared Talaq-ul-Biddat (instant triple talaq) unconstitutional.

Report this question

9The Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Marriage) Act, which criminalised instant triple talaq, was enacted in:

  1. 2018
  2. 2017
  3. 2019
  4. 2020
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 2019

The Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Marriage) Act, 2019 criminalised the practice of instant triple talaq (Talaq-ul-Biddat).

Report this question

10Under Muslim Law, 'Khula' is:

  1. Divorce initiated by the husband
  2. Divorce by mutual agreement
  3. Divorce at the instance of the wife with return of dower
  4. Divorce by the husband in state of intoxication
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Divorce at the instance of the wife with return of dower

Khula is a form of divorce initiated by the wife, where she returns the dower (Mehr) to the husband in exchange for release from the marriage.

Report this question

11'Mubaraat' under Muslim Law is:

  1. Divorce by husband's unilateral declaration
  2. Divorce by mutual agreement where both parties are willing to separate
  3. Divorce through court
  4. Judicial separation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Divorce by mutual agreement where both parties are willing to separate

Mubaraat is a form of divorce by mutual agreement and release, where both parties are desirous of separation.

Report this question

12'Ila' under Muslim Law refers to:

  1. Divorce through court decree
  2. Husband comparing wife's back to mother's back
  3. Wife's right to divorce upon non-payment of dower
  4. Husband's vow of abstinence from sexual relations for 4 months or more
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Husband's vow of abstinence from sexual relations for 4 months or more

Ila is a form of constructive divorce where the husband takes an oath of abstinence for four months or more. After that period, the marriage dissolves.

Report this question

13'Zihar' under Muslim Law means:

  1. A form of mutual divorce
  2. Husband's oath of continence
  3. Husband comparing wife's back to a female relative within prohibited degrees
  4. Divorce by husband's declaration
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Husband comparing wife's back to a female relative within prohibited degrees

Zihar is a form of injurious assimilation where the husband compares his wife's back to that of his mother or another female within prohibited degrees, rendering conjugal relations unlawful until expiation.

Report this question

14Under Section 13(2) of the HMA, 1955, which of the following is a ground for divorce available ONLY to the wife?

  1. Desertion
  2. Adultery
  3. Cruelty
  4. Rape, sodomy or bestiality committed by the husband
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Rape, sodomy or bestiality committed by the husband

Section 13(2) provides additional grounds available only to the wife: pre-Act polygamous marriage, rape/sodomy/bestiality by husband, non-compliance of maintenance decree, and marriage before age 15 (repudiated before 18).

Report this question

15Judicial separation under the HMA, 1955 is provided under which Section?

  1. Section 11
  2. Section 10
  3. Section 13
  4. Section 9
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Section 10

Section 10 of the HMA, 1955 provides for judicial separation. It does not dissolve the marriage but suspends the obligation to cohabit.

Report this question

16Restitution of conjugal rights under the HMA, 1955 is provided under which Section?

  1. Section 9
  2. Section 10
  3. Section 8
  4. Section 7
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Section 9

Section 9 of the HMA, 1955 provides for restitution of conjugal rights when one spouse has withdrawn from the society of the other without reasonable excuse.

Report this question

17The Supreme Court in which case upheld Section 9 of the HMA (restitution of conjugal rights) as not violating the right to privacy?

  1. Shah Bano Begum case (1985)
  2. T. Sareetha v. T. Venkatasubbaiah (1983)
  3. Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978)
  4. Saroj Rani v. Sudarshan Kumar Chadha (1984)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Saroj Rani v. Sudarshan Kumar Chadha (1984)

The Supreme Court in Saroj Rani v. Sudarshan Kumar Chadha (1984) upheld Section 9 HMA. The Andhra Pradesh HC in T. Sareetha (1983) had struck it down as unconstitutional.

Report this question

18The period of Iddat for a widow under Muslim Law is:

  1. 1 year
  2. 4 months and 10 days
  3. 3 months
  4. 3 months and 10 days
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 4 months and 10 days

The iddat period for a widow under Muslim Law is 4 months and 10 days. For a divorced woman, it is 3 menstrual cycles (or 3 months if she does not menstruate).

Report this question

19Which ground of divorce was added by the Marriage Laws (Amendment) Act, 1976 to the Hindu Marriage Act?

  1. Cruelty
  2. Leprosy
  3. Venereal disease
  4. Both (a) and (c)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Cruelty

Cruelty was added as an independent ground of divorce by the Marriage Laws (Amendment) Act, 1976. Earlier, cruelty was only a ground for judicial separation.

Report this question

20Which of the following correctly identifies all four major forms of Talaq under Muslim Law?

  1. Talaq-ul-Ahsan, Talaq-ul-Hasan, Talaq-ul-Biddat, Lian — all correctly matched
  2. Talaq-ul-Ahsan and Talaq-ul-Hasan only
  3. Talaq-ul-Ahsan, Talaq-ul-Hasan, Talaq-ul-Biddat only
  4. None of the above
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Talaq-ul-Ahsan, Talaq-ul-Hasan, Talaq-ul-Biddat, Lian — all correctly matched

Talaq-ul-Ahsan (most proper, revocable), Talaq-ul-Hasan (second proper form), Talaq-ul-Biddat (triple talaq, now void), and Lian (divorce by mutual imprecation) are all recognised forms.

Report this question

21Under Section 13(1A) of the Hindu Marriage Act, either party can seek divorce after a decree of judicial separation if cohabitation has not been resumed for how long?

  1. 3 years
  2. 6 months
  3. 1 year
  4. 2 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1 year

Section 13(1A) provides that either party may present a petition for dissolution of marriage if cohabitation has not been resumed for one year after a decree of judicial separation.

Report this question

22In which case did the Supreme Court hold that the 6-month cooling-off period under Section 13B HMA can be waived?

  1. Naveen Kohli v. Neelu Kohli (2006)
  2. Saroj Rani v. Sudarshan Kumar Chadha (1984)
  3. Amardeep Singh v. Harveen Kaur (2017)
  4. Shah Bano Begum case (1985)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Amardeep Singh v. Harveen Kaur (2017)

In Amardeep Singh v. Harveen Kaur (2017), the Supreme Court held that the 6-month waiting period under Section 13B(2) is directory, not mandatory, and can be waived in appropriate cases.

Report this question

23Under Section 2 of the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939, which ground entitles a Muslim wife to seek divorce?

  1. Husband's cruelty
  2. Husband's failure to maintain for 2 years
  3. Husband imprisoned for 7 years or more
  4. All of the above
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. All of the above

Section 2 of the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939 provides 9 grounds including failure to maintain for 2 years, imprisonment for 7+ years, cruelty, and others.
