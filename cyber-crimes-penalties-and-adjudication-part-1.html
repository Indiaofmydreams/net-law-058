About this question set

14 practice questions with answers

1Section 66A of the IT Act, 2000 was struck down as unconstitutional by the Supreme Court in:

  1. Avnish Bajaj v. State (2005)
  2. Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978)
  3. Bennett Coleman v. Union of India (1972)
  4. Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015)

In Shreya Singhal v. Union of India, decided on 24 March 2015, a Supreme Court bench of Justices Chelameshwar and Nariman struck down Section 66A as unconstitutional.

Report this question

2Section 66A was struck down on the ground that it violated:

  1. Article 14 (Right to Equality)
  2. Article 19(1)(a) (Freedom of Speech and Expression)
  3. Article 21 (Right to Life)
  4. Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Article 19(1)(a) (Freedom of Speech and Expression)

The Supreme Court held that Section 66A was vague, overbroad, and not saved by any of the permissible restrictions under Article 19(2), and therefore violated the fundamental right to freedom of speech and expression under Article 19(1)(a).

Report this question

3In Shreya Singhal v. Union of India, while striking down Section 66A, the Supreme Court:

  1. Upheld Section 69A (blocking of information) and read down Section 79 (intermediary liability)
  2. Also struck down Sections 69A and 79
  3. Struck down the entire IT Act
  4. Referred the matter to a Constitution Bench
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Upheld Section 69A (blocking of information) and read down Section 79 (intermediary liability)

The court struck down only Section 66A, upheld Section 69A (power to block access to information) as constitutionally valid, and read down Section 79 to require that intermediaries act on court orders or government notifications to remove content.

Report this question

4Section 65 of the IT Act, 2000 deals with:

  1. Tampering with computer source documents
  2. Computer-related fraud
  3. Identity theft
  4. Cyber terrorism
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Tampering with computer source documents

Section 65 provides that whoever knowingly or intentionally conceals, destroys, or alters any computer source code required to be kept or maintained by law shall be punished.

Report this question

5The punishment under Section 65 (tampering with computer source documents) is:

  1. Imprisonment up to 1 year or fine up to ₹1 lakh
  2. Imprisonment up to 3 years or fine up to ₹2 lakh or both
  3. Imprisonment up to 5 years or fine up to ₹5 lakh
  4. Imprisonment for life
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Imprisonment up to 3 years or fine up to ₹2 lakh or both

Section 65 prescribes punishment of imprisonment up to 3 years, or a fine up to ₹2,00,000, or both.

Report this question

6Section 66F of the IT Act, 2000 deals with:

  1. Cyber terrorism
  2. Identity theft
  3. Publishing obscene content online
  4. Violation of privacy
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Cyber terrorism

Section 66F criminalises any act with intent to threaten the unity, integrity, security, or sovereignty of India or to strike terror in the people by accessing or causing damage to computer resources that could cause injury to persons or damage to critical infrastructure.

Report this question

7The maximum punishment for cyber terrorism under Section 66F of the IT Act is:

  1. 7 years imprisonment
  2. 10 years imprisonment
  3. 14 years imprisonment
  4. Imprisonment for life
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Imprisonment for life

Section 66F prescribes imprisonment for life as the maximum punishment for cyber terrorism, reflecting the gravity of the offence.

Report this question

8Section 67 of the IT Act, 2000 deals with:

  1. Identity theft
  2. Cyber terrorism
  3. Tampering with source code
  4. Publishing obscene material in electronic form
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Publishing obscene material in electronic form

Section 67 punishes whoever publishes or transmits or causes to be published or transmitted in the electronic form any material which is lascivious or appeals to the prurient interest.

Report this question

9The punishment on the FIRST conviction for publishing obscene material online under Section 67 is:

  1. 1 year and ₹1 lakh
  2. 3 years and ₹5 lakh
  3. 5 years and ₹10 lakh
  4. 7 years and ₹10 lakh
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 3 years and ₹5 lakh

On first conviction under Section 67, the offender may be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to 3 years and a fine which may extend to ₹5 lakh.

Report this question

10Section 66C of the IT Act, 2000 deals with:

  1. Hacking
  2. Child pornography
  3. Violation of privacy
  4. Identity theft (fraudulently using another's electronic signature or password)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Identity theft (fraudulently using another's electronic signature or password)

Section 66C punishes whoever fraudulently or dishonestly makes use of the electronic signature, password, or any other unique identification feature of any other person, with imprisonment up to 3 years and/or fine up to ₹1 lakh.

Report this question

11Section 66E of the IT Act, 2000 deals with:

  1. Cheating by impersonation
  2. Identity theft
  3. Receiving stolen computer resources
  4. Violation of privacy (capturing and publishing private area images)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Violation of privacy (capturing and publishing private area images)

Section 66E punishes whoever intentionally or knowingly captures, publishes or transmits the image of a private area of any person without consent, under circumstances violating the privacy of that person.

Report this question

12Under the IT Act 2000, the Adjudicating Officer has jurisdiction to adjudicate claims up to:

  1. ₹1 crore
  2. ₹10 crore
  3. Unlimited jurisdiction
  4. ₹5 crore
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. ₹5 crore

Section 46 empowers Adjudicating Officers to adjudicate whether any person has committed a contravention of the provisions of this Act — the pecuniary jurisdiction is up to ₹5 crore.

Report this question

13The Cyber Appellate Tribunal (CAT) under the IT Act is established under:

  1. Section 43
  2. Section 46
  3. Section 48
  4. Section 65
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Section 48

Section 48 of the IT Act provides for the establishment of the Cyber Appellate Tribunal to hear appeals from orders of Adjudicating Officers under Section 46.

Report this question

14An appeal against an order of the Cyber Appellate Tribunal under the IT Act lies to:

  1. Supreme Court
  2. District Court
  3. High Court
  4. Sessions Court
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. High Court

Section 62 of the IT Act provides that any person aggrieved by any decision or order of the Cyber Appellate Tribunal may file an appeal to the High Court within 60 days of the communication of the decision or order.
