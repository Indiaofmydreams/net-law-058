About this question set

This set has 24 multiple-choice questions on Concept of Rights and Duties from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 1, Jurisprudence. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 24 of the 24 questions carry a written explanation.

24 practice questions with answers

1Hohfeld's analysis of legal rights is contained in his work:

  1. Fundamental Legal Conceptions as Applied in Judicial Reasoning
  2. The Concept of Law
  3. The Pure Theory of Law
  4. The Morality of Law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Fundamental Legal Conceptions as Applied in Judicial Reasoning

Hohfeld's framework of jural relations was published in Yale Law Journal in 1913 and 1917.

Report this question

2According to Hohfeld, the correlative of a 'right' is:

  1. Privilege
  2. Duty
  3. Power
  4. Immunity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Duty

Right ↔ Duty is a jural correlative: one person's right creates another's duty.

Report this question

3According to Hohfeld, the correlative of a 'privilege' (liberty) is:

  1. Right
  2. Duty
  3. Disability
  4. No-right
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. No-right

Privilege ↔ No-right: If A has a privilege to do X, B has no right that A not do X.

Report this question

4According to Hohfeld, the correlative of 'power' is:

  1. Immunity
  2. Disability
  3. Duty
  4. Liability
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Liability

Power ↔ Liability: A's power to change B's legal position means B has a liability to have their position changed.

Report this question

5According to Hohfeld, the correlative of 'immunity' is:

  1. Liability
  2. No-right
  3. Disability
  4. Duty
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Disability

Immunity ↔ Disability: A's immunity means B has a disability to change A's legal position.

Report this question

6The opposite of 'right' in Hohfeld's analysis is:

  1. No-right
  2. Duty
  3. Privilege
  4. Disability
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. No-right

In Hohfeld's jural opposites table: Right ↔ No-right.

Report this question

7Ihering defined a legal right as:

  1. A command of the sovereign
  2. A power recognised by the state
  3. A duty imposed on others
  4. A legally protected interest
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A legally protected interest

Ihering's Interest Theory: rights are legally protected interests.

Report this question

8Austin defined a legal right as:

  1. A faculty given to persons by law
  2. An interest protected by law
  3. A privilege granted by the sovereign
  4. A power over another person's acts
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A power over another person's acts

Austin: A right is a faculty conferred on one person by another's relative duty.

Report this question

9Which of the following jurists gave the 'Will Theory' of rights?

  1. Ihering
  2. Austin
  3. Savigny and Austin
  4. Salmond
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Savigny and Austin

Will Theory (Savigny and Austin): a right is an expression of individual will/power.

Report this question

10'Perfect rights' and 'imperfect rights' were distinguished by:

  1. Salmond
  2. Holland
  3. Paton
  4. Austin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Austin

Perfect rights = those the law will enforce. Imperfect rights = moral claims not enforceable by law (Austin's classification).

Report this question

11A 'public right' is different from a 'private right' in that:

  1. It vests in the State alone
  2. It cannot be enforced in courts
  3. It has no corresponding duty
  4. It is available to every member of the community
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. It is available to every member of the community

A public right is available to every member of the public (e.g., right to use public roads).

Report this question

12Which of the following is a characteristic of a legal duty according to Salmond?

  1. It must be moral in nature
  2. It is an act recognised by law as obligatory
  3. It must flow from a contract only
  4. It must be owed to the sovereign
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. It is an act recognised by law as obligatory

Salmond: a legal duty is an act or forbearance that is recognised by law as obligatory.

Report this question

13'Jural correlatives' and 'jural opposites' are concepts introduced by:

  1. Salmond
  2. Dworkin
  3. Hohfeld
  4. Hart
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Hohfeld

Hohfeld introduced the framework of jural correlatives and jural opposites.

Report this question

14Human rights are distinguished from legal rights because:

  1. Human rights require legislation
  2. Human rights are inherent and universal, independent of legal recognition
  3. Human rights are enforceable in domestic courts
  4. Human rights apply only to citizens
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Human rights are inherent and universal, independent of legal recognition

Human rights are inherent to all persons by virtue of humanity, regardless of legal recognition.

Report this question

15According to Salmond, a legal right has five essential elements. Which of the following is NOT one of them?

  1. Subject of the right (person of inherence)
  2. Object of the right (subject matter)
  3. Duration of the right
  4. Content of the right (act or forbearance)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Duration of the right

Salmond's five elements: person of inherence, person of incidence, content, subject matter, and title. Duration is not one of them.

Report this question

16'Relative duties' according to Austin are:

  1. Duties owed to the sovereign
  2. Self-regarding duties
  3. Duties that correspond to rights of other persons
  4. Duties owed to God
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Duties that correspond to rights of other persons

Relative duties are those that correspond to and correlate with the legal rights of other persons.

Report this question

17'Absolute duties' according to Austin include:

  1. Duties owed to indefinite persons
  2. Duties correlating to specific rights
  3. Only self-regarding duties
  4. Only duties to the sovereign
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Duties owed to indefinite persons

Austin's absolute duties include duties to God, self-regarding duties, duties to the sovereign, and duties to indefinite persons (public duties).

Report this question

18'In rem' rights are:

  1. Rights available against a specific person
  2. Rights available against the world at large
  3. Rights of property owners only
  4. Rights arising from contract
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Rights available against the world at large

In rem = against all (e.g., property rights, rights of ownership). In personam = against a specific person.

Report this question

19'In personam' rights are:

  1. Rights against the world at large
  2. Rights available against the State
  3. Rights available against all citizens
  4. Rights available against a specific determinate person
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Rights available against a specific determinate person

In personam = rights against a specific determinate person (e.g., contractual rights).

Report this question

20'Positive rights' require the duty-holder to:

  1. Refrain from doing something
  2. Perform an active act or service
  3. Pay compensation only
  4. Surrender property
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Perform an active act or service

Positive rights impose an active duty (e.g., right to education = State must provide schools). Negative rights impose duty to refrain.

Report this question

21'Negative rights' impose on the duty-holder an obligation to:

  1. Perform a positive act
  2. Pay a fine
  3. Render a service
  4. Refrain from interfering with the right-holder
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Refrain from interfering with the right-holder

Negative rights = duty to refrain (e.g., Right to life = duty not to kill).

Report this question

22'Proprietary rights' are rights:

  1. Relating to personal status
  2. Having economic/monetary value (rights over property)
  3. That are non-transferable
  4. Available only to citizens
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Having economic/monetary value (rights over property)

Proprietary rights have economic value (e.g., property rights). Personal rights relate to status and personality.

Report this question

23Hohfeld's analysis was a critique of which problem in legal reasoning?

  1. The ambiguous use of the single word 'right' to mean different things
  2. Excessive reliance on natural law
  3. The lack of codified law
  4. Judicial activism
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The ambiguous use of the single word 'right' to mean different things

Hohfeld showed that 'right' was used loosely to mean right, privilege, power, and immunity — four distinct concepts.

Report this question

24According to Hohfeld, 'immunity' means:

  1. Freedom from a legal duty
  2. A privilege not to act
  3. A right that cannot be waived
  4. Exemption from another's power to change one's legal position
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Exemption from another's power to change one's legal position

Immunity = exemption from another person's power to alter your legal position.
