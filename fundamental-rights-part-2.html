15 practice questions with answers

1The 'Doctrine of Eclipse' in Constitutional Law means:

  1. A law void ab initio becomes valid retrospectively when the constitutional bar is removed
  2. A pre-constitutional law inconsistent with a Fundamental Right becomes dormant/eclipsed (not void) and revives if the inconsistency is removed
  3. A law is eclipsed by a subsequent constitutional amendment
  4. Both A and B
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A pre-constitutional law inconsistent with a Fundamental Right becomes dormant/eclipsed (not void) and revives if the inconsistency is removed

The Doctrine of Eclipse applies to pre-constitutional laws. Such a law is not void ab initio — it is merely eclipsed/dormant to the extent of inconsistency. If the constitutional bar is removed (e.g., by amending the FR), the law revives. Leading case: Bhikhaji Narain Dhakras v. State of MP (1955).

2Article 20 protects against:

  1. Arbitrary detention
  2. Forced labour
  3. Custodial torture
  4. Ex post facto laws, double jeopardy, and self-incrimination
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Ex post facto laws, double jeopardy, and self-incrimination

Article 20 provides triple protection: (1) No ex post facto criminal law — no person convicted of offence which was not an offence when committed; (2) No double jeopardy — no person prosecuted and punished more than once for the same offence; (3) No self-incrimination — no person compelled to be a witness against himself.

3The maxim 'Nemo bis punitur pro eodem delicto' means:

  1. Hear the other side
  2. Act of the court shall not harm anyone
  3. No one shall be a judge in his own cause
  4. No one shall be punished twice for the same offence — relates to double jeopardy (Article 20(2))
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. No one shall be punished twice for the same offence — relates to double jeopardy (Article 20(2))

Nemo bis punitur pro eodem delicto means 'no one shall be punished twice for the same offence.' It is the foundation of the double jeopardy rule in Article 20(2) of the Constitution which provides that no person shall be prosecuted and punished for the same offence more than once.

4In Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan (1997), the Supreme Court:

  1. Declared sexual harassment at workplace as a criminal offence
  2. Ordered Parliament to legislate within 3 months
  3. Laid down guidelines treating sexual harassment at workplace as violation of Articles 14, 19, and 21 — filled the legislative vacuum
  4. Upheld conviction under existing law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Laid down guidelines treating sexual harassment at workplace as violation of Articles 14, 19, and 21 — filled the legislative vacuum

Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan (1997) — the SC laid down the Vishaka Guidelines treating sexual harassment at workplace as a violation of Articles 14, 15, 19(1)(g), and 21. These Guidelines had the force of law until the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013 was enacted.

5In I.R. Coelho v. State of Tamil Nadu (2007), the nine-judge bench held regarding the Ninth Schedule:

  1. All laws in the Ninth Schedule are immune from all Constitutional challenges
  2. The Ninth Schedule is itself unconstitutional
  3. Only property-related laws in Ninth Schedule are protected
  4. Laws before April 24, 1973 are fully protected; laws after that date can be challenged if they violate basic structure or damage FRs forming part of basic structure
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Laws before April 24, 1973 are fully protected; laws after that date can be challenged if they violate basic structure or damage FRs forming part of basic structure

I.R. Coelho v. State of Tamil Nadu (2007) — 9-judge bench held: laws inserted in 9th Schedule before April 24, 1973 (date of Kesavananda Bharati) are fully protected; laws inserted after that date are open to challenge if they violate the basic structure or if fundamental rights forming part of basic structure are abrogated.

6Article 32(4) provides that the right under Article 32 shall NOT be suspended except as otherwise provided by the Constitution. This means:

  1. Art. 32 can be suspended by the Supreme Court
  2. Art. 32 can be suspended only under Art. 359 (National Emergency Presidential Order) — and even then only if Arts. 20 & 21 are not involved
  3. Art. 32 can be suspended by Parliament anytime
  4. Art. 32 cannot be suspended under any circumstances whatsoever
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Art. 32 can be suspended only under Art. 359 (National Emergency Presidential Order) — and even then only if Arts. 20 & 21 are not involved

Article 32(4) provides that the right under Article 32 cannot be suspended except as otherwise provided by the Constitution. The only provision that allows suspension is Article 359 (Presidential Order during National Emergency). But even then, Arts. 20 and 21 cannot be suspended, so the right to enforce them under Art. 32 remains.

7In Sita Soren v. Union of India (2024), the Supreme Court's 7-judge bench overruled P.V. Narasimha Rao v. State (1998) and held:

  1. MPs who take bribes to vote have NO immunity under Article 105 — bribery is not protected by parliamentary privilege
  2. MPs who take bribes to vote are protected under Article 105(2)
  3. Members of State Legislatures are also protected from bribery charges
  4. All parliamentary activities are absolutely protected
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. MPs who take bribes to vote have NO immunity under Article 105 — bribery is not protected by parliamentary privilege

Sita Soren v. Union of India (2024) — a 7-judge constitutional bench unanimously overruled P.V. Narasimha Rao v. State (1998) and held that the immunity under Article 105(2) does not extend to bribery. A Member of Parliament who takes a bribe to vote in a particular manner cannot claim the protection of parliamentary privilege.

8In Zee Telefilms Ltd. v. Union of India (2005), the Supreme Court held that the BCCI:

  1. Is 'State' under Art. 12 because it controls a national sport
  2. Is 'State' only for the purposes of Art. 14
  3. Is not 'State' under Art. 12, though its public-duty actions may still be amenable to writ jurisdiction under Art. 226
  4. Is a statutory body created under the Societies Registration Act and hence 'State'
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Is not 'State' under Art. 12, though its public-duty actions may still be amenable to writ jurisdiction under Art. 226

By a 3:2 majority the BCCI was held not to be 'State', but writs may lie for enforcement of public duties.

9Which of the following Fundamental Rights are expressly available against private individuals also?
1. Art. 15(2) - access to shops, hotels, wells
2. Art. 17 - abolition of untouchability
3. Art. 23 - prohibition of traffic in human beings and forced labour
4. Art. 20(1) - protection against ex post facto laws

  1. 2, 3 and 4 only
  2. 1, 3 and 4 only
  3. 1, 2 and 3 only
  4. 1 and 4 only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1, 2 and 3 only

Art. 20 protects only against State action (conviction under law).

10In Maqbool Hussain v. State of Bombay (1953), the Supreme Court held that confiscation by Sea Customs authorities did NOT bar a later criminal trial under Art. 20(2) because:

  1. Art. 20(2) applies only to civil penalties
  2. The Customs authorities were not a court or judicial tribunal, so there was no 'prosecution and punishment'
  3. The offence was different in character
  4. Double jeopardy applies only to offences under the IPC
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The Customs authorities were not a court or judicial tribunal, so there was no 'prosecution and punishment'

Art. 20(2) requires prosecution and punishment before a court or judicial tribunal.

11Under Art. 22(4), preventive detention beyond ___ requires the report of an Advisory Board (unless Parliament acts under Art. 22(7)). The reduction of this period by the 44th Amendment has:

  1. Six months; been brought into force in 1978
  2. Two months; been in force since 1979
  3. Three months; been replaced by six months by the 44th Amendment
  4. Three months; not been brought into force
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Three months; not been brought into force

The original three-month period still applies as the 44th Amendment's change to two months was never notified.

12Which decision held that the 'right to die' is not part of Art. 21 and overruled P. Rathinam v. Union of India?

  1. Aruna Shanbaug v. Union of India
  2. Maruti Shripati Dubal v. State of Maharashtra
  3. Common Cause v. Union of India
  4. Gian Kaur v. State of Punjab
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Gian Kaur v. State of Punjab

Gian Kaur (1996, 5 judges) upheld s.309 IPC and rejected a right to die; Common Cause (2018) allowed passive euthanasia/living wills.

13The doctrine of 'essential religious practices' was first articulated in:

  1. Commissioner, H.R.E., Madras v. Sri Lakshmindra Thirtha Swamiar of Sri Shirur Mutt
  2. Durgah Committee, Ajmer v. Syed Hussain Ali
  3. Sardar Syedna Taher Saifuddin v. State of Bombay
  4. S.P. Mittal v. Union of India
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Commissioner, H.R.E., Madras v. Sri Lakshmindra Thirtha Swamiar of Sri Shirur Mutt

Shirur Mutt (1954) held that what constitutes an essential part of a religion is to be decided with reference to its doctrines.

14In its 2024 seven-judge decision on the minority status of Aligarh Muslim University, the Supreme Court:

  1. Held AMU is a minority institution as it was established by Muslims
  2. Overruled the reasoning in S. Azeez Basha, holding that an institution does not lose minority character merely because it was incorporated by statute, and left the final determination to a regular Bench
  3. Unanimously reaffirmed S. Azeez Basha v. Union of India
  4. Held Art. 30(1) does not apply to universities
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Overruled the reasoning in S. Azeez Basha, holding that an institution does not lose minority character merely because it was incorporated by statute, and left the final determination to a regular Bench

The decision was 4:3; the test looks to who established the institution and for whom, not mere statutory incorporation.

15State of West Bengal v. Anwar Ali Sarkar (1952) struck down the West Bengal Special Courts Act, 1950 because:

  1. It created courts without legislative competence
  2. It violated the right to counsel under Art. 22
  3. It vested uncontrolled discretion in the Government to refer cases to Special Courts without a guiding policy, violating Art. 14
  4. It authorised ex post facto punishment
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. It vested uncontrolled discretion in the Government to refer cases to Special Courts without a guiding policy, violating Art. 14

The Act lacked an intelligible differentia and a nexus; classification was left to executive discretion.
