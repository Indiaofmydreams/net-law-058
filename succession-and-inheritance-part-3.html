About this question set

This set has 18 multiple-choice questions on Succession and Inheritance from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 7, Family Law. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 18 of the 18 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Family Law notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

18 practice questions with answers

1Under Section 6 of the HSA, 1956 (as amended 2005), if a coparcener dies after September 9, 2005 leaving female heirs entitled to a share, the property shall:

  1. Be frozen until court order
  2. Pass entirely by survivorship to male coparceners
  3. Pass to the government
  4. Devolve by testamentary or intestate succession and NOT by survivorship — a notional partition is effected
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Devolve by testamentary or intestate succession and NOT by survivorship — a notional partition is effected

After the 2005 amendment, on death of a coparcener, their share devolves by intestate or testamentary succession. A notional partition is effected immediately before death to determine the share.

2A Hindu coparcener's 'notional share' under the amended Section 6 of the HSA for the purpose of succession is determined as:

  1. By the will of the senior-most member
  2. By dividing the total joint family property equally among all members
  3. As the entire joint family property
  4. As if a partition had taken place immediately before his death — giving him the share he would have received in such a partition
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. As if a partition had taken place immediately before his death — giving him the share he would have received in such a partition

Section 6 — the interest of a Hindu Mitakshara coparcener is determined as if a partition had taken place immediately before his death.

Report this question

3Under the Hindu Succession Act, 1956, 'Cognates' are:

  1. Relations through males only
  2. Relations through females only, or through both males and females — anyone not connected wholly through males
  3. Adopted relatives
  4. Spouses and their children
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Relations through females only, or through both males and females — anyone not connected wholly through males

Section 3(1)(c) HSA — a person is a cognate of another if related by blood or adoption but not wholly through males. Agnates are defined in Section 3(1)(a).

Report this question

4Under Section 22 of the Hindu Succession Act, 1956, a preferential right to acquire an interest arises in favour of:

  1. The widow only
  2. The eldest son of the deceased
  3. Other Class I heirs, when one of them proposes to transfer his or her interest in the intestate's immovable property or business
  4. The government
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Other Class I heirs, when one of them proposes to transfer his or her interest in the intestate's immovable property or business

Section 22 — where Class I heirs inherit immovable property or a business of the intestate, and one proposes to transfer his/her interest, the other Class I heirs have a preferential right to acquire it.

Report this question

5Under Section 14(2) of the HSA, 1956, if a female Hindu acquires property through a will or gift which specifically restricts her right:

  1. She can convert it to full ownership after 5 years
  2. She must surrender it to the joint family
  3. She takes as a limited owner — Section 14(1) does not override Section 14(2) where the instrument creating the right itself limits it
  4. The restriction is void and she gets full ownership
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. She takes as a limited owner — Section 14(1) does not override Section 14(2) where the instrument creating the right itself limits it

Section 14(2) — when property is acquired by a female under a written instrument (gift, will, decree) that itself imposes restrictions, those restrictions are valid and she does not get absolute ownership.

Report this question

6Under Section 29 of the HSA, 1956, if an intestate Hindu leaves no heir qualified to succeed under the Act, the property devolves upon:

  1. The local Panchayat
  2. Nearest friend of the deceased
  3. The nearest neighbour
  4. The Government
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The Government

Section 29 — Escheat: in default of all heirs, the property of a Hindu dying intestate devolves on the Government.

Report this question

7The rule of preference among Class II heirs under the HSA, 1956 means:

  1. Class II heirs take per stirpes
  2. Class II heirs only take if specified in the will
  3. Among Class II heirs, those in higher entries exclude those in lower entries — Entry I excludes Entry II
  4. Within Class II, all heirs share equally regardless of entry
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Among Class II heirs, those in higher entries exclude those in lower entries — Entry I excludes Entry II

Section 11 — heirs in an earlier entry in the Schedule shall be preferred over those in a later entry; within the same entry they take simultaneously and equally.

Report this question

8Prakash v. Phulavati (2016) held daughters get coparcenary rights only if both father and daughter were alive on September 9, 2005. This was overruled by:

  1. Arunachala Gounder v. Ponnusamy (2022)
  2. Revanasiddappa v. Mallikarjun (2011)
  3. Danamma v. Amar (2018)
  4. Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma (2020)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma (2020)

Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma (2020) — three-judge bench overruled Prakash v. Phulavati; held the right of daughters as coparceners is by birth and does not depend on the father being alive on 9.9.2005.

Report this question

9Under Section 15(2)(a) of the HSA, 1956, property a Hindu woman inherited from her father or mother, where she dies without any son or daughter (including children of a predeceased son or daughter), devolves upon:

  1. Her husband's heirs
  2. The Government
  3. The heirs of her father
  4. Her own sons and daughters
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The heirs of her father

Section 15(2)(a) — in the absence of her own children or their descendants, property inherited from her father or mother goes to the heirs of the father, not to her husband's heirs.

10The Supreme Court in Arunachala Gounder v. Ponnusamy (2022) held that:

  1. A daughter has no right in her father's self-acquired property
  2. The 2005 amendment applies only prospectively
  3. Daughters cannot inherit agricultural land
  4. A daughter can inherit her father's self-acquired and ancestral property even if he died before the HSA 1956 came into force, under Mitakshara principles
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A daughter can inherit her father's self-acquired and ancestral property even if he died before the HSA 1956 came into force, under Mitakshara principles

Arunachala Gounder (2022) — SC held that the daughter of a propositus dying intestate under Mitakshara law would inherit the self-acquired property as a preferential heir even for deaths before HSA 1956.

Report this question

11Under Section 25 of the HSA, 1956, a person who commits or abets murder of the deceased is:

  1. Entitled to only half the share
  2. Entitled to inherit but must pay compensation
  3. Not disqualified if acquitted in criminal court
  4. Disqualified from inheriting from the deceased
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Disqualified from inheriting from the deceased

Section 25 — a person who commits or abets the commission of murder of the deceased shall be disqualified from succeeding to the property of that person.

Report this question

12Under Section 23 of the HSA (before its omission by the 2005 Amendment), a female heir's right to claim partition of a dwelling house was:

  1. Available only after 5 years
  2. Unrestricted and immediate
  3. Available only through court order
  4. Restricted — she could not claim partition until the male heirs chose to divide or ceased to use it as a dwelling
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Restricted — she could not claim partition until the male heirs chose to divide or ceased to use it as a dwelling

Section 23 (now omitted) restricted a female heir from claiming partition of a dwelling house occupied by male heirs until they chose to divide it. This was removed by the 2005 Amendment.

Report this question

13Under the Hindu Succession Act, 1956, a step-mother (who has no surviving children) inherits from a stepson as:

  1. Only if specifically named in a will
  2. She has no right to inherit from a stepson
  3. A Class I heir
  4. A Class II heir — Entry VI (father's widow)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A Class II heir — Entry VI (father's widow)

Class II, Entry VI of the Schedule includes 'father's widow' (i.e., step-mother) and brother's widow.

Report this question

14Under the Hindu Succession Act, 1956, a Hindu male dies intestate leaving only a widow and one son (no other Class I heir). Each takes:

  1. Widow 1/4, son 3/4
  2. Widow 1/2, son 1/2 (one share each)
  3. Widow 1/3, son 2/3
  4. Son takes everything and maintains the widow
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Widow 1/2, son 1/2 (one share each)

Section 10, Rule 1 — widow takes one share, each surviving son takes one share. Since there are 2 Class I heirs, each takes 1/2 of the estate.

15The Hindu Code Bills of 1955-56 were primarily aimed at:

  1. Implementing the Uniform Civil Code under Article 44
  2. Codifying and reforming Hindu personal law to make it uniform and progressive
  3. Abolishing the caste system
  4. Implementing Article 17 (abolition of untouchability)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Codifying and reforming Hindu personal law to make it uniform and progressive

The Hindu Code Bills — HMA, HSA, HMGA, HAMA — were enacted to codify, reform and modernise Hindu personal law by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar as Law Minister.

16Under the Doctrine of Aul (increase) in Muslim inheritance law, when total shares of Quranic heirs exceed the whole estate:

  1. Each sharer's fraction is proportionally reduced
  2. The last-named sharer gets nothing
  3. The residue goes to the State
  4. Only daughters' shares are reduced
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Each sharer's fraction is proportionally reduced

Doctrine of Aul: when the total of all Quranic heirs' shares exceeds 1 (the whole estate), each share is reduced proportionally — akin to paying creditors when debts exceed assets.

Report this question

17The correct order of priority in Sunni Muslim intestate succession is:

  1. Asabat → Dhawul Furud → Dhawul Arham → State
  2. State → Dhawul Arham → Asabat → Dhawul Furud
  3. Dhawul Arham → Dhawul Furud → Asabat → State
  4. Dhawul Furud → Asabat → Dhawul Arham → State
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Dhawul Furud → Asabat → Dhawul Arham → State

Sunni Muslim Inheritance order: (1) Dhawul Furud/Zawil Furud (Quranic sharers), (2) Asabat (residuaries), (3) Dhawul Arham (distant kindred), (4) State (escheat).

Report this question

18Under Sunni Muslim Law, the share of a daughter when she is the only child and there is no son is:

  1. 1/6
  2. 1/2
  3. 2/3
  4. 1/8
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1/2

Quran 4:11 — if there is only one daughter and no son, she takes 1/2 of the estate. If two or more daughters and no son, they together take 2/3.
