About this question set

This set has 21 multiple-choice questions on Commercial Law from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 6, Commercial Law. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 15 of the 21 questions carry a written explanation.

21 practice questions with answers

1Under Section 37(2), where the seller delivers more than he contracted to sell, the buyer may:

  1. Accept the contracted quantity and reject the rest
  2. Reject the whole
  3. Accept the whole and pay for it at the contract rate
  4. Do any of the above
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Do any of the above

The buyer has three options.

Report this question

2Under Section 38(1), unless otherwise agreed, the buyer:

  1. Must accept delivery by instalments
  2. Is not bound to accept delivery by instalments
  3. Must accept only half
  4. Must accept in not more than two instalments
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Is not bound to accept delivery by instalments

Instalment delivery needs the buyer's consent or a contract term.

Report this question

3Which of the following does NOT amount to acceptance under Section 42?

  1. The buyer intimates to the seller that he has accepted the goods
  2. The buyer does an act inconsistent with the seller's ownership
  3. The buyer retains the goods beyond a reasonable time without intimating rejection
  4. The buyer merely examines the goods delivered
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The buyer merely examines the goods delivered

Section 41 gives a reasonable opportunity to examine, which is not acceptance.

Report this question

4Under Section 43, a buyer who rightly refuses to accept the goods delivered:

  1. Is not bound to return them, but must intimate his refusal to accept
  2. Must return them to the seller
  3. Must return them within a week
  4. Must store them for the seller
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Is not bound to return them, but must intimate his refusal to accept

Buyer's protection on rightful rejection.

Report this question

5Consider the following statements on indemnity and guarantee: I. Indemnity involves two parties; guarantee involves three. II. In guarantee, the surety's liability is secondary, arising on the principal debtor's default. III. A guarantee presupposes an existing or promised debt or duty of the principal debtor.

  1. I only
  2. I and II only
  3. II and III only
  4. I, II and III
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. I, II and III

All three statements are correct.

Report this question

6In Osman Jamal & Co. v. Gopal Purshottam, the Calcutta High Court held that:

  1. Sections 124-125 exhaust the law of indemnity in India
  2. An indemnity must always be in writing
  3. Indemnity is limited to loss caused by the promisor
  4. Sections 124-125 are not exhaustive, and equitable principles of indemnity can apply
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Sections 124-125 are not exhaustive, and equitable principles of indemnity can apply

Indemnity is not confined to the narrow wording of s.124.

Report this question

7Which of the following is NOT treated as a contract of indemnity?

  1. Fire insurance
  2. Marine insurance
  3. Life insurance
  4. Motor insurance
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Life insurance

Life insurance is a contingent contract to pay a sum on an event, not to make good a loss.

Report this question

8An example of an implied contract of indemnity under the Act is:

  1. A bailee's duty to pay hire
  2. A finder's duty to take care of goods
  3. A pawnee's duty to sell after notice
  4. The principal's duty to indemnify the agent for lawful acts done in exercising his authority (s.222)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The principal's duty to indemnify the agent for lawful acts done in exercising his authority (s.222)

Section 222 creates an implied indemnity in agency.

Report this question

9An essential element of a valid pledge is:

  1. Registration
  2. A written deed
  3. Transfer of ownership
  4. Delivery of possession of the goods, actual or constructive
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Delivery of possession of the goods, actual or constructive

Handing over documents of title or keys of a godown can be constructive delivery.

Report this question

10Under Section 173, the pawnee may retain the pledged goods for:

  1. The debt or promise, interest on the debt, and necessary expenses incurred for possession or preservation
  2. Any unrelated debt owed by the pawnor
  3. The principal debt only
  4. Future advances only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The debt or promise, interest on the debt, and necessary expenses incurred for possession or preservation

Section 174 covers retention for other debts.

Report this question

11Under Section 175, the pawnee is entitled to receive from the pawnor:

  1. Nothing beyond the debt
  2. Compensation for loss of profits
  3. A fixed penalty
  4. Extraordinary expenses incurred by him for the preservation of the goods pledged
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Extraordinary expenses incurred by him for the preservation of the goods pledged

Covers expenses beyond the ordinary.

Report this question

12Under Section 177, a pawnor who has defaulted may redeem the goods:

  1. Only before the date fixed for payment
  2. Only with the court's leave
  3. Never
  4. At any subsequent time before actual sale, on paying the debt, interest, and expenses incurred because of the default
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. At any subsequent time before actual sale, on paying the debt, interest, and expenses incurred because of the default

Pawnor's right of redemption after default.

Report this question

13Under Section 178, a pledge made by a mercantile agent in possession of goods with the owner's consent, acting in the ordinary course of business, is valid if the pawnee:

  1. Acts in good faith and has no notice that the pawnor lacks authority
  2. Knows of the lack of authority
  3. Is the agent's relative
  4. Pays in cash
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Acts in good faith and has no notice that the pawnor lacks authority

An exception to nemo dat.

Report this question

14Under Section 179, where the pawnor has only a limited interest in the goods pledged and the pledge is made:

  1. The pledge is valid only to the extent of that interest
  2. The pledge is wholly void
  3. The pledge is voidable by the true owner
  4. The pledge is valid for the full value
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The pledge is valid only to the extent of that interest

Pawnee gets only what the pawnor could validly give.

Report this question

15Under Section 180, if a third person wrongfully deprives the bailee or injures the goods:

  1. Only the bailor may sue
  2. Only the bailee may sue
  3. Neither may sue
  4. Either the bailor or the bailee may sue the wrongdoer, with relief apportioned under s.181
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Either the bailor or the bailee may sue the wrongdoer, with relief apportioned under s.181

Remedy same as if the wrong had been done to the plaintiff.

Report this question

16Under Section 135(1), CSR provisions apply to a company having, in the immediately preceding financial year:

  1. Net worth of Rs. 100 crore or turnover of Rs. 500 crore or net profit of Rs. 1 crore
  2. Net worth of Rs. 500 crore or turnover of Rs. 1,000 crore or net profit of Rs. 5 crore
  3. Net worth of Rs. 1,000 crore and turnover of Rs. 5,000 crore
  4. Net worth of Rs. 50 crore or net profit of Rs. 1 crore
Show answer

Answer: B. Net worth of Rs. 500 crore or turnover of Rs. 1,000 crore or net profit of Rs. 5 crore

Report this question

17Consider the following on CSR spending: I. A company must spend at least 2% of the average net profits of the three immediately preceding financial years. II. Administrative overheads must not exceed 5% of total CSR expenditure for the year. III. CSR activities must be undertaken in India.

  1. I only
  2. I and II only
  3. II and III only
  4. I, II and III
Show answer

Answer: D. I, II and III

Report this question

18Under Section 135 and the CSR Rules, the CSR Committee must consist of:

  1. Two directors, including the managing director
  2. Five directors, all independent
  3. Three or more directors, including at least one independent director; not required where the CSR obligation does not exceed Rs. 50 lakh
  4. The Board chairman alone
Show answer

Answer: C. Three or more directors, including at least one independent director; not required where the CSR obligation does not exceed Rs. 50 lakh

Report this question

19For an ongoing CSR project, the unspent amount must be transferred to the Unspent CSR Account within ___ from the end of the financial year, and spent within ___ financial years:

  1. 60 days; two
  2. Six months; one
  3. One year; five
  4. 30 days; three
Show answer

Answer: D. 30 days; three

Report this question

20Which of the following is eligible as a CSR activity under Schedule VII?

  1. Rural development projects
  2. Contribution to a political party
  3. Activities exclusively for the benefit of the company's employees
  4. Activities carried out outside India
Show answer

Answer: A. Rural development projects

Report this question

21Under Section 135, for failure to transfer unspent CSR amounts, the company is liable to a penalty of:

  1. Rs. 10,000 only
  2. Twice the amount required to be transferred or Rs. 1 crore, whichever is less
  3. Imprisonment of 3 years
  4. The amount unspent only
Show answer

Answer: B. Twice the amount required to be transferred or Rs. 1 crore, whichever is less
