About this question set

16 practice questions with answers

1Which of the following is NOT a 'neighbouring right' (related right)?

  1. Rights of performers
  2. Rights of authors of literary works
  3. Rights of broadcasting organisations
  4. Rights of producers of phonograms
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Rights of authors of literary works

Neighbouring (related) rights protect performers, producers of phonograms, and broadcasting organisations. Authors' rights are copyright, not neighbouring rights.

2Amar Nath Sehgal v. Union of India (2005) — Delhi High Court — is a landmark case on:

  1. Patent infringement
  2. Trademark passing off
  3. Geographical Indication protection
  4. Moral rights of an author (integrity right)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Moral rights of an author (integrity right)

In Amar Nath Sehgal v. Union of India, the Delhi High Court recognised and enforced the moral right of integrity under Section 57 of the Copyright Act when the government destroyed the sculptor's mural.

Report this question

3R.G. Anand v. M/s Deluxe Films (1978) is a landmark case on:

  1. Patent infringement
  2. Idea-expression dichotomy in films
  3. Trademark passing off
  4. Geographical Indication protection
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Idea-expression dichotomy in films

In R.G. Anand v. Deluxe Films, the Supreme Court of India laid down the principle that no copyright can be claimed in ideas, subject matter, themes, or plots — only in the specific expression thereof.

Report this question

4Under Section 19 of the Copyright Act, 1957, an assignment of copyright is valid only if it is:

  1. In writing and signed by the assignor or his authorised agent
  2. Registered with the Copyright Office
  3. Published in the Official Gazette
  4. Attested by a notary
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. In writing and signed by the assignor or his authorised agent

Section 19 mandates that every assignment of the copyright in a work shall be in writing signed by the assignor or by his duly authorised agent.

Report this question

5Under Section 19A of the Copyright Act (inserted by 2012 Amendment), if the assignee does not exercise his rights under the assignment within ________, the assignment shall be deemed to have lapsed:

  1. 6 months
  2. 3 years
  3. 1 year
  4. 5 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1 year

Section 19A(2) provides that if the assignee fails to exercise the assigned rights within one year from the date of assignment, the assignment in respect of those rights shall be deemed to have lapsed.

Report this question

6The 'Broadcast Reproduction Right' under Section 37 of the Copyright Act subsists for:

  1. 25 years from the year in which the broadcast is made
  2. 50 years from the year of broadcast
  3. 60 years from the year of broadcast
  4. Life of the broadcaster + 50 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 25 years from the year in which the broadcast is made

Section 37(2) of the Copyright Act provides that broadcast reproduction rights in a broadcast shall subsist for 25 years from the beginning of the calendar year next following the year in which the broadcast is made.

Report this question

7The duration of a 'Performer's Right' under Section 38 of the Copyright Act, 1957 (post-2012) is:

  1. 25 years from the year of performance
  2. 50 years from the year of performance
  3. 60 years from the year of performance
  4. Life of performer + 60 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 50 years from the year of performance

After the 2012 amendment, Section 38(3) provides that performer's rights shall subsist for 50 years from the beginning of the calendar year next following the year in which the performance is made.

Report this question

8Section 38B of the Copyright Act, 1957 (inserted by 2012 Amendment) provides:

  1. Moral rights of performers (paternity and integrity)
  2. Economic rights of performers
  3. Duration of performers' rights
  4. Compulsory licensing for broadcasting organisations
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Moral rights of performers (paternity and integrity)

Section 38B, inserted by the 2012 Amendment, grants performers the moral rights of paternity (right to claim authorship of the performance) and integrity (right to restrain or claim damages for any distortion of the performance).

9Assertion (A): Once a performer consents by written agreement to incorporation of his performance in a cinematograph film, he cannot thereafter object to the film producer's use of the performance. Reason (R): Section 38 of the Copyright Act provides that after giving written consent for a film, the performer's rights in that performance are deemed assigned to the film producer.

  1. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are correct but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is correct but R is incorrect
  4. Both A and R are incorrect
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A

Section 38(4) provides that where a performer has, by written agreement, consented to the incorporation of his performance in a cinematograph film, the performer shall not object to the enjoyment of rights in the film by the producer.

Report this question

10Civil remedies for copyright infringement available under Section 55 of the Copyright Act include:

  1. Only damages
  2. Only injunction
  3. Only account of profits and delivery up
  4. Injunction, damages, account of profits, and delivery up
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Injunction, damages, account of profits, and delivery up

Section 55 provides that the copyright owner may bring a civil suit and obtain an injunction, damages, accounts of profits, and delivery-up of infringing copies.

Report this question

11Section 31D of the Copyright Act (inserted 2012) deals with:

  1. Statutory licence for broadcasting literary, musical works and sound recordings
  2. Compulsory licence for works withheld from public
  3. Assignment of copyright in government works
  4. Moral rights in unpublished works
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Statutory licence for broadcasting literary, musical works and sound recordings

Section 31D provides a statutory licence enabling any broadcasting organisation to communicate to the public any published literary or musical work or sound recording on payment of royalties.

Report this question

12The 'secondary liability' for copyright infringement includes:

  1. Direct infringement only
  2. Contributory infringement and vicarious liability
  3. Civil and criminal liability only
  4. Only liability under Section 63 of the Copyright Act
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Contributory infringement and vicarious liability

Secondary liability arises where one party facilitates or benefits from another's infringement. It includes contributory infringement (knowingly causing/contributing to infringement) and vicarious liability (right and ability to control infringer + financial benefit).

Report this question

13Under Section 15 of the Copyright Act, 1957, copyright in a design that is registered under the Designs Act:

  1. Ceases as soon as the design is registered or reproduced industrially more than 50 times
  2. Coexists with design protection indefinitely
  3. Lasts for life of designer + 60 years
  4. Is extended to 25 years automatically
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Ceases as soon as the design is registered or reproduced industrially more than 50 times

Section 15 provides that copyright shall not subsist under this Act in any design which is registered under the Designs Act, 2000, or which, not being so registered, is capable of being so registered and has been applied to an article more than fifty times by an industrial process.

Report this question

14Which of the following correctly states the scope of 'fair dealing' under Section 52(1)(a) of the Copyright Act, 1957?

  1. Any reproduction of a copyrighted work for any purpose
  2. Reproduction for the purpose of research, private study, criticism, or review
  3. Public performance of a copyrighted work without licence
  4. Any use of a copyrighted work by a government authority
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Reproduction for the purpose of research, private study, criticism, or review

Section 52(1)(a) permits fair dealing with a literary, dramatic, musical or artistic work for the purpose of research or private study, criticism or review of that or any other work.

Report this question

15The 'Doctrine of Merger' in copyright law provides that:

  1. Copyright in a work merges with the patent on the underlying invention
  2. Copyright merges into trademark upon registration
  3. When there is only one or very few ways to express an idea, expression merges with the idea and gets no copyright protection
  4. Co-authors' rights merge into a single copyright on publication
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. When there is only one or very few ways to express an idea, expression merges with the idea and gets no copyright protection

The Merger Doctrine holds that when there are only a limited number of ways to express an idea, the idea and expression 'merge', and the expression cannot receive copyright protection (as it would effectively protect the underlying idea).

Report this question

16Under which provision can a compulsory licence be granted for a work that has been withheld from the public?

  1. Section 18
  2. Section 30
  3. Section 31
  4. Section 52
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Section 31

Section 31 of the Copyright Act allows any person to apply to the Copyright Board / Appellate Board for a compulsory licence to republish or perform in public a literary, dramatic, musical or artistic work if the owner of copyright has refused to republish or allow the republication and such refusal is unreasonable.
