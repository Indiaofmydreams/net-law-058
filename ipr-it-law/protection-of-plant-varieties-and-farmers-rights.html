9 practice questions with answers

1India's Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act, 2001 was enacted as a:

  1. Patent system for plant varieties
  2. Sui generis system permitted under TRIPS Article 27(3)(b)
  3. System identical to the UPOV Convention
  4. Extension of the Biological Diversity Act, 2002
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Sui generis system permitted under TRIPS Article 27(3)(b)

TRIPS Article 27(3)(b) allows member states to provide protection for plant varieties either by patents, or by an effective sui generis system, or by any combination. India chose to enact its own sui generis system through the PPV&FR Act, 2001.

2Under TRIPS Article 27(3)(b), member states MAY exclude from patentability:

  1. All biological inventions
  2. Plants and animals (other than micro-organisms) and essentially biological processes
  3. Pharmaceutical inventions
  4. Computer programs
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Plants and animals (other than micro-organisms) and essentially biological processes

TRIPS Article 27(3)(b) permits, but does not require, members to exclude from patentability plants, animals and essentially biological processes for the production of plants or animals (other than non-biological and microbiological processes).

3The essential criteria for registration of a plant variety under the PPV&FR Act, 2001 are:

  1. Distinctiveness, Utility, Stability
  2. Novelty, Originality, Utility, Non-obviousness
  3. Creativity, Uniformity, Stability, Commercial value
  4. Novelty, Distinctiveness, Uniformity, Stability
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Novelty, Distinctiveness, Uniformity, Stability

Under the PPV&FR Act, a variety must satisfy the DUS criteria (Distinctiveness, Uniformity, Stability) plus the requirement of Novelty (NDUS) to be eligible for registration.

4Under the PPV&FR Act, 2001, which of the following is correct regarding Farmers' Rights?

  1. Farmers may sell branded seed of a protected variety freely
  2. Farmers have no rights once a variety is registered by a breeder
  3. Farmers can register any traditional variety as their own variety
  4. Farmers may save, use, sow, re-sow, exchange, share, or sell farm produce of a protected variety but cannot sell branded seed of that variety
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Farmers may save, use, sow, re-sow, exchange, share, or sell farm produce of a protected variety but cannot sell branded seed of that variety

Section 39(1)(iv) of the PPV&FR Act grants farmers the right to save, use, sow, resow, exchange, share or sell their farm produce including seed of a variety protected under the Act, except that farmers shall not sell such seed as branded seed of the protected variety.

5The term of protection for a newly bred (non-tree/vine) variety under the PPV&FR Act, 2001 is:

  1. 10 years
  2. 15 years
  3. 18 years
  4. 20 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 15 years

Under Section 24(6) of the PPV&FR Act, the duration of protection for a new variety is 15 years from the date of registration (18 years for trees and vines).

6The term of protection for trees and vines under the PPV&FR Act, 2001 is:

  1. 15 years
  2. 18 years
  3. 20 years
  4. 25 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 18 years

Section 24(6) of the PPV&FR Act provides that the duration of protection for trees and vines is 18 years from the date of registration.

7The authority that administers the PPV&FR Act, 2001 in India is:

  1. Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Authority (PPV&FRA), New Delhi
  2. National Biodiversity Authority, Chennai
  3. CGPDTM, Mumbai
  4. Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Authority (PPV&FRA), New Delhi

The Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Authority (PPV&FRA) is the statutory body established under the PPV&FR Act, 2001, headquartered in New Delhi.

8India's approach under the PPV&FR Act differs from UPOV primarily because:

  1. India does not provide any breeder's rights
  2. India requires patent registration for all plant varieties
  3. India provides farmers' rights in addition to breeders' rights — an element absent in UPOV
  4. India provides indefinite protection for plant varieties
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. India provides farmers' rights in addition to breeders' rights — an element absent in UPOV

Unlike UPOV, which focuses primarily on plant breeders' rights, India's PPV&FR Act additionally recognises and protects farmers' rights — including the right to save, use and sell farm-saved seed, and community rights over traditional plant genetic resources.

9The International Convention for the Protection of New Varieties of Plants (UPOV Convention) is relevant in India in the context of:

  1. Copyright in agricultural works
  2. Biological Diversity Act, 2002
  3. Patents Act, 1970 — Section 3(j)
  4. Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act, 2001
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act, 2001

While India is not a member of UPOV, the PPV&FR Act, 2001 was influenced by UPOV concepts (breeder's rights, DUS criteria) while departing from it significantly by incorporating farmers' rights and community rights.
