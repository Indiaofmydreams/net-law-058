19 practice questions with answers

1Under the Patents Act 1970, Section 2(1)(j) defines:

  1. Inventive step
  2. Patentee
  3. Prior art
  4. Invention
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Invention

Section 2(1)(j) defines 'invention' as a new product or process involving an inventive step and capable of industrial application.

2Section 2(1)(ja) of the Patents Act, 1970 defines:

  1. Invention
  2. Industrial application
  3. Prior art
  4. Inventive step
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Inventive step

Section 2(1)(ja), inserted by the 2002 amendment, defines 'inventive step' as a feature of an invention that involves technical advance as compared to existing knowledge or economic significance and makes the invention not obvious to a person skilled in the art.

3Section 25 of the Patents Act, 1970 deals with:

  1. Pre-grant and post-grant opposition
  2. Revocation of patent by the High Court
  3. Compulsory licensing
  4. Rights of the patentee
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Pre-grant and post-grant opposition

Section 25 provides for opposition to grant of patent — Section 25(1) deals with pre-grant opposition (any person may oppose before grant) and Section 25(2) deals with post-grant opposition (any interested person may oppose within 12 months of publication of grant).

4Section 39 of the Patents Act, 1970 deals with:

  1. Compulsory licensing
  2. Government's right to use an invention
  3. Revocation of patent
  4. Residents not to apply for patents outside India without prior permission
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Residents not to apply for patents outside India without prior permission

Section 39 prohibits a person resident in India from making an application for a patent outside India without first filing an application in India and obtaining prior permission of the Controller, unless 6 weeks have elapsed since Indian filing.

5Section 100 of the Patents Act, 1970 deals with:

  1. Compulsory licensing
  2. Power of Central Government to use inventions for purposes of government
  3. Post-grant opposition
  4. Revocation by Controller
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Power of Central Government to use inventions for purposes of government

Section 100 empowers the Central Government to use any patented invention for the purposes of the government at any time after the patent has been granted, subject to payment of adequate remuneration to the patentee.

6Under the Trade Marks Act 1999, Section 9 deals with:

  1. Relative grounds for refusal
  2. Infringement of registered trademark
  3. Well-known trademarks
  4. Absolute grounds for refusal of registration
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Absolute grounds for refusal of registration

Section 9 of the Trade Marks Act sets out the absolute grounds for refusal of registration — these are intrinsic defects in the mark itself, such as lack of distinctiveness, descriptiveness, deceptiveness, or being contrary to public morality.

Report this question

7Under the Trade Marks Act 1999, Section 11 deals with:

  1. Absolute grounds for refusal
  2. Relative grounds for refusal of registration
  3. Assignment of trademark
  4. Criminal remedies for infringement
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Relative grounds for refusal of registration

Section 11 of the Trade Marks Act sets out the relative grounds for refusal — these arise when the mark is similar or identical to an earlier trademark for similar or identical goods/services causing likelihood of confusion.

Report this question

8Section 29 of the Trade Marks Act, 1999 deals with:

  1. Registration of trademarks
  2. Assignment of trademarks
  3. Passing off
  4. Infringement of registered trademarks
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Infringement of registered trademarks

Section 29 defines the acts that constitute infringement of a registered trademark, including use of an identical/similar mark for identical/similar goods/services without the consent of the registered proprietor.

Report this question

9Section 134 of the Trade Marks Act, 1999 deals with:

  1. Criminal remedies for trademark infringement
  2. Assignment of registered trademark
  3. Well-known trademarks
  4. Jurisdiction of courts in suits for infringement
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Jurisdiction of courts in suits for infringement

Section 134 provides that no suit for infringement of trademark or passing off shall be instituted in any court inferior to a District Court having jurisdiction to try the suit. It also provides for special jurisdiction — suit may be filed where the plaintiff resides/carries on business.

10The term 'copyright' in the Copyright Act 1957 is defined in Section:

  1. Section 2
  2. Section 13
  3. Section 14
  4. Section 17
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Section 14

Section 14 of the Copyright Act defines 'copyright' by enumerating the exclusive rights that the copyright owner has in respect of each category of work (literary, dramatic, musical, artistic, film, sound recording).

11The term 'author' in relation to a cinematograph film under the Copyright Act 1957 means:

  1. The director
  2. The screenplay writer
  3. The lead actor
  4. The producer
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The producer

Section 2(d)(v) of the Copyright Act defines 'author' in relation to a cinematograph film as the producer of the film.

Report this question

12The term 'author' in relation to a sound recording under the Copyright Act 1957 means:

  1. The producer of the sound recording
  2. The singer
  3. The lyricist
  4. The music director
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The producer of the sound recording

Section 2(d)(vi) defines 'author' in relation to any sound recording as the producer of the sound recording.

13Under Section 22 of the Copyright Act 1957, the term of copyright for a literary work is:

  1. Life of author plus 60 years
  2. 50 years from publication
  3. 60 years from the year of publication
  4. Life of author plus 50 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Life of author plus 60 years

Section 22 provides that the term of copyright for literary, dramatic, musical and artistic works shall subsist until 60 years from the beginning of the calendar year next following the year in which the author dies.

Report this question

14Under which Section of the Copyright Act 1957 does the government hold copyright in government works?

  1. Section 17(a)
  2. Section 17(b)
  3. Section 17(d)
  4. Section 17(cc)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Section 17(cc)

Section 17(dd) (as amended) provides that in the case of a work made or published by or under the direction or control of any public undertaking, the public undertaking shall be the first owner of copyright. Government works are covered by proviso (a) to Section 17.

Report this question

15Section 16 of the Copyright Act, 1957 embodies which important doctrine?

  1. Doctrine of Fair Use
  2. Doctrine of Exhaustion
  3. Doctrine of Merger
  4. Idea-Expression Dichotomy
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Idea-Expression Dichotomy

Section 16 provides that no person shall be entitled to copyright or any similar right in any work otherwise than under and in accordance with the provisions of this Act — impliedly codifying that copyright does not protect ideas, only their specific expression.

16Section 31C of the Copyright Act (inserted 2012) deals with:

  1. Statutory licence for broadcasting
  2. Compulsory licence for unpublished works
  3. Assignment of copyright in films
  4. Statutory licence for cover versions of musical works
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Statutory licence for cover versions of musical works

Section 31C provides for statutory licence for cover versions — any person may make a cover version of a sound recording of any literary or musical work after 5 years from the year of first publication, on payment of royalties.

Report this question

17Under the GI Act 1999, the term of registration of a geographical indication is:

  1. 7 years, indefinitely renewable
  2. 10 years, indefinitely renewable
  3. 15 years, renewable once
  4. 20 years, not renewable
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 10 years, indefinitely renewable

Under the GI Act, 1999, the period of registration of a geographical indication is 10 years and it may be renewed from time to time for periods of 10 years each.

Report this question

18Under the GI Act 1999, who can file an application for registration of a GI?

  1. Any association of persons, producers, organisation or authority established by law
  2. Any individual producer
  3. Only the Central Government
  4. Only state government bodies
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Any association of persons, producers, organisation or authority established by law

Section 11 of the GI Act allows any association of persons or producers or any organisation or authority established by or under any law for the time being in force representing the interest of the producers of the concerned goods to apply for GI registration.

Report this question

19Under Section 84(1) of the Patents Act 1970, which of the following is NOT a ground for compulsory licensing?

  1. Reasonable requirements of the public not satisfied
  2. Patented invention not available at a reasonably affordable price
  3. Patented invention not worked in India
  4. The patent was obtained fraudulently
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The patent was obtained fraudulently

Fraudulent procurement of a patent is a ground for revocation under Section 64, not compulsory licensing under Section 84. The three grounds for compulsory licensing under Section 84 are: (a) public requirements not satisfied; (b) not available at reasonably affordable price; (c) not worked in India.
