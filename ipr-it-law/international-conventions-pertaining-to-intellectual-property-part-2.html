About this question set

14 practice questions with answers

1Arrange the following conventions in chronological order: (i) TRIPS Agreement (ii) Paris Convention (iii) Berne Convention (iv) PCT

  1. iii, ii, iv, i
  2. ii, iv, iii, i
  3. i, ii, iii, iv
  4. ii, iii, iv, i
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. ii, iii, iv, i

Paris Convention (1883) → Berne Convention (1886) → PCT (1970) → TRIPS Agreement (1994).

Report this question

2The 'national treatment' principle in IP treaties means:

  1. Protection only for nationals of a country
  2. Each country applies the same IP law uniformly
  3. A member state must give foreigners the same IP protection it gives its own nationals
  4. IP law applies nationally, not internationally
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A member state must give foreigners the same IP protection it gives its own nationals

National treatment requires each member state to accord to nationals of other member states treatment no less favourable than it accords to its own nationals.

Report this question

3The Rome Convention (1961) protects the rights of:

  1. Authors of literary works
  2. Inventors
  3. Performers, producers of phonograms, and broadcasting organisations
  4. Geographical indication holders
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Performers, producers of phonograms, and broadcasting organisations

The International Convention for the Protection of Performers, Producers of Phonograms and Broadcasting Organisations (Rome Convention, 1961) is the foundational treaty for neighbouring rights.

4The Beijing Treaty on Audiovisual Performances was adopted in:

  1. 2010
  2. 2012
  3. 2013
  4. 2015
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 2012

The Beijing Treaty on Audiovisual Performances was adopted by the Diplomatic Conference at Beijing on 26 June 2012.

5The 'Doha Declaration on TRIPS and Public Health' (2001) affirmed that:

  1. TRIPS should be interpreted to extend patent protection in all circumstances
  2. Compulsory licensing is prohibited under TRIPS
  3. Pharmaceutical patents cannot be granted in developing countries
  4. TRIPS should be interpreted to support the right of WTO members to protect public health and promote access to medicines
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. TRIPS should be interpreted to support the right of WTO members to protect public health and promote access to medicines

The Doha Declaration confirmed that TRIPS does not and should not prevent members from taking measures to protect public health, and reaffirmed the right to grant compulsory licences.

Report this question

6Paragraph 6 of the Doha Declaration (implemented through TRIPS Amendment Article 31bis) addresses:

  1. Geographical indication disputes
  2. Protection of traditional knowledge under TRIPS
  3. Extension of patent terms for pharmaceuticals
  4. The right of countries with insufficient manufacturing capacity to import generic drugs through compulsory licensing
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The right of countries with insufficient manufacturing capacity to import generic drugs through compulsory licensing

The Paragraph 6 mechanism (Article 31bis) allows countries with insufficient manufacturing capacity to import generic medicines produced under compulsory licence in another country.

Report this question

7The Madrid Agreement (1891) and Madrid Protocol (1989) relate to:

  1. International protection of patents
  2. International registration of trademarks
  3. International protection of geographical indications
  4. International filing of industrial designs
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. International registration of trademarks

The Madrid System (Madrid Agreement and Madrid Protocol) provides the mechanism for international registration of trademarks, administered by WIPO.

Report this question

8The 'minimum rights' principle under the Berne Convention means:

  1. Countries must provide at least the rights specified in the Convention but may provide more
  2. Countries can provide less protection than the Convention requires
  3. All member countries must have identical copyright laws
  4. Authors have only the minimum rights they negotiate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Countries must provide at least the rights specified in the Convention but may provide more

The Berne Convention sets minimum standards of protection. Member states are free to provide higher levels of protection ('TRIPS-plus' in WTO terminology).

Report this question

9The WIPO Performances and Phonograms Treaty (WPPT, 1996) grants rights to:

  1. Performers and producers of phonograms
  2. Authors of literary works
  3. Broadcasting organisations
  4. Authors of cinematograph films
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Performers and producers of phonograms

The WPPT (1996) provides protection to performers (in audio performances) and producers of phonograms in the digital environment.

Report this question

10The Geneva Convention for the Protection of Producers of Phonograms (1971) protects:

  1. Authors of musical compositions
  2. Performers of musical works
  3. Producers of phonograms against unauthorised duplication
  4. Broadcasting organisations
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Producers of phonograms against unauthorised duplication

The Geneva Phonograms Convention (1971) protects producers of phonograms against the making, importation, and distribution of duplicate copies without their consent.

Report this question

11The Budapest Treaty on the International Recognition of the Deposit of Microorganisms (1977) allows:

  1. A single deposit at an International Depositary Authority to satisfy national deposit requirements for patent purposes
  2. Patenting of microorganisms in all member states automatically
  3. Protection of microorganisms as geographical indications
  4. Biosafety protocols for genetically modified organisms
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A single deposit at an International Depositary Authority to satisfy national deposit requirements for patent purposes

The Budapest Treaty allows inventors to deposit a microorganism at one recognized IDA, and that single deposit is recognised for patent procedures in all member states.

Report this question

12Match the following TRIPS provisions with their subject matter: (i) Article 22 → (ii) Article 27 → (iii) Article 33 → (iv) Article 39

  1. i-Patents, ii-GI, iii-Trade secrets, iv-Term of patent
  2. i-Trade secrets, ii-Term of patent, iii-GI, iv-Patents
  3. i-Geographical Indications general, ii-Patents patentable subject matter, iii-Term of patent, iv-Trade secrets
  4. i-Term of patent, ii-Trade secrets, iii-Patents, iv-GI
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. i-Geographical Indications general, ii-Patents patentable subject matter, iii-Term of patent, iv-Trade secrets

TRIPS Article 22 = GI general protection; Article 27 = patentable subject matter; Article 33 = 20-year patent term; Article 39 = undisclosed information/trade secrets.

Report this question

13Under TRIPS, which provision specifically deals with the protection of undisclosed information (trade secrets)?

  1. Article 22
  2. Article 27
  3. Article 39
  4. Article 40
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Article 39

TRIPS Article 39 requires protection of undisclosed information (trade secrets), specifying that such information must be secret, have commercial value because it is secret, and be subject to reasonable steps to keep it secret.

Report this question

14The 'exhaustion of rights' doctrine (first-sale doctrine) in international IP trade is reflected in which provision of TRIPS?

  1. Article 6
  2. Article 22
  3. Article 27
  4. Article 33
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Article 6

TRIPS Article 6 leaves member states free to determine their own exhaustion regime (national, regional, or international), reflecting the first-sale/exhaustion of rights doctrine.
