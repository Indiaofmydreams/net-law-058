13 practice questions with answers

1Section 72 of the IT Act, 2000 deals with:

  1. Cyber terrorism
  2. Child pornography
  3. Identity theft
  4. Breach of confidentiality and privacy
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Breach of confidentiality and privacy

Section 72 punishes any person who, in pursuance of any of the powers conferred under the Act, has secured access to any electronic record, book, register, etc., and discloses such information to any other person — breach of confidentiality and privacy.

2Section 70 of the IT Act, 2000 deals with:

  1. Surveillance by government
  2. Blocking of websites
  3. E-governance
  4. Unauthorised access to protected systems
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Unauthorised access to protected systems

Section 70 empowers the Central Government to declare any computer resource as a 'protected system' and makes it an offence for any person to access or secure access to such protected system without authorisation.

3Section 69 of the IT Act, 2000 empowers the government to:

  1. Intercept, monitor, or decrypt any information
  2. Block websites
  3. Appoint Adjudicating Officers
  4. License Certifying Authorities
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Intercept, monitor, or decrypt any information

Section 69 empowers the Central Government or State Government to direct any agency to intercept, monitor, or decrypt or cause to be intercepted, monitored, or decrypted any information transmitted through any computer resource in the interests of sovereignty and integrity of India, etc.

4Section 69A of the IT Act, 2000 empowers the government to:

  1. Monitor electronic communications
  2. Prosecute cyber offenders
  3. Issue Digital Signature Certificates
  4. Issue directions to block public access to information on specified grounds
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Issue directions to block public access to information on specified grounds

Section 69A empowers the Central Government to direct any agency or intermediary to block access by the public to any information through any computer resource for reasons relating to sovereignty and integrity of India, defence, security, friendly relations with foreign states, or public order.

5Section 79 of the IT Act, 2000 (as read down in Shreya Singhal) provides:

  1. Criminal liability for intermediaries for all third-party content
  2. Mandatory monitoring of all online content by intermediaries
  3. Strict liability for social media platforms
  4. Safe harbour/exemption from liability for intermediaries if they comply with court/government takedown orders
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Safe harbour/exemption from liability for intermediaries if they comply with court/government takedown orders

Section 79 provides that an intermediary shall not be liable for any third-party information, data, or communication link made available or hosted by it, provided certain conditions are met. After Shreya Singhal, intermediaries must act on court orders or government notifications to take down content.

6Section 43 of the IT Act deals with:

  1. Penalty and compensation for damage to computer systems (civil liability)
  2. Criminal offences for hacking
  3. Cyber terrorism
  4. Obscenity online
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Penalty and compensation for damage to computer systems (civil liability)

Section 43 provides civil remedies — if any person without permission of the owner of a computer resource accesses, downloads, introduces viruses, damages, or disrupts any computer, computer system, or network, he shall be liable to pay damages by way of compensation to the person so affected.

7Section 43A of the IT Act (inserted 2008) deals with:

  1. Identity theft
  2. Cyber terrorism
  3. Violation of privacy
  4. Compensation for failure to protect sensitive personal data
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Compensation for failure to protect sensitive personal data

Section 43A holds body corporates that possess, deal or handle sensitive personal data or information liable to pay compensation to the affected person if they are negligent in maintaining reasonable security practices and procedures.

8The concept of 'chilling effect' on free speech, applied in Shreya Singhal, refers to:

  1. The deterrent effect of vague/overbroad laws on legitimate speech
  2. Physical harm caused to a person's reputation
  3. The cooling-off period before filing a cyber complaint
  4. Government surveillance of online communications
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The deterrent effect of vague/overbroad laws on legitimate speech

The 'chilling effect' describes the inhibition or discouragement of the legitimate exercise of a fundamental right (especially free speech) caused by vague, overbroad, or disproportionate legal provisions.

9Assertion (A): Section 66A of the IT Act, 2000 was struck down by the Supreme Court in Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015). Reason (R): Section 66A was vague and overbroad, and fell outside the permissible restrictions on free speech under Article 19(2) of the Constitution.

  1. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are correct but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is correct but R is incorrect
  4. Both A and R are incorrect
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A

Both statements are correct. The Supreme Court struck down Section 66A precisely because it was unconstitutionally vague and overbroad, and the restrictions it imposed on speech were not saved by Article 19(2) of the Constitution.

10Assertion (A): An electronic record has the same legal validity as a paper document under the IT Act 2000. Reason (R): Section 4 of the IT Act gives legal recognition to electronic records, and Section 5 gives legal recognition to electronic signatures.

  1. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are correct but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is correct but R is incorrect
  4. A is incorrect but R is correct
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A

Both statements are correct and the reason correctly explains the assertion. Sections 4 and 5 together provide the foundational legal recognition for electronic records and electronic signatures respectively, giving them the same validity as their paper/handwritten counterparts.

11Under Section 67B, publishing child pornography in electronic form on first conviction is punishable by:

  1. 3 years and ₹5 lakh
  2. 7 years and ₹10 lakh
  3. Life imprisonment
  4. 5 years and ₹10 lakh
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 5 years and ₹10 lakh

Section 67B prescribes punishment on first conviction of imprisonment up to 5 years and fine up to ₹10 lakh for publishing or transmitting material depicting children in sexually explicit act in electronic form.

12Match Section of IT Act with Subject Matter: (i) Section 43 → (ii) Section 66C → (iii) Section 66F → (iv) Section 67

  1. i-Damage to computer systems (civil), ii-Identity theft, iii-Cyber terrorism, iv-Obscenity in electronic form
  2. i-Cyber terrorism, ii-Damage to computer, iii-Obscenity, iv-Identity theft
  3. i-Identity theft, ii-Obscenity, iii-Damage to computer, iv-Cyber terrorism
  4. i-Obscenity, ii-Cyber terrorism, iii-Identity theft, iv-Damage to computer
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. i-Damage to computer systems (civil), ii-Identity theft, iii-Cyber terrorism, iv-Obscenity in electronic form

Section 43 = civil penalty for damage to computer systems; Section 66C = identity theft; Section 66F = cyber terrorism (life imprisonment); Section 67 = publishing obscene material in electronic form.

13The correct appeal hierarchy under the IT Act 2000 is:

  1. Cyber Appellate Tribunal → Adjudicating Officer → High Court
  2. High Court → Adjudicating Officer → Cyber Appellate Tribunal
  3. Adjudicating Officer → High Court → Supreme Court
  4. Adjudicating Officer → Cyber Appellate Tribunal → High Court
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Adjudicating Officer → Cyber Appellate Tribunal → High Court

The appeal hierarchy is: (1) Adjudicating Officer (Section 46); (2) Cyber Appellate Tribunal on appeal (Section 48/57); (3) High Court on further appeal (Section 62).
