About this question set

This set has 16 multiple-choice questions on Copyright and Neighbouring Rights from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 9, Intellectual Property Rights and Information Technology Law. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 16 of the 16 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the IPR & IT Law notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

16 practice questions with answers

1The Indian Copyright Act, 1957 came into force on:

  1. 4 June 1957
  2. 1 January 1957
  3. 21 January 1958
  4. 15 August 1958
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 21 January 1958

Although the Copyright Act was enacted in 1957, it came into force on 21 January 1958.

Report this question

2Under the Copyright Act 1957, which Section defines the 'meaning of copyright'?

  1. Section 13
  2. Section 14
  3. Section 17
  4. Section 22
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Section 14

Section 14 of the Copyright Act, 1957 defines 'copyright' by specifying the exclusive rights granted to the copyright owner for each category of work.

Report this question

3Under Section 13 of the Copyright Act 1957, copyright subsists in:

  1. Ideas, concepts, and methods
  2. All published works regardless of originality
  3. Government documents and official publications
  4. Original literary, dramatic, musical, artistic works, films, and sound recordings
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Original literary, dramatic, musical, artistic works, films, and sound recordings

Section 13 lists the categories of works in which copyright subsists: original literary, dramatic, musical and artistic works; cinematograph films; and sound recordings.

Report this question

4Who is the first owner of copyright in a work created by an author employed under a 'contract of service'?

  1. The author
  2. The employer
  3. The government
  4. The publisher
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The employer

Section 17 of the Copyright Act provides that where a work is made by an author in the course of his employment under a contract of service or apprenticeship, the employer shall be the first owner of copyright in the absence of any agreement to the contrary.

Report this question

5Under Section 57 of the Copyright Act 1957, 'moral rights' include:

  1. Right to receive royalties
  2. Right of first publication and right to assign
  3. Right of reproduction and right of communication to the public
  4. Right of paternity and right of integrity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Right of paternity and right of integrity

Section 57 provides for two moral rights: (i) the right of paternity (attribution) and (ii) the right of integrity (to restrain or claim damages for any distortion, mutilation or modification prejudicial to the author's honour or reputation).

Report this question

6The term of copyright for a literary work in India under the Copyright Act 1957 is:

  1. Life + 50 years
  2. Life + 70 years
  3. 60 years from publication
  4. Life + 60 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Life + 60 years

Section 22 of the Copyright Act provides a term of copyright for literary, dramatic, musical and artistic works of the lifetime of the author plus 60 years (India extended beyond the Berne minimum of 50 years).

Report this question

7The term of copyright for a cinematograph film in India is:

  1. Life of director + 60 years
  2. 50 years from publication
  3. 60 years from the year of publication
  4. 70 years from the year of publication
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 60 years from the year of publication

Section 26 of the Copyright Act provides that copyright in a cinematograph film subsists until 60 years from the beginning of the calendar year next following the year of publication.

Report this question

8The 'idea-expression dichotomy' in copyright law means:

  1. Both ideas and their expression are equally protected
  2. Copyright protects the expression of ideas but not the ideas themselves
  3. Ideas can be copyrighted if sufficiently original
  4. Copyright protects only the title of a work
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Copyright protects the expression of ideas but not the ideas themselves

Copyright protects the specific expression of an idea, not the underlying idea itself. This principle is codified in Section 16 of the Copyright Act, 1957.

Report this question

9Which Section of the Copyright Act 1957 deals with 'fair dealing'?

  1. Section 14
  2. Section 52
  3. Section 57
  4. Section 63
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Section 52

Section 52 of the Copyright Act lists acts which do not constitute infringement of copyright, including fair dealing for research, private study, criticism, review, and news reporting.

Report this question

10The most significant amendment to the Copyright Act was made in:

  1. 1994
  2. 1999
  3. 2005
  4. 2012
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 2012

The Copyright (Amendment) Act, 2012 was the most significant amendment, aligning India with WIPO Internet Treaties (WCT and WPPT), introducing digital rights management provisions, statutory licensing for broadcasting, and moral rights for performers.

Report this question

11Moral rights under the Indian Copyright Act are:

  1. Inalienable and survive assignment of copyright
  2. Assignable and can be waived by contract
  3. Available only during the author's lifetime
  4. Available only to the publisher
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Inalienable and survive assignment of copyright

Moral rights under Section 57 are perpetual and inalienable — they cannot be transferred by assignment, waived, or extinguished by contract.

Report this question

12In Eastern Book Company v. D.B. Modak (2008), the Supreme Court of India applied the test of:

  1. 'Modicum of creativity' / 'Skill and Judgement' test
  2. 'Sweat of the brow' doctrine
  3. 'Fair use' doctrine
  4. 'First publication' test
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 'Modicum of creativity' / 'Skill and Judgement' test

The Supreme Court in Eastern Book Company rejected the 'sweat of the brow' doctrine and held that copyright in compilations requires a modicum of creativity and the exercise of skill and judgment.

Report this question

13The criminal penalty for copyright infringement under Section 63 of the Copyright Act includes imprisonment of:

  1. 6 months to 3 years
  2. 3 months to 1 year
  3. 1 year to 5 years
  4. 2 years to 7 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 6 months to 3 years

Section 63 of the Copyright Act provides that any person who knowingly infringes copyright shall be punishable with imprisonment for a term of not less than 6 months but not more than 3 years, and a fine between ₹50,000 and ₹2,00,000.

Report this question

14Under the Copyright Act 1957, a 'work of architecture' enjoys protection as:

  1. Literary work
  2. Artistic work
  3. Dramatic work
  4. Cinematograph film
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Artistic work

Section 2(c) of the Copyright Act defines 'artistic work' to include a painting, a sculpture, a drawing, an engraving, a photograph, a work of architecture, and a work of artistic craftsmanship.

Report this question

15In copyright law, the 'Doctrine of Originality' requires:

  1. Complete novelty and inventive step
  2. Independent creation with at least a minimal degree of creativity
  3. Prior registration
  4. Publication in a recognised form
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Independent creation with at least a minimal degree of creativity

Copyright originality requires that the work be independently created by the author (not copied) and possess at least some minimal degree of creativity.

Report this question

16In India, copyright registration is:

  1. Mandatory for protection
  2. Not possible under law
  3. Voluntary; protection exists without registration
  4. Mandatory only for cinematograph films
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Voluntary; protection exists without registration

Copyright in India arises automatically upon creation. Registration under Chapter X of the Copyright Act is optional but provides a public record and is useful as evidence in infringement proceedings.
