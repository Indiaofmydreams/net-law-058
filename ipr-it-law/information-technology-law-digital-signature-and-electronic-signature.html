8 practice questions with answers

1A digital signature under the IT Act, 2000 is based on:

  1. Asymmetric cryptosystem and hash function
  2. Symmetric cryptosystem
  3. Simple password encryption
  4. Biometric verification
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Asymmetric cryptosystem and hash function

Section 3 of the IT Act defines digital signature as authentication of an electronic record by a subscriber by means of an electronic method or procedure in accordance with the provisions of Section 3, using an asymmetric crypto system and hash function.

2The legal recognition of electronic signatures in India is provided under which Section of the IT Act, 2000?

  1. Section 3
  2. Section 3A
  3. Section 5
  4. Section 15
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Section 5

Section 5 of the IT Act provides that where any law provides that information or any other matter shall be authenticated by affixing the signature or any document shall be signed or bear the signature of any person, then such requirement shall be deemed to have been satisfied if such information is authenticated by means of electronic signature.

3The concept of 'electronic signature' (technology-neutral approach) was introduced in the IT Act by the amendment of:

  1. 2000
  2. 2006
  3. 2008
  4. 2012
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 2008

The IT (Amendment) Act, 2008 introduced Section 3A to recognise electronic signatures as a technology-neutral concept, broadening the scope beyond the PKI-based digital signature.

Report this question

4The Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA) under the IT Act, 2000 is established under:

  1. Section 15
  2. Section 17
  3. Section 35
  4. Section 48
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Section 17

Section 17 of the IT Act provides for the appointment of a Controller of Certifying Authorities by the Central Government to perform the functions under the Act.

5In Public Key Infrastructure (PKI), the private key is used to:

  1. Create/sign the digital signature
  2. Verify the signature
  3. Issue the Digital Signature Certificate
  4. Encrypt the public key
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Create/sign the digital signature

In PKI, the private key (held exclusively by the signer) is used to create/sign the digital signature on the document. The corresponding public key is used by recipients to verify that the signature was made by the holder of the private key.

Report this question

6In Public Key Infrastructure (PKI), the public key is used to:

  1. Verify the digital signature
  2. Create the digital signature
  3. Sign the electronic record
  4. Generate the private key
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Verify the digital signature

The public key, which is shared openly and available to anyone, is used to verify that a digital signature was made using the corresponding private key, thus confirming the authenticity of the signer.

Report this question

7A Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) in India is issued by:

  1. The Controller of Certifying Authorities directly
  2. NASSCOM
  3. Ministry of Electronics and IT (MeitY)
  4. Certifying Authorities licensed by the CCA
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Certifying Authorities licensed by the CCA

Under the IT Act, Certifying Authorities (CAs) are licensed entities authorised by the Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA) to issue Digital Signature Certificates.

Report this question

8Under the IT Act, 2000, which of the following documents CANNOT be authenticated by a digital/electronic signature?

  1. Tax returns filed online
  2. A negotiable instrument such as a promissory note
  3. An application to a government department
  4. A commercial contract
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A negotiable instrument such as a promissory note

Section 1(4) read with the First Schedule of the IT Act excludes: (a) Negotiable Instruments (excluding cheques) under the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881; (b) power of attorney; (c) trust deeds; (d) wills; (e) contracts for sale or conveyance of immovable property.
