1The GI Registry in India is located in:

  1. Mumbai
  2. Delhi
  3. Chennai
  4. Kolkata
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Chennai

The Geographical Indications Registry is located in Chennai (Intellectual Property India — GI Registry).

2Assertion (A): A geographical indication is a collective right and cannot be owned by any individual. Reason (R): A GI identifies a product as originating from a territory where a given quality or reputation is essentially attributable to its geographical origin — it belongs to the community of producers in that region.

  1. Both A and R are correct but R is not the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A
  3. A is correct but R is incorrect
  4. Both A and R are incorrect
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A

A GI is a collective IP right — no single person or company can own it exclusively. It belongs to the producers from that geographical region who share the quality/reputation linked to the place of origin.

3The Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD) was adopted in:

  1. 1990
  2. 1992
  3. 1994
  4. 2000
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1992

The Convention on Biological Diversity was opened for signature at the United Nations Conference on Environment and Development (the Rio Earth Summit) in June 1992.

Report this question

4The Nagoya Protocol (2010) under the CBD deals with:

  1. Prevention of biopiracy only
  2. Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS) of genetic resources
  3. Protection of traditional knowledge as IP
  4. Conservation of endangered species
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS) of genetic resources

The Nagoya Protocol on Access to Genetic Resources and the Fair and Equitable Sharing of Benefits Arising from their Utilization (2010) implements the CBD's third objective of fair and equitable benefit sharing.

Report this question

5The Biological Diversity Act 2002 (India) established which apex body?

  1. National Biodiversity Commission
  2. Central Biodiversity Board
  3. National Genetic Resources Registry
  4. National Biodiversity Authority (NBA)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. National Biodiversity Authority (NBA)

The Biological Diversity Act, 2002 establishes a three-tier structure: the National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) at the national level, State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs) at the state level, and Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs) at the local level.

6The Nagoya Protocol entered into force in:

  1. 2010
  2. 2012
  3. 2014
  4. 2016
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 2014

The Nagoya Protocol, adopted at the 10th meeting of the Conference of the Parties to the CBD in Nagoya, Japan in 2010, entered into force on 12 October 2014.

7Under the Designs Act, 2000, the initial period of registration for a design is:

  1. 5 years
  2. 10 years
  3. 15 years
  4. 20 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 10 years

Under Section 11 of the Designs Act, 2000, the initial period of registration of a design is 10 years from the date of registration.

Report this question

8The maximum total period of protection for a registered industrial design in India (including renewal) is:

  1. 10 years
  2. 15 years
  3. 20 years
  4. 25 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 15 years

The Designs Act provides for initial registration of 10 years with a possibility of one renewal for a further 5 years, making the maximum protection period 15 years.

Report this question

9A 'design' under Section 2(d) of the Designs Act, 2000 specifically EXCLUDES:

  1. Shape features of an article
  2. Colour combinations applied to an article
  3. Ornamental features of an article
  4. Artistic works and trademarks
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Artistic works and trademarks

The definition of 'design' in Section 2(d) expressly excludes any mode or principle of construction, or anything which is in substance a mere mechanical device, and does not include any trademark or property mark or any artistic work.

10The Design Registry in India is located at:

  1. Kolkata
  2. Mumbai
  3. Delhi
  4. Chennai
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Kolkata

The Office of the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks (CGPDTM) maintains the Design Wing at Kolkata, which is the headquarter for design registrations.

11Under TRIPS, Articles 25 and 26 deal with protection of:

  1. Geographical indications
  2. Industrial designs
  3. Patents for pharmaceutical products
  4. Layout designs of integrated circuits
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Industrial designs

TRIPS Articles 25 and 26 deal with industrial designs, requiring members to provide protection for independently created designs that are new or original, for a period of at least 10 years.

12The Hague Agreement (WIPO) concerning the international registration of industrial designs is administered by:

  1. WIPO
  2. WTO
  3. UNCTAD
  4. UNESCO
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. WIPO

The Hague Agreement Concerning the International Registration of Industrial Designs is administered by WIPO and allows for international registration of industrial designs in multiple countries through a single application.

Report this question

13The Semiconductor Integrated Circuits Layout-Design (SICLD) Act, 2000 was enacted to comply with which TRIPS provision?

  1. TRIPS Article 27 (Patents)
  2. TRIPS Part II, Section 6 (Layout-Designs of Integrated Circuits)
  3. TRIPS Article 22 (Geographical Indications)
  4. TRIPS Article 39 (Trade Secrets)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. TRIPS Part II, Section 6 (Layout-Designs of Integrated Circuits)

The SICLD Act, 2000 was enacted to give effect to Part II, Section 6 of TRIPS which requires member states to protect layout-designs (topographies) of integrated circuits.

Report this question

14The term of protection for a registered semiconductor layout-design in India is:

  1. 5 years from filing
  2. 10 years from date of filing or first commercial exploitation, whichever is earlier
  3. 15 years, renewable once
  4. 20 years from filing
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 10 years from date of filing or first commercial exploitation, whichever is earlier

Under the SICLD Act, the registration of a layout-design is valid for 10 years from the date of filing of the application or the date of first commercial exploitation, whichever is earlier.

Report this question

15Unlike patents and trademarks, the registration of a semiconductor layout-design in India:

  1. Cannot be renewed
  2. Can be renewed indefinitely
  3. Can be renewed for one additional term
  4. Automatically renews for the same period
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Cannot be renewed

The SICLD Act does not provide for any renewal of the registration of a layout-design. Once the 10-year period expires, the protection lapses permanently.

16The Semiconductor Integrated Circuits Layout-Design Registry in India is under the administrative control of the:

  1. Ministry of Commerce and Industry
  2. Ministry of Science and Technology
  3. Ministry of Law and Justice
  4. Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology

The Semiconductor Integrated Circuits Layout-Design Registry is established under the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY), with its head office at New Delhi.

17Assertion (A): The Washington Treaty on Intellectual Property in Respect of Integrated Circuits (1989) never came into force. Reason (R): TRIPS Agreement Article 35 incorporated the substantive provisions of the Washington Treaty, making a separate international instrument unnecessary for WTO members.

  1. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are correct but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is incorrect; R is correct
  4. Both A and R are incorrect
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A

The Washington Treaty (IPIC Treaty, 1989) never received the required number of ratifications to enter into force. However, TRIPS Article 35 obliges WTO members to comply with Articles 2 through 7 and 12 of the Washington Treaty, making its substantive provisions effective.

Report this question

18India's protection of trade secrets is primarily through:

  1. A dedicated Trade Secrets Protection Act
  2. The Copyright Act, 1957
  3. The Patents Act, 1970
  4. Contract law, equity (breach of confidence), and tort law — there is no dedicated statute
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Contract law, equity (breach of confidence), and tort law — there is no dedicated statute

India does not have a dedicated trade secrets legislation. Protection is available through contractual obligations (NDAs), the equitable doctrine of breach of confidence, tort law, and limited provisions under the IT Act 2000 for electronic records.

Report this question

19Under TRIPS Article 39, for information to qualify as protectable 'undisclosed information' (trade secret), it must satisfy which three conditions?

  1. Novelty, inventive step, industrial application
  2. Secrecy, commercial value by virtue of its secrecy, and reasonable steps taken to keep it secret
  3. Originality, publication, and registration
  4. Distinctiveness, exclusivity, and non-disclosure
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Secrecy, commercial value by virtue of its secrecy, and reasonable steps taken to keep it secret

TRIPS Article 39(2) specifies three cumulative conditions: the information must (a) be secret (not generally known or accessible); (b) have commercial value because it is secret; and (c) have been subject to reasonable steps by the person lawfully in control of the information to keep it secret.

20In the UK case Coco v. A.N. Clark (Engineers) Ltd. (1969), the three elements required for breach of confidence are:

  1. Registration, secrecy, and loss
  2. Novelty, secrecy, and commercial value
  3. Quality of confidence, impartation in circumstances of confidence, and unauthorised use causing detriment
  4. Publication, misappropriation, and harm
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Quality of confidence, impartation in circumstances of confidence, and unauthorised use causing detriment

Megarry J in Coco v A.N. Clark held that three elements are required for a breach of confidence action: (1) the information must have the necessary quality of confidence; (2) it must have been imparted in circumstances importing an obligation of confidence; and (3) there must be an unauthorised use of that information to the detriment of the party communicating it.
