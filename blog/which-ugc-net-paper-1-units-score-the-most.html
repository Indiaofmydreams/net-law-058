Quick answer
  • By design, no unit is worth more: 10 units, about 5 questions (10 marks) each.
  • In the four Paper 1 papers on this site, every unit appeared 19 to 21 times out of 200 questions, so the real spread is narrow.
  • "Most scoring" therefore means most marks per hour of effort, which depends on your starting point.

Many aspirants hope to find the one or two units that "carry everything". For UGC NET Paper 1, that unit does not exist in the official structure. What does exist is a difference in how quickly each unit repays your study time. This post separates the two ideas and keeps to what can be shown.

Which unit has the highest weightage in UGC NET Paper 1?

None, officially. Paper 1 is reported to have 50 questions of 2 marks each (100 marks), and the syllabus has ten units, so the planning assumption is about five questions per unit. NTA does not publish a unit-wise count before each session, and some published analyses report that Teaching Aptitude, Research Aptitude and Logical Reasoning sometimes carry slightly more than five. Treat that as a tendency, not a rule.

So what does "most scoring" mean?

If marks per unit are almost equal, the useful question is: how many marks can I gain per hour of study? That depends on the type of unit and on you. The table below is reasoning, not data.

Unit typeExamplesWhy it can pay back fastThe catch
Fact-based knowledgeHigher Education System, ICT, People, Development & EnvironmentAnswers are definite; notes plus repeated MCQs lift accuracy steadilyLarge factual range; policy items need updating
Concept-based knowledgeTeaching Aptitude, Research Aptitude, CommunicationConcepts link together, so one idea unlocks several questionsOptions can be close; statement-style questions need care
Calculation skillMathematical Reasoning, Data InterpretationAnswers are exact once the method is automaticSlow start, and time pressure if methods are shaky
Reading and logic skillComprehension, Logical ReasoningGains are transferable once you learn the patternsNeeds weeks of practice rather than reading

A note on format: in the 200 Paper 1 questions we counted, 82 used lettered statements such as "(A) and (C) only". Knowing a concept is not enough; you also need practice with this elimination style.

How should I use this?

  1. Take a timed Paper 1 mock and score each unit separately out of five.
  2. Mark the units where you score 2 or less. A move from 2 to 4 gains four marks, the same as moving a strong unit from 4 to 5 twice over, and it is usually easier.
  3. Spend the first block of time on those low units, and keep the strong units alive with short MCQ sets.
  4. Re-test after a week and update the ranking.

For the full study order, see the Paper 1 syllabus and priority guide, and for the paper-by-paper view, the unit-wise PYQ analysis.

Frequently asked questions

Which unit has the highest weightage in UGC NET Paper 1?

None officially. The syllabus has ten units for 50 questions, so the design is about five questions each. In the four Paper 1 papers on this site, Research Aptitude and Logical Reasoning each had one or two extra questions in a paper, but the totals are close.

Is Paper 1 weightage the same every year?

The design is, but the actual count can shift by a question or two between sessions. NTA does not announce unit-wise counts in advance.

Which Paper 1 units are easiest to score in?

It depends on you. Fact-based units such as Higher Education System, ICT and People, Development and Environment reward notes and revision. Skill units reward practice. Pick the units where your effort per mark is lowest.

Should I skip the units with fewer questions?

No. Counts differ by only a question or so, and each unit is worth about ten marks in the design. Skipping one gives up a lot for little saved time.

Where do these counts come from?

We counted questions tagged to each unit in the four full Paper 1 sets in the PYQ file on this site (UGC NET 2021, 2024, June 2025 and December 2025). Tags are assigned by content, and a few borderline questions could sit in two units.
