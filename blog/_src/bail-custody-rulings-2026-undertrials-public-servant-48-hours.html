Bail and personal liberty produced a steady stream of rulings in 2026. Competitive exams love this area because it ties Article 21, Article 22 and the criminal procedure statutes together. This guide gathers the key rulings and explains the principle behind each.

What did the Supreme Court order on undertrials granted bail but still in jail?

On 9 November 2026, in In Re: Policy Strategy for Grant of Bail, a bench of Justices Sundresh and Varale directed Uttar Pradesh to identify undertrials who have bail orders but remain in jail in 55 districts, with the help of District Legal Services Authorities.

The problem is practical: many prisoners are granted bail but cannot furnish sureties or the paperwork stalls. Identifying them with the help of DLSAs aims to turn a paper order into actual release.

What happens if a public servant is in custody for over 48 hours?

In Vishwajeet Singh v. State of Bihar (9 November 2026) a bench of Justices Pardiwala and Manmohan held that a public servant in custody for more than 48 hours is deemed suspended.

Can bail be refused on moral grounds?

In Kanha v. State of M.P. (23 September 2026) the Supreme Court set aside a bail denial based on morality. Bail turns on legal factors such as flight risk, tampering and gravity, not the court's moral view of the accused.

This reflects the settled position that bail is the rule and jail the exception, a principle the Court has repeated in many cases. See also the 2026 debate on bail under special statutes in our note on UAPA bail.

What if grounds of arrest are not communicated?

The Delhi High Court in Bhola v. State (9 October 2026) dealt with a case where the grounds of arrest were not communicated. Article 22(1) requires that an arrested person be informed of the grounds of arrest.

Courts have held that failure to communicate grounds can make an arrest illegal. The requirement is both constitutional and statutory, and exam questions often test it with Article 22(2), the 24-hour production rule.

Why was an NSA detention quashed in 2026?

In October 2026 an NSA detention in the Sambhal matter was quashed because it rested on a confession alone, and the Court imposed 10 lakh rupees in costs on Uttar Pradesh.

Preventive detention is an exception to ordinary criminal procedure and is tested strictly. A detention based on thin material fails the subjective satisfaction standard. Related: the 2026 release policy for elderly and ill prisoners in our post on early release.
