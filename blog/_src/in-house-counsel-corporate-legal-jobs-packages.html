Not every lawyer wants a firm career. In-house roles offer more regular hours and closer work with the business. Here is how to see them as a serious path.

What does an in-house counsel do?

They advise their own company on contracts, compliance, disputes, employment and regulatory matters, and manage outside counsel. Roles range from legal executive to General Counsel, and the work is closer to business than in a firm.

Typical sectors include IT, banking, manufacturing, pharma and e-commerce.

What do in-house roles pay?

Entry pay in mid to large companies is often reported between ₹6 and ₹15 lakh a year, with senior roles much higher. Figures are estimates from reports and vary by sector and city, so verify with the employer.

How do I enter?

Most join through campus, referrals, job portals or a move from a law firm after a few years. Fresh graduates can enter smaller teams, while larger companies often prefer some experience.

Internships with in-house teams during your LLM help.

What skills do companies want?

Drafting and negotiation, commercial sense, knowledge of company, contract and compliance law, and clear communication with non-lawyers. Familiarity with data protection and labour codes is useful.

Read Unit 6 for commercial law.

What is the growth path?

From executive to manager, head of legal and General Counsel, often with a move between companies. Some lawyers also move to compliance or business roles. Growth depends on company size and your results.
