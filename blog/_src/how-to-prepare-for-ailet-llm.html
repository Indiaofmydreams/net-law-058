Quick answer
  • The paper is reported as 100 MCQs in 120 minutes with -0.25: about 72 seconds a question.
  • Cover the five core branches first, then the rest, then spend a third of your time on timed papers.
  • Guess only after eliminating at least one option.
  • Competition is intense, so train for accuracy, not just attempts.

AILET LLM is short, factual and unforgiving. With 100 standalone questions, there are no passages to hide in. You either know the principle or you do not, and every wrong answer costs a quarter mark. This guide gives a subject order, a 10-week plan and an exam-day approach. The pattern is as reported, so confirm it in the NLU Delhi notification. For a view of how the exam is built, see the pattern and syllabus guide.

Which subjects should you study first?

  1. Constitutional Law and Jurisprudence: the vocabulary and the biggest blocks in most analyses.
  2. Criminal Law, now under the BNS, BNSS and BSA, with old section numbers beside the new ones.
  3. Contract and Torts: principle-rich and case-rich.
  4. Administrative, Family, Property, Company and Public International Law.
  5. Tax, Environmental and Labour Law, as smaller blocks.

NLU Delhi names thirteen branches but no weightage, so treat the order as a tendency. Track coverage on the syllabus tracker.

How should you handle negative marking?

Each wrong answer costs 0.25, so four wrong answers erase one right one. A blind guess among four options has a 25 percent chance of being right, so its expected value is barely above zero. Remove one option and the chance rises to about 33 percent, which makes guessing worthwhile. A quick rule: answer when you can rule out at least one option, and leave the rest.

Options you can rule outChance of a correct guessExpected marks per guess
None25%+0.06
One33%+0.17
Two50%+0.38

What does a good exam approach look like?

  • First pass (about 75 minutes): answer everything you know, skip the doubtful ones.
  • Second pass (about 35 minutes): eliminate options on the skipped ones and answer only where you are above 33 percent.
  • Last 10 minutes: check your OMR and your marks.

How to use mocks

  1. Sit them under the real timer.
  2. Log each miss by branch and cause: did not know, misread, or a close option.
