If you love law and teaching, the assistant professor route is a strong option. It needs patience, since exams and appointments move slowly. This guide explains how the pieces fit.

Is an LLM enough to become an assistant professor?

Generally no. UGC rules ask for a master's degree with the required marks plus qualifying UGC NET, or a PhD as per UGC regulations. Many universities and NLUs also expect research output, so an LLM alone is the starting point, not the finish.

Check the latest UGC Regulations on minimum qualifications, since the rules have been revised from time to time.

What is the difference between NET, JRF and PhD?

NET qualifies you for assistant professor. JRF gives a research fellowship with the NET eligibility. A PhD, under the UGC regulations in force, can also give eligibility. Check the current UGC notice for how these apply to you.

How much does an assistant professor earn?

Government and aided institutions follow the pay matrix, with entry basic pay reported around ₹57,700 plus allowances, and private universities and NLUs set their own. Treat these as approximate and check each advertisement.

See our post on assistant professor pay.

What is the JRF stipend?

The JRF stipend has been reported at ₹37,000 per month for the first two years in the recent revision, plus HRA, then a higher amount as SRF. Confirm the current figure on the official UGC and NTA notices.

The stipend is for fellows who register for a PhD, and it is a fellowship and not a salary.

How long does the path take?

LLM takes one year, NET can be attempted during or after it, and a PhD takes three or more years. Many aspirants clear NET within one to three attempts with steady preparation across Paper 1 and Law (058).
