Quick summary
  • CLAT PG covers 13 core law subjects, all asked through passage-based MCQs.
  • Criminal Law now means the BNS, BNSS and BSA, which replaced the IPC, CrPC and Evidence Act.
  • Revise first: Constitutional Law, Jurisprudence, Criminal Law, Contract and Torts.
  • Questions test principles and reasoning. Case names help you recognise the principle fast.

CLAT PG does not reward a long reading list. It rewards knowing the principle behind a rule and applying it to a fresh passage. This guide walks through the syllabus subject by subject and tells you what to look at first. The subject list is as reported from the 2027 notification, so always check the Consortium's own notice for the final wording.

Where to start: a priority order

  1. Constitutional Law and Jurisprudence. They supply the vocabulary of most legal passages, and passages on rights, state power and legal theory are common.
  2. Criminal Law under the new Sanhitas. Learn the new section numbers beside the old ones. See our IPC to BNS and CrPC to BNSS mapping.
  3. Contract and Torts. They are principle-rich and give you many case-based questions.
  4. Family, Property and Company Law. Statute-driven, so short notes and quick practice work well.
  5. Public International, Tax, Environmental and Labour Law. Smaller blocks. Learn the key principles and landmark cases and move on.

How the syllabus is actually tested

Each question follows a passage, often a short extract from a judgment, article or statute. You must read, extract the legal principle and answer what follows. That has three consequences for your revision:

  • Principles over provisions. Know what a rule is for and when it applies. Section numbers matter less than the idea.
  • Case names as shortcuts. When you recognise a case in the passage, you already know the rule. Use the landmark case vault to build this recall.
  • Reading speed. Practise passages under time. Our method for solving passage-based questions helps.

Pairing syllabus with practice

For every subject, do three things in order: skim the notes, attempt 20 passage questions on it, and write down every error in a log. The full syllabus page and the practice arena are built for this loop. If the exam is within days, use the last-week plan instead of covering everything again.
