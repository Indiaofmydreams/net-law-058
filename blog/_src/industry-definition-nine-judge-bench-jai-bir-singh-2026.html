Quick answer
  • A nine-judge bench decided State of U.P. v. Jai Bir Singh in 2026 on what counts as an "industry" under Section 2(j) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947.
  • Bangalore Water Supply (1978) was not overruled. The judges split: four proposed a narrower test, four affirmed the triple test, and one left it undisturbed.
  • The proposed test requires a "discernible commercial character analogous to trade or business".
  • The Industrial Relations Code now defines industry in Section 2(p) and must be read on its own text.

Few labour law questions have been argued as long as the meaning of "industry". It decides whether hospitals, universities, clubs, charities and government departments are covered by the law on retrenchment and industrial disputes. In 1978 the Supreme Court gave the definition its widest reading. In 2026 a nine-judge bench revisited it, with a divided result. This guide sets out who said what, what the law is today and why exam setters will love it.

What did the nine-judge bench decide in State of UP v. Jai Bir Singh?

The nine judges split on whether to revisit Bangalore Water Supply (1978). Four, led by CJI Surya Kant, proposed a narrower test; four affirmed the original triple test; one left it undisturbed because the Industrial Disputes Act has been repealed. Bangalore Water Supply was not overruled.

The bench was CJI Surya Kant and Justices Satish Chandra Sharma, Alok Aradhe, Vipul Pancholi, B.V. Nagarathna, Dipankar Datta, Ujjal Bhuyan, Joymalya Bagchi and P.S. Narasimha. The CJI wrote for himself and Justices Sharma, Aradhe and Pancholi. Justices Nagarathna, Datta, Bhuyan and Bagchi held that the triple test correctly states the law and needed no reconsideration. Justice Narasimha accepted that the reference had been needed when made but considered it unnecessary now. Because the opinions do not add up to one majority view, reports describe the result differently, so students should state the position carefully: the earlier precedent was left standing, and the CJI's reformulation is a considered opinion that does not displace it for pending matters.

What is the triple test for industry?

In Bangalore Water Supply a systematic activity, organised by cooperation between employer and employee, for the production or distribution of goods and services calculated to satisfy human wants, is prima facie an industry. Profit motive is irrelevant.

Justice Krishna Iyer's judgment brought hospitals, educational institutions, research bodies and many welfare undertakings within the Act. Purely spiritual or religious activity was excluded, though material goods linked to religious activity can still fall within the definition. Critics, including the government, argued that the test stretched a law about trade disputes far beyond what Parliament meant. A 1982 amendment to the definition that would have narrowed it was never brought into force, which is why the old test kept governing.

What is the new test proposed by the CJI's opinion?

The third limb is narrowed: the goods or services must have a discernible commercial character analogous to trade or business. The inquiry is functional and looks at the nature of the activity and the employer-employee relationship.

LimbBangalore Water Supply (1978)CJI's reformulation (2026)
1Systematic activitySystematic and organised activity
2Cooperation between employer and employeeSame
3Production or distribution of goods or services to satisfy human wantsGoods or services with a discernible commercial character analogous to trade or business, satisfying material wants
Profit motiveIrrelevantIrrelevant, but commercial nature is not wholly irrelevant

For composite undertakings the dominant nature test applies. A charitable or philanthropic label does not decide the matter if the inquiry shows an organised commercial-type activity. The opinion said the wide words of Section 2(j) need "contextual and associational shrinkage". Casual activity, domestic service, individual professionals and small unorganised clubs fall outside the Act.

Are sovereign functions an industry?

Functions under a distinct constitutional or statutory framework, such as those governed by Articles 310 and 311, stay outside the Act. The sovereign exception is read narrowly, and a severable industrial unit within a sovereign department can still qualify.

Justice Bagchi's opinion gave examples of core sovereign work: defence, foreign affairs, legislation, taxation, justice and law and order. The bench rejected the idea that every government body is automatically an industry, but also said that state performance alone does not make an activity sovereign. A public water supply department or a government printing press, for example, may be treated differently from a police department. The judges differed on how far the sovereign label should matter.

Why does this matter now that the Industrial Relations Code is in force?

The Industrial Disputes Act has been repealed, and the Industrial Relations Code defines "industry" in Section 2(p). The Court said the Code must be read on its own text and scheme, so Bangalore Water Supply should not be its sheet anchor.

Justice Narasimha's opinion noted that the Act was repealed with effect from 21 November 2025. That date helps with the commencement question discussed in our guide to the labour codes. For pending cases under the old Act, the earlier law governs. Final judgments, awards and settlements are undisturbed, and any reformulation operates prospectively.

How can this be asked in UGC NET Law, CLAT PG or AIBE?

Expect questions on the triple test, the judges in Bangalore Water Supply, the 1982 amendment and the difference between the 1947 Act and the 2020 Code.
