Quick answer
  • UGC NET Law (058) Paper 2 has 10 units, with 100 questions for 200 marks as reported.
  • Units are not equal in the papers on this site: Constitutional and Administrative Law led with 77 of 500 questions.
  • Study first: Constitutional and Administrative Law, Public International Law, Commercial Law, then Environment, IPR and Family Law, with Jurisprudence early for vocabulary.
  • Do not drop the smallest units. They still gave about 6 or 7 questions a paper.

Most aspirants start NET Law by collecting books. A better start is the unit list and a view of where the marks have actually come from. This guide gives the ten units, what each covers in practice, and a priority order built from the five Law papers in our previous-year file. Wording of topics can be refined between sessions, so confirm against the NTA syllabus before you finalise your plan.

What is the UGC NET Law syllabus?

Paper 2 is reported to be 100 questions of 2 marks each (200 marks), and Paper 1 adds 100 marks, for 300 in all. The Law syllabus is divided into ten units:

How many questions come from each unit?

NTA does not publish a unit-wise split in advance. We counted, with a short script, the Law questions tagged to each unit in the PYQ file on this site: five papers (UGC NET 2021, 2024, June 2025, December 2025 and June 2026) with 100 Law questions each. Tags follow question content, so a few borderline questions could sit in two units. Treat the counts as a guide from five papers, not a rule.

The pattern is that Unit 2 is the single biggest block, a middle group of five units gives about 10 to 12 percent each, and four units give 6 to 9 percent. See the paper-by-paper analysis for how each unit moved from year to year.

Which unit should you study first?

  1. Constitutional and Administrative Law. It is the largest block, and its cases and doctrines recur in other units.
  2. Jurisprudence. Smaller, but it supplies the vocabulary (rights, duties, ownership) used across the paper and is quick to revise.
  3. Public International Law and IHL. A big, memory-heavy unit (treaties, courts, conventions) that rewards early notes and repetition.
  4. Commercial Law. Several Acts in one unit, so start early and revise often.
  5. Environmental Law and Human Rights, IPR and IT Law, Family Law. Statute and case driven, and each gives roughly 10 percent.
  6. Law of Crimes, Torts and Consumer Protection, Comparative Public Law. Smaller blocks. Learn the key principles and cases, and note the new criminal laws in Unit 4.
Priority is not permission to skip

Each of the smallest units still produced about 6 or 7 questions a paper on average. Because there is no negative marking as reported, an informed attempt in every unit is worth more than a perfect score in three.

Do not forget Paper 1

Paper 1 is common to all subjects and carries 100 of the 300 marks. Plan time for it alongside Law. See the Paper 1 syllabus guide and the 70-plus plan.
