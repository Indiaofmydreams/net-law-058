CUET PG is where many LL.M. aspirants begin: one computer-based test, one score, many universities. Before the form opens, it helps to understand who is eligible, what it costs and what you must prepare. This guide groups the questions applicants ask most. For the whole exam, read our CUET PG LLM guide.

What is the eligibility for CUET PG LLM?

NTA asks only for a bachelor's degree or equivalent. For LL.M. admission, each university requires an LL.B. (three-year or five-year integrated), usually with a minimum such as 50 percent, relaxed for reserved categories. Final-year students could apply in 2026. Read your target universities' rules.

The percentage is the part that trips people: NTA does not check it. The university does, at admission. A candidate with a lower percentage can take the exam and still be rejected at the counselling stage.

Is there an age limit for CUET PG LLM?

No. NTA's 2026 bulletin and FAQs state there is no age limit for appearing in CUET (PG). A university may impose its own age or eligibility condition at admission, so check each prospectus before applying.

If you graduated years ago, you can still sit the test. Look for a clause on age, gap years or professional practice in the university's own LL.M. notice.

Can final-year LL.B. students apply for CUET PG LLM?

Yes. Final-year candidates were eligible in the 2026 cycle and the same is expected for 2027. You must complete the degree, with the percentage the university requires, before admission, and produce proof as the university asks.

Keep your semester mark-sheets ready. If your final result arrives after the university's cut-off date for documents, ask in writing whether provisional admission is allowed.

What is the CUET PG application fee?

For 2026 the fee for up to two test papers was Rs 1,400 (General), Rs 1,200 (Gen-EWS, OBC-NCL), Rs 1,100 (SC, ST, Third Gender) and Rs 1,000 (PwBD), with Rs 600 to Rs 700 for each extra paper. The 2027 fee will be in the new bulletin.

When does CUET PG 2027 registration start?

Registration is expected in December 2026, with the Information Bulletin. As of 22 November 2026 NTA had not announced it. The exam is scheduled from 2 March 2027 over 17 days, so the application window will probably close in January.

NTA's revised calendar of 26 October 2026 removed buffer dates and kept the 17-day window. The Law paper's own date and shift come later, in the subject-wise timetable. Last year's LL.M. paper was on 17 March 2026.

How do I apply, step by step?

Register on the NTA portal, fill the form, pick the Law (LL.M.) paper code, upload photo and signature, pay the fee, and use the correction window. Then apply to each university separately after the result. The paper code was COQP14 in recent cycles; do not choose COQP11, which is the LL.B. general test.

  1. Create an account with a valid mobile number and email.
  2. Fill personal, qualification and category details exactly as in your certificates.
  3. Choose the test paper and exam cities.
  4. Upload scanned documents in the format NTA specifies.
  5. Pay and save the confirmation page.
  6. Use the correction window to fix mistakes.
