When the State violates a person's fundamental rights, the Supreme Court can order compensation under Article 32, separate from any criminal case or civil suit. In 2026 several rulings applied that principle with large awards and firm directions to the police. This guide groups them by the question they answer, so you can see the pattern in one place.

What did the Supreme Court order in Lahra Bai Tamre v. State of Chhattisgarh?

On 12 August 2026 Justices Nath and Mehta ordered a CBI probe into a custodial death, action against officials, 25 lakh rupees as interim compensation within four weeks and a report on 13 October 2026.

The facts show why the Court was strict. The man was arrested on 18 January 2024 and died on 21 January 2024. No FIR was registered until 30 July 2026, more than two years later, and the High Court had given only 1 lakh rupees. The Court treated the delay and the low compensation as signs of an inadequate local response and shifted the investigation to the CBI.

What did the Court say in Daudayal v. State of Rajasthan?

Reported around 29 May 2026, the Court awarded 11 lakh rupees for 24 days of illegal detention after a parole order had been passed.

Once a competent authority orders release, further detention has no legal basis. The Court calculated compensation to reflect the loss of liberty and the failure of officials to act on an order. Compare the rule on written grounds in our grounds of arrest post.

What did the Court say in Hari Dutta Sharma v. State of U.P.?

The Court condemned the forcible seizure of a vehicle at 1 am and ordered a refund of 4.5 lakh rupees with 6 per cent interest, 10 lakh rupees in compensation and 50,000 rupees in costs (2026 INSC 998).

This case is about property and dignity, not only liberty. A vehicle taken at night without proper process, for the recovery of money, is an abuse of power. Article 300A protects property from deprivation without authority of law, and Article 21 protects dignity. The refund with interest shows the Court's view that the State must put the person back to where they would have been, and the additional compensation and costs mark the wrong.

Who is responsible for an accused person's safety in custody?

In State of A.P. v. Suda Suresh (2026 INSC 744), the Court said the investigating officer and jail authorities are jointly responsible for the physical safety of accused persons.

The joint responsibility closes a gap where each authority points to the other. A person in police custody or judicial custody is entirely in the State's control, so the State holds a duty of care. This links to the long line of cases on custodial deaths, including D.K. Basu (1997), which set guidelines for arrest and detention, and Nilabati Behera (1993), which allowed compensation as a public law remedy under Articles 32 and 226.

Why is public law compensation different from a civil suit?

Public law compensation is a remedy for violation of a fundamental right, awarded by a constitutional court, without waiting for a full civil trial. It does not replace prosecution or a damages suit.

In Rudul Sah (1983) the Supreme Court first awarded compensation for illegal detention under Article 32, and in Nilabati Behera it explained that sovereign immunity is no defence to this remedy. The State can later recover the amount from the officials responsible. The 2026 cases extend that tradition, with larger sums that reflect present-day values.
