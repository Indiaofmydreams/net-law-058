A fire at a judge's official residence in March 2025 led to a long process that has now produced an inquiry report. Beyond the facts, it is a lesson in how India balances judicial independence with accountability. This guide explains the removal process step by step and what the Yashwant Varma committee reportedly found.

How is a High Court judge removed in India?

Under Articles 217 and 124(4), a judge can be removed only for proved misbehaviour or incapacity, by an order of the President after an address by each House passed by a majority of the total membership and two-thirds of members present and voting.

The high threshold is intended to protect the independence of the judiciary. A judge cannot be removed because of a disagreement with a judgment or because a government dislikes a decision. Article 124(5) lets Parliament regulate the procedure, which it did through the Judges (Inquiry) Act, 1968.

What does the Judges (Inquiry) Act, 1968 provide?

A removal motion needs 100 Lok Sabha or 50 Rajya Sabha members. If admitted, the Speaker or Chairman forms a three-member committee with a Supreme Court judge, a High Court Chief Justice and a distinguished jurist to frame charges and inquire.

StepWhat happens
1Motion signed by 100 LS or 50 RS members
2Speaker or Chairman admits it and forms a committee (Section 3)
3Committee frames charges and gives the judge a chance to respond
4Report goes to the Speaker or Chairman and is laid before the House
5If misbehaviour is found, the House may vote on removal by the special majority

If the committee clears the judge, the motion cannot proceed. Only if the charges are proved does the motion go to a vote.

What did the Yashwant Varma inquiry committee find?

Reports say the committee of Justice Aravind Kumar, Chief Justice Shree Chandrashekhar and Senior Advocate B.V. Acharya found all three charges proved: unexplained currency at his residence, interference with the scene and evasive explanations.

The reported background is that burnt notes were found in a storeroom of the official residence after a fire on the night of 14 to 15 March 2025. The committee reportedly held that the storeroom was under the judge's effective control, that the scene was not preserved and that the judge's explanations changed. It also reportedly said that the judge's withdrawal from the proceedings in April 2026 could not end the inquiry. Reports differ on whether the judge has resigned, and the report was laid before the Lok Sabha on 12 August 2026. What Parliament does next, as far as the sources used here show, is not clear, so check the latest.

What was the in-house procedure?

Before the statutory process, an in-house inquiry by three High Court Chief Justices examined the matter in 2025 under the Supreme Court's 1999 procedure, which lets the Chief Justice of India act on complaints against judges without invoking Parliament.

The in-house procedure grew out of C. Ravichandran Iyer (1995), where the Court said that the judiciary should protect its own integrity within the Constitution. It is not a statutory removal power. It can lead to advice to resign, transfer or withdrawal of work, and the Chief Justice can recommend removal proceedings to the President and Prime Minister, but only Parliament can remove.

Has any Indian judge ever been removed?

No judge has been removed by Parliament so far. The motion against Justice V. Ramaswami failed in the Lok Sabha in 1993, and Justice Soumitra Sen resigned in 2011 before the Lok Sabha could vote.

The K. Veeraswami (1991) decision held that a judge is a public servant under the Prevention of Corruption Act, but that no criminal case can be registered without consulting the Chief Justice of India. That safeguard is part of the same balance. Supporters of reform say the process is too slow and rarely used; defenders say that a hard process protects judges from political pressure.
