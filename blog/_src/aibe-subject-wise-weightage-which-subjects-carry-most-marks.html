Quick summary
  • The 100 AIBE questions are spread across 19 subject groups (as reported for AIBE 22).
  • Constitutional Law, CrPC and CPC carry 10 questions each.
  • Criminal law, Evidence, Family Law and Contract/Property carry 8 each, so seven subjects cover 62 marks.
  • Short subjects add up too: 11 of the 19 groups have 4 questions or fewer.

AIBE is a pass-or-fail exam with a qualifying mark of 45 percent, so you do not need to be strongest everywhere. You need a reliable base in the heavy subjects and enough in the smaller ones to cross the line. This guide shows the weightage as reported for AIBE 22 and how to turn it into a study plan.

The groups that decide your score

36 marksCriminal and civil procedure core: CrPC, CPC, IPC, Evidence
10 marksConstitutional Law, the single largest subject
16 marksFamily Law plus Contract and Property
38 marksEverything else, spread over 12 groups

The four core procedure and criminal subjects alone give 36 questions. Add Constitutional Law and you reach 46 questions, which on paper is above the 45-mark General qualifying line. In practice nobody scores full marks even in strong subjects, so you still need the rest, but it shows where a weak base is most expensive.

How to split your study time

  • About 50 percent of your hours on the seven subjects with 8 to 10 questions each.
  • About 30 percent on Torts, ADR, PIL, Ethics, Labour and Taxation. These repeat patterns and reward revision.
  • About 20 percent on the 2-question subjects. Learn the headline Acts and key definitions, then stop.

Scoring tips by subject type

  • Procedure subjects (CrPC, CPC, Evidence): questions test stages, time limits and who can do what. Make a one-page flow of each. The new laws matter, so use our IPC to BNS and CrPC to BNSS mapping.
  • Constitutional Law: articles and landmark cases. Link each right to its leading case.
  • Professional Ethics and PIL: small syllabus, predictable questions. These are the easiest marks to secure.
  • Short statutes: definitions, authorities and penalties. Skim notes, then practise questions.
