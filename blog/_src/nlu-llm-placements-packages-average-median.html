A placement number sounds precise, but it hides many choices about what to include. This guide explains how to read LLM placement figures and what has been reported.

What do average, median and highest packages mean?

Average is the total pay divided by the number of placed students, so a few big offers can raise it. Median is the middle offer and is a fairer guide. Highest is a single best offer and is not typical.

Ask whether the figure is cost to company or fixed pay, and whether it includes bonuses.

What LLM packages are reported at NLUs?

Reports and aggregator pages put median LLM offers in a range of roughly ₹8 to ₹15 lakh a year across top NLUs, with the highest offers much larger. These numbers vary by year, and we could not confirm each, so use them cautiously.

Why are LLM placements different from LLB?

LLM batches are small, and many students already work or plan to teach, practise or study further. Firms mostly hire at the UG level, so LLM placement is often lateral and smaller in scale.

Some students opt out of placement for PSU, judiciary or academia, which also changes the percentages.

Which sectors hire LLM graduates?

Law firms, corporate legal teams, consultancies, regulators, academia, think tanks and some PSUs. Offers for specialised LLM streams such as tax, IP or ADR depend on the market.

How should I read a placement report?

Check how many students registered, how many were placed, the median, and whether offers include joining bonuses or variable pay. A high average with a low placed share can be misleading.
