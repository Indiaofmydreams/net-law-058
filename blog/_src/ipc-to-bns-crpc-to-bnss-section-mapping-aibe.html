Quick summary
  • The BNS, BNSS and BSA replaced the IPC, CrPC and Evidence Act from 1 July 2024.
  • Learn both numbers for each key section, because exams and judgments still use the old ones.
  • Sizes: BNS has 358 sections, BNSS 531 and BSA 170.
  • Always confirm a section against the bare Act before you rely on it.

For AIBE aspirants, the biggest trap in criminal law is the numbering. Many books and past papers still use IPC and CrPC numbers, while recent questions use the new codes. The table below pairs the most-asked sections. It is a revision aid, not a replacement for the bare Act.

New in the BNS: organised crime and terrorist acts now have their own sections, and murder by a group of five or more on grounds such as race, caste or community is addressed separately. The old sedition offence under section 124A of the IPC has no direct counterpart; the BNS instead has a provision on acts endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity of India.

Note the coincidence that causes mistakes: the old section 482 CrPC (inherent powers) is now section 528 BNSS, while section 482 BNSS is anticipatory bail. Learn them as a pair, not as one number.

The BSA keeps most of the Evidence Act's structure, but numbers have changed across relevancy, admissions, confessions and documentary evidence. Read the Evidence and BSA notes alongside the bare Act, and note the new treatment of electronic and digital records.

How to use this for AIBE

  1. Make a flashcard for each row: old number on one side, new number and one-line idea on the other.
  2. Revise the pairs for ten minutes a day, then do MCQs on the practice sets.
  3. For procedure, learn the stages in order: arrest, FIR, investigation, remand, charge, trial, bail. Our BNSS notes cover each.
  4. If the Bare Act is permitted in your exam, practise finding sections by index so you are fast on the day. Check the AIBE 22 pattern and rules.
