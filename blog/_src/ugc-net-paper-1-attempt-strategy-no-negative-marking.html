Paper 1 is the part of UGC NET every candidate writes, and the part where strategy matters most, because the units are short, the time is limited and there is no negative marking. This guide gives an attempt order, a time budget per unit, ways to handle passages and data sets, and a practice routine, with every question answered.

What is the best strategy to attempt UGC NET Paper 1?

Attempt fast, recall-based units first, then reasoning and data interpretation, and leave the passage for when you are settled. Spend about 60 minutes in all, answer every question since there is no negative marking, and mark doubtful ones for a second look. Never leave a block blank.

Is there negative marking in UGC NET Paper 1?

No. NTA's pattern for UGC NET gives two marks for each correct answer and nothing is deducted for wrong or unattempted questions, as reported. That means you should attempt all 50 Paper 1 questions. Always confirm in the current information bulletin before the exam.

With no penalty, a blind guess has a 25 percent chance of earning two marks and no downside, so a blank is always worse. Contrast this with CLAT PG, AILET and CUET PG, where guessing needs more care.

In what order should I attempt the units in Paper 1?

Start with the units you are strongest in and that need the least time, usually Teaching, Research, ICT and Higher Education. Then do Communication and Environment, then reasoning, then data interpretation and the comprehension passage. If you are confident in reasoning, take it earlier, while your mind is fresh.

In a computer-based test you can usually move between questions, but check NTA's instructions and demo interface for how the sections are presented. A common effective order is: recall units (about 15 minutes), reasoning (about 16 minutes), data interpretation (10 minutes), comprehension (8 minutes), then review. Practise your own order in mocks so that it becomes automatic. The units themselves are covered in our Paper 1 syllabus guide.

How do I handle the comprehension passage and data interpretation?

Read the questions first, then scan the passage for the answers, so that you read with a purpose. In data interpretation, estimate rather than calculate exactly, and eliminate options that are far off. If a set looks long, answer the easy question in it and move on; there is no negative marking.

  • Comprehension: read the five questions, then the passage once, noting where each answer is; do not memorise the passage.
  • Data interpretation: identify what the table or chart actually shows before reading questions; use rounding to estimate.
  • Mathematical reasoning: know the 15 or so common formulae and patterns; skip anything that needs more than two steps unless time is left.
  • Logical reasoning: draw a quick diagram for syllogisms and arrangements.

How do I use the last minutes in Paper 1?

Make sure every question is answered, including guesses. Then revisit the marked questions in order of how close you were. Do not change an answer unless you can name the specific error. Because the same three hours cover Law as well, protect time for Law before you start reviewing Paper 1.

The biggest strategic decision is not inside Paper 1 at all: it is how much of the three hours you give it. See how to split three hours between Paper 1 and Law. For a 30-day improvement plan, read How to score 70+ in UGC NET Paper 1.

Common Paper 1 mistakes

  • Starting with data interpretation and burning ten minutes at the start.
  • Leaving questions blank because the answer is unclear; with no negative marking, always guess.
  • Over-investing in Paper 1 and having too little time for Law, which has twice the marks.
  • Cramming formulae the night before instead of practising them for two weeks.
