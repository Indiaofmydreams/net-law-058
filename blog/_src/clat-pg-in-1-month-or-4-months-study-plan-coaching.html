"How long do I need?" is the question behind most CLAT PG panic. The paper has 120 passage-based questions in 2 hours, drawn from core LLB subjects, so the preparation is about reading speed, principle recall and accuracy more than volume. Here is how four months and one month each look, and how to decide about coaching. Timelines are counted back from your exam date, so check the date in the Consortium's notification for your cycle.

Is 4 months enough for CLAT PG?

Yes, for most LLB graduates. Four months is about 16 weeks, enough to learn the core subjects, revise them and build exam-day stamina. It assumes you have studied the LLB subjects before and can give about 3 to 4 hours on weekdays and 6 hours on weekends.

WeeksGoalWhat to do
1 to 2BaselineTake one full mock cold. Read the syllabus guide. Start a daily 30-minute passage routine.
3 to 8Core subjectsConstitutional Law, Jurisprudence, criminal law (BNS, BNSS, BSA), Contract, Torts. Notes plus 20 passage questions per subject.
9 to 12Remaining subjects and casesFamily, Property, Company, Administrative, Public International, Tax, Environmental, Labour. Learn the landmark cases.
13 to 15Mocks and repairTwo full mocks a week. Spend as long reviewing as testing. Fix your top three error types.
16Final revisionError log, case names, principles. Follow the last-week plan.

If you start with weak English comprehension, add 45 minutes a day of long-form reading (judgments, editorials) from week 1.

Can I prepare for CLAT PG in 1 month?

Yes, you can attempt it well, but be realistic about the goal. One month works if your LLB basics are fresh and you triage ruthlessly. It is less likely to deliver a top-NLU rank unless your base is already strong.

DaysFocus
1 to 3One full mock to find your level. Learn the pattern and the 5-step passage method.
4 to 12Constitutional Law and Jurisprudence, then the new criminal laws. Notes in the morning, 30 passage questions in the evening.
13 to 20Contract, Torts, Family and Property. Key cases only.
21 to 26A mock every second day with full review. Skim Company, Administrative, International, Tax, Environmental and Labour at headline level.
27 to 30Error log, case names, one last mock, rest.
  • Do not start new books. Use one set of notes per subject.
  • Passage practice every single day, even on revision days.
  • Attempt only questions where you can eliminate at least one option, because 0.25 negative marking punishes blind guessing.

Which coaching is best for CLAT PG?

We do not rank coaching institutes. There is no official ranking, advertised results are hard to verify and the right choice depends on your learning style and budget. Coaching is optional, and many candidates succeed through self-study. If you are considering a programme, check these before you pay:

  • A demo class. Do the teaching and explanations make sense to you?
  • Mock quality. Are mocks passage-based, close to the 120-question, 2-hour pattern, with explanations for wrong options?
  • Doubt support. How fast and how well are doubts answered?
  • Refund and batch terms. Read them in writing.
  • Verifiable results. Treat unnamed "toppers" and unverifiable claims with caution, and ask what share of batch students attempt the exam.

What a smart self-study routine looks like

  1. Morning (90 minutes): one subject, notes first, then principles in your own words.
  2. Afternoon (45 minutes): case recall: facts, issue, held, principle.
  3. Evening (60 minutes): a timed set of passage questions, then a written error log.
  4. Weekly: one or two full mocks, with the review taking as long as the test.
