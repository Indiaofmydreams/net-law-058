Why the "one minute a question" rule matters

Because every question rests on a passage, reading time is the real cost. Two hours for 120 questions leaves no spare minutes for slow reading. In practice, a good attempt looks like this:

  • First pass (about 75 minutes): answer everything you can solve confidently. Skip anything that needs a long calculation of doctrine or a second reading.
  • Second pass (about 35 minutes): return to the skipped questions and eliminate options.
  • Last 10 minutes: bubble carefully and check that the OMR matches your paper. Do not leave this to the final minute.

The negative-marking maths: when to guess

A wrong answer costs 0.25 and a right answer earns 1. So a guess has a positive expected value when your chance of being right is above 20 percent.

Options you can rule outChance of a correct guessExpected marks per guess
None (blind guess)25%+0.06
One option33%+0.17
Two options50%+0.38

The lesson: do not guess blindly in bulk, but do guess when you can honestly remove at least one option. Random guessing on 30 questions gains almost nothing and adds risk.
