Salary is the first thing candidates ask about PSU jobs, and the second is why the numbers on the internet do not match. The answer lies in how PSU pay is built. This guide explains the structure and gives cautious ranges.

What are E-1 and E-2 pay scales in PSUs?

They are executive grades in the Industrial Dearness Allowance (IDA) pay system used by many central PSUs. E-1 and E-2 are entry or early executive scales, with basic pay bands that differ by company, so check the notice for the exact figures.

What is the IDA pay structure?

Basic pay rises by increments each year, and a dearness allowance linked to the consumer price index is added. On top come HRA or housing, a perks allowance, medical benefits, provident fund, gratuity and often performance-related pay.

Since DA moves with inflation, the gross figure rises between pay revisions. Companies set their own perk rules within DPE guidelines.

What is the difference between CTC and in-hand pay?

CTC (cost to company) counts everything the employer spends, including PF contribution, gratuity, perks, medical and performance pay. In-hand pay is what reaches your account after tax and deductions, so it is lower, sometimes by a third.

For example, a CTC of ₹15 lakh a year is not ₹1.25 lakh a month in hand. Employee PF, tax, perks that are paid as cost and performance pay timing all change the monthly figure. Ask HR for a sample salary slip.

What do law officers in PSUs earn at entry level?

Reports and aggregator sites put the early annual CTC for entry-level law officers roughly in the ₹10 to ₹18 lakh range, depending on company and scale. These are estimates and not guaranteed, so check the notice.

We could not confirm these figures from each company's own salary data, so they should be treated as a rough guide only.

How does pay grow over time?

Pay grows through yearly increments, promotions after a fixed number of years, and revisions at pay revision cycles. Promotion in PSUs is largely time-bound with performance review, which makes growth predictable but slower than at top firms.
