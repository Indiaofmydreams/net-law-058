A score of 70 or more in Paper 1 gives you a strong start on the 300-mark total, and it is within reach in a month if you work on the units that respond to practice. Here is the arithmetic, the units to attack first and a day-by-day plan. The pattern is as reported from NTA material, so confirm it in the Information Bulletin.

How many questions do you need for 70 in Paper 1?

Paper 1 is reported to have 50 questions of 2 marks each, for 100 marks. So 70 marks needs 35 correct answers, which means you can miss 15. Because there is no negative marking as reported, a blank earns nothing, so attempt every question and use elimination.

Where do marks come fastest?

In the four Paper 1 papers on this site (2021, 2024, June 2025, December 2025), two units arrived as a single block of five linked questions: Comprehension (one passage) and Data Interpretation (one table or data set). That matters, because a skill you practise lifts five questions at once. See the Comprehension and Data Interpretation unit pages.

  • Skill units (practice-led): Comprehension, Data Interpretation, Mathematical Reasoning, Logical Reasoning.
  • Fact units (revision-led): Higher Education System, ICT, People, Development and Environment, Communication.
  • Concept units: Teaching Aptitude and Research Aptitude, where definitions and the sequence of steps recur.

Habits that move the score

  1. Time every set. Paper 1 is reported to share a 3-hour window with Paper 2, so aim for about 40 to 45 minutes for Paper 1.
  2. Learn the formats. Match-the-list, assertion-reason, statement and "arrange in order" questions appear across units in the papers on this site, so practise reading them quickly.
  3. Keep an error log. Write the unit, the topic and whether you lacked knowledge or misread.
  4. Attempt everything. With no negative marking as reported, never leave a blank.
