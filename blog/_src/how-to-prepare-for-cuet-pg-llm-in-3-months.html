Three months is enough for CUET PG LLM if you are already an LL.B. graduate, because the paper covers what you have studied. The plan must be ruthless about priorities: 30-plus subjects cannot all be studied with equal depth. This guide groups the preparation questions into one plan. How do I prepare for CUET PG LLM in 3 months?

Spend month one on the core subjects, month two on the regular subjects and bare acts, and month three on mocks, previous questions and revision. Keep a case-and-section notebook and review every wrong answer weekly.

Which books are best for CUET PG LLM?

Commonly recommended: M.P. Jain or D.D. Basu for the Constitution, Fitzgerald's Salmond or Dias for Jurisprudence, Avtar Singh for Contract and Company Law, Bangia for Torts, Paras Diwan for family law, plus bare acts for the BNS, BNSS and BSA. Your college notes often suffice.

Do not buy a book per subject. For the occasional subjects, a good short guide or your LL.B. notes are enough. Always use an edition that reflects the new criminal codes, or read the BNS, BNSS and BSA bare acts with a concordance table.

How many hours should I study for CUET PG LLM?

Five to six focused hours a day for three months is a workable target for a working LL.B. graduate, and four hours if you are already revising your degree subjects. Consistency and weekly timed tests matter more than long single sessions.

  • Two hours of reading or notes.
  • One hour of bare act or case-file work.
  • One to two hours of MCQs and review.
  • Weekends: a full 75-question paper and its review.

Do I need coaching for CUET PG LLM?

Not necessarily. The paper tests standard LL.B. content, so notes, bare acts, textbooks and regular timed practice are enough for many candidates. Coaching helps with structure and discipline; the deciding factor is how many papers you solve and review.

If you do join a course, still keep your own mistake notebook. Our Practice Arena builds one automatically.

How many mock tests should I take for CUET PG LLM?

Take at least eight to ten full 75-question papers, one a week from month two and two a week in the last fortnight, and spend as long reviewing each as taking it. Track three numbers: attempts, accuracy and marks lost to wrong answers.

How do I avoid losing marks to negative marking?

Leave a question blank when you cannot remove at least two options, and go back to mark it only if time remains. With +4 and -1, a 50 percent guess is worth +1.5 marks on average; a 25 percent guess is worth +0.25. Use our score planner to test strategies.

What should I revise in the last week?

Revise your one-page section and case sheets, the old-to-new criminal code concordance, your wrong-answer notebook and recent Acts. Do not start new subjects. Sleep, and take one light paper two days before your exam.
