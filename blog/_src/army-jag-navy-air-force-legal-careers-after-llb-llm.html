For those who want a uniformed career, JAG is the main legal route. It is small, selective and demanding, but it offers a different life from an office job. This guide covers what has been reported.

What is the Army JAG Entry Scheme?

It recruits law graduates as commissioned officers in the Judge Advocate General's department through the Short Service Commission. Notices appear for courses such as the 124th and 125th, with a small number of vacancies for men and women.

Work covers military law, court-martial proceedings, legal advice to commanders and international law.

What is the eligibility?

An LLB with at least 55% marks, enrolment eligibility with a Bar Council, and an age range around 21 to 27, as reported in recent notices. Check the current notice, since age and marks rules are fixed there.

How is selection done?

Applicants are shortlisted by marks and, in some cycles, a CLAT PG score, then go through an SSB interview at a selection centre, followed by a medical and a merit list. Read the notice for the exact method used.

The SSB tests personality and officer-like qualities over several days and needs separate preparation. Our eligibility guide explains CLAT PG score validity.

What are pay and career?

Officers start as Lieutenant with pay in Level 10 of the pay matrix, reported at about ₹56,100 basic plus military service pay and allowances. Terms include a short service commission which may be extended or made permanent.

Permanent commission rules and post-service options depend on policy in force.

What about the Navy and Air Force?

Both have legal branches or recruit law officers through their own notices. Vacancies are very few and conditions differ, so check the Navy and Air Force career pages each year.
