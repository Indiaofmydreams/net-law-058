Quick answers
  • Pattern: 75 questions, 90 minutes, +4 and −1.
  • Three passes: sure answers, two-option answers, review.
  • Break-even: 20 percent accuracy; skip when you cannot remove two options.
  • Mocks: eight to ten, with rising accuracy before rising attempts.

CUET PG LLM looks easy: 75 direct questions in 90 minutes. But with plus 4 and minus 1 marking and 30-plus subjects, how you attempt the paper matters as much as what you know. This guide gives a three-pass method, subject order, pacing checkpoints, a mock ladder and a review method built for the Law (LL.M.) paper.

How should I attempt the CUET PG LLM paper in 90 minutes?

Use three passes. Spend about 45 minutes answering only questions you are sure of, 30 minutes on questions where you can remove two options, and 15 minutes checking marked answers. Leave pure guesses blank. With 75 questions in 90 minutes you have about 72 seconds per question on average.

The 2026 Law (LL.M.) paper was 90 minutes with 75 compulsory questions and +4, −1 marking, as set out in our CUET PG exam pattern guide. The test is computer-based, available in English and Hindi, and because questions are standalone you can move freely between them.

2About 30 minMarked questions where you can remove two options for a specific reason

How does plus 4 and minus 1 marking change my attempt strategy?

A wrong answer costs a quarter of a right one, so break-even accuracy is 20 percent. Guessing with two options removed earns about 1.5 marks on average; a blind guess only 0.25 with high swings. Aim for 60 to 70 attempts at high accuracy rather than 75 with guesses.

Use the planner in our CUET PG paper analysis or the interactive score planner. For example, 66 attempts with 50 right and 16 wrong scores 184 out of 300; 75 attempts with 52 right and 23 wrong scores 185, almost the same score with nine more risky questions. The general maths is in the negative marking guide.

In what order should I attempt subjects in CUET PG LLM?

Attempt Constitutional Law, Criminal Law, Contract, Torts and Jurisprudence first, because they are your core and the largest blocks, then regular subjects, then occasional ones. The paper is not divided into sections, so you can reorder as you like. Skip a subject you find hard and return if time allows.

The core subjects supply roughly half of the paper in our editorial estimate, and are the ones where your accuracy should be highest. Starting with them banks marks early and settles nerves. Occasional subjects such as Banking, Insurance, Taxation and Competition give one or two questions each: attempt those only when the answer is clear. See the subject-wise syllabus for the tiers.

What pacing checkpoints should I use in a CUET PG mock?

Finish the first pass by minute 45, the second by minute 75 and the review by minute 85, leaving a five-minute buffer. If you are behind at minute 30 with fewer than 35 questions answered, speed up recall questions and move straight past statement-based items on first pass.

ClockTarget
20 minAround 25 questions answered or marked
45 minFirst pass complete over all 75 questions
75 minSecond pass complete
85 minReview done; five-minute buffer

On a computer-based test, use the question palette to see which questions you have answered, marked for review or not visited. Practise with an on-screen format if you can: reading statement questions on screen is slower than on paper for many people.

How many mock tests should I take and what should I target?

Take eight to ten full mocks, one every ten days early on and one a week later, with two a week in the last fortnight. Target rising accuracy first, then attempts. Aim to finish your last mocks above about 70 percent of the marks, since that range kept most options open in our editorial reading.

Cut-offs depend on each university and category; there is no single score to chase. The practical goal is stable accuracy above 80 percent on attempted questions with 60 to 70 attempts. Follow the ladder below, and use the 3-month plan for the subject schedule.

Reviewing a CUET PG mock

Use the error log in our mock analysis guide and add two CUET PG columns: subject tier (core, regular, occasional) and whether the question was a blind guess. After three mocks, look at the share of wrong answers that were blind guesses and the share in occasional subjects. If either is high, you are paying for attempts you should have skipped.

  • Core subject errors: the highest priority; fix before the next mock.
  • Occasional subject errors: often best handled by skipping; learn the basics only.
  • Blind guess errors: tighten your rule; require two options removed.
  • Misreads: underline qualifiers in statement and assertion-reason questions.

Three habits that raise CUET PG scores

  • A written pass plan on your rough sheet before you start.
  • A strict rule for blind guesses that you decided before the exam.
  • A weekly mock with a full review the next day.
