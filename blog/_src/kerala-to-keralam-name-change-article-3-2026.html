States get renamed through a particular constitutional route, and exam setters like it. The proposal to rename Kerala as Keralam is a clean example. This guide explains the process, the articles and the cases.

What is the Kerala (Alteration of Name) Bill, 2026?

It proposes to rename the State of Kerala as Keralam. It was passed in the Monsoon Session 2026 and was introduced after a reference to the state legislature under the proviso to Article 3.

The new name reflects the Malayalam form of the state's name. Renaming requires amending the First Schedule of the Constitution, which lists the states.

Which Article lets Parliament change a state's name?

Article 3 allows Parliament by law to form new states, and to alter the areas, boundaries or names of existing states. Article 4 says such laws are not constitutional amendments under Article 368.

ArticleRole
Article 3Parliament may alter a state's name, area or boundaries
Proviso to Article 3Bill needs President's recommendation and a reference to the state legislature
Article 4Not an Article 368 amendment; First Schedule changes follow

Is the state's consent required?

Under the proviso to Article 3 the Bill must be referred by the President to the state legislature for its views. The views are not binding on Parliament.

This was settled in Babulal Parate v. State of Bombay (1960), where the Court held that Parliament need not accept the state legislature's views and that reference is enough. The Constitution thus lets the Union reshape states, a feature that gives India the description "indestructible Union of destructible states".

Does renaming a state need a special majority?

No. Article 4 provides that laws under Article 3 are not amendments of the Constitution for Article 368, so a simple majority suffices, though the First Schedule is amended.

Compare that with the special-majority route in the failed 131st Amendment Bill, covered in our post on the 131st Amendment.

Has India renamed states before?

Yes. Madras became Tamil Nadu in 1969, Orissa became Odisha in 2011 and Uttaranchal became Uttarakhand in 2007, using the same constitutional route.

Orissa's renaming was accompanied by a constitutional amendment in the case of the language name for Oriya. Check specific cases before quoting details, because exam questions often play on those differences.
