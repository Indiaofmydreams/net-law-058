Quick answer
  • The Kovind committee (March 2024) recommended simultaneous elections in two phases.
  • The Constitution (129th Amendment) Bill, 2024 would insert Article 82A and amend Articles 83, 172 and 327.
  • A Joint Parliamentary Committee is examining it; its chair has spoken of a 2029 rollout.
  • Core debate: cost and stability versus federalism and accountability.

Few proposals divide constitutional lawyers as sharply as holding every election in the country in one cycle. Supporters see savings and stable governance; critics see a threat to federalism. This guide explains what is proposed, where the Bill stands and how each side argues. It is an explainer and takes no side.

What is One Nation One Election?

It is a proposal to hold Lok Sabha and State Assembly elections together, and later local body elections, so that voters choose all governments in one cycle. It needs constitutional amendments, because Articles 83 and 172 fix the terms of the legislatures.

Until 1967, India held simultaneous elections as a matter of course. The cycle broke when some Assemblies were dissolved early and the Lok Sabha was dissolved in 1970 and 1979. Support for returning to a single cycle has come from the Law Commission's 170th Report (1999), a parliamentary committee in 2015 and NITI Aayog in 2017.

What did the Kovind committee recommend?

In March 2024 the committee led by former President Ram Nath Kovind recommended simultaneous Lok Sabha and State elections in the first phase and local body elections within 100 days after that, with a single electoral roll and a new Article 82A.

StepRecommendation
Phase 1Lok Sabha and State Assembly elections together
Phase 2Municipal and panchayat elections within 100 days
Hung HouseFresh election only for the unexpired term
Electoral rollOne common roll and voter ID
RatificationNeeded from half the States for the local body part

The unexpired term idea answers a key question: if a government falls midway, the new House serves only the rest of the cycle, so the schedule stays aligned.

What is the 129th Constitution Amendment Bill, 2024?

It was introduced on 17 December 2024 to insert Article 82A and amend Articles 83, 172 and 327 so that elections can be held together. It was referred to a Joint Parliamentary Committee, which has had its term extended several times.

A companion Bill amends laws for Union Territories. The Bill gives the President power to fix an "appointed date" after a general election, from which the terms of State Assemblies end with the Lok Sabha term. The Joint Committee is chaired by P.P. Chaudhary. Reports from July 2026 say that he spoke of a possible rollout by the 2029 elections and that the committee had visited several States. Reports also quote a figure of about 7 lakh crore rupees as an economic loss from separate elections, which critics dispute. The committee's final report and any vote in Parliament are still to come, as far as the sources used here show.

What are the main arguments in favour?

Supporters point to cost, administrative time lost to the model code of conduct, security deployment and "permanent campaign mode", and argue that governments can focus on long-term policy if elections come once in five years.

They also note that the Constitution does not prescribe separate cycles, and that Parliament has power to amend Articles 83 and 172 by the special procedure in Article 368. Practical safeguards such as the unexpired-term rule are meant to answer concerns on stability.

What are the main arguments against?

Critics argue that cutting short an Assembly's term overrides the mandate voters gave, that national issues will dominate State polls and that the plan weakens federalism, which the Court held to be part of the basic structure in S.R. Bommai (1994) and Kesavananda Bharati (1973).

They add that the logistics of more voting machines and security forces are heavy, and that early dissolution under Article 356 is restricted after Bommai, so a synchronised cycle may not be sustainable in practice. A balanced answer notes that the basic structure test applies to amendments, and the Court would decide whether this amendment crosses the line only if it is challenged after enactment.
