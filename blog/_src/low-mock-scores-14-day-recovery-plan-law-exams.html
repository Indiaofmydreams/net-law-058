Every aspirant has a bad mock. The difference between those who recover and those who spiral is what they do in the next fourteen days. This guide gives a diagnosis method, a day-by-day recovery plan and a way to decide honestly whether to attempt or postpone, for any of the exams on this site.

Why are my law mock test scores so low?

Low mock scores usually come from one of four causes: knowledge gaps, misreading questions, poor elimination and time pressure. Most aspirants assume it is always the first, but misreads and pacing often account for a third of lost marks. Classify your errors before you decide what to study.

The cause decides the remedy. A candidate who loses marks to unfamiliar topics needs more study; a candidate who loses marks to misreading needs a slower first read and an underlining habit; a candidate who loses to time needs pacing drills. Spending two weeks reading more when the problem is technique is the commonest and costliest mistake. Use the error log from our mock analysis guide on your last three mocks to find the split.

How can I improve my mock test score in two weeks?

Fix the cheapest errors first: misreads and time pressure respond within days, while knowledge gaps need weeks. Follow a fixed daily cycle: 90 minutes on your top two weak topics, 40 fresh questions, same-day review and a short recall session. Take two full mocks in the fortnight.

How many marks can I realistically improve in two weeks?

A realistic gain is a few marks to ten or so, mostly from fixing careless errors and skipping bad guesses. Large jumps of thirty or more marks in two weeks are rare, and usually come from candidates whose scores were held down by technique, not knowledge. Aim for steady improvement across mocks.

In a 120-mark exam such as CLAT PG, each wrong answer turned into a right one is worth 1.25 marks, so converting eight careless errors is worth about ten marks. That is a good outcome for two weeks. Expect more if your errors are mostly misreads and less if they are mostly gaps. Treat any promise of a guaranteed score jump with suspicion.

Should I postpone my law exam if my mock scores are low?

Decide with evidence: if your recent mock scores are below the usual cut-off range for your target and your errors are knowledge gaps in core subjects, postponing may be wise. If they are close to target and the errors are technique, attempt the exam. A low score alone is not a reason to skip.

Check each exam's attempt limits before deciding: our eligibility guide reports no limit on CLAT PG attempts, and most of these exams run every year. Appearing once costs you the fee and a few hours; it also gives you a real paper to analyse.

How do I stay motivated after a bad mock score?

Separate the score from your worth: it is one reading under one set of conditions. Write down the top three causes, pick one fix for tomorrow, and do it. Motivation follows action. Avoid comparing yourself with others' scores online, which usually makes things worse.

  • Use the 24-hour rule: no big decisions about the exam for a day after a bad mock.
  • Review first, feel second: a structured error log turns a bad result into a to-do list.
  • Track the trend over five mocks, not one.
  • Talk to someone, a friend, mentor or counsellor, if low mood is lasting more than a couple of weeks.

Common recovery mistakes

  • Taking more mocks to feel better. A mock without a review is just a mood swing.
  • Switching books and coaching every week. Each change resets your learning curve.
  • Studying only your favourite subjects after a bad score, to feel competent.
  • Studying longer hours at the cost of sleep. Memory consolidation needs sleep, and tired candidates misread more.

A note on the first mocks

Most aspirants score poorly in their first two or three mocks. This is expected: you are meeting new question styles, pacing and negative marking. The useful question is not 'why is my score low?' but 'is it moving?' If your fifth mock is better than your first, your preparation is working, even if the absolute number still looks low.
