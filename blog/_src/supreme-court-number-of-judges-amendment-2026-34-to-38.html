  • An ordinance in May 2026 came first; the bill replaced it in the Monsoon Session.
  • Article 124(1) lets Parliament fix the number by law.
  • Pendency stood at 94,313 cases on 30 June 2026.

The size of the Supreme Court is not fixed in the Constitution. It is set by Parliament, and 2026 saw another increase. This guide explains how the number is decided, what changed this year and why the increase matters beyond arithmetic.

How many judges does the Supreme Court have in 2026?

The sanctioned strength is now 38 including the Chief Justice of India, raised from 34 by an ordinance in May 2026 and then by the Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Bill, 2026. The sitting strength depends on vacancies and appointments.

Five judges took oath on 2 June 2026, and three vacancies remained at that point. In October three more judges, Justices Sunita Agarwal, D.K. Upadhyaya and A.K. Singh, were sworn in after Collegium recommendations. A September Collegium recommendation noted these elevations would bring the sitting strength to 37. A gap between sanctioned and working strength is normal because of retirements and the time appointments take.

Who decides the number of Supreme Court judges?

Article 124(1) says the Supreme Court consists of a Chief Justice and, until Parliament by law prescribes a larger number, not more than seven other judges. Parliament has raised the number several times through the Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Act.

Constitution (1950)8
After the 2019 amendment34
After the 2026 amendment38

The mechanism is a simple law, not a constitutional amendment, so it needs only an ordinary majority. That is why the Government could first act by ordinance under Article 123 when Parliament was not in session and then bring a bill.

Why was the number of judges increased?

The Court has a very large backlog, with 94,313 cases pending on 30 June 2026 according to the National Judicial Data Grid. More judges allow more benches, including for Constitution Bench matters, which also have a heavy pendency.

As of mid-2026 there were 22 five-judge matters, 5 seven-judge matters and 2 nine-judge matters pending, with many more tagged cases behind them. Constitution Benches take several judges out of regular work for long periods, so extra strength helps keep ordinary benches running.

How are Supreme Court judges appointed?

Judges are appointed by the President under Article 124(2) after consultation with judges, in practice through the Collegium system. The Collegium recommends names, and the Government processes and notifies them.

The system comes from the Three Judges Cases and was left standing when the National Judicial Appointments Commission was struck down in 2015. The Collegium system continues to attract debate, which was renewed in 2026 by a former judge's memoir.

Will more judges end the pendency?

More judges help but do not solve it alone. Pendency also depends on case management, listing, vacancies, procedure and the number of new cases filed each year. A larger bench strength mainly increases the Court's capacity to hear matters.

Related reforms matter too. A National Tribunals Commission, for example, could reduce delay in the tribunal system that feeds appeals to the Court. See our explainer on the Tribunals Reforms Bill, 2026.
