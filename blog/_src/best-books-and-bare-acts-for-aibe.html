AIBE tests whether you know the working law, so the books that matter are the Acts themselves. The exam is reported to allow printed bare acts, which makes speed in finding a provision as important as memory. We have not verified any AIBE-specific guide book, so we name none. Instead, here is what to read, what to carry, and how to avoid losing time in the hall.

Can you carry bare acts in AIBE?

One preparation guide reports that printed bare acts and study material are allowed, and that handwritten notes are not. Other sources have described the rules differently across cycles, and our own AIBE 22 guide advises carrying only clean copies and treating anything not clearly permitted as not allowed. The safe approach is to read the instructions in your admit card, and to prepare as if you may have only your memory.

Which textbooks are worth using?

Because the questions are direct, one concise source per subject is enough. For depth in a weak area, standard law textbooks that preparation guides commonly cite include D.D. Basu and J.N. Pandey for the Constitution, R.K. Bangia for torts and contract, and Paras Diwan for Hindu family law. Take care with older criminal-law textbooks written on the IPC and CrPC. They are still useful for concepts, but you must map them to the new codes using our section mapping.

How to prepare for an open-book style exam

  1. Learn the structure of each Act. Know the chapters, so you can open the right page in seconds.
  2. Practise with the book open and a timer. 100 questions in 3 hours 30 minutes is generous, but searching is slow.
  3. Memorise the high-yield facts. Time limits, punishments and definitions should not need looking up.
  4. Keep the book clean. Do not rely on notes or annotations being permitted.

Drill with the practice arena, and see the previous year questions guide for how to review.

Verify the rule and the edition

The open-book rule is as reported and has been described differently in different sources. Confirm it in your admit card and on the Bar Council's official notice, and use current editions of every Act.
