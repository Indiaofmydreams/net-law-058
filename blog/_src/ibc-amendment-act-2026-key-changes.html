The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code is among the most heavily tested commercial laws, and the 2026 amendment is its biggest rewrite in years. It tackles delay at admission, loosens the strict control of the debtor in some cases and adds frameworks for corporate groups and cross-border cases. This guide explains each major change.

What is the IBC Amendment Act, 2026?

It is an amendment to the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 that received assent on 6 April 2026. It changes withdrawal under Section 12A, adds a creditor-initiated resolution process, sets admission timelines and adds frameworks for group and cross-border insolvency.

What changed in Section 12A?

Section 12A is substituted. Withdrawal of an application is allowed only after the committee of creditors is constituted and before the first invitation for resolution plans, with approval of 90 per cent of the committee.

Earlier, withdrawal could happen at various stages, and the Supreme Court had allowed it in limited circumstances, including through its Article 142 power in settlements. The new text fixes a window, which gives certainty but reduces room for last-minute settlements.

What is the creditor-initiated insolvency resolution process?

A new Chapter IV-A (Sections 58A to 58K) allows creditors to start a resolution process in which the debtor remains in possession, unlike the standard CIRP where an interim professional takes over management.

The idea is to preserve value in businesses where management is still useful and where creditors prefer a faster, less disruptive route. It sits beside the standard CIRP, and the details of eligibility and procedure are in the new chapter.

How soon must the NCLT admit or reject an application?

Under the amended Section 7(5) the Adjudicating Authority must admit or reject within 14 days, recording reasons if it takes longer. Sections 9(5) and 10(4) carry similar timelines.

Delay at the admission stage has been a long-standing problem, with applications waiting months. A 14-day rule, with a duty to record reasons for delay, aims to improve discipline. Note too that appeals to the NCLAT must now be filed within three months under Section 61(6).

What are the new group insolvency and cross-border provisions?

A new Chapter VA with Section 59A deals with group insolvency, Section 240C creates a cross-border insolvency framework and Section 240B provides for an electronic portal.

Other changes touch the committee of creditors: Section 21(11) lets the CoC supervise liquidation, Section 22(3)(a) deems the resolution professional appointed, Sections 34 and 34A allow the CoC to replace the liquidator and Section 5(28) limits voting share to eligible financial creditors. Section 28A deals with guarantors' assets. Together these shift more control to creditors.

How can this be asked in UGC NET Law, CLAT PG or AIBE?

Expect questions on Section 7, 9 and 10 applications, the CoC, Section 12A and timelines.
