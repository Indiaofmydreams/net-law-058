Quick answers
  • Eligibility: LLB (3-year or 5-year integrated), 50% marks, 45% for SC, ST and PwD (as reported). Final-year students have been allowed to apply.
  • Pattern: 100 MCQs, 100 marks, 120 minutes, offline, English.
  • Marking: +1 for a right answer, -0.25 for a wrong one, nothing for a blank.
  • Syllabus: thirteen branches of law, with no published weightage.

AILET is the entrance test of National Law University Delhi, and the LL.M. paper (also called AILET PG) is the only route into its one-year LL.M. This guide answers the questions aspirants search most: who can apply, what the paper looks like and what you must study. Where a fact is official it is marked so; the rest is "as reported" by admission-news sites, so check it on NLU Delhi's website before you rely on it. Our AILET exam guide carries the full picture.

What is the AILET LLM exam pattern?

The paper is a single section of objective questions. There is no descriptive part in the current format.

Older articles describe a different format with more questions and less time. Third-party sites disagree on when the format changed, so treat anything that does not say 100 questions and 120 minutes as outdated, and check the front page of any past paper you solve.

Is there negative marking in AILET LLM?

Yes: 0.25 mark off for each wrong answer. Four wrong answers wipe out one right one, so the break-even point is a one-in-five chance of being right. A quick example: 80 attempted, 60 right and 20 wrong gives 60 - 5 = 55 marks. A pure four-option guess (one in four) is only marginally positive on average, so guess when you can remove at least one option, and leave a question blank when you cannot.

What is the AILET LLM syllabus?

NLU Delhi does not publish a topic list. It states that the questions come from different branches of law, and the branches named in the scheme are:

  1. Constitutional Law
  2. Jurisprudence
  3. Administrative Law
  4. Law of Contract
  5. Law of Torts
  6. Family Law
  7. Criminal Law
  8. Property Law
  9. Company Law
  10. Public International Law
  11. Tax Law
  12. Environmental Law
  13. Labour and Industrial Law

Sites that analyse past papers also report questions from allied areas such as evidence, procedure, arbitration and intellectual property, though no official weightage exists. Our syllabus and tracker page splits each branch into topics and cases, marked clearly as our own breakdown and not an official list.

Which subjects should you start with?

In our reading of past-paper analyses, Constitutional Law is usually the largest block, followed by Jurisprudence, Criminal Law, Contract and Torts. That is a trend, not a rule, and it moves year to year. A safe plan is to cover the five core branches first, then the regular ones (Administrative, Family, Property, Company, Public International Law) and leave tax, environment and labour for the last stretch. For the criminal-law portion, learn the new codes along with their old section numbers.

How is AILET LLM different from CLAT PG?

The marks and time look similar, but the feel is not. AILET LLM has 100 standalone questions on legal knowledge, while CLAT PG has 120 passage-based questions. Read our comparison of AILET LLM and CLAT PG, or the CLAT PG pattern guide.
