Environmental law is one of the most consistently tested areas in law exams, and 2026 added important rulings. This guide explains the Vanashakti decision on environmental clearance, the green crackers order and the continuing M.C. Mehta litigation.

What did Vanashakti v. Union of India (2026) decide?

In July 2026 the Supreme Court held that prior environmental clearance is mandatory, that projects started without it generally cannot be regularised afterwards, and it upheld the 2017 Notification.

The debate was over "ex post facto" clearance, where a project builds first and seeks approval later. The Court's view is that prior clearance is the heart of the environmental impact assessment scheme under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 and the EIA rules. Allowing retrospective approval would defeat its purpose. The 2017 Notification, which provided a one-time window for such projects, was upheld.

Are green crackers allowed in Delhi-NCR in 2026?

In Arjun Gopal v. Union of India (29 October 2026) the Court allowed certified green crackers in Delhi-NCR on 7 and 8 November 2026 under conditions, with the ban restored from 9 November.

The case continues a decade of orders starting with Arjun Gopal (2017) and later directions that balance livelihood and tradition against air pollution. The right to a clean environment is part of Article 21.

What are the M.C. Mehta suo motu petitions registered in 2026?

Four M.C. Mehta suo motu petitions were registered on 11 March 2026, including on the Taj Trapezium, continuing the Court's long-running supervision of environmental protection.

M.C. Mehta v. Union of India is a series of cases that created much of India's environmental jurisprudence, including absolute liability (Oleum gas leak case, 1987). The Taj Trapezium Zone was created to protect the monument from industrial pollution.

What happened on illegal sand mining in the Chambal?

On illegal sand mining in the National Chambal Sanctuary, the Central Empowered Committee recommended a three-year phase-out.

The Chambal is a habitat for the gharial and other protected species. Mining within a protected area raises questions under the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972 and the Environment (Protection) Act.

What principles guide environmental law cases?

Indian courts apply the precautionary principle, the polluter pays principle and sustainable development, all read into Article 21 and Article 48A. These principles come from cases such as Vellore Citizens and M.C. Mehta.
