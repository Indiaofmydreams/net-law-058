Exam day is where the work of weeks becomes marks, and where small mistakes decide the result. A wrong barcode, a late arrival, a burst of panic can cost more than a gap in knowledge. This guide walks through the night before, what to carry, the first ten minutes, OMR and computer-based tips and the last ten minutes for the exams on this site.

What should I do the night before a law exam?

Pack your bag with the admit card, ID and permitted items, check the route and reporting time, eat a normal dinner and sleep at your usual time. Do only a light skim of your sheets for an hour. Do not take a mock, start a new topic or stay up late.

  • Documents: printed admit card (two copies if the notice asks), valid photo ID, passport photographs if required.
  • Writing items: only those the instructions allow; for OMR exams, the pen type specified.
  • Clothing: comfortable and compliant with the dress rules in the notice.
  • Route: plan to reach the centre well before reporting time and note an alternate route.
  • Sleep: aim for your normal hours; one slightly short night does little harm, but a changed routine can.

What should I carry to a law entrance exam?

Carry what your admit card and the official notice list, usually the printed admit card, a valid photo ID and permitted writing items. Do not carry phones, watches, notes or books unless the notice allows it. For AIBE, recent notices allow only clean bare acts without notes or commentary.

What should I do in the first ten minutes of the exam?

Read the instructions fully, write your checkpoint times on the rough sheet, and scan the paper once if the format allows. Start with a section or passage you find easiest to settle your nerves. Do not rush the first five questions; accuracy there sets your rhythm.

The first ten minutes are for calming and organising, not speed. Fill in personal details exactly as asked (in OMR exams a single coding error can cost far more than any question), note the clock time of the end, and write a checkpoint table: for example, 30 questions by minute 30 in CLAT PG. How do I mark an OMR sheet or use a computer-based test correctly?

In OMR exams, mark answers as you go, darken fully with the permitted pen and never leave a block of questions for the end. In computer-based tests, use the question palette, mark questions for review and save answers regularly. In both, confirm that each answer matches its question number.

What if my mind goes blank during the exam?

Stop for ten seconds, breathe slowly and move to a different question. Blanking is usually stress, not forgotten knowledge. Skip the question, answer an easy one and return later. If two or three go blank in a row, switch sections or passages to regain momentum.

Everyone meets a hard stretch in a long exam. The skill is to avoid spiralling. Mark the question, note the number, and move to something you can answer. Early wins settle the mind. Return to the blank question with fresh attention, and apply elimination for a specific legal reason if the exam has negative marking: see when to guess and when to skip.

Eight small mistakes that cost marks on exam day

  1. Reaching late. Many centres close the gate at reporting time; arriving with ten minutes spare is not luck, it is planning.
  2. Carrying a banned item. A watch, phone or slip of notes can lead to being sent out. Leave anything doubtful at home.
  3. Shading the wrong bubble set. Check roll number, set code and question numbers before starting.
  4. Reading the question too fast. Words like only, not, except and always reverse an answer.
  5. Spending five minutes on one question. One hard question is one question; five minutes is about five easy ones.
  6. Leaving OMR transfer to the end. If you must transfer from a question booklet, do it in blocks of ten.
  7. Changing answers on a hunch. First instincts are right more often than late guesses unless you spot a specific error.
  8. Letting one bad section ruin the rest. Each question is independent; reset after each one.

The last ten minutes

Use the final ten minutes in the order that earns the most marks. First, fill every unanswered question if there is no negative marking (AIBE and UGC NET). Second, in exams with negative marking, answer only questions where you can remove two options. Third, check that your OMR or on-screen answers match what you intended. Fourth, do not change an answer unless you can name the specific mistake.

  • No negative marking: fill everything.
  • With negative marking: answer where two options are removed.
  • Always: check question numbers against bubbles or palette status.

After the exam, avoid analysing every question with friends. Compare the official answer key when published, and learn from your errors for the next attempt rather than replaying them on the way home.
