Quick answer
  • Marks are out of 300: Paper 1 (100) plus Law Paper 2 (200).
  • Three outcomes: JRF (highest line), Assistant Professor, and PhD only.
  • Recent Unreserved cut-offs: JRF 200 to 236, Assistant Professor 176 to 196, PhD only 158 to 174.
  • Cut-offs change every session, so aim for a margin above the recent range.

"What score do I need?" has no single answer, because NTA sets the line after each session from the results. What you can do is read past lines, understand the three categories and train for a score with a margin. The tables here come from the cut-off data we maintain on this site, which records each figure only where sources agree. See the full tables, with all reserved categories, on the cut-off page.

What is the minimum percentage to qualify?

Preparation sites report a floor of 40 percent for General and 35 percent for SC, ST, OBC and PwD candidates. Reports differ on whether this applies to the aggregate or to each paper, so read the NTA Information Bulletin for your session. Also note that reaching the floor does not make you qualified. You must meet the cut-off for your category, and one report puts it plainly: a 40 percent average guarantees nothing until you are on the merit list.

What score should you aim for?

  • Assistant Professor (Unreserved): recent lines sit between 176 and 194, so a target of about 200 gives a margin.
  • JRF (Unreserved): recent lines sit between 200 and 236, so train for 220 or higher.
  • Reserved categories: use your category's line from the cut-off page and add a margin.

Convert your mock scores: 70 in Paper 1 and 140 in Paper 2 gives 210, for example. There is no negative marking as reported, so attempt everything. To raise a score, see the Paper 1 plan and the Law unit priorities.

Why cut-offs move

The cut-off follows how the cohort performs and how many candidates appear in each category, so a harder paper tends to lower the line and an easier one to raise it. That is why a plan should target a margin, not last session's number.

Verify

Cut-offs are as compiled on this site from NTA notices and agreeing published reports. Confirm every figure and the three-category rules on the NTA website. Do not rely on a past cut-off to predict the next one.
