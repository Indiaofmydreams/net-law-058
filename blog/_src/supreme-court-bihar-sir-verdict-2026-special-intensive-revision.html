Quick answer
  • In May 2026 the Supreme Court upheld the Election Commission's Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls in Bihar.
  • The bench, CJI Surya Kant and Justice Joymalya Bagchi, held the power flows from Article 324 and Section 21(3) of the RP Act, 1950.
  • It rejected the argument that SIR conflicts with the Representation of the People Act or the Registration of Electors Rules, 1960.

Few election-law rulings of 2026 have been as watched as the one on the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls. It arrived against a political backdrop of protests, claims of disenfranchisement and a fight over how far the Election Commission of India can go on its own. This guide explains what the Court decided, the legal reasoning behind it and what is still open. It is written for readers, and for law aspirants who need the rule and the reasoning.

What did the Supreme Court hold on the Bihar SIR?

A bench of CJI Surya Kant and Justice Joymalya Bagchi unanimously upheld the Special Intensive Revision in May 2026. It held that the Election Commission's power to choose its own method of revising rolls flows from Article 324 and Section 21(3) of the Representation of the People Act, 1950.

The case was brought by the Association for Democratic Reforms and others. They argued that SIR was out of step with the statute and the 1960 Rules and that it threatened free and fair elections by removing genuine voters. The Court did not accept that SIR was inconsistent with either the Act or the Rules. In effect, it treated SIR as a lawful species of the revision power the Commission already has, not as a new power invented by the Commission.

What is Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls?

SIR is a door-to-door, form-based exercise in which the Election Commission re-verifies voters instead of only updating the existing roll. Electors fill enumeration forms, and those who do not or cannot establish eligibility can be left out of the draft roll, subject to claims and objections.

Ordinary revisions are usually summary or intensive in a limited sense: names are added, deleted or corrected on the existing base. SIR treats the roll as something to be re-established, which is why it drew so much argument about the burden placed on citizens to prove their eligibility. The Bihar exercise began in 2025, and the Supreme Court had already, during the hearings, directed that Aadhaar be accepted as proof of identity, though not of citizenship.

Which law gives the Election Commission power to revise electoral rolls?

Article 324 vests the superintendence, direction and control of the preparation of electoral rolls and the conduct of elections in the Election Commission. Section 21 of the Representation of the People Act, 1950 deals with preparation and revision of rolls, and its third sub-section lets the Commission order a special revision.

Source of powerWhat it does
Article 324Superintendence, direction and control of rolls and elections
Section 21, RP Act 1950Preparation and revision of electoral rolls; special revision under sub-section (3)
Registration of Electors Rules, 1960Procedure for registration and revision

The Court's reading is that these provisions together are wide enough to let the Commission design the method, so long as it does not breach the statute or the Rules. That reasoning follows the long-standing view that Article 324 fills gaps where the legislation is silent, but cannot override a law made under Articles 327 and 328.

Why was the SIR challenged?

Petitioners said SIR shifted the burden to the voter to prove eligibility, risked deleting genuine electors, and was imposed without a statutory basis. They also argued that the timing, close to elections, left too little time for correction.

The Court's answer was that the Commission has authority over its own procedure and that safeguards of notice, claims and objections remain. It did not hold that every possible application of SIR is immune from challenge. A voter wrongly excluded can still pursue the remedies the revision process itself provides.

What is still pending after the verdict?

Several matters are still before the Supreme Court. A review of the May judgment has been sought by AIMIM MP Asaduddin Owaisi. The Court has issued notice on a plea that SIR notices were issued mechanically in Delhi, and a challenge to the modification of Form 6 during SIR is pending.

A separate plea seeks the removal of the Chief Election Commissioner, and the Commission has filed its response in the case about the CEC's powers. The legality of the 2023 law on appointing election commissioners has been sent to a Constitution Bench. Read our explainer on the CEC appointment reference.

How can this be asked in UGC NET Law, CLAT PG or AIBE?

Expect questions on the source and limits of the Election Commission's powers, the relationship between Article 324 and legislation, and the statutory scheme of the 1950 and 1951 Acts. A typical question asks which provision empowers the Commission to revise rolls, or whether Article 324 can override an Act of Parliament.
