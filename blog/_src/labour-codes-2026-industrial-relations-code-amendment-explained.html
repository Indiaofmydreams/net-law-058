The consolidation of India's labour laws into four codes is one of the largest legal reforms in decades. 2026 is the year they became operational. This guide explains the structure, what the February amendment fixed and the constitutional background that exam setters like to test.

What are India's four labour codes?

They are the Code on Wages, the Code on Social Security, the Industrial Relations Code and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code. Together they consolidate 29 central labour laws and became fully operational in January 2026.

CodeSubject
Code on WagesWages, bonus and related matters
Code on Social SecuritySocial security, including for gig and platform workers
Industrial Relations CodeTrade unions, industrial disputes, standing orders
OSH CodeSafety, health and working conditions

Sources conflict on the exact commencement dates of individual provisions, so treat dates beyond "January 2026" with caution and check the notifications.

What is the Industrial Relations Code (Amendment) Act, 2026?

It received assent on 16 February 2026 and substitutes Section 104(1) of the Industrial Relations Code to make clear that the Trade Unions Act 1926, the Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act 1946 and the Industrial Disputes Act 1947 stand repealed.

This is a clarifying amendment. It removes doubt over whether the three older statutes had been repealed and from when, which matters for pending proceedings and for citing the correct law. It does not rewrite the substance of the Code.

Which old labour laws were replaced?

The codes replaced 29 central laws, including the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, the Trade Unions Act, 1926 and the Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act, 1946, which are now repealed through the Industrial Relations Code.

For students, the practical point is vocabulary. Questions that once asked about the "Industrial Disputes Act" may now be framed around the Industrial Relations Code, so learn both names and which one is current.

What is the National Social Security Board for gig workers?

The Code on Social Security provides for social security for gig and platform workers through a National Social Security Board, which was notified on 2 November 2026.

Gig and platform workers were recognised as a category in labour law for the first time in these codes. The Board's role is to recommend and oversee schemes for them. Check the notification for its composition and functions.

Why is labour law a concurrent subject?

Labour welfare, trade unions and industrial disputes fall in the Concurrent List (Entries 22 to 24), so both Parliament and state legislatures can legislate, with Parliament's law prevailing in case of repugnancy under Article 254.

That is why the codes are central laws but states also frame rules under them. It also links labour law to Article 43 in the Directive Principles, which speaks of a living wage and decent conditions of work.
