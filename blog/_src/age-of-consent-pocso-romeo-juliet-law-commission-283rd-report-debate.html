Quick answer
  • The age of consent is 18 (POCSO 2012; Section 63 BNS); it was raised from 16 in 2013.
  • Law Commission 283rd Report (2023): do not lower it, but allow guided judicial discretion for 16 to 18 cases.
  • No "Romeo and Juliet" exception exists in India.
  • Debate: protecting children versus criminalising adolescent relationships.

Courts regularly see cases in which a teenage couple's relationship leads to a criminal case against the older partner, often brought by the girl's family. The law treats a person under 18 as unable to consent. This guide explains the current rule, what the Law Commission recommended and how the arguments run on each side.

What is the age of consent in India?

It is 18 for all sexual activity. The POCSO Act, 2012 treats any person under 18 as a child, and since the 2013 amendment the rape provision treats sexual intercourse with a girl under 18 as an offence even with her consent, now in Section 63 of the BNS.

Before 2013 the age in the IPC was 16. The change followed the Justice Verma Committee and the Criminal Law (Amendment) Act, 2013. The POCSO Act applies to both boys and girls and is gender-neutral for victims. The age of 18 also matches the age of majority and the legal age at which a person can enter a contract, though the minimum age for marriage is 18 for women and 21 for men.

What did the Law Commission recommend in 2023?

In its 283rd Report the Law Commission advised against lowering the age of consent from 18, saying it could affect the fight against child abuse and trafficking, but suggested guided judicial discretion in sentencing for cases involving tacit approval by a child aged 16 to 18.

The Commission was responding to a reference from the Law Ministry after repeated judicial observations. It said that the law should clearly distinguish exploitation from adolescent relationships, but did so through sentencing and not through a change in the age. The report was submitted in 2023 and Parliament has not changed the law, as far as the sources used here show.

What is the Romeo and Juliet exception?

It is a rule in some countries that exempts consensual sexual activity between teenagers close in age. India has no such exception, so a consensual relationship between adolescents can lead to prosecution of the older partner.

Courts have tried to soften the effect in individual cases. Some High Courts have quashed cases where the couple later married or where the relationship was clearly consensual, and some judges have called for legislative change. The Supreme Court has also asked the Government to examine the issue and has used its power under Article 142 in rare cases. These are case-specific remedies, and they do not change the rule.

What is the problem with mandatory reporting?

Section 19 POCSO requires anyone who knows of a sexual offence against a child to report it, including doctors. Critics say that it stops adolescents from seeking contraception or health care for fear of police action.

Health experts and some courts have noted that the combination of a high age of consent and mandatory reporting leads adolescents to avoid clinics. Defenders say that reporting is essential to detect abuse, especially within families, and that exemptions would be misused. This is one of the points where child protection and adolescent health priorities pull in different directions, and the law has not resolved it. What are the arguments to keep or lower the age?

To keep 18: it protects children from coercion and trafficking, it is consistent with other laws and a lower age could be misused by exploiters. To lower or add an exception: criminalising consensual teenage relationships harms the people the law is meant to protect and burdens courts.

A middle path is a close-in-age exception, for example, allowing consensual activity between partners within a small age gap, with safeguards against coercion or power imbalance. The Commission preferred sentencing discretion instead. An exam answer should name the rule, the 2013 change, the Commission's view and the tension, and should treat the discussion as policy and not as settled law.
