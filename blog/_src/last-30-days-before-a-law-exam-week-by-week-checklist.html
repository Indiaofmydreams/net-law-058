The last month decides how much of your preparation turns into marks. Aspirants waste it by starting new topics, taking too many mocks or burning out by week three. This checklist gives a week-by-week plan that works for any of the exams on this site, with a taper in the final days.

What should I do in the last 30 days before a law exam?

Spend week one closing the top three gaps, weeks two and three on full mocks and revision of weak topics, and week four tapering: light revision, notes and rest. Do not start new books. Keep a fixed sleep time, and stop all new learning three days before the exam.

How many mock tests should I take in the last month?

About five to six full mocks in the final month, concentrated in weeks two and three, each fully reviewed. Take the last full mock about five to seven days before the exam, so that its review does not collide with exam nerves. After that, practise only short sets.

Exam-wise numbers are in how many mock tests are enough. The key point for the last month is spacing: do not take a hard mock the day before the exam; a poor score then only damages confidence.

Should I stop studying a day before the exam?

Study lightly. Skim your one-page sheets and error log for an hour or two, but do not learn new topics or take a full mock. Use the rest of the day for documents, travel planning and rest. Sleeping well helps recall more than another hour of reading.

A realistic last day: a morning skim of sheets, an afternoon walk or light activity, an early dinner, documents checked, alarm set and bed early. Our exam-day guide covers the night before in detail.

How do I deal with exam anxiety in the last few weeks?

Keep the routine boring: fixed sleep and study times, daily movement and one full mock a week. Anxiety usually comes from uncertainty, so replace it with a plan you can see: a list of tasks and a calendar. If anxiety interferes with sleep or daily life for weeks, speak to a doctor or counsellor.

A few practical steps help most people: stop comparing your preparation with others online, switch off study groups that raise stress, and limit mock-score checking to review sessions. Five minutes of slow breathing before a mock can also help. Seek support if anxiety is more than the usual pre-exam nerves.

What documents and logistics should I check before the exam?

Check your admit card and the reporting time, a valid photo ID as named in the notice, the centre address and route, permitted writing items, and what you may not carry. Visit the centre or study the route a day early if it is in a new city, and prepare two copies of documents.

Weak-subject triage in the last month

Everyone reaches the last month with a subject they have not finished. Do not try to fix everything. Triage by marks at stake and effort needed:

SituationDecisionExample
Core subject, weakFix first; give it the most timeConstitution or Criminal Law at 55 percent accuracy
Regular subject, weakCover the basics and standard cases onlyProperty or Company Law: definitions, doctrines, main sections
Occasional subject, untouchedOne pass of a short summary; accept the lossBanking, Insurance, Taxation
Strong subjectMaintain with questions onlyNo new reading

The goal in week one is to move your weakest core subject from unreliable to adequate, not to make it perfect. Two extra points of accuracy in a core subject are worth more than a first read of an occasional subject that may supply only one question.

Seven daily habits for the final month

  1. Fixed wake and sleep times, matched to your exam slot if you know it.
  2. One block of recall without notes before you open any book.
  3. 30 to 40 questions every day, reviewed the same day.
  4. An error log entry for every mistake.
  5. A short walk or exercise daily.
  6. Limit on screens and social media, especially before sleep.
  7. A weekly review of the plan with one change for the next week.

None of this is glamorous, and that is the point. The final month is a test of consistency. Aspirants who stay steady in these thirty days almost always do better than those who push harder for ten days and collapse for twenty.
