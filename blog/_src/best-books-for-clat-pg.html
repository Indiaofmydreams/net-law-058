Quick answer
  • Commonly recommended: D.D. Basu or J.N. Pandey (Constitution), R.K. Bangia (contract and torts), Paras Diwan (Hindu law), V.K. Ahuja (IPR).
  • Pair every textbook with the bare act and a short list of landmark cases.
  • For criminal law, use current editions that cover the BNS, BNSS and BSA.
  • CLAT PG is passage-based, so timed practice matters more than another book.

The CLAT PG paper rewards understanding of principles and the ability to read fast, not the number of books you own. This list reflects titles that preparation guides commonly recommend for the main subjects. It is not a ranking and we have no tie to any publisher. Check the latest edition before buying, and be especially careful with criminal law, which changed in 2024.

Which is the best book for CLAT PG?

No single book covers the syllabus. The sensible plan is one standard text per major subject, the relevant bare act and a case list, then timed passages. See the syllabus guide for the subjects and the priority order.

Criminal law has changed

The IPC, CrPC and Evidence Act were replaced by the BNS, BNSS and BSA. Older books remain useful for concepts, but learn the new section numbers with our mapping guide.

What else do you need besides textbooks?

  • Bare acts for the statutory subjects.
  • A case notebook. Two or three lines per landmark judgment, or use the case vault.
  • Question banks. Guides cite Lexis Nexis' CLAT PG question bank and Universal's guide to LL.M. entrance exams for topic drills. Add timed passages from the practice arena.
  • Mock tests. Full 2-hour papers from our mocks.
  • Current legal developments from legal news sites, with attention to environmental law, IPR and data privacy.

How to avoid overbuying

  1. Choose one source per subject and set a finish date.
  2. Spend the time you save on passages: see how to solve them.
  3. Use previous papers to see what you really need.
  4. Plan the weeks with the 1-month and 4-month plans.
