Quick answers
  • Target: 8 to 12 full mocks for CLAT PG; 6 to 8 for AILET; 8 to 10 for CUET PG; 4 to 6 for AIBE; 6 to 8 for UGC NET.
  • Start: after 60 to 70 percent of the syllabus is covered.
  • Review: at least as long as the test itself.
  • One mock a day: never more.

Aspirants ask how many mock tests are enough as if there were a magic number. There is not, but there is a useful range, and a more important question: how much you learn from each. This guide gives exam-wise targets, a rhythm for when to start and how often, and a way to tell whether your mocks are working.

How many mock tests should I take before a law entrance exam?

Eight to twelve full-length mocks is enough for most candidates, provided each is reviewed properly. Fewer than six leaves you without a trend; more than fifteen usually means you are taking tests instead of fixing mistakes. The review of a mock matters more than the number of mocks.

When should I start taking full-length mock tests?

Start when you have covered about 60 to 70 percent of the syllabus, not at the beginning and not after finishing everything. Early mocks reveal gaps while there is time to fix them. Before that point, topic-wise questions will teach you more than full papers.

A common mistake is to wait until the syllabus is complete. By then there may be three weeks left, which is too little for the feedback to change your preparation. Equally, taking a full mock in week one measures nothing but your starting point. A sensible rhythm is one mock every ten days during coverage, one a week during consolidation, and two a week in the last fortnight.

Are previous year papers better than mock tests?

Use both for different reasons. Past papers show the real style, difficulty and topic spread of your exam, so solve them early and analyse them carefully. Mock tests give practice in fresh questions and pacing. When past papers are few, as in CLAT PG, mocks supply the volume.

Past papers should be solved once, analysed deeply and never wasted by treating them as a test you can retake from memory. See how to analyse CLAT PG past papers and how to use AIBE past questions. For UGC NET, unit-wise analysis of Law papers helps you decide which units to prioritise.

Is it useful to take more than one mock test in a day?

Rarely. A second mock the same day gives you the same score information while leaving no time to review the first, which is where the learning is. Take one full mock, review it fully the same day or next, and use the rest of the time to fix what it showed.

The exception is the last week, when some candidates take a short sectional test to stay warm. Even then, keep the load light and avoid anything that leaves you anxious going into the exam.

What do I do if my mock scores are not improving?

Look at causes, not scores. Tag every wrong answer as knowledge gap, misread, elimination failure or time pressure, and compare across your last five mocks. If misreads and time pressure dominate, change technique; if knowledge gaps dominate, go back to coverage. See our recovery plan for details.

A flat score for three mocks is a signal to change your routine, not to take more mocks. Our 14-day recovery plan gives a step-by-step reset.

A simple rule for mock quantity

For every hour you spend taking a mock, spend at least an hour on review and another hour on fixing what it showed. A three-hour cycle for each two-hour paper gives you about four mock cycles a fortnight, which fits an eight-week plan well.

The diagnostic in week two is the most useful single test you will take: it shows where you stand while there is still time to change course.

Signs you are taking too many mocks

  • You cannot remember what you changed after the last one.
  • Your error log has not been updated for two mocks.
  • You are checking only your score and rank.
  • Your topic coverage has stalled since you started mocks.

If two or more of these apply, stop taking new mocks for a week and spend it on review and revision. More tests only help once the lessons of the last ones are absorbed.
