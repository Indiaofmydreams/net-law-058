A clerkship is a way to learn from the best judges and strengthen your profile. It is short but valuable. Here is what has been reported for the 2026–27 term.

What is the Law Clerk-cum-Research Associate post?

It is a short-term contract role in which law graduates assist Supreme Court judges with research, drafting notes and case summaries. The Court advertised 90 posts for the 2026–27 term, with a consolidated pay of about ₹1 lakh a month, as reported.

High Courts have similar posts, with different pay and terms.

What is the eligibility?

A law degree, with candidates in the final year sometimes allowed as per the notice, and age and marks as stated. Many successful clerks come from NLUs, but the notice sets the exact rule, so read it.

Strong writing and research habits matter more than any single score.

How is the selection made?

A written test on legal reasoning and writing, followed by an interview, as described in recent notices. Check the Supreme Court recruitment page for the schedule, since it is short.

StepWhat it tests
Written testLegal reasoning, drafting, language
InterviewResearch interest and temperament

What is the pay and term?

Reported consolidated pay is ₹1,00,000 per month for a fixed one-year term, which may be extended as per the notice. It is a contract and does not give a regular post, so plan the next step.

For regular government jobs see the career options post.

How does it help a career?

It gives close exposure to research and judgment writing and is respected by firms, chambers and academia. Many clerks go on to LLM abroad, litigation, judiciary or academia.

Pair it with study abroad plans if you want an international LLM.
