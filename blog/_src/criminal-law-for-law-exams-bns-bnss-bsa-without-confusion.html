Criminal law used to be one set of numbers. Now it is two, and exams still use both. Aspirants lose marks by knowing only the old IPC numbers or only the new BNS ones. This guide gives a study method, side-by-side tables for the IPC, CrPC and Evidence Act against the BNS, BNSS and BSA, and a two-week plan to learn them without confusion.

Should I study IPC or BNS for law exams?

Study both, with the BNS as the main text. The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita replaced the IPC from 1 July 2024, but recent papers have still used IPC terms, and exam setters may use either numbering. Learn the BNS section with its IPC equivalent side by side, so you can answer in both.

This is true for the CrPC and the Indian Evidence Act too: the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita and the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam replaced them from the same date. Our CUET PG analysis found that 2026 papers still used the IPC terms, while AIBE and judicial exams increasingly use the new codes. A candidate who knows only one numbering will lose marks in the other. The complete map is in IPC to BNS and CrPC to BNSS section mapping.

Which BNS sections and IPC equivalents are asked most often?

Learn the general exceptions, the homicide block, the property offences and the group-liability provisions first. Examples: culpable homicide IPC 299 and BNS 100, murder IPC 300 and 302 and BNS 101 and 103, theft IPC 378 and BNS 303, cheating IPC 420 and BNS 318, common intention IPC 34 and BNS 3(5).

What are the key CrPC to BNSS changes I should learn?

Learn the procedural sections that exams test: FIR (CrPC 154 to BNSS 173, with zero FIR and e-FIR), bail in non-bailable cases (437 to 480), anticipatory bail (438 to 482), special bail powers of High Courts (439 to 483), maintenance (125 to 144) and inherent powers (482 to 528).

How do I memorise the new criminal codes without confusing them?

Use side-by-side tables, learn anchor sections per chapter rather than every number, and always recall in both directions: old to new and new to old. Do a five-minute recall drill daily for two weeks. Learn the traps first, such as the swapped 482 and the renumbered homicide provisions.

  • Two-column drill: cover one column and recall the other.
  • Chapter anchors: BNS 100 to 105 for homicide; 303 for theft; 318 for cheating.
  • Concept first: know what each offence requires before memorising the number.
  • Trap list: keep a short list of confusing pairs and quiz it weekly.

For the general method, see how to memorise cases, Articles and sections.

How are the Evidence Act and BSA tested in law exams?

Mainly through definitions, admissions and confessions, dying declarations, burden of proof and electronic evidence. The Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam places electronic records under section 63, which corresponds to section 65B of the Evidence Act, and confessions to police under section 23, which corresponds to section 25. Learn those pairs first.

What to avoid

  • Learning only the BNS and being surprised by an IPC-numbered question.
  • Memorising numbers without the offence. The question is rarely just a number.
  • Skipping procedure. BNSS questions often decide close scores.
Verify before you rely on it

Section numbers follow the BNS 2023, BNSS 2023 and BSA 2023 and the older codes; confirm in the bare act before relying on a number.
