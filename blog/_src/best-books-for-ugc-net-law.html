Quick answer
  • Use one exam guide plus a few standard textbooks, not a book per unit.
  • Commonly recommended: Mani Tripathi (Jurisprudence), V.N. Shukla or M.P. Jain (Constitution), K.D. Gaur (crimes), R.K. Bangia (torts), Avtar Singh (contract).
  • For statute-led units, the Act plus concise notes is often enough.
  • Always buy a current edition, especially for the new criminal laws.

A shelf of unread books is the usual way a NET Law plan fails. The paper is objective and tests breadth, so your aim is one reliable source per unit and a lot of practice. This list reflects titles that preparation guides commonly recommend. It is not an endorsement or a ranking, and we have no tie to any publisher. Check the latest edition before you buy.

Which is the best book for UGC NET Law?

There is no single best book. A sensible set is one exam-focused guide that covers all ten units for breadth, a handful of standard textbooks for the units where you need depth, and previous year papers. Preparation guides also commonly mention an exam guide from Arihant and a previous-year book from IFAS Publications as general aids.

How many books are too many?

  • One guide for all units, plus at most one textbook for each of your three weakest units.
  • If you have not finished a book, do not buy another on the same topic.
  • Spend the saved time on previous papers, which teach you how questions are set.
