Laws on national symbols sit where patriotism meets free speech and freedom of conscience. In 2026 Parliament passed a bill extending the 1971 law to the National Song. This guide explains what it does and the constitutional questions it raises.

What does the Prevention of Insults to National Honour (Amendment) Bill, 2026 do?

It extends the penalties of the 1971 Act to the National Song alongside the National Anthem, providing for imprisonment up to three years, a fine, or both.

The 1971 Act, as originally framed, covered the National Flag, the Constitution and the National Anthem. By adding the National Song, the bill brings it into the same protective scheme.

What is the Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971?

It punishes insults to the Indian National Flag, the Constitution of India and the National Anthem, including preventing the singing of the anthem or causing disturbance to an assembly engaged in singing it.

Is standing for the national anthem compulsory?

In Bijoe Emmanuel v. State of Kerala (1986) the Supreme Court held that students who refused to sing the anthem out of religious conviction but stood respectfully could not be penalised, protecting freedom of conscience under Article 25.

The Court said no law compels anyone to sing, and that respectful silence is not disrespect. Later, in Shyam Narayan Chouksey (2016/2018) the Court first mandated and then relaxed standing for the anthem in cinemas. Any prosecution under the new provision will test how far Bijoe Emmanuel still applies.

Which fundamental duty relates to national symbols?

Article 51A(a) makes it a fundamental duty of every citizen to abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals and institutions, the National Flag and the National Anthem.

Fundamental duties are not directly enforceable by courts, but they guide interpretation. Note that Article 51A(a) names the Anthem, not the Song, a point the amendment's critics and supporters may both use.

Was the bill passed?

It was among the 12 bills passed in the Monsoon Session 2026. Check the Gazette for assent and the date it comes into force.
