AIBE has no negative marking, 100 questions and about two minutes a question, and many candidates are unsure what they can carry. The difference between a pass and a fail often comes from using the bare act well and trusting memory for the rest. This guide covers what recent sessions allowed, how to use a bare act without losing time and a three-pass plan for the 3 hours 30 minutes.

Is AIBE an open-book exam, and which bare acts can I carry?

AIBE is not fully open book. Recent sessions have permitted only clean bare acts without notes, commentary or annotations; books and handwritten notes were not allowed, and reports on highlighting differ. Read the BCI notice and your admit card for the final list, and assume anything not clearly permitted is not.

This is the most misunderstood point of AIBE preparation, and the wrong assumption can cost you time or cause trouble at the centre. Our AIBE 22 guide summarises what recent sessions allowed. The sensible rule is to carry the cleanest possible bare act, plan as if you will have little time to use it, and treat it as a verification tool for the sections you half-remember.

How do I use a bare act during AIBE without losing time?

Use it after your first pass, not during it. Answer everything you know from memory, mark the questions where a section number or wording would decide the answer, then open the bare act for those only. Know each act's chapter structure in advance so that you open at the right page in seconds.

The bare act is a time sink when used as a first resort. At 126 seconds a question on average, you cannot look up every doubtful item. A good rule is to allow three minutes for a looked-up question and cap the number of lookups at about fifteen to twenty. That works out to roughly an hour of lookups, leaving the rest for the first pass and the final check.

  1. Pass 1 (about 100 minutes): answer from memory; mark the lookup candidates.
  2. Pass 2 (about 60 minutes): use the bare act for marked questions, in the order of the subject you know best.
  3. Pass 3 (about 30 minutes): fill every blank and check the OMR against your booklet.
  4. Last 10 minutes: final OMR check; do not change answers without a reason.

How many marks do I need to pass AIBE and how should I plan around it?

As reported for recent sessions, the pass mark is 45 percent for General and OBC and 40 percent for SC, ST and PwD, so 45 marks out of 100 for General. With no negative marking, aim for a safe 55 to 60 by answering every question and securing your high-weightage subjects first.

Because there is no negative marking, every question should be answered. A realistic approach is a three-tier plan: bank marks in subjects you know well (about 40 to 45), pick up marks in medium subjects through elimination (about 15 to 20), and guess the rest. Which sections and acts should I know by heart even if bare acts are allowed?

Know the structure and the most-tested provisions of the new criminal codes (BNS, BNSS, BSA), the Constitution's key Articles, the Advocates Act and the professional conduct rules, plus the main sections of the Contract Act, CPC and Transfer of Property Act. Lookups work for details, not for concepts.

A bare act helps with exact wording and section numbers; it does not help with understanding. Spend your memory budget on the concepts and the section anchors, and let the bare act cover the rest. Use the IPC to BNS and CrPC to BNSS mapping to learn both numberings, because AIBE questions may use either.

What should I do in the last week before AIBE?

Revise your one-page sheets, do two or three timed sets of 50 questions, practise a bare act lookup drill and prepare logistics. Do not start new subjects. Confirm centre, reporting time, admit card and what you may carry, and pack the night before. If your exam is within days, follow the AIBE 22 last-week plan.

A lookup drill to practise before the exam

Take twenty questions from any previous-year paper or the AIBE Practice Arena. For each, write down the act and the section you would open, without looking. Then time how long it takes to find the provision in a clean bare act. After a week of this drill you will find most sections in under a minute, and you will learn which questions are better skipped to the second pass.

Common AIBE strategy mistakes

  • Arriving with an annotated or commentary-heavy book. It may be taken away and cause trouble at the centre.
  • Spending 30 minutes on the first subject. Hold to your checkpoints.
  • Leaving OMR bubbles to the end. With no negative marking, a blank bubble is a missed chance.
  • Ignoring new criminal codes. Questions may use BNS, BNSS and BSA numbering.
Verify before you rely on it

Rules on materials can change between sessions and are set by the BCI. The notice and admit card for your session are the final word.
