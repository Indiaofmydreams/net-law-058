Quick answer
  • Yes. The syllabus, papers and practice material are available without paying a coaching fee.
  • Self-study needs four things: a plan, previous papers, timed tests and an error log.

"Do I need coaching?" is the most honest question a UGC NET Law aspirant can ask, and the honest answer is no, but you do need what coaching supplies: a schedule, a way to measure progress and someone to clear doubts. This guide shows how to build those yourself. It is built for candidates who already hold an LLB or LLM and are preparing for Law (058) and the common Paper 1.

Can you clear UGC NET Law without coaching?

Yes. The exam is objective: as reported, Paper 1 has 50 questions and Paper 2 has 100, for 300 marks, with no negative marking. The syllabus is published, previous papers are available and the topics repeat in new wording. That makes it well suited to self-study. Where people struggle is not content but consistency, so the plan below is about routine first and resources second.

What does self-study actually need?

  • The syllabus as a checklist. See the unit list and priorities.
  • One main source per unit. Concise notes or one trusted book, not five. See unit-wise book suggestions.
  • Previous papers. Use the PYQ page and read our paper analysis.
  • Timed practice. Full-length tests teach pace and stamina.
  • An error log. Every miss goes in with unit, topic and reason.

A 12-week self-study plan

WeeksFocusDaily routine
1BaselineAttempt one old Law paper cold. Read the syllabus. Set a daily Paper 1 slot.
2 to 5Big unitsConstitutional and Administrative Law, Jurisprudence, Public International Law, Commercial Law. Notes, then 30 MCQs per unit.
6 to 8Middle unitsEnvironment and Human Rights, IPR and IT Law, Family Law, Law of Crimes. Statutes and landmark cases.
9Small unitsTorts and Consumer Protection, Comparative Public Law.
10 to 11MocksTwo full papers a week, with as long on review as on the test.
12RevisionError log, key cases, treaty and Act details. Rest the day before.

Paper 1 should run in parallel, with 60 to 90 minutes a day on one unit. See the Paper 1 hub and the 70-plus plan. If you have fewer weeks, use the shorter plans on the preparation plan page.

A weekly routine that holds

  1. Mon to Fri: one Law unit in the morning, Paper 1 practice in the evening.
  2. Saturday: a timed paper and review.
  3. Sunday: revise the error log and rest.

Fix times, not goals. "I study at 6 am" survives bad days better than "I will finish a unit this week".

When is coaching worth it?

Coaching can help if you cannot keep a schedule alone, have no way to measure progress or need live doubt clearing. We do not rank institutes, and results claims are hard to verify. If you consider one, check a demo class, the quality of the tests, how fast doubts are answered, and refund terms in writing. Compare it with what you can do free first.
