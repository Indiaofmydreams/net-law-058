Quick answer
  • Section 63 BSA governs electronic records; Section 63(4) needs a certificate.
  • Pune Bar Association v. Union of India (July 2026) upheld the certificate requirement; scope of Part B expert certification left open.
  • The Bankers' Books Evidence Bill, 2026 covers electronic and cloud-based records and digital authentication.
  • Older rule: Section 65B(4) and Arjun Panditrao Khotkar (2020).

Digital evidence now decides cases. The Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 replaced the Indian Evidence Act, and 2026 brought the first major Supreme Court word on its electronic evidence rules, along with a new bill on bankers' books. This guide explains both.

What is the Section 63 certificate under the BSA?

Section 63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 governs admissibility of electronic records. Section 63(4) requires a certificate to accompany such evidence, replacing the Section 65B certificate under the old Evidence Act.

The certificate confirms the electronic record's origin and the conditions in which it was produced. The BSA's schedule gives a format with two parts, and Part B involves an expert's certification.

What did Pune Bar Association v. Union of India decide?

In July 2026 the Supreme Court upheld the Section 63(4) certificate requirement, while leaving open the scope of expert certification under Part B of the certificate.

IssuePosition
Certificate under Section 63(4)Upheld
Scope of expert certification (Part B)Left open

Leaving Part B open means the question of when and how experts must certify will be decided in later cases.

What is the Bankers' Books Evidence Bill, 2026?

It broadens bankers' books to any form, including electronic and cloud-based records, and recognises digital authentication, updating the Bankers' Books Evidence Act, 1891.

The 1891 Act let certified copies of bank entries be received as evidence. The new bill, passed in the Monsoon Session, aims to match how banks actually store records today.

What was the rule under Section 65B of the Evidence Act?

In Arjun Panditrao Khotkar (2020) the Supreme Court held that a Section 65B(4) certificate is mandatory for secondary electronic evidence, with limited exceptions. The BSA's Section 63 carries this idea forward.

Earlier cases such as Anvar P.V. (2014) and Shafhi Mohammad (2018) had pulled in different directions, which Khotkar resolved. For law students the sequence of cases is a favourite question.

Why does electronic evidence law matter for exams?

Evidence law is a core subject and electronic records are now central to criminal and commercial cases. Questions often test the certificate requirement, admissibility conditions and landmark cases.
