For information only

This is a sensitive and contested subject. The post sets out the court decisions and each side's legal arguments and does not take a position.

In early 2022 some students in Karnataka were stopped from entering classrooms wearing a hijab. The dispute went from the school gate to the High Court and then to the Supreme Court, which was divided. This guide explains the decisions, the legal test involved and the arguments made on each side.

What did the Karnataka High Court decide on the hijab?

On 15 March 2022 a full bench held that wearing a hijab is not an essential religious practice of Islam, that a prescribed school uniform is a reasonable restriction and that the Government order of 5 February 2022 was valid.

The case was Resham v. State of Karnataka. The High Court looked at Article 25, which protects freedom of conscience and the right to practise religion, subject to public order, morality and health. It held that uniform is a restriction on a classroom, which is a "qualified public place", and that it promotes equality and discipline.

What did the Supreme Court decide?

On 13 October 2022 Justices Hemant Gupta and Sudhanshu Dhulia gave a split verdict in Aishat Shifa v. State of Karnataka. Justice Gupta upheld the High Court; Justice Dhulia set it aside, so the matter was to go to a larger bench.

When judges differ, the case is placed before the Chief Justice to constitute a larger bench. The two opinions are not binding as a ruling of the Court, so the High Court's decision still applies in Karnataka until the larger bench decides.

What is the essential religious practices test?

It is a doctrine developed from Shirur Mutt (1954) under which only practices essential to a religion are protected by Article 25. Courts decide what is essential, which critics say places judges in the role of interpreting scripture.

The test has been used in cases on animal sacrifice, burial and temple entry. In Sabarimala (2018) Justice Chandrachud questioned the test and in 2020 the matter was referred to a larger bench; see our post on the Sabarimala reference. The hijab case is another occasion on which the test is questioned, which is why an answer on one case often draws on the other.

What are the arguments on each side?

For the restriction: uniform promotes equality and discipline, the State can regulate conduct in schools and the hijab is not essential. Against: Article 25 protects practice beyond what is essential, personal choice and dignity under Article 21 matter and a ban may deny some girls an education.

Justice Dhulia stressed that the question should not be framed only through the essential practices test and that forcing a girl to choose between her faith and her school is not acceptable. Justice Gupta saw the uniform as part of the school's secular discipline. Notice that both opinions use constitutional principles; they differ on how to weigh them.

What is the state of the case now?

As far as reports show, the matter awaits a larger bench, so the High Court's decision continues to be the leading ruling in Karnataka. No date for the larger bench is known from the sources used here.

A student should therefore describe the law accurately: there is a High Court decision, a split Supreme Court decision and a pending reference. The wider debate on schools and uniform interacts with other recent rulings on education rights, such as the one on mother tongue education.

How can this be asked in UGC NET Law, CLAT PG or AIBE?

Expect questions on Article 25, the essential practices test, Shirur Mutt, split verdicts and reasonable restrictions.
