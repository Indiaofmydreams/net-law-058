Quick answer
  • AIBE is reported to cover 19 subjects in 100 MCQs, with no negative marking.
  • Seven subjects carry about 62 questions: Constitutional Law, CrPC/BNSS, CPC (10 each), and IPC/BNS, Evidence/BSA, Family Law, Contract-Property-NI Act (8 each).
  • The new criminal laws (BNS, BNSS, BSA) now replace the IPC, CrPC and Evidence Act in your reading.
  • Question counts are as reported and can shift between editions.

The AIBE syllabus is wide but shallow: many subjects, each tested through straightforward objective questions. That makes the order of study matter more than the depth. This guide lists the 19 subjects with the question counts reported in one preparation guide, tells you where to start and points to free notes. Always check the current notice on the official AIBE site, because the Bar Council can change the format.

Which AIBE subjects should you study first?

  1. The three 10-question subjects: Constitutional Law, CrPC/BNSS and CPC. Thirty marks sit here.
  2. The four 8-question subjects: IPC/BNS, Evidence/BSA, Family Law and Contract-Property-NI Act, another 32.
  3. Torts and Consumer Protection (5), then the 4-question group: ADR, PIL, Professional Ethics, Labour Law and Taxation.
  4. The 2 and 3-question subjects last, as quick skims.
The criminal laws changed

The IPC, CrPC and Evidence Act were replaced by the BNS, BNSS and BSA. Learn the new section numbers beside the old, with our mapping guide, because papers may use either.

How to study the syllabus

  • For each subject, read the notes, then attempt MCQs on the practice arena.
  • Keep a short sheet of numbers for each Act: limitation periods, time limits, punishments, definitions.
  • Track coverage on the syllabus tracker.
  • With the exam close, switch to the last-week plan.
