Paper 1 and Law (058) share one three-hour sitting, so one clock governs 150 questions. Most candidates prepare each paper well and then lose marks because they never decided how to share the time. This guide gives time budgets, an attempt order, checkpoints and a rehearsal routine you can use in your mocks.

How should I split three hours between Paper 1 and Law in UGC NET?

A common split is about 55 to 60 minutes for the 50 Paper 1 questions and 110 to 115 minutes for the 100 Law questions, keeping 5 to 10 minutes as a buffer at the end. That gives roughly 70 seconds per question in each paper. Test the split in mocks and adjust.

Should I attempt Paper 1 or Law first?

Attempt the paper you are more nervous about first, if the interface allows free movement. Most candidates start with Paper 1, because its short units settle nerves, then give Law the bulk of their time. If you are strong in Law and slow in reasoning, take Law first and keep a strict cap on Paper 1.

There is no universally best order, and what you do matters less than the fact that you rehearse it. Check NTA's instructions and demo interface for how the sections appear and whether you can move between them. Then practise your chosen order in at least four full combined mocks.

How many minutes should I spend on each Law (058) question?

About one minute eight seconds on average. Recall-based questions on cases, Articles and sections need 30 to 45 seconds, while statement and assertion questions need 90 seconds or more. Bank time on the first kind to spend on the second, and never exceed two minutes on any single question.

Law (058) has 100 questions across ten units. In our past-paper analysis, Constitutional and Administrative Law supplied the largest share. Use a first pass to answer all questions you know quickly; use a second pass for lengthy statement-based items. With no negative marking, the third pass is for guesses.

What do I do if I run short of time in Law?

Guess every remaining question, since there is no negative marking. Keep checkpoints inside the Law block: about question 55 at 60 minutes and question 85 at 90 minutes. If you are behind, skip any item needing more than a minute and return to it only if time is left.

The last five minutes of the whole sitting are for filling every blank in both papers. Even random guesses have an expected value of 0.5 marks each with no penalty.

How do I practise a three-hour UGC NET sitting?

Take a full 150-question combined mock in one go, at the time of day of your exam shift, using the split above and with no breaks. Do at least three before the exam, spaced a week apart, and review each fully. Practise reading on a screen, because fatigue is real in a computer-based test.

  • Match the shift: a 9 am paper needs an early routine for the preceding week; a 3 pm paper needs a light lunch plan.
  • Use a screen: reading passages and tables on a screen is slower than on paper for many people; practise it.
  • Rehearse the buffer: in one mock, finish early on purpose and use the last ten minutes to fill blanks.
  • Review by block: in your error log, note whether each wrong answer came in the first, second or third hour.

Our error-log method works for combined mocks as well. If you notice that most errors come in the third hour, you have a stamina problem, and the cure is longer practice sessions, not more reading.
