The LLM specialisation shapes your first job for a few years, though not forever. This guide compares the common choices and who they suit.

Which LLM specialisation has the most jobs?

Corporate and commercial law has the widest private sector demand, followed by IP, tax and dispute resolution. Constitutional, criminal and human rights law lead to academia, litigation, public policy and NGOs more than to large corporate hiring.

Is IP law a good choice?

Yes for those interested in trademarks, patents and technology, with demand at IP boutiques, firms and corporates. Patent work may need a science background. The market is niche, so pick it for interest and skill, not only for a package.

What about tax and ADR?

Tax law serves firms, Big Four advisory and in-house tax teams. ADR (arbitration and mediation) serves firms, institutions and independent arbitrators, and has grown with the mediation law and institutional arbitration.

Which specialisations suit teaching or the judiciary?

Constitutional, criminal, jurisprudence and public law fit teaching and UGC NET well. For judiciary, any specialisation works, but broad command of procedural and substantive laws matters more than the LLM subject.

How should I pick?

Match the subject to the work you want in five years, check which NLU offers a strong department and see recent placements. Choose a subject you can read deeply, since the dissertation and research habits count in every path.
