District judges are the first judicial officers most citizens meet. Their service conditions are governed by the Constitution and state rules, and a 2026 Supreme Court direction raised their retirement age in several states. This guide explains the order and the constitutional framework behind it.

What did the Supreme Court order on district judges' retirement age?

On 1 September 2026 a bench of CJI Surya Kant and Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V. Mohana directed seven states to raise the retirement age of district judges from 60 to 62.

Retirement age in the subordinate judiciary varies by state, and the Court has long encouraged uniformity. The direction removes a gap for officers in the seven states concerned.

Can officers who already retired come back?

Yes. Officers who retired on or after 31 March 2026 may opt to rejoin service under the direction.

This addresses officers who retired at 60 during the period while the issue was pending. They can come back and serve until 62 if they choose.

Which Articles govern district judges?

Articles 233 to 237 in Chapter VI of Part VI cover subordinate courts. Article 233 deals with appointment of district judges, and Article 235 gives control over them to the High Court.

Who appoints district judges?

The Governor appoints district judges in consultation with the High Court under Article 233. A person already in the service of the Union or State, or an advocate with seven years' practice recommended by the High Court, can be appointed.

In Rejanish K.V. v. K. Deepa (2020) the Supreme Court held that the seven-year requirement must be met at the time of application. The Court also decided in the All India Judges Association cases on service conditions of judicial officers.

Why does retirement age matter for the judiciary?

A higher retirement age retains experienced officers and helps with vacancies and pendency. It also tackles disparity, since judges of High Courts and the Supreme Court retire at 62 and 65.

Capacity at the top and the bottom both matter, which links to the 2026 increase in Supreme Court strength covered in our post on the judges amendment.
