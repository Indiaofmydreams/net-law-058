Quick answer
  • The MSME Development (Amendment) Act, 2026 received assent on 13 August 2026.
  • Section 15A: central public sector enterprises must settle MSME invoices through TReDS.
  • Section 18: mediation within 90 days; arbitral award within 90 days of pleadings (Section 18(4A)).
  • Section 19: 75% pre-deposit to challenge an award; at least 50% payable if pending beyond six months.

Late payment is the biggest daily problem of India's small businesses. A supplier delivers goods, waits months for money and cannot afford to sue. The MSME Development Act, 2006 tried to fix that with a payment deadline and interest, and the 2026 amendment tightens the screws. This guide explains the new provisions, why they matter for commercial law and what has still to be notified.

What is the MSME Development (Amendment) Act, 2026?

It amends the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development Act, 2006 and received the President's assent on 13 August 2026. It routes certain MSME receivable settlements through TReDS, tightens dispute timelines and strengthens enforcement of awards.

The 2006 Act already says that a buyer must pay within the agreed period, not exceeding 45 days, and pay compound interest at three times the bank rate on delays. The new Act focuses on how disputes are decided and how awards are enforced, because delay in the dispute process was often as damaging as the delay in payment. The date of commencement was not stated in the reports used for this guide, so check the notification.

What is TReDS?

TReDS is the Trade Receivables Discounting System, an RBI-authorised electronic platform where MSME invoices can be financed. Under new Section 15A every central public sector enterprise must route settlement of MSME invoices through it, and governments can extend this to other entities.

The idea is that if an MSME's invoice to a large buyer is uploaded and accepted on the platform, financiers can pay the MSME immediately at a discount, and the buyer pays the financier on the due date. Notified entities must also disclose details of MSME invoices routed and settled through TReDS, which creates transparency about who pays late.

What are the new timelines for disputes?

Mediation before the Facilitation Council must be completed within 90 days of the first appearance date, the Council must act within 30 days after mediation ends and under new Section 18(4A) an arbitral award must be made within 90 days of completion of pleadings.

Jurisdiction follows the supplier's registered address, wherever the buyer is in India, which prevents buyers from forcing suppliers to travel. The Centre may also set up online mediation and arbitration by video conferencing. Mediated settlements and awards can be recovered as arrears of land revenue and treated as debts recognised under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016.

What is the pre-deposit rule?

A challenger who is not the supplier must deposit 75 per cent of the awarded amount before a court entertains the challenge, and if a challenge stays pending for more than six months the court must order payment of at least 50 per cent.

This is a stronger version of the existing pre-deposit rule, which was 75 per cent under Section 19 of the 2006 Act and which the Supreme Court has examined in cases about challenges to awards. The courts may release part of the deposit to the supplier while the case is pending. The effect is to remove the incentive to challenge awards just to delay payment. The reform fits a broader trend in the 2026 laws toward faster enforcement, and sits next to the changes in the IBC amendment.

What are the penalties and the Facilitation Council changes?

A buyer contravening Section 22 faces a warning for the first offence, 10,000 to 50,000 rupees for the second and 50,000 to 1 lakh rupees for later offences. State governments must set up enough Facilitation Councils, each with 3 to 5 members including a legal member.

The Development Commissioner is the adjudicating officer under the new Section 27A, and minimum penalty amounts rise by 10 per cent every three years from commencement. Unpaid penalties can be recovered as arrears of land revenue. Section 22 requires medium and larger buyers to disclose in their financial statements the amounts due to MSMEs beyond 45 days, which is how contraventions come to light. Classification of enterprises is by investment and turnover together, and certain items, such as pollution control equipment and R&D costs, are excluded from the investment calculation.

How can this be asked in UGC NET Law, CLAT PG or AIBE?

Expect questions on the 2006 Act's payment rules, Section 18 reference to the Facilitation Council, the relationship with the Arbitration Act and the pre-deposit requirement.
