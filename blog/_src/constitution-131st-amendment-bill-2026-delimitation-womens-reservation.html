Quick answer
  • The Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026 was introduced on 16 April 2026 and failed in the Lok Sabha on 17 April: 298 for, 230 against.
  • It tried to speed up one-third women's reservation by using the 2011 census for delimitation.
  • The Delimitation Bill would have raised the House to a maximum of 850 seats. It lapsed with the amendment.
  • The 106th Amendment still stands, linked to the next census and delimitation.

A constitutional amendment that fails on the floor is rare, and this one touched two sensitive subjects at once: women's reservation in legislatures and the redrawing of constituencies. This guide explains what the bill proposed, why it fell short and how the constitutional rules on amendment decided the outcome.

What was the Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026?

It was a bill introduced in the Lok Sabha on 16 April 2026 by Law Minister Arjun Ram Meghwal to implement one-third reservation for women in the Lok Sabha and state assemblies without waiting for the next census, by basing delimitation on the 2011 census and expanding the House.

The 106th Amendment of 2023, known as the Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam, promised about 33 percent reservation for women, but tied it to the first census after the amendment and the delimitation exercise that follows. The government argued that waiting for that cycle would delay women's effective participation. The 131st Amendment tried to bring the timetable forward by using the existing 2011 census data.

Why did the 131st Amendment Bill fail?

A constitutional amendment needs a special majority under Article 368. The bill got 298 votes for and 230 against on 17 April 2026, short of the two-thirds of members present and voting required, so the Speaker declared that it failed to secure the requisite special majority.

With 528 members voting, two-thirds would have required about 352 votes, so the bill fell short by a wide margin. There was broad political agreement on women's representation, but not on the mechanism. The disputed points were the link between women's reservation, delimitation based on the 2011 census, and the increase in the House's overall strength.

What was the Delimitation Bill, 2026?

It was a companion bill that would have raised the maximum Lok Sabha strength from 550 to 850, with up to 815 members from states and up to 35 from Union Territories. It was designed to operate only if the amendment passed, so it did not proceed.

A Union Territories Laws (Amendment) Bill was introduced with it for the same purpose. Once the constitutional amendment was defeated, the government said it would not go ahead with either.

What is the special majority under Article 368?

A constitutional amendment must be passed in each House by a majority of the total membership and by at least two-thirds of members present and voting. Some amendments, such as those affecting federal provisions, also need ratification by half the state legislatures.

The rule is deliberately demanding. It means an amendment needs support beyond the ruling coalition and, for matters touching the states, beyond Parliament. It is also why a simple majority, enough to pass most laws, is not enough to change the Constitution. A bill that only reaches a simple majority fails even if more members vote for it than against.

Is women's reservation still law after the bill failed?

Yes. The Constitution (106th Amendment) Act remains in force. It ties implementation to the first census after it and the delimitation that follows, so the failure of the 131st Amendment leaves that timetable as it was and pushes the reform window further out.

The reservation is for one-third of seats in the Lok Sabha and state legislative assemblies, including within SC and ST quotas, and is to operate for a period set by the Act. Any attempt to bring it forward again would need a fresh amendment and a fresh special majority.
