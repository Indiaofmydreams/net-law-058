National Law University Delhi runs its own LL.M. admission through AILET, separate from the Consortium's CLAT PG. For many candidates the question is not just "how do I get in?" but "is it worth the fee and the competition?". This guide covers the process and the reported fee, and is plain about what we could not verify. The official source is NLU Delhi's website.

How do you get into the NLU Delhi LLM?

  1. Check eligibility. As reported, an LLB (3-year or 5-year) with 50 percent marks (45 percent for SC, ST and PwD). See the AILET LLM guide.
  2. Apply during NLU Delhi's application window, with the documents the notification asks for.
  3. Take the AILET LL.M. paper. The pattern is reported as 100 MCQs in 120 minutes with 0.25 negative marking.
  4. Merit lists. Admission is by rank within category, in one or more lists. Candidates then follow the instructions for document verification and fee payment.

NLU Delhi uses its own test for this programme, so a CLAT PG score does not substitute for it. If both matter to you, read CLAT PG vs AILET.

What is the NLU Delhi LLM fee?

A third-party compilation of NLU fees lists about 2.52 lakh rupees a year for the LLM at NLU Delhi, and notes that its own FAQ gives about 2.68 lakh, a discrepancy it does not resolve. The compilation also says hostel and mess charges are separate and that totals are approximate. Compare it with other NLUs in NLU LLM seats and fees, and read NLU Delhi's own fee notice for your year before you plan.

How many seats are there?

We could not verify the number of LL.M. seats at NLU Delhi, and we prefer to say so rather than quote an unsourced figure. The cut-off ranks reported for recent years are very small for the General category, which indicates a small intake and strong competition. Check the current brochure for the seat matrix, including any reserved and horizontal quotas.

What about placements?

We found no verified placement figures for the LL.M. specifically, so we give none. An LL.M. often leads to research, teaching, policy or practice rather than campus recruitment, so the usual measures are different. To judge value, look for:

  • The university's published outcomes or placement report.
  • Specialisation options and faculty research areas.
  • Internship and clinic opportunities.
  • What recent graduates do, from alumni you can contact.

How competitive is it?

One compilation reports a General closing rank of 58 in 2025 (up to Round 7) and 51 in 2024 (up to Round 5), with higher ranks for reserved categories. The numbers are given as ranks, not marks.
