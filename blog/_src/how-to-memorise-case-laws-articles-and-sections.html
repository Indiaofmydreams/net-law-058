Law exams reward recall: the number of the Article, the name of the case, the right section. Many aspirants try to hold all of it by rereading, and find on exam day that familiarity does not equal recall. This guide gives seven methods that work, with worked examples from the Constitution and the new criminal codes, so you can practise recall instead of rereading.

How do I remember case laws for law exams?

Reduce each case to a card with four lines: name, facts in one line, the holding in one line, and the Article or section it concerns. Review the cards at spaced intervals and always recall the holding before turning the card over. Learn the principle first and the name second.

Most exam questions on cases ask one of three things: which case laid down a principle, what a case held, or which provision it interprets. A four-line card answers all three. Write each card in your own words; copying a headnote word for word does not help memory. Example card:

LineContent
CaseKesavananda Bharati v State of Kerala (1973)
Facts (one line)Challenge to the Kerala land reform laws and the 24th, 25th and 29th Amendments
HoldingParliament can amend any part of the Constitution under Art 368 but cannot destroy its basic structure
ProvisionArt 368; basic structure doctrine

How many case laws do I need to know for a law entrance exam?

Quality matters more than quantity. A working target is 100 to 150 landmark cases across the core subjects, each known by principle, name and provision. Beyond that, the extra recall rarely pays, because most questions test the leading case of a doctrine, not the tenth case on it.

This is a planning figure, not an official number. Start from the cases that recur in your exam's past papers and in standard textbooks. Our CLAT PG Case Law Vault is a good starting list for constitutional and criminal cases, and past paper analysis shows which areas test cases most often.

How do I remember section numbers like BNS and IPC sections?

Group numbers by theme and learn the anchor of each group, not every digit. Learn that BNS 100 to 105 deal with homicide, and then the individual sections fall into place. Write old and new numbers side by side and recall them with the page covered.

What is the best way to memorise Articles of the Constitution?

Learn them in blocks with a one-line theme, not as a list of numbers. Equality is Articles 14 to 18, freedoms 19 to 22, exploitation 23 and 24, religion 25 to 28, culture and education 29 and 30, and remedies Article 32. Then attach one case to each block.

Do mnemonics work for law exams?

Yes for lists and sequences, and no for reasoning. A mnemonic helps you recall five writs or the order of steps in a procedure, but it cannot replace understanding a doctrine. Invent your own, because the effort of creating one fixes the list in memory better than borrowing one.

Use mnemonics where order or membership is the whole question: the five writs (habeas corpus, mandamus, certiorari, prohibition, quo warranto), the grounds of judicial review, the kinds of mortgage, or the essentials of a valid contract. Do not use them for doctrines such as the rule against bias or the test for strict liability, where an exam may twist the facts.

The common thread is recall. Reading a case five times produces familiarity, which feels like knowledge but fails under exam pressure. Writing the holding from memory three times produces knowledge. Our spaced repetition schedule shows when to do it.

How do I stop forgetting cases after a month?

Forgetting is normal; the fix is review at increasing gaps. Put each new card in a box labelled by its next review date and move it forward only if you recalled it correctly. Failed cards go back to the one-day box. After four successful reviews a case usually stays for the length of an exam season. If you only have two weeks, run the same boxes at shorter gaps of one, two, four and seven days.
