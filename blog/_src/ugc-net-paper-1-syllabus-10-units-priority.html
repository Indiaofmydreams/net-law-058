Quick answer
  • The Paper 1 syllabus has 10 units, and the paper design spreads its 50 questions (100 marks) across them, about five questions per unit.
  • It is common to every subject: it tests teaching, research and reasoning skills, not your degree subject.
  • Six units are knowledge units (learn, then recall). Four are skill units (learn by solving).
  • The order below is a study-sequence suggestion based on how each unit is learned, not an official ranking.

If you are searching for the UGC NET Paper 1 syllabus, you want two things: the list of units, and a way to decide where to begin. Both are below. The pattern is as reported by several preparation sites (50 multiple-choice questions, 2 marks each, no negative marking, one three-hour session shared with Paper 2). Confirm it in the current NTA bulletin before you plan around it.

What is in the UGC NET Paper 1 syllabus?

Paper 1 is the General Paper on Teaching and Research Aptitude. The syllabus is split into ten units. Topic wording here is shortened, so check the full text on the official NTA site.

How many questions come from each unit?

The design is ten equal units for 50 questions, so about five questions (ten marks) per unit. NTA does not publish the exact count per unit in advance, so treat five as a planning assumption. For what the papers on this site actually show, see which Paper 1 units score the most.

Which unit should I study first?

The syllabus does not rank units, so priority here means order of study. A sensible sequence, judged by how each unit is learned:

  1. Start the skill units early, in small daily doses. Comprehension, Mathematical Reasoning, Logical Reasoning and Data Interpretation improve only through repetition. Twenty minutes a day for several weeks beats one long weekend session.
  2. Learn Teaching and Research Aptitude next. They are the heart of the paper's name, the concepts connect to each other, and a graduate from any stream can read them without prior knowledge.
  3. Add the fact-heavy units in the middle weeks. ICT, People, Development and Environment, and Higher Education System reward notes plus repeated MCQs. Higher Education System also changes with policy, so read it closer to the exam.
  4. Keep Communication as a connector. It is short and overlaps with Teaching Aptitude (classroom communication), so it fits easily alongside other work.

This is reasoning from how people learn, not a guarantee of marks. If one of the later units is already your strength, move it down the list.

Is the Paper 1 syllabus the same for Law and other subjects?

Yes. Every candidate attempts the same Paper 1. Your subject, such as Law (058), is tested in Paper 2. If you are a Law candidate, plan Paper 1 together with the Law units using our Law syllabus priority guide.

A simple way to use the units

  • Read the short topic list for a unit, then attempt 20 to 30 MCQs from its page.
  • Note every topic where you guessed. Those are your gaps.
  • Return to the notes only for those gaps, then retest.
  • After each unit is covered once, switch to previous year questions to see how the topics are framed.
