Quick answer
  • Prajwala (29 May 2026): rehabilitation is part of Article 21; a Victim Protection Plan from pre-rescue to prosecution.
  • Surendra Manjhi (2 Nov 2026): directions to identify, rescue, release and rehabilitate bonded labourers under Articles 21 and 23.
  • Article 23 bans trafficking, begar and forced labour.

Trafficking and bonded labour are among the gravest violations of human dignity, and the Supreme Court addressed both in 2026. This guide explains the two rulings, the constitutional provisions behind them and the statutes that apply.

What did Prajwala v. Union of India (2026) hold?

On 29 May 2026 a bench of Justices Pardiwala and Mahadevan held that rehabilitation is part of Article 21, ordered a Victim Protection Plan covering pre-rescue to prosecution and recommended legislative reform.

The ruling treats rescue as only the first step. Without shelter, healthcare, counselling and livelihood support, victims can be re-trafficked. By linking rehabilitation to Article 21, the Court made it a constitutional entitlement and not just a policy goal.

What did Surendra Manjhi v. Union of India direct?

On 2 November 2026 the Court issued directions on identifying, rescuing, releasing and rehabilitating bonded labourers, relying on Articles 21 and 23.

What does Article 23 prohibit?

Article 23 prohibits traffic in human beings, begar and other similar forms of forced labour. It is enforceable against private persons as well as the State, as the Court held in People's Union for Democratic Rights (1982).

The same case, the Asiad Workers case, held that paying less than the minimum wage can amount to forced labour. Article 23(2) allows the State to impose compulsory service for public purposes, without discrimination.

What is a Victim Protection Plan?

It is a framework covering support for a trafficking victim from before rescue through rescue, shelter, rehabilitation and the criminal trial, so that protection does not end with the rescue.

Trafficking is also an offence under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, which replaced Section 370 of the IPC, and under special laws such as the Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act, 1956.

Which law abolished bonded labour?

The Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976 abolished the system, freed bonded labourers and cancelled their debts. Bandhua Mukti Morcha (1984) made the State's duty to identify and rehabilitate bonded labourers enforceable.
