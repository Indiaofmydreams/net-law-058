Students ask for "the most repeated AIBE questions", but the better question is "what does AIBE keep testing, and in what style?". The answer is plain provisions of the major Acts, asked in short objective questions. We have not seen verifiable, paper-by-paper topic counts, so this guide avoids inventing them. It gives you what can be shown (subject-wise question counts) and a method for turning any paper into revision.

Do AIBE questions repeat?

Exact questions rarely return, but themes do. Expect repeated attention to the core provisions: fundamental rights and writs, bail and procedure under the criminal procedure law, the stages of a civil suit and limitation, offences and defences, relevancy and admissibility of evidence, contract basics, transfer of property, marriage and succession rules, and professional ethics for advocates. The questions are usually direct: a definition, a time limit, a punishment or which provision applies.

How to use a previous year paper

  1. Sit it like the real exam. One sitting, no breaks. The pattern is reported as 100 questions in 3 hours 30 minutes.
  2. Answer every question. There is no negative marking as reported, so blanks only lose marks.
  3. Mark each answer as sure, guessed or blind.
  4. Review. For each miss, write the subject, the provision and whether you did not know it or misread.
  5. Fix by subject. Open the matching notes, revise that provision and attempt questions on the practice arena.

Common traps in AIBE questions

  • Old versus new criminal law. The BNS, BNSS and BSA replaced the IPC, CrPC and Evidence Act, so a question may use either numbering. Use our mapping guide.
  • Numbers. Time limits and punishments are favourite testing points. Keep a one-page sheet for them.
  • Look-alike options. Two options often differ by one word. Read all four before you mark.
  • Exceptions. The rule is easy, and the exception is the question.
