POWERGRID, the central transmission utility, is one of the best known PSUs for law graduates. Its law posts are rare but visible, and interest rises every time an advertisement is published. This guide covers what has been reported about eligibility, pay, bond and selection, with the caution that each cycle's notice decides.

Does POWERGRID recruit law officers through CLAT PG?

Yes, in recent cycles POWERGRID advertised law posts through CLAT PG scores, with an advertisement in its CC series. Details such as vacancies, scale and date change each year, so confirm in the current notice on the POWERGRID careers page.

The company works on transmission projects, so law work includes contracts, right-of-way and land compensation, electricity regulation before CERC and State commissions, arbitration and service matters.

What is the pay scale?

Reports and notices describe the entry scale for executive posts in the E-2 band, around ₹50,000 to ₹1,60,000 basic, with allowances, performance pay and other benefits on top. Treat these as approximate and check the notice for the exact figures.

What are the eligibility rules?

Candidates need a law degree (LLB or integrated) with a minimum percentage, usually 60% for General, and a valid CLAT PG score, with age limits and relaxation as per the notice. Check the cut-off date for the degree and your category certificate.

Age limits for General candidates have been reported around 28, with relaxation for reserved categories. Verify in the current notice before applying.

What is the training period and bond?

Selected candidates join as trainees for about a year with a stipend and training, then are absorbed as executives. A service bond with a financial amount is common in PSUs; read the exact bond term and amount in your offer.

See our guide on the PSU service bond and probation for how bonds work and what to ask HR.

How is the selection made?

Shortlisting is by CLAT PG score, followed by an interview or group stage as the notice states, then document and medical checks. Prepare for questions on contracts, arbitration, service law and the power sector.

Revise the Electricity Act, 2003 at a basic level, since it governs the sector.
