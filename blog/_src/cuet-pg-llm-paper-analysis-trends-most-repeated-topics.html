Is the CUET PG LLM paper tough?

No. Candidates rated the 2025 and 2026 LL.M. papers easy to moderate, with direct questions on basic subjects such as Constitutional Law, Contract, Torts and the IPC. The challenge is the breadth of 30-plus subjects and the negative marking.

The 2026 paper (17 March 2026) was described by Careers360 as easy to moderate, with an emphasis on basic subjects and no general awareness questions. Earlier reports describe the 2023 paper as largely theoretical and memory-based, and the 2024 paper as adding case-based questions on judicial review and PIL.

Which subject has the most questions in CUET PG LLM?

Constitutional Law, which usually supplies about 8 to 12 of the 75 questions in editorial estimates. Criminal Law, Contract, Torts, Jurisprudence and Hindu Law follow. NTA does not publish subject-wise counts, so treat these as planning ranges.

What types of questions are asked in CUET PG LLM?

Mostly direct recall of Articles, sections and case names, with statement-based questions, match-the-following and assertion-reason items. Short application questions are rarer than in CLAT PG. Qualifier words such as only, always and except decide many options.

  • Direct recall: which Article, section or Act.
  • Case to principle: which case laid down a doctrine.
  • Statements I and II: choose the correct combination.
  • Match the following: schools with jurists, cases with principles.

How many candidates appear for CUET PG LLM?

In 2026 about 14,701 candidates registered for the LL.M. paper and roughly 11,000 appeared, according to figures reported after NTA's result. In 2025 about 13,335 registered. Seats are limited and spread across universities, so competition depends on where you apply.

Because each university sets its own cut-off and has few LL.M. seats, a national rank matters less than your score against each university's earlier merit lists.

Which topics and cases are most repeated in CUET PG LLM?

Basic structure and fundamental rights cases, the general exceptions and murder provisions in criminal law, free consent and frustration in contract, negligence and strict liability in torts, and the major schools of jurisprudence. These are standard LL.B. staples, which is why the paper reads as easy to moderate.

  • Constitution: Kesavananda Bharati, Maneka Gandhi, Puttaswamy, S.R. Bommai, Minerva Mills, Indra Sawhney.
  • Criminal: Bachan Singh, Joseph Shine, common intention versus common object, murder versus culpable homicide.
  • Contract and torts: Carlill, Hadley v Baxendale, Mohori Bibee, Donoghue v Stevenson, Rylands v Fletcher, M.C. Mehta.
  • Jurisprudence: Austin, Kelsen, Hart, Savigny, Pound, Hohfeld.
  • Family: Shayara Bano, Vineeta Sharma, Shilpa Sailesh, Danial Latifi.

How should the trends change my preparation?

Prepare broad and accurate rather than narrow and deep: cover every subject to the basics, drill the core subjects to near-perfect recall, and learn old and new criminal-law numbering. Practise with negative marking so you learn which questions to skip.
