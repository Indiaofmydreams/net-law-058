Quick answers
  • Marks: Paper 1 is 100 and Law is 200, in a single three-hour sitting.
  • Daily split: about 65 percent Law and 35 percent Paper 1.
  • Law first: Constitutional and Administrative Law, Public International Law, Commercial Law.
  • Mocks: combined mock about day 30; weekly after that.

UGC NET Law (058) is two papers in one sitting, and most aspirants plan for one. A balanced 60-day plan covers both, gives Law the bigger share and keeps Paper 1 ticking over. This guide gives a daily time split, a unit order for each paper, and a mock and revision schedule for the next session or any late start.

Can I prepare UGC NET Paper 1 and Law together?

Yes, and you should, because both papers are written in one three-hour sitting and qualification depends on your combined performance. Give Law the larger share of study time, since it is 200 of 300 marks, but keep Paper 1 in your daily routine so that reasoning and comprehension do not go rusty.

Paper 1 is 50 questions (100 marks) and Law (058) is 100 questions (200 marks), as reported; the three hours cover both. A Paper 1 that is neglected until the last fortnight is a common source of avoidable loss, because its units are short and learnable. Qualifying criteria are set in NTA's information bulletin; check them for your category on ugcnet.nta.nic.in.

How many days do I need to prepare for UGC NET Law?

Sixty days is workable for a law graduate with a recent LL.B., if you study three to five hours daily. Candidates a few years out of law school, or aiming for a JRF-level score, need ninety to one hundred and twenty days. With three weeks, switch to the revision plan on this site.

If your session is the December 2026 one (14 to 18 and 21 December), you have under three weeks: follow the 3-week revision plan. If you are planning for the next session, this 60-day plan gives a fuller coverage cycle.

How should I split daily study time between Paper 1 and Law?

Use roughly 65 percent for Law and 35 percent for Paper 1 in the first month, then shift to 70 and 30 as mocks start. On a four-hour day, that is about two hours forty minutes for Law and one hour twenty for Paper 1. Keep Paper 1 daily, even if short.

Which units should I study first in UGC NET Law and Paper 1?

In Law, start with Constitutional and Administrative Law, then Public International Law and Commercial Law, and bring in Environment, IPR and Family Law next, with Jurisprudence early for vocabulary. In Paper 1, no unit dominates, so start with Teaching and Research Aptitude, then reasoning units and data interpretation.

Our past-paper analysis found that in the Law papers on this site Constitutional and Administrative Law supplied the largest share of questions, while in Paper 1 every unit appeared close to the same number of times. How do I schedule mocks and revision in a 60-day plan?

Take sectional tests from week three, the first combined three-hour mock around day 30, then one combined mock a week, increasing to two a week in the last ten days. Reserve the last three days for revision of sheets and your error log; no new topics.

Weekly rhythm for the 60 days

  • Monday to Thursday: two Law blocks and one Paper 1 block.
  • Friday: 50 mixed questions across both papers; review.
  • Saturday: a sectional test (Paper 1 or a Law unit set), then weak-topic work.
  • Sunday: weekly review; a combined mock from week five; rest in the evening.

What to do if you fall behind schedule

If you lose a week, do not compress everything. Drop the occasional units in Law, keep Paper 1 at 45 minutes a day and protect your mocks. An incomplete syllabus with strong mock practice usually beats a complete syllabus with no timed practice, especially because there is no negative marking and every attempted question has a chance of earning marks.
