Quick answer
  • On 23 September 2026 the Supreme Court referred the challenge to the Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners (Appointment) Act, 2023 to a Constitution Bench.
  • The case is Jaya Thakur v. Union of India; the referring bench was Justices Dipankar Datta and S.C. Sharma.
  • The core issue: the selection panel under Sections 6 and 7 versus the CJI-inclusive panel in Anoop Baranwal (2023).
  • Related pleas on CEC powers and removal of the CEC are pending.

Who appoints the people who run India's elections is a question about the independence of the Election Commission itself. In 2023 the Supreme Court set an interim system, and within months Parliament passed a law that changed it. In 2026 the Court sent the challenge to that law to a Constitution Bench. This guide explains the background and the legal issues.

Why was the CEC appointment law sent to a Constitution Bench?

On 23 September 2026 a division bench of Justices Dipankar Datta and S.C. Sharma, after four days of hearing, unanimously referred the challenge in Jaya Thakur v. Union of India to a Constitution Bench. The judges had earlier split on whether a larger bench was needed.

Justice Datta initially saw no substantial question of law that required a Constitution Bench, while Justice Sharma saw a need for authoritative interpretation. After hearing the matter further, the bench agreed to refer it. Under Article 145(3), cases involving a substantial question of law as to the interpretation of the Constitution must be heard by at least five judges.

What did Anoop Baranwal v. Union of India decide?

In March 2023 a Constitution Bench held that until Parliament makes a law under Article 324(2), the CEC and Election Commissioners are to be appointed by the President on the advice of a committee of the Prime Minister, the Leader of Opposition and the Chief Justice of India.

Article 324(2) says the President appoints the CEC and other Election Commissioners subject to any law made by Parliament, but Parliament had not made one for decades. The Court treated that silence as a gap and filled it with an interim, more neutral mechanism. It said the arrangement would hold until Parliament legislated.

What does the 2023 law say about appointing the CEC?

The 2023 Act makes appointments on the recommendation of a selection committee. Petitioners argue Sections 6 and 7 leave appointments heavily dependent on the executive, because the committee is not the one the Supreme Court had directed in Anoop Baranwal.

The Act can be defended as exactly the kind of law Article 324(2) anticipates, while the challenge says it hollows out the independence the Court meant to secure.

Who is on the selection committee under the 2023 Act?

The Act provides for a committee of the Prime Minister, the Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha and a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the Prime Minister. It replaced the Chief Justice of India, who was on the panel under Anoop Baranwal.

With the Prime Minister and a Cabinet Minister he nominates, the executive has two of the three votes. That arithmetic is what the petitioners say compromises the Commission's independence, while the Union has defended the law as a valid exercise of legislative power.

Is there a plea to remove the Chief Election Commissioner?

A plea seeking removal of CEC Gyanesh Kumar is pending in the Supreme Court, along with a challenge on the CEC's powers. The Election Commission filed its response on 26 October 2026.

A CEC can be removed only in the same manner and on the same grounds as a Supreme Court judge, under the proviso to Article 324(5), which is a significant protection. The pending pleas sit alongside challenges to the Special Intensive Revision of rolls. See our explainer on the Bihar SIR verdict.
