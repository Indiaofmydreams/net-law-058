Quick answer
  • AI changes tasks, not the need for judgment.
  • Most affected: research, review, first drafts.
  • Growing roles: privacy, AI governance, legal tech.
  • Always verify citations yourself.

Every law student now hears that AI will take their job. The truth is more nuanced, and the preparation is practical. This guide explains what is changing and what you can do.

Will AI replace lawyers and law officers?

Not as a whole, but it is changing tasks. Research, first drafts, summaries and contract review are increasingly done with AI tools, while judgment, advocacy, negotiation and accountability remain with humans. Roles are likely to shift, not vanish.

PSUs and regulators use AI more slowly than firms, but they too are adopting tools.

Which legal tasks are most affected?

Document review, case-law search, first-draft contracts, due diligence and summaries. Routine work that once trained juniors is the part most automated, so entry-level learning paths are changing.

TaskEffect
Case-law searchFaster, needs verification
Contract reviewPartly automated
DraftingFirst drafts by AI, human edit
Advocacy and negotiationRemain human

What new roles are emerging?

Legal technology, AI governance and compliance, data protection, privacy and tech policy roles are growing. Firms and companies also look for lawyers who can supervise AI output and manage risk.

What are the risks courts have flagged?

Courts have warned against filings with fake or unverified citations produced by AI. Lawyers remain responsible for what they file, so every citation and fact must be checked against the source.

Treat AI as a junior assistant whose work you must check.

What should LLM graduates do?

Learn how to use AI tools carefully, build strong fundamentals in law and writing, add a niche such as data protection, IP or tech regulation, and keep records of your verification. Judgment is the skill that grows in value.
