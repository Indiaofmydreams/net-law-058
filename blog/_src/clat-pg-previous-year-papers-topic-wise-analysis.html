Quick answer
  • Past papers show how the paper is built: 120 passage-based questions, 2 hours, -0.25 (as reported).
  • Themes repeat, not questions. Track the principle behind each question.
  • Run each paper like the real exam, then spend as long on review as on the test.
  • Keep an error log with subject, principle and cause.

Many aspirants solve a previous year paper, note the score and move on. That wastes most of its value. A past paper is a record of how the examiner thinks, and it is worth more as a diagnostic than as practice. We do not have a verified, paper-by-paper count of topics across years, so this guide does not claim one. It shows how to do the analysis yourself, which gives better results than any list of "most repeated topics".

Where do you find CLAT PG previous year papers?

Start with the Consortium of NLUs website, which is reported to publish past papers and answer keys. Coaching and news sites also compile them. Check that a paper matches the current pattern (120 passage-based MCQs in 2 hours) before you rely on its timing. Our 20-question sample and mock tests give extra practice in the same style.

What should you track in each paper?

ColumnWhat to write
SubjectConstitutional, criminal, contract, torts, jurisprudence, etc.
Principle or caseThe rule or landmark case the passage rests on
TimeRoughly how long the passage plus questions took
ResultRight, wrong or skipped
Cause of errorDid not know, misread the passage, fell for a close option, ran out of time

After three or four papers, patterns emerge in your own log: perhaps you lose marks on criminal-law passages through misreading, or on jurisprudence through unfamiliar vocabulary. That is far more useful than a list of topics from someone else.

Do CLAT PG questions repeat?

Exact questions do not return, but the underlying material does. Rights and state power, the structure of criminal liability, contract formation and remedies, negligence, and landmark cases appear again and again in new passages. Learn the principle, then see how it is dressed up. Our landmark case vault and syllabus guide help here.

A method for each paper

  1. Sit it like the real exam. Use a 2-hour timer and an OMR-style sheet. See the negative-marking maths before you decide what to guess.
  2. Mark confidence on each answer: sure, doubtful or guess.
  3. Review every wrong and every doubtful answer, even if it turned out right.
  4. Rewrite the principle in one line in your own words.
  5. Retest in a week with questions on the same principles from the practice arena.

Using past papers across a study plan

  • Early: one untimed paper to see the style and find weak subjects.
  • Middle: one timed paper a week, with topic repair in between.
  • Final fortnight: two papers a week, plus a light revision of your error log.
