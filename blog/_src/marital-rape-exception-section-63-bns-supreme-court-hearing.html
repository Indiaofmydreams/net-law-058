This is a sensitive and contested subject. The post explains the legal positions and arguments and does not argue for either outcome.

India is among the countries where the criminal law still carves out an exception for rape within marriage. The exception has been challenged for years, and the Supreme Court has now posted the main petitions for final hearing. This guide explains the law, the earlier decisions and how each side argues.

What is the marital rape exception?

Exception 2 to Section 63 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, carried over from Section 375 of the IPC, says that sexual intercourse by a man with his own wife, who is not under 18, is not rape. Challenges say it violates Articles 14, 15, 19 and 21.

The exception dates from the colonial penal code of 1860. Section 63 defines rape and lists the circumstances, such as lack of consent, in which intercourse is rape. The exception takes marital intercourse outside that definition, no matter whether the wife consented. Other laws, such as the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act and cruelty provisions, still apply.

What did Independent Thought (2017) decide?

The Supreme Court read down the exception so that it no longer protects intercourse with a wife aged 15 to 18, bringing it in line with the age of consent of 18. It did not rule on marital rape of adult wives.

The Court in Independent Thought v. Union of India reasoned that the exception conflicted with the POCSO Act and with the child's rights. It specifically left the question of adult wives open, so the debate continued. The Justice Verma Committee in 2013, formed after the Delhi gang rape, had recommended removing the exception, but Parliament did not accept that recommendation.

What happened in the Delhi High Court?

In May 2022 a two-judge bench gave a split verdict. One judge held the exception unconstitutional and the other upheld it, so the matter went to the Supreme Court through appeals.

A related appeal arises from a Karnataka High Court decision, which allowed a prosecution for rape against a husband to proceed. That case is titled Hrishikesh Sahoo v. State of Karnataka and is the first matter the Court plans to hear.

What is the Union's stand?

In a 2024 affidavit the Union opposed criminalising marital rape, saying existing remedies protect married women, that a rape charge within marriage may be excessively harsh and that the issue is social as much as legal and needs consultation with the States.

In court the Solicitor General said that the question of what is "natural" intercourse in marriage is for Parliament. The Union has also argued that courts should not create a new offence, a point that links to the Court's 2026 ruling that courts cannot create offences. Petitioners reply that they are not asking for a new offence but for a declaration that an exception is unconstitutional.

Where is the case now, and what is the Court asking?

On 9 September 2026 a bench of CJI Surya Kant, Justice Joymalya Bagchi and Justice V. Mohana posted the batch for final hearing, with the hearing date to be notified, and said it would consider both reading down and striking down the exception.

Justice Bagchi raised a pointed question: can a prosecution proceed before the Court decides whether the exception is valid, given Article 20 on penal laws and the need not to surprise citizens? The answer will shape how the case is argued. Check the Court's website for hearing dates, since the position can change.

How can this be asked in UGC NET Law, CLAT PG or AIBE?

Expect questions on Section 63 BNS and the old Section 375 IPC, Independent Thought, Articles 14 and 21 and the limits of judicial law-making.
