Quick answer
  • Four Paper 1 papers on this site (2021, 2024, June 2025, December 2025) give 200 questions, spread almost evenly across 10 units.
  • Comprehension and Data Interpretation were each a single set of five linked questions in every paper.
  • The format repeats more than the content: match lists, assertion-reason, statements and ordering.
  • Use papers to learn how each unit is asked, then revise the underlying topics.

Because Paper 1 is common to every subject, its questions are carefully balanced. That makes previous year papers especially useful, since they show the shape of the paper, not just its topics. This analysis uses the four full Paper 1 sets in the PYQ file on this site. It is a guide to patterns, not an NTA publication, and tags follow question content.

What does a Paper 1 paper look like?

Each paper in the file has 50 questions and follows the ten-unit structure, with five questions per unit in almost every case. Counts differ by at most one question in a paper, so the structure is predictable. See the unit-by-unit numbers in which units score the most.

Which formats repeat?

  • Match List I with List II appears in several units in nearly every paper.
  • Assertion and reason and two statements questions recur, mostly in the later papers.
  • Arrange in order (sequence of research steps, chronology of commissions, memory sizes) is common in fact-based units.
  • Direct questions still appear, such as a single definition or a name.

The point for practice is to get used to long option sets, where you must read all four lists before eliminating. Train that with timed sets, not with passive reading.

How to use the papers

  1. Take one paper on the PYQ page under a 45-minute timer.
  2. Tag each miss by unit and topic. Is it a format error, a knowledge gap or a speed problem?
  3. Send the knowledge gaps to the unit notes, for example Research Aptitude and Higher Education System.
  4. Retake a similar paper a week later and compare the error log.
