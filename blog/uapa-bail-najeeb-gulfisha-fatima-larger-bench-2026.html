Quick answer
  • Section 43D(5) of the UAPA bars bail if the accusation appears prima facie true.
  • Najeeb (three judges) lets courts grant bail despite that bar when delay and long custody breach Article 21.
  • In May 2026, Syed Iftikhar Andrabi v. NIA treated Najeeb as binding; on 22 May 2026 a bench referred the question to a larger bench.
  • Delay is a strong factor, but not an automatic right to bail.

Bail under the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act is one of the most contested areas of criminal law. Two threads run through 2026: a judgment that says Article 21 limits what the statute can do, and a reference to a larger bench because another bench had doubts about an earlier ruling. This guide explains the law and the current position, in plain language.

What does Section 43D(5) of the UAPA say about bail?

Section 43D(5) bars a court from releasing an accused on bail if, on perusal of the case diary or the police report, it believes the accusations are prima facie true. It sets a stricter bail test than ordinary criminal law, which makes UAPA bail hard.

The proviso applies to offences under Chapters IV and VI of the Act. In practice, an accused can spend years in custody before trial, because the court is not meant to weigh evidence in depth at the bail stage. This is the pressure point between the statute and the constitutional right to liberty.

What is the Najeeb principle on UAPA bail?

In Union of India v. K.A. Najeeb, a three-judge bench held that statutory restrictions on bail like Section 43D(5) do not oust constitutional courts' power to grant bail where prolonged incarceration and delay in trial violate the right to speedy trial under Article 21.

Najeeb sits alongside the 2019 Watali ruling, which told courts to take the prosecution case at face value at the bail stage. Reading the two together, the usual route to bail on a UAPA charge is delay and the length of custody, not a weak case on the merits.

What did the Supreme Court say in Syed Iftikhar Andrabi v NIA?

A bench of Justices B.V. Nagarathna and Ujjal Bhuyan said that "bail is the rule and jail is the exception" flows from Article 21 and cannot be displaced by legislation. It treated Najeeb as binding, said smaller benches cannot dilute it, and added that Najeeb does not create an automatic right to bail based on delay.

The bench also considered whether a smaller bench can weaken a larger bench's decision without expressly disagreeing with it. That question connects to the Gulfisha Fatima ruling, which another bench had doubts about.

Why was the UAPA bail question sent to a larger bench?

On 22 May 2026 Justices Aravind Kumar and P.B. Varale referred the question to a larger bench after a coordinate bench expressed serious reservations about the Gulfisha Fatima ruling. The issue is how Article 21 interacts with Section 43D(5).

Decision or eventBenchPoint
Watali (2019)Two judgesTake prosecution material at face value at bail stage
K.A. NajeebThree judgesDelay can justify bail despite Section 43D(5)
Gulfisha FatimaDivision benchDrew "serious reservations" from a coordinate bench
Syed Iftikhar Andrabi v. NIA (May 2026)Nagarathna and Bhuyan, JJ.Najeeb binding; no automatic right on delay
Reference (22 May 2026)Aravind Kumar and Varale, JJ.Question referred to a larger bench

Until the larger bench decides, courts will continue to apply Najeeb but with uncertainty about how far it can be read down.

Does a long wait in jail automatically get UAPA accused bail?

No. Courts have said delay is a strong factor under Article 21, but it does not create an automatic right to bail. They weigh the seriousness of the charge, the stage of the trial, the likelihood of a timely trial and the material against the accused.

A practical way to think about it is that Section 43D(5) sets the default, and Article 21 lets courts depart from it in cases of exceptionally long custody without trial. For related developments in bail law, see our note on bail and custody rulings of 2026.

How can this be asked in UGC NET Law, CLAT PG or AIBE?

Expect questions on the text of Section 43D(5), the Najeeb principle, the idea of precedent and the hierarchy of benches, and the maxim "bail is the rule". A question may ask which case allowed bail under the UAPA on the ground of delay.

  • Link it to: the doctrine that a smaller bench cannot overrule a larger bench.
