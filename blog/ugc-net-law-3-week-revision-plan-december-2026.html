The plan in one line

Week 1 rebuilds the base, week 2 tests it, week 3 sharpens and rehearses. Every day ends with a short MCQ set and a note of what you got wrong.

The UGC NET December 2026 exam begins on 14 December. If you are reading this in the last weeks of November, you have roughly three weeks. That is not time to learn everything again. It is enough to revise what you know, find the gaps and rehearse the actual exam. This plan assumes about four to five focused hours a day. Scale it down if you have less.

The ground rules

  • Revise from notes, not new books. Switching sources now costs time you do not have.
  • Test every day. Reading feels productive, but MCQs show what you really remember.
  • Keep an error log. One page: the question, why you got it wrong, the correct rule. Read it every night.
  • Protect sleep. Recall drops sharply when you are tired.

Week 3: Sharpen and rehearse

  • Three full-length mocks spread through the week. Take at least one Law mock and one Paper 1 mock at the same time of day as your exam shift, once you know it.
  • Previous year questions. Work through the PYQs to see how examiners frame questions and which topics repeat.
  • Weak-unit repair. From your error log, pick the two or three units that cost you the most marks and re-read only those notes.
  • Know your target. The cut-off trend helps you judge how many correct answers you need.

How to review a mock properly

  1. Mark every question you guessed, even if you got it right.
  2. For each wrong or guessed question, write the rule in one line in your error log.
  3. Sort mistakes into three types: did not know, confused two options, misread the question. Each needs a different fix.
  4. Check your pacing. About a minute a question leaves time to review.

The final 48 hours

  • Stop new topics. Read only your error log, short notes and key case names or section numbers.
  • Download the admit card (if you have not) and check your centre, shift and reporting time.
  • Pack the documents listed on your admit card, and plan the journey the day before.
  • Sleep well. A rested mind recalls more than an extra hour of cramming.

Not sure about your dates? See our post on the UGC NET December 2026 schedule and check your subject's date in NTA's official notice.

Frequently asked questions

Can I finish the Law syllabus in three weeks?

Not from scratch. Three weeks is enough to revise all ten units once, practise under exam conditions and fix weak areas. If you are far behind, prioritise the units where you already score 50 percent or more, and use the 2-week plan for a tighter schedule.

How many mock tests should I take?

Aim for about two full-length Law mocks a week early on and three a week in the last ten days, each followed by a proper review. One reviewed mock teaches more than three attempted and ignored.
