12 practice questions with answers

1The Biological Diversity Act 2002 establishes which three-tier structure for biodiversity governance?

  1. Central Board, State Board, District Board
  2. National Biodiversity Authority, State Biodiversity Boards, Biodiversity Management Committees
  3. Ministry, CPCB, Local Committees
  4. NBA, State Forests Departments, Gram Panchayats
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. National Biodiversity Authority, State Biodiversity Boards, Biodiversity Management Committees

Biological Diversity Act 2002 establishes: National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) at national level, State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs) at state level, and Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs) at local body level. BMCs are responsible for preparing People's Biodiversity Registers (PBRs).

2Under the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act 1974, a person can discharge sewage or trade effluent into a stream or well only with:

  1. Permission of the District Collector
  2. Approval of the Central Pollution Control Board
  3. Consent of the State Pollution Control Board
  4. Permission of the Ministry of Environment
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Consent of the State Pollution Control Board

Section 25 of the Water Act 1974: No person shall, without the previous consent of the State Board, establish or take any steps to establish any industry likely to discharge sewage or trade effluent into a stream or well. Consent of SPCB is mandatory - violation is punishable under Section 44.

3The Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act 1981 empowers the State Government to declare any area as an:

  1. Eco-sensitive zone
  2. Air pollution control area
  3. Critical pollution zone
  4. Environmental protection zone
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Air pollution control area

Section 19 of the Air Act 1981: The State Government may, after consultation with the State Board, by notification in the Official Gazette, declare in such manner as may be prescribed, any area or areas within the State as air pollution control area. Once declared, no person can use any fuel other than approved fuel.

4The Hazardous and Other Wastes (Management and Transboundary Movement) Rules 2016 replaced which earlier rules?

  1. Hazardous Waste Rules 1989
  2. Toxic Substances Rules 2006
  3. Chemical Accidents Rules 1996
  4. Hazardous Wastes (Management, Handling and Transboundary Movement) Rules 2008
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Hazardous Wastes (Management, Handling and Transboundary Movement) Rules 2008

The Hazardous and Other Wastes Rules 2016 replaced the Hazardous Wastes Rules 2008. The 2016 Rules introduced a new category called 'other wastes' (like paper waste, metal scrap) separate from hazardous wastes and streamlined procedures for transboundary movement.

5The Wetlands (Conservation and Management) Rules 2017 were framed under:

  1. Wildlife Protection Act 1972
  2. Forest Conservation Act 1980
  3. Water Act 1974
  4. Environment Protection Act 1986
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Environment Protection Act 1986

Wetlands (Conservation and Management) Rules 2017 replaced the Wetlands Rules 2010 and were framed under EPA 1986. They prohibit certain activities in wetlands (industrialization, solid waste dumping, construction) and provide for Wetland Authorities at state level.

6The Ozone Depleting Substances (Regulation and Control) Rules 2000 were enacted to implement:

  1. Vienna Convention 1985 only
  2. Montreal Protocol 1987 obligations
  3. UNFCCC obligations
  4. Basel Convention obligations
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Montreal Protocol 1987 obligations

ODS Rules 2000 (framed under EPA 1986) implement India's obligations under the Montreal Protocol 1987 by regulating production, import, export, sale and use of ozone-depleting substances including CFCs, HCFCs, halons, carbon tetrachloride and methyl bromide.

7The Wild Life (Protection) Amendment Act 2022 introduced the concept of:

  1. Community reserves as a new protected area category
  2. Conservation reserves - to connect protected areas
  3. Invasive alien species regulation
  4. Both (B) and (C)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Both (B) and (C)

Wild Life (Protection) Amendment Act 2022 introduced: regulation of invasive alien species (IAS) for the first time in Indian wildlife law; simplified protected area management; reduced schedules from 6 to 4; brought CITES implementation under the Act; and strengthened provisions on captive animals and zoos.

8The National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS) in India are prescribed by:

  1. Central Government under EPA 1986
  2. Ministry of Health and Family Welfare
  3. Bureau of Indian Standards
  4. Central Pollution Control Board under the Air Act 1981
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Central Pollution Control Board under the Air Act 1981

National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS) are prescribed by the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) under the Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act 1981. The current NAAQS were revised in 2009. They set permissible concentration limits for 12 pollutants including PM10, PM2.5, SO2, NO2, CO, Ozone, Lead.

9The Chemical Accidents (Emergency Planning, Preparedness and Response) Rules 1996 were framed under:

  1. Factories Act 1948
  2. Disaster Management Act 2005
  3. Public Liability Insurance Act 1991
  4. Environment Protection Act 1986
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Environment Protection Act 1986

Chemical Accidents Rules 1996 were framed under Section 3 of the EPA 1986. They require major accident hazard (MAH) units to prepare On-Site Emergency Plans. District/state/central authorities must prepare Off-Site Emergency Plans for chemical accidents. Enacted following Bhopal and other industrial disasters.

10The Wetlands (Conservation and Management) Rules 2017 provide for Wetland Authorities at which level?

  1. National level only
  2. State level - one Wetland Authority per state
  3. District level
  4. Central and State levels both
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. State level - one Wetland Authority per state

Wetlands (Conservation and Management) Rules 2017 provide for State Wetland Authorities (SWAs) - one per state - to oversee implementation of wetland conservation within their jurisdiction. The National Wetlands Committee at the central level provides overall guidance and coordination.

11The Environment (Protection) Act 1986 gives power to the Central Government under Section 3(3) to:

  1. Only regulate industries
  2. Constitute an authority or authorities for the purpose of exercising and performing such powers and functions as the Central Government deems necessary
  3. Only prosecute offenders
  4. Regulate only water pollution
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Constitute an authority or authorities for the purpose of exercising and performing such powers and functions as the Central Government deems necessary

Section 3(3) of EPA 1986: The Central Government may, if it considers it necessary, constitute an authority or authorities for exercising and performing such of the powers and functions of the Central Government under this Act. This provision has been used to constitute the NCZMA, REIA authorities, and other bodies.

12The 'Extended Producer Responsibility' (EPR) concept is incorporated in which Indian rules?

  1. Only in the E-Waste Management Rules
  2. Only in the Hazardous Waste Rules
  3. Only in the Chemical Accidents Rules
  4. E-Waste Management Rules, Plastic Waste Management Rules, and Battery Waste Management Rules under EPA 1986
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. E-Waste Management Rules, Plastic Waste Management Rules, and Battery Waste Management Rules under EPA 1986

Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) is incorporated in multiple rules under EPA 1986: E-Waste (Management) Rules (manufacturers/importers responsible for e-waste collection); Plastic Waste Management Rules (brand owners responsible for plastic waste); Battery Waste Management Rules; and Construction and Demolition Waste Management Rules.
