UGC NET Law exam: pattern, eligibility and dates

The exam basics. For anything that can change, we point you to the official source.

What is the UGC NET Law (058) exam pattern?

UGC NET is a single three-hour computer-based session with two objective papers: Paper 1 (general aptitude) and Paper 2 (your subject, here Law 058). Every question is multiple choice and carries 2 marks.

  • Paper 1: 50 questions, 100 marks.
  • Paper 2 (Law): 100 questions, 200 marks.
  • Total: 150 questions, 300 marks.

Paper 2 carries two-thirds of the marks, which is why NET Law 058 is built around Law practice. Patterns can change between sessions, so check the current Information Bulletin.

Official NTA UGC NET site

How many questions and how much time are there in UGC NET Law?

Across both papers there are 150 questions in one continuous three-hour session: 50 in Paper 1 and 100 in Paper 2 (Law).

As a pacing target, not a rule, about one minute per question leaves roughly 30 minutes to review. NET Law 058's full-length Law mocks are 100 questions in 90 minutes, with a harder set at 120 minutes. Confirm the exact instructions for your session in the NTA bulletin.

Is there negative marking in UGC NET Law?

No. Recent UGC NET sessions have awarded 2 marks for each correct answer and no deduction for wrong or unattempted questions.

Because wrong answers cost nothing, attempting every question is the usual advice. All NET Law 058 mocks follow the same rule: 2 marks per correct answer, no negative marking. Rules can change, so confirm in the bulletin each cycle.

What is the eligibility for UGC NET Law?

Eligibility depends on your qualifying degree, the minimum marks required, and (for the fellowship) an age limit. Exact criteria are fixed in each year's NTA Information Bulletin, so read it before you apply.

In general, a master's degree in Law (typically an LL.M. or equivalent) with the prescribed minimum marks is the usual route, with relaxations for reserved categories and provisions for final-year candidates and four-year undergraduate programmes. An upper age limit applies to the JRF, while Assistant Professor eligibility has had no upper age limit.

Percentages, age cut-off dates and relaxations change, so we deliberately do not quote them here.

What is the difference between JRF, Assistant Professor and PhD qualification?

UGC NET results place qualified candidates in three categories: JRF with Assistant Professor and PhD eligibility, Assistant Professor with PhD eligibility, or PhD admission only.

Which category you fall into depends on your score and the cut-offs NTA declares for that session. Qualifying makes you eligible; appointments and admissions are decided by the institutions concerned.

How are NET Law 058 mock tests timed and scored?

Each question carries 2 marks, so a 100-question mock is out of 200 marks, with no negative marking. A countdown timer runs throughout and the test is submitted automatically at zero.

You are warned at 15, 5 and 1 minute remaining. Use the question palette to see answered, not-visited and marked-for-review questions at a glance.

Pomodoro timer and study tools

A free focus timer and the small features that make daily practice easier.

What is the Pomodoro timer on NET Law 058?

It is a free focus timer built into the site: work in focused rounds (25 minutes by default) with short breaks in between, and a longer break after four rounds.

  • Two presets: 25 minutes focus with a 5-minute break, or 50 minutes focus with a 10-minute break.
  • Long break after every four focus rounds (15 or 30 minutes).
  • Controls: start, pause, optional sound, and a count of rounds completed today.

How do I use the Pomodoro timer while practising?

Open any MCQ practice list and the timer appears at the bottom left of the screen. Start a round, practise until it ends, then rest.

It keeps running as you move between pages, so you will not lose a round by switching units. A good rhythm is one round for a block of questions, then use the break to review mistakes.

Why is there no Pomodoro timer inside mock tests?

Mock tests use their own exam timer, and a second clock would be a distraction. The Pomodoro timer is for study and practice, not for timed papers.

Does the Pomodoro technique help with UGC NET preparation?

It can help you stay consistent: short, focused rounds with planned breaks make long preparation easier to start and sustain. It is a study habit, not a guarantee of results.

Treat each round as one clear task, such as one unit's 30 questions or one case-law revision, and finish it before checking your phone.

Preparation strategy

Practical ways to plan your time. Adapt them to your own schedule.

How should I prepare for UGC NET Law in 30, 60 or 90 days?

Whatever the timeline, cover the syllabus once, practise unit-wise every day, and shift to timed mocks and mistake review in the final weeks.

  • 90 days: about 50 days of unit-wise study with daily MCQs, then revision with a weekly mock, then a mock every 2 to 3 days with careful review.
  • 60 days: about 30 days covering units by importance, then revision and mocks on alternate days.
  • 30 days: skip fresh deep reading. Revise notes and bare Acts, drill MCQs by unit and take frequent mocks.

These are suggestions, not a guarantee of results.

How should I use MCQs and mock tests together?

Use unit-wise MCQs to learn and fix gaps, and mocks to practise timing, stamina and exam temperament.

After each mock, spend as long on the review as on the test: read every explanation for the questions you got wrong or guessed, then re-practise those units with the Mistakes filter.

How do I stay consistent while studying for UGC NET?

Make the habit small and visible: a daily question target, one or two Pomodoro rounds, and a streak you do not want to break.

Combine the daily goal with the Pomodoro timer, and plan breaks as seriously as study. Consistency usually beats long, irregular sessions.

How do I revise case law effectively?

Revise each case as a trio: the principle it established, the provision or doctrine it relates to, and one fact that helps you recall it.

Group cases by unit, use MCQ explanations to spot how cases are tested, and re-check holdings from the judgment before relying on them.

How do I manage time in the 3-hour exam?

Paper 2 carries two-thirds of the marks, so protect time for it. Aim for roughly a minute per question, mark doubtful ones for review and return after finishing the rest.

Since wrong answers carry no penalty, do not leave any question blank. Rehearse this pacing in the timed mocks before exam day.
