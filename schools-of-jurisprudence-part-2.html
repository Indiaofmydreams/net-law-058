About this question set

This set has 19 multiple-choice questions on Schools of Jurisprudence from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 1, Jurisprudence. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 19 of the 19 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Jurisprudence notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

19 practice questions with answers

1Who said 'Law is the cement of society and an essential medium of change'?

  1. Pound
  2. Friedmann
  3. Hart
  4. Kelsen
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Friedmann

W. Friedmann made this statement highlighting law's dual role in social cohesion and change.

Report this question

2The 'command theory' of law fails to account for which type of law?

  1. Criminal law
  2. Permissive/power-conferring rules (e.g., law of contract and wills)
  3. Constitutional law
  4. Both power-conferring rules and constitutional law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Both power-conferring rules and constitutional law

Hart's critique: Austin's command theory cannot explain power-conferring rules or constitutional law.

Report this question

3Which of the following correctly states the chronological development of Natural Law?

  1. Medieval → Greek → Roman → Modern
  2. Roman → Greek → Medieval → Modern
  3. Modern → Medieval → Greek → Roman
  4. Greeks → Roman → Medieval → Renaissance → Modern
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Greeks → Roman → Medieval → Renaissance → Modern

Natural law developed: Stoics/Greeks → Roman jurists → Aquinas (Medieval) → Grotius (Renaissance) → Social contract theorists → Fuller (Modern revival).

Report this question

4The concept of 'positive morality' was given by:

  1. Kelsen
  2. Hart
  3. Fuller
  4. Austin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Austin

Austin divided norms into positive law, positive morality, and divine law. Only positive law is the proper subject of jurisprudence.

Report this question

5'Sociological jurisprudence' differs from 'sociology of law' in that:

  1. Sociological jurisprudence uses sociology to improve law; sociology of law studies law as a social fact
  2. They are identical
  3. Sociology of law is normative; sociological jurisprudence is empirical
  4. Sociological jurisprudence ignores society
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Sociological jurisprudence uses sociology to improve law; sociology of law studies law as a social fact

Sociological jurisprudence is normative (how law should serve society); sociology of law is empirical (how law functions in society).

Report this question

6The 'living law' concept was given by:

  1. Roscoe Pound
  2. Eugen Ehrlich
  3. Savigny
  4. Holmes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Eugen Ehrlich

Ehrlich: The 'living law' is the law actually followed in society, which may differ from official state law.

Report this question

7Which of the following pairs is INCORRECTLY matched?

  1. Savigny — Historical School
  2. Kelsen — Pure Theory
  3. Austin — Analytical School
  4. Roscoe Pound — Realist School
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Roscoe Pound — Realist School

Roscoe Pound belongs to the Sociological School, NOT the Realist School.

Report this question

8The 'historical' school attributes the origin of law primarily to:

  1. Custom and popular consciousness
  2. Reason
  3. Divine will
  4. Sovereign command
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Custom and popular consciousness

The Historical School: law springs from the spirit/consciousness of the people, expressed through custom.

Report this question

9'Law is a social fact' — this view is most closely associated with:

  1. Analytical positivism
  2. Natural law
  3. Historical school
  4. Sociological jurisprudence
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Sociological jurisprudence

Sociological jurisprudence treats law as embedded in social reality, a social fact.

Report this question

10The Natural Law theory underwent a 'renaissance' after World War II primarily due to:

  1. Kelsen's Pure Theory
  2. The rise of the welfare state
  3. Hart's critique of Austin
  4. The Nuremberg Trials and atrocities of Nazism
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The Nuremberg Trials and atrocities of Nazism

The Nazi regime's use of positivist law to justify atrocities led to a natural law revival — Radbruch's formula, Fuller's work.

Report this question

11Who among the following said that law is 'the prediction of what courts will do'?

  1. Karl Llewellyn
  2. Oliver Wendell Holmes
  3. Jerome Frank
  4. Roscoe Pound
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Oliver Wendell Holmes

Holmes stated this in 'The Path of the Law' (1897) — the foundational statement of American Legal Realism.

Report this question

12Jerome Frank's contribution to American Realism is his focus on:

  1. Statutory interpretation
  2. 'Fact skepticism' — uncertainty arising from the trial court's finding of facts
  3. Appellate court decisions
  4. Legislative history
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 'Fact skepticism' — uncertainty arising from the trial court's finding of facts

Frank was a 'fact skeptic' — he focused on the unpredictability of fact-finding at trial level.

Report this question

13Karl Llewellyn is associated with which type of skepticism in American Realism?

  1. Fact skepticism
  2. Value skepticism
  3. Rule skepticism
  4. Moral skepticism
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Rule skepticism

Llewellyn was a 'rule skeptic' — he doubted that stated legal rules actually determine judicial decisions.

Report this question

14Who among the following is NOT associated with the Natural Law school?

  1. Cicero
  2. Jeremy Bentham
  3. St. Thomas Aquinas
  4. Hugo Grotius
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Jeremy Bentham

Bentham was a legal positivist and utilitarian who strongly opposed natural law as 'nonsense upon stilts'.

Report this question

15Bentham called natural rights:

  1. 'Nonsense upon stilts'
  2. The foundation of law
  3. Divinely inspired
  4. The basis of justice
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 'Nonsense upon stilts'

Bentham attacked natural rights in 'Anarchical Fallacies' as 'nonsense upon stilts' — a famous positivist critique.

Report this question

16Savigny's view that law cannot be made but only found/discovered is called:

  1. The Command Theory
  2. The Volksgeist Theory
  3. The Organic Theory
  4. The Normative Theory
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The Volksgeist Theory

Volksgeist theory: law is not made but grows organically from the spirit/consciousness of the people.

Report this question

17The 'sociological movement' in jurisprudence was pioneered in Europe by:

  1. Montesquieu and Comte
  2. Roscoe Pound
  3. Oliver Wendell Holmes
  4. Savigny
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Montesquieu and Comte

Montesquieu's 'Spirit of Laws' (1748) and Comte's positivist sociology laid the European foundation for sociological jurisprudence.

Report this question

18Eugen Ehrlich's concept of 'living law' means:

  1. Constitutional law that is enforceable
  2. Natural law principles
  3. Law reformed by legislation
  4. Law actually observed in society, which may differ from official law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Law actually observed in society, which may differ from official law

Ehrlich: the 'living law' is what people actually follow in their associations and groups, separate from state law.

Report this question

19Hart's primary rules are rules that:

  1. Confer powers on individuals or officials
  2. Specify how other rules may be created or changed
  3. Define the ultimate source of law
  4. Impose obligations/duties on individuals
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Impose obligations/duties on individuals

Primary rules impose duties and obligations on persons — they are the first-order rules of a legal system.
