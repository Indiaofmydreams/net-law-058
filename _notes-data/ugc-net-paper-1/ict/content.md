# ICT: Detailed Notes for UGC NET Paper 1

## Chapter 1: Basics of Information and Communication Technology

### 1.1 Meaning and Scope of ICT

ICT stands for **Information and Communication Technology**. It is the umbrella term for all technologies used to **capture, store, process, retrieve, transmit and exchange information** in digital form. It is an extended form of Information Technology (IT), with extra emphasis on **telecommunications and unified communications**: telephony, wireless signals, middleware, audio-visual systems and networks. IT focuses on computing and data processing. ICT adds the communication layer, so a person can send, share and collaborate across distances.

**ICT tools** are hardware, software and networks, such as smartphone, laptop, tablet, projector, smart board, internet, e-mail, LMS and video-conferencing. **Chalk and duster are not ICT tools.** This is a favourite "which is NOT" question.

### 1.2 Components of ICT

- **Hardware:** physical devices, such as computers, phones, routers and projectors.

- **Software:** programs that run on hardware.

- **Networks and connectivity:** LAN, WAN, internet, wireless.

- **Data and content:** text, audio, video, databases.

- **People and procedures:** users, trainers, policies.

### 1.3 Role of ICT in Teaching and Learning

The correct exam answer is that ICT **enhances access, interactivity and quality of instruction**. It does *not* replace teachers, remove assessment or reduce learner participation. Key points:

- Anytime, anywhere learning (flexible access).

- Multimedia content improves understanding and retention.

- Supports different learning styles and personalised learning.

- Enables collaboration (shared documents, discussion forums).

- Provides quick assessment and feedback (online quizzes).

- Makes quality content available to remote learners (SWAYAM, DIKSHA).

- Helps teachers with record-keeping, lesson planning and reuse of resources.

### 1.4 ICT in Research

- Literature review using online databases (Scopus, Web of Science, JSTOR, Google Scholar) and **reference managers** (Zotero, Mendeley, EndNote).

- Data collection with online surveys (Google Forms) and sensors.

- Data analysis with software (SPSS, R, Python, NVivo for qualitative data).

- Plagiarism checking (Turnitin, URKUND/Ouriginal in India through Shodhshuddhi).

- Publishing and sharing (institutional repositories, Shodhganga, ORCID, preprint servers).

- Collaboration through cloud tools (Google Drive, Overleaf).

A question may ask which is an application of ICT in research. Pick the option involving online databases or software, not hand notes or print-only reliance.

### 1.5 ICT in Administration

Universities use **ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning)** software for admissions, fees, examination, payroll and student records. The same idea appears as MIS (Management Information System), digital attendance, biometric systems and online grievance portals. If a scenario describes admissions, fees and records, the answer is **administration**.

### 1.6 Digital Divide

The **digital divide** is the gap between those who have access to ICT and those who do not. Causes include income, location (urban versus rural), gender, age, disability, language barrier and low digital literacy. Types are the access divide (devices and internet), the skills divide and the usage divide. Remedies are BharatNet, Common Service Centres, low-cost devices, regional language content and digital literacy programmes (PMGDISHA). Do not confuse **digital divide** (gap in access) with **digital literacy** (skills) or **digital library** (a collection).

### 1.7 Data, Information, Knowledge and Wisdom (DIKW)

| Level           | Meaning                                                    | Example                                                           |
|-----------------|------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Data**        | Raw, unprocessed facts and figures                         | Marks of 50 students                                              |
| **Information** | Processed, organised data with meaning                     | "The class average is 62%"                                        |
| **Knowledge**   | Understanding gained by analysing and applying information | Knowing which teaching method works best after analysing results  |
| **Wisdom**      | Judgement to apply knowledge ethically and effectively     | Deciding which method to adopt for the long-term good of learners |

**Order (lowest to highest): Data → Information → Knowledge → Wisdom.** Exam questions give a scenario and ask you to label the level. Use this trick: a list or number alone is data; a summary or statistic is information; an insight about what works or why is knowledge; a decision with judgement is wisdom. Also remember **data is plural of datum**, and information needs context.

### Memory Capsule: Chapter 1

- ICT = IT + communication.

- Digital divide = access gap.

- Moodle and Google Classroom = Learning Management Systems (LMS).

- DIKW order is a frequent "arrange in order" question.

## Chapter 2: Computer Fundamentals

### 2.1 What Is a Computer

A computer is an electronic device that accepts **input**, **processes** it under stored instructions and produces **output**, with the ability to **store** results. Basic cycle: **Input → Processing → Output → Storage**. The modern design follows the **Von Neumann architecture**, in which the program and data are stored in the same memory (the stored-program concept).

**Characteristics:** speed, accuracy, diligence (no fatigue), versatility, storage capacity, no IQ (cannot think on its own), automation.

### 2.2 Generations of Computers

| Generation | Period             | Key technology                                         | Features and examples                                                                                            |
|------------|--------------------|--------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **First**  | 1940–1956          | **Vacuum tubes**                                       | Machine language, huge size, heat, high power use, punched cards. ENIAC, UNIVAC, EDVAC                           |
| **Second** | 1956–1963          | **Transistors**                                        | Assembly language and high-level languages (**COBOL, FORTRAN**), smaller, faster, magnetic core memory. IBM 1401 |
| **Third**  | 1964–1971          | **Integrated Circuits (IC)**                           | Keyboards and monitors replaced punched cards and printouts, operating systems, multiprogramming. IBM 360        |
| **Fourth** | 1971–present       | **Microprocessors (LSI/VLSI)**                         | The **Intel 4004 (1971)** began this era, personal computers, GUI, networking. IBM PC, Apple Macintosh           |
| **Fifth**  | Present and future | **Artificial Intelligence, ULSI, parallel processing** | Natural language processing, expert systems, robotics, quantum computing research                                |

**Memory aids:** Vacuum → Transistor → IC → Microprocessor → AI (V-T-I-M-A). Machine language was used in the first generation. COBOL and FORTRAN belong to the second generation. Keyboard and monitor arrived in the third. Microprocessor arrived in the fourth. AI is the fifth. Watch for the period traps: the second generation starts in 1956 and the first runs from 1940 to 1956.

### 2.3 Types of Computers

**By size and power:**

- **Supercomputer:** fastest and most powerful. Used for weather forecasting, nuclear simulation, space research and genomics. Speed is measured in **FLOPS** (floating point operations per second). India's **PARAM** series was developed by **C-DAC** under Dr Vijay Bhatkar. Other Indian examples are Pratyush (IITM Pune) and Mihir (NCMRWF). Foreign examples are Cray and Fugaku.

- **Mainframe:** large organisations such as banks, airlines and railways. Supports **bulk transaction processing and many simultaneous users**. Example: IBM Z series.

- **Minicomputer:** midsize, between microcomputers and mainframes, multi-user.

- **Microcomputer:** built around a **microprocessor**. Desktop, laptop, tablet, smartphone, single-user.

**Supercomputer versus mainframe:** a supercomputer solves a few extremely complex scientific calculations quickly. A mainframe handles a vast number of transactions reliably. This is a concept question.

**By working principle:**

- **Analog:** processes continuous data (temperature, speed, pressure). Example: thermometer, speedometer.

- **Digital:** processes discrete binary data (0 and 1). Example: PC.

- **Hybrid:** combines both. Example: ICU patient monitors, which measure analog body signals and display digital readings.

### 2.4 The CPU (Central Processing Unit)

The CPU is the brain of the computer. Parts:

- **ALU (Arithmetic Logic Unit):** performs arithmetic (add, subtract) and logical (AND, OR, NOT, comparison) operations.

- **Control Unit (CU):** directs and coordinates all operations, fetches and decodes instructions and controls data flow. It does not process data itself.

- **Registers:** very small, fastest storage inside the CPU that hold data and instructions being processed. Examples: **Program Counter (PC)** holds the address of the next instruction, **Instruction Register (IR)** holds the current instruction, **Accumulator** holds intermediate results, **MAR** holds a memory address, **MDR** holds data read or written.

**Instruction cycle:** Fetch → Decode → Execute → Store. **Clock speed** is measured in Hz (MHz, GHz). Other performance factors are the number of cores, cache size and word length. **Bus** types: data bus, address bus and control bus.

### 2.5 Number Systems

| System      | Base | Digits                       |
|-------------|------|------------------------------|
| Binary      | 2    | 0, 1                         |
| Octal       | 8    | 0 to 7                       |
| Decimal     | 10   | 0 to 9                       |
| Hexadecimal | 16   | 0 to 9, A to F (A=10 … F=15) |

**Conversions to remember:**

- **Decimal to binary:** divide repeatedly by 2 and read remainders bottom to top. Example: 10 = 1010.

- **Binary to decimal:** multiply each bit by powers of 2. Example: 1101 = 8+4+0+1 = 13.

- **Binary to octal:** group bits in threes from the right. **Binary to hex:** group in fours.

- Quick values: 1010 = 10, 1111 = 15 = F, 11111111 = 255 = FF, 10000 = 16.

- Computers use binary because electronic circuits have two states (on and off).

**Codes:** ASCII is 7-bit (128 characters), extended ASCII is 8-bit (256), Unicode supports all world languages (UTF-8 and UTF-16), and BCD and EBCDIC are other codes.

### 2.6 Units of Data

Bit (0 or 1) → Nibble (4 bits) → **Byte (8 bits)** → KB → MB → GB → TB → PB → EB → ZB → YB. Each step is **1024** times the previous one in the traditional binary convention (1 KB = 1024 bytes). Order from smallest: Bit \< Nibble \< Byte \< KB \< MB \< GB \< TB \< PB. Network speeds are in **bits per second** (Mbps), and storage is in **bytes**. A byte stores one character.

## Chapter 3: Hardware and Software

### 3.1 Hardware

Hardware means the physical, touchable parts: CPU, memory, input and output devices, storage, motherboard, power supply and peripherals. Classes: **input, output, processing (CPU), storage and communication hardware.**

### 3.2 Software

Software is the set of programs and instructions that make hardware work. It is intangible.

**System software** manages the hardware and gives a platform for other programs:

- **Operating system** (Windows, Linux, macOS, Android).

- **Utility programs:** antivirus, disk defragmenter, backup, file compression.

- **Language translators:** **Assembler** (assembly to machine code), **Compiler** (translates the whole program at once, reports all errors, faster execution, e.g. C and C++), **Interpreter** (line by line, stops at first error, e.g. Python and JavaScript).

- **Device drivers** and **firmware** (permanent software on ROM chips, e.g. BIOS).

**Application software** performs user tasks:

- **General purpose:** word processor (MS Word), spreadsheet (Excel), presentation (PowerPoint), browser, database (Access).

- **Specific purpose:** tally accounting, railway reservation, hospital management.

- **Custom (bespoke)** versus **packaged** software.

### 3.3 Software Licensing

- **Proprietary:** paid, source code closed (MS Office).

- **Freeware:** free but not modifiable (Adobe Reader).

- **Shareware:** free trial, then payment.

- **Open source:** source code available, modifiable and shareable (Linux, LibreOffice, Moodle).

- **Free software** emphasises freedom (GNU/GPL), not price.

### 3.4 Hardware versus Software versus Firmware

| Hardware            | Software                     | Firmware                      |
|---------------------|------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Physical            | Programs                     | Software embedded in hardware |
| Cannot be installed | Can be installed and updated | Stored in ROM, rarely changed |

## Chapter 4: Input and Output Devices

### 4.1 Input Devices (send data into the computer)

- **Keyboard** (QWERTY), **mouse**, **trackball**, **joystick**, **light pen**, **touch screen**, **stylus**.

- **Scanner:** converts paper documents or images into digital form.

- **OMR (Optical Mark Reader):** detects marks made in bubbles, used for **checking MCQ answer sheets**, such as NET and entrance exams.

- **OCR (Optical Character Recognition):** converts printed or handwritten text to editable text.

- **MICR (Magnetic Ink Character Reader):** reads magnetic ink characters on **bank cheques**.

- **Barcode reader:** reads product codes in **retail** and libraries. **QR code scanner** is a 2-D variant.

- **Microphone, webcam, digital camera, biometric devices** (fingerprint, iris, face).

**Trap pairs:** OMR is marks and exams, OCR is text, MICR is cheques and banks, barcode is retail.

### 4.2 Output Devices (present results)

- **Monitors:** CRT, LCD, LED, OLED. Quality depends on resolution (pixels) and refresh rate.

- **Printers:** **impact** (dot-matrix, line printer, daisy wheel, with a physical strike, noisy, can make carbon copies) and **non-impact** (inkjet, laser, thermal, quiet, higher quality). Laser printers are fast and give high-quality text. Print speed is in **PPM** (pages per minute), and resolution in **DPI**.

- **Plotter:** large engineering drawings and maps.

- **Speakers, headphones, projector.**

### 4.3 Devices That Do Both

Touch screen, modem, headset with microphone, multifunction printer (print and scan). Some are classed as input-output devices. Hard disks and pen drives are **storage** devices, not typical I/O in exam logic.

### 4.4 Exam Notes

A monitor, printer, speaker and plotter are output. Keyboard, mouse, scanner and light pen are input. Resolution is described in pixels (monitor) and DPI (printer).

## Chapter 5: Memory and Storage

### 5.1 Memory Hierarchy

From fastest, smallest and most expensive to slowest, largest and cheapest: **Registers → Cache → RAM (main memory) → Secondary storage (SSD or HDD) → Tertiary and offline (optical, tape).** The principle is a trade-off of speed, cost and capacity.

### 5.2 Primary Memory

**RAM (Random Access Memory):** volatile (data lost when power is off), read and write, holds currently running programs and data.

- **SRAM (Static RAM):** faster, costlier, used in **cache**, no refreshing.

- **DRAM (Dynamic RAM):** slower, cheaper, needs constant refreshing, used as main memory.

**ROM (Read Only Memory):** non-volatile, holds start-up instructions (BIOS or firmware). Types: **PROM** (programmable once), **EPROM** (erased with UV light), **EEPROM** (erased electrically) and **Flash memory** (block-erase, used in pen drives and SSD).

**Cache memory:** a small, very fast memory between CPU and RAM that holds frequently used data. Levels L1 (inside CPU, fastest), L2 and L3. **Virtual memory:** use of disk space to extend RAM.

### 5.3 Secondary Storage

- **Magnetic:** hard disk (HDD), floppy, magnetic tape (sequential access, used for backup and archives).

- **Optical:** CD (about 700 MB), DVD (about 4.7 GB), Blu-ray (about 25 GB per layer).

- **Solid-state:** **SSD**, pen drive, memory card. No moving parts, faster and more durable than HDD.

- **Cloud storage:** Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox.

**Access methods:** sequential (tape), direct or random (disk, RAM).

### 5.4 Key Differences

| RAM                     | ROM                             |
|-------------------------|---------------------------------|
| Volatile                | Non-volatile                    |
| Read and write          | Mostly read-only                |
| Temporary working space | Permanent start-up instructions |

| Primary memory           | Secondary memory      |
|--------------------------|-----------------------|
| Directly accessed by CPU | Not directly accessed |
| Fast, costly, small      | Slow, cheap, large    |
| Volatile (RAM)           | Non-volatile          |

**Quick traps:** Cache is faster than RAM. Registers are the fastest of all. ROM is non-volatile. An SSD has no moving parts.

## Chapter 6: Operating Systems

### 6.1 Meaning

An **operating system (OS)** is system software that acts as an **interface between the user and the computer hardware** and manages all hardware and software resources. Without an OS the computer cannot run applications. The OS is loaded into RAM at **booting** (start-up), when the BIOS or firmware runs a power-on self-test and loads the OS from disk. The **kernel** is the core of the OS. The **shell** is the layer the user interacts with.

### 6.2 Functions of an OS

- **Process management:** scheduling the CPU, multitasking.

- **Memory management:** allocating and freeing RAM, virtual memory.

- **File management:** creating, naming, organising, protecting files and folders.

- **Device management:** drivers for printers, keyboards and disks.

- **Security and access control:** passwords, user accounts, permissions.

- **User interface:** CLI or GUI.

- **Error detection, resource allocation and job accounting.**

### 6.3 Types of Operating Systems

| Type                            | Key idea                                                         | Example or use                                 |
|---------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| **Batch**                       | Jobs with similar needs grouped and run without user interaction | Early mainframes, payroll                      |
| **Multiprogramming**            | Several programs in memory, CPU switches when one waits for I/O  | Mainframes                                     |
| **Time-sharing (multitasking)** | CPU time sliced among many users or tasks                        | Unix, Windows                                  |
| **Real-time (RTOS)**            | Must respond within a strict deadline                            | Air-traffic control, medical devices, missiles |
| **Distributed**                 | Many computers appear as one system                              | Large clusters                                 |
| **Network OS**                  | Manages servers and shared resources on a network                | Windows Server, Novell NetWare                 |
| **Embedded**                    | Fixed-function device OS                                         | Washing machine, ATM                           |
| **Mobile**                      | Touch-based OS                                                   | Android, iOS                                   |

**Single-user versus multi-user, and single-tasking versus multi-tasking** are other classifications.

### 6.4 Popular Operating Systems

- **Windows (Microsoft):** proprietary, GUI-based.

- **Linux:** open-source, kernel by **Linus Torvalds (1991)**. Distributions include Ubuntu, Fedora and Red Hat. Based on Unix ideas.

- **Unix:** multi-user, multi-tasking, developed at Bell Labs in the 1970s.

- **macOS and iOS (Apple)**, **Android (Google, Linux-based, open source)**.

- **BOSS (Bharat Operating System Solutions):** Indian Linux-based OS developed by **C-DAC**. **Maya OS** is a Linux-based OS developed for the Indian defence sector.

### 6.5 User Interfaces

**CLI (Command Line Interface):** text commands (DOS, Linux terminal). Fast and powerful, but harder to learn. **GUI (Graphical User Interface):** windows, icons, menus and pointer (WIMP). Easy and intuitive. Examples are Windows and macOS. Xerox PARC pioneered the GUI.

### 6.6 Useful Basics (frequently asked)

- **File extension** identifies type: .docx (Word), .xlsx (Excel), .pptx (PowerPoint), .pdf, .txt, .jpg, .mp3, .mp4, .exe.

- **Shortcuts:** Ctrl+C copy, Ctrl+X cut, Ctrl+V paste, Ctrl+Z undo, Ctrl+Y redo, Ctrl+S save, Ctrl+P print, Ctrl+A select all, Ctrl+F find, Alt+Tab switch windows, Ctrl+Alt+Del task manager options.

- **Folder (directory)** versus **file**. **Path** shows the location of a file.

- **MS Office suite:** Word (documents), Excel (spreadsheets, cell address, formulas such as SUM and AVERAGE), PowerPoint (slides), Access (database), Outlook (e-mail).

**Traps:** Linux is open source and Windows is not. Android is not a Microsoft product. The OS is system software, whereas MS Word is application software.

## Chapter 7: Internet and World Wide Web

### 7.1 History of the Internet

- **ARPANET (1969):** funded by the US Department of Defense (ARPA). It connected four nodes and used packet switching. It is the **ancestor of the internet**.

- **TCP/IP** was formally adopted on **1 January 1983**, creating the internet as a network of networks.

- **1989 to 1991:** **Tim Berners-Lee** at **CERN** proposed the World Wide Web, then built the first web server, browser and website (1991). He created **HTML, HTTP and URL**.

- **Mosaic (1993)** was the first widely used graphical browser. Netscape followed.

- **India:** ERNET began for academic use, and **VSNL launched public internet access on 15 August 1995**.

### 7.2 Internet versus World Wide Web

| Internet                                    | World Wide Web                                                                  |
|---------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Global network of networks (infrastructure) | Collection of web pages and resources accessed through the internet (a service) |
| Uses TCP/IP                                 | Uses HTTP/HTTPS                                                                 |
| Older (1969/1983)                           | Newer (1989/1991)                                                               |
| Includes e-mail, FTP, VoIP and more         | Only linked hypertext and multimedia pages                                      |

The web is **one service on the internet**. This is among the most repeated concept questions.

### 7.3 Types of Internet Connections

- **Dial-up:** uses telephone line and modem, slow.

- **Broadband:** always-on high-speed (DSL, cable, fibre).

- **DSL/ADSL:** over telephone line.

- **Fibre optic (FTTH):** fastest wired option, light pulses.

- **Leased line:** dedicated, for organisations.

- **Satellite and VSAT:** remote areas.

- **Wi-Fi and mobile data:** 3G, 4G (LTE) and 5G.

- **WiMAX:** wireless broadband over a wider area.

### 7.4 Protocols and Ports

A **protocol** is a set of rules for communication.

| Protocol   | Full form                                         | Use                                                                             | Port |
|------------|---------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------|
| **TCP/IP** | Transmission Control Protocol / Internet Protocol | Core suite for the internet, TCP splits data into packets and IP addresses them | \-   |
| **HTTP**   | HyperText Transfer Protocol                       | Web page transfer                                                               | 80   |
| **HTTPS**  | HTTP Secure (SSL/TLS)                             | Secure web                                                                      | 443  |
| **FTP**    | File Transfer Protocol                            | Upload and download files                                                       | 21   |
| **SMTP**   | Simple Mail Transfer Protocol                     | Sending e-mail                                                                  | 25   |
| **POP3**   | Post Office Protocol                              | Downloading e-mail                                                              | 110  |
| **IMAP**   | Internet Message Access Protocol                  | Syncing e-mail on server                                                        | 143  |
| **Telnet** | Terminal Network                                  | Remote login (unencrypted, replaced by SSH)                                     | 23   |
| **DNS**    | Domain Name System                                | Domain name to IP address                                                       | 53   |
| **DHCP**   | Dynamic Host Configuration Protocol               | Assigns IP addresses automatically                                              | \-   |

**IP address:** IPv4 is 32-bit (for example 192.168.1.1) and IPv6 is 128-bit. A **URL** (Uniform Resource Locator) has scheme, domain and path: https://www.ugc.gov.in/page. **Domain suffixes:** .com (commercial), .org (organisation), .edu (education), .gov (government), .net (network), .in (India), .ac.in (academic, India), .gov.in (Indian government).

### 7.5 Browsers and Search Engines

**Web browser:** software to view web pages. Examples: Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, Microsoft Edge, Safari, Opera. Features include tabs, bookmarks, history, cache, cookies, incognito (private) mode.

**Search engine:** a program that searches web content. Examples: Google, Bing, Yahoo, DuckDuckGo, Baidu. Remember: a browser is not a search engine. Tips: use quotation marks for exact phrase, AND, OR and NOT as Boolean operators, site: and filetype: operators. **Google Scholar** and **Semantic Scholar** are academic search engines.

### 7.6 Web 1.0, 2.0 and 3.0

| Version     | Nature                                               | Features                                                                         |
|-------------|------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Web 1.0** | **Read-only** static web                             | Static pages, few creators, many readers                                         |
| **Web 2.0** | **Read-write**, social web                           | User-generated content, blogs, wikis, social media, YouTube                      |
| **Web 3.0** | **Semantic and intelligent** web, read-write-execute | AI, machine learning, blockchain-style decentralisation, personalised experience |

### 7.7 Social Media and Educational Uses

Social media includes Facebook, X (Twitter), Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube, WhatsApp, Telegram, Reddit and blogs. **Educational uses:** class groups, content sharing, collaboration, video lectures, discussion, professional networking (LinkedIn, ResearchGate and Academia.edu for researchers). **Risks:** distraction, misinformation, privacy violations, cyberbullying, addiction. Other web tools: **blog** (online journal), **wiki** (collaboratively edited, Wikipedia), **podcast** (audio series), **RSS** (feeds), **MOOC**.

### 7.8 Intranet and Extranet

- **Intranet:** private network within an organisation using internet technology, accessible only to its members (staff portal).

- **Extranet:** an intranet extended to **selected outside users** such as suppliers, partners or students.

- **Internet:** public.

Order of openness: Intranet (most restricted) \< Extranet \< Internet.

## Chapter 8: Computer Networks

### 8.1 Meaning and Purpose

A computer network is two or more connected devices that share data and resources. Benefits: resource sharing (printers, files), communication, centralised data, cost savings and reliability.

### 8.2 Types by Geography

| Type    | Area               | Example                               |
|---------|--------------------|---------------------------------------|
| **PAN** | A few metres       | Bluetooth between phone and earphones |
| **LAN** | Building or campus | College computer lab                  |
| **CAN** | Campus             | University network                    |
| **MAN** | City               | Cable TV network, city Wi-Fi          |
| **WAN** | Country or world   | **The Internet**                      |

By size: PAN \< LAN \< CAN \< MAN \< WAN. **VPN** (Virtual Private Network) builds a secure private tunnel over a public network.

### 8.3 Network Topologies

- **Bus:** one common cable, cheap, a break stops the network, collisions.

- **Star:** all devices connect to a central hub or switch. **Most common in LANs**, easy to add devices, central failure stops all.

- **Ring:** circular, data moves in one direction, token passing.

- **Mesh:** every node connected to every other, very reliable, costly. Number of links in full mesh = n(n-1)/2.

- **Tree:** hierarchical, combination of star and bus.

- **Hybrid:** combination of topologies.

### 8.4 Transmission Modes

- **Simplex:** one direction only (keyboard to computer, TV broadcast).

- **Half-duplex:** both directions, but one at a time (walkie-talkie).

- **Full-duplex:** both directions simultaneously (telephone, mobile call).

### 8.5 Transmission Media

- **Guided (wired):** twisted pair (UTP and STP, used in LAN cables), coaxial cable (cable TV), **optical fibre** (light, highest speed, no electromagnetic interference, most secure).

- **Unguided (wireless):** radio waves, microwave, infrared, satellite, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi.

**Bandwidth** is the data-carrying capacity, in bits per second. **Latency** is delay.

### 8.6 Models and Architecture

**OSI model (7 layers, top to bottom):** Application → Presentation → Session → Transport → Network → Data Link → Physical. Mnemonic from the bottom: *Please Do Not Throw Sausage Pizza Away*. **TCP/IP model (4 layers):** Application, Transport, Internet, Network Access.

**Client-server:** central server provides services to clients (web, e-mail). **Peer-to-peer (P2P):** all nodes are equal and share resources directly (torrent).

**Switching:** circuit switching (dedicated path, telephone) versus packet switching (data split into packets, internet).

## Chapter 9: Networking Devices

### 9.1 Devices, Functions and Key Points

| Device                           | Function                                                                                     | Key point                             |
|----------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------|
| **NIC (Network Interface Card)** | Connects a computer to a network                                                             | Has a unique **MAC address** (48-bit) |
| **Hub**                          | Connects devices in a LAN, **broadcasts** data to all ports                                  | Unintelligent, shared bandwidth       |
| **Switch**                       | Sends data only to the intended device using **MAC addresses**                               | Intelligent, replaces hub             |
| **Bridge**                       | Connects two LAN segments                                                                    | Filters traffic                       |
| **Router**                       | Connects different networks and forwards packets using **IP addresses**, finds the best path | Connects LAN to internet              |
| **Gateway**                      | Connects networks using different protocols, a translator                                    | Entry and exit point                  |
| **Modem**                        | **Modulator-Demodulator**, converts digital to analog and back                               | Used for telephone and cable lines    |
| **Repeater**                     | Regenerates and amplifies weak signals                                                       | Extends distance                      |
| **Access Point (AP)**            | Provides Wi-Fi to wired network                                                              | Wireless connectivity                 |
| **Firewall**                     | Filters incoming and outgoing traffic by rules                                               | Hardware or software security         |

### 9.2 Layer Mapping, Addresses and Look-Alike Pairs

**Layer mapping:** Hub and repeater work at the physical layer, switch and bridge at the data link layer, router at the network layer. **Identification:** MAC address (hardware, physical, fixed) versus IP address (logical, changes with network). **Modem versus router:** modem connects to the ISP, router distributes the connection to devices.

## Chapter 10: Communication Technologies

### 10.1 Wireless and Short-Range Technologies

- **Bluetooth:** short range (about 10 metres), low power, creates PAN, IEEE 802.15.

- **Wi-Fi:** wireless LAN, IEEE 802.11 standard.

- **NFC (Near Field Communication):** a few centimetres, used in contactless payment and tap-to-pay.

- **Infrared:** line-of-sight, used in remotes.

- **RFID:** radio tags for tracking, used in libraries and toll collection (FASTag).

- **GPS:** satellite-based location. India's regional system is **NavIC**.

### 10.2 Mobile Communication Generations

| Gen          | Main feature                                                                                         |
|--------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **1G**       | Analog voice                                                                                         |
| **2G**       | Digital voice, SMS (GSM, CDMA)                                                                       |
| **3G**       | Mobile internet, video calling                                                                       |
| **4G (LTE)** | High-speed broadband, HD streaming                                                                   |
| **5G**       | Very high speed, ultra-low latency, IoT and smart cities. India launched 5G services in October 2022 |

**GSM** (Global System for Mobile) uses SIM cards. **CDMA** (Code Division Multiple Access).

### 10.3 Satellite Communication

Satellites relay signals using **uplink** and **downlink**. INSAT and GSAT are Indian communication satellites. **EDUSAT (2004)** was India's first dedicated educational satellite. Types: geostationary (about 36,000 km altitude, appears fixed) and low-earth orbit. Used in DTH television, VSAT, weather, navigation and distance education.

### 10.4 E-mail

**Invention:** Ray Tomlinson sent the first network e-mail in 1971 and introduced the **@** symbol.

**Address structure:** username@domain.com. The username is the user's mailbox, and the domain identifies the mail server.

**Parts of a message:** **To** (main recipient), **Cc** (carbon copy, visible to all), **Bcc** (blind carbon copy, hidden from others), **Subject**, **Body**, **Attachment** and **Signature**.

**Folders:** **Inbox** (received mail), **Outbox** (mail waiting to be sent), **Sent**, **Drafts** (unsent saved mail), **Spam or Junk** (unsolicited mail) and **Trash**.

**Protocols:** **SMTP** sends mail (outgoing). **POP3** downloads mail to one device, usually removing it from the server. **IMAP** keeps mail on the server and syncs across devices.

**Advantages:** fast, cheap, global, attachments, searchable record, asynchronous. **Limitations:** spam, phishing, viruses, overload, no tone or emotion, needs internet.

**E-mail netiquette:** clear subject, polite and concise, do not type in all capitals (it is shouting), proofread, use Reply All carefully, do not forward chain mails, respect privacy, use Bcc for large groups.

### 10.5 Audio and Video Conferencing

**Audio-conferencing:** voice-only communication among multiple locations. **Video-conferencing:** audio and video in real time, which allows face-to-face interaction at a distance. **Types:** point-to-point (two sites) and multipoint (many sites). **Requirements:** device (computer or phone), camera, microphone, speaker, **stable internet bandwidth**, and conferencing software.

**Platforms:** **Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Cisco Webex.** **Educational uses:** virtual classes, guest lectures, thesis viva, remote meetings, webinars and workshops. **Advantages:** saves travel cost and time, flexible, recordable, reaches remote learners. **Limitations:** needs bandwidth and power, technical glitches, privacy and security concerns, less personal interaction, "Zoom fatigue". **Synchronous** means at the same time (live class). **Asynchronous** means at different times (recorded lecture).

### 10.6 Other Technologies

**VoIP** (Voice over IP) sends voice using the internet (WhatsApp calls, Skype). **IoT (Internet of Things)** connects everyday objects to the internet. **Cloud computing** gives on-demand computing resources over the internet (SaaS, PaaS, IaaS, for example Google Docs, AWS, Azure). **AI and ML** let machines simulate intelligence, with generative AI producing text, images and code. **Big data** is characterised by volume, velocity, variety (the 3 Vs). **Blockchain** is a decentralised, tamper-resistant ledger. **AR and VR** give augmented and immersive experiences.

## Chapter 11: Digital Learning

### 11.1 Meaning and Scope

**Digital learning** is any learning that uses digital technology, content or tools to improve the learning experience. It is wider than e-learning, and covers e-learning, blended learning, mobile learning, flipped classrooms, gamification, adaptive and personalised learning, microlearning, simulations, AR and VR, and AI-based tutoring. It also develops **digital literacy** (skills to find, evaluate, create and communicate information digitally) and **digital citizenship** (safe, ethical and responsible use).

### 11.2 Features

- Learner-centred and self-paced.

- Anytime, anywhere access.

- Multimedia, interactive and immersive content.

- Instant feedback and analytics to track progress.

- Collaboration through discussion forums and shared documents.

- Scalable to large numbers at low marginal cost.

### 11.3 Key Theories and Models (analysis-type questions)

- **Connectivism (George Siemens):** learning happens through networks and connections, and it underlies cMOOCs. Called the learning theory of the digital age.

- **SAMR model (Ruben Puentedura):** four levels of technology integration. **S**ubstitution (tech replaces a tool with no change), **A**ugmentation (tech adds functional improvement), **M**odification (task is significantly redesigned) and **R**edefinition (tasks that were previously impossible). The first two are *enhancement*, the last two *transformation*.

- **TPACK (Mishra and Koehler):** overlap of **T**echnological, **P**edagogical and **C**ontent **K**nowledge. An effective teacher integrates all three.

- **Bloom's Digital Taxonomy (Andrew Churches):** adapts Bloom's levels to digital tasks.

- **Constructivism:** learners construct knowledge, supported by collaborative digital tools.

### 11.4 Policy Link: NEP 2020

NEP 2020 gives strong support to technology in education: the **National Educational Technology Forum (NETF)** for exchange of ideas on technology use, expansion of digital infrastructure, online and digital education, digital repositories, virtual labs, content in regional languages, teacher training for technology, and the **Academic Bank of Credits** for flexible, multidisciplinary learning. When asked about NEP and ICT, remember NETF, ABC, blended learning and the emphasis on equity and access.

### 11.5 Challenges

Digital divide, poor connectivity and power, limited teacher training, cost of devices, language barriers, privacy and security risks, screen-time and health concerns, lack of motivation or interaction and assessment integrity.

## Chapter 12: E-Learning

### 12.1 Meaning

**E-learning** is learning delivered through electronic media, mostly the internet, in which content, instruction and interaction are provided online. It is a form of **technology-enhanced learning**.

### 12.2 Modes

- **Synchronous:** live and in real time (virtual class on Zoom, live chat).

- **Asynchronous:** at the learner's own time (recorded lectures, forums, e-mail, self-paced modules).

### 12.3 Models and Concepts (very frequently asked)

|----------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| **Blended learning**             | Combination of face-to-face and online learning                                                                        | Best of both worlds, NEP-supported      |
| **Flipped classroom**            | Students study content (video, reading) at home, and class time is used for discussion, problem solving and activities | Reverses the traditional sequence       |
| **M-learning (mobile learning)** | Learning through smartphones and tablets                                                                               | Anytime, anywhere, bite-sized           |
| **MOOC**                         | Massive Open Online Course, open enrolment, large scale                                                                | Free to learn, usually paid certificate |
| **Virtual classroom**            | Online space with live teaching and interaction                                                                        | Zoom, Teams, Meet                       |
| **OER**                          | Open Educational Resources, free materials that can be reused, revised and shared                                      | Creative Commons licences               |
| **Microlearning**                | Small learning units                                                                                                   | Short videos                            |
| **Gamification**                 | Game elements in learning                                                                                              | Points, badges                          |

**cMOOC versus xMOOC:**

| cMOOC                                  | xMOOC                                      |
|----------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| Connectivist, network-based            | Content-based, instructor-led              |
| Learners create and share knowledge    | Lectures, quizzes and assessments          |

### 12.4 Learning Management Systems (LMS)

An **LMS** is software to deliver, track and manage courses, assessments and learner records. Examples: **Moodle (open source)**, Blackboard, Canvas, **Google Classroom**, Microsoft Teams for Education, Edmodo. **SCORM** (Sharable Content Object Reference Model) is a standard that makes e-learning content work across LMS.

### 12.5 Advantages and Limitations

**Advantages:** flexibility, wider reach, cost-effectiveness, personalised pace, updated and multimedia content, record of progress, eco-friendly. **Limitations:** needs devices and internet, requires self-discipline, low social interaction, dropout rates, assessment and plagiarism concerns, not ideal for practical skills without simulation.

### 12.6 Open Access and Open-Source Resources

- **Open access:** free, immediate online access to scholarly literature. **Gold** (published open in the journal) and **Green** (self-archiving in a repository). **DOAJ** is the Directory of Open Access Journals. The **Budapest Open Access Initiative (2002)** is a landmark.

- **Open-source software:** source code open to all (Linux, Moodle, LibreOffice, Python).

- **Creative Commons (CC):** licences such as CC BY (attribution), NC (non-commercial), ND (no derivatives) and SA (share-alike). **OER** concept was adopted at UNESCO in 2002.

### 13.1 Roles of ICT

- **Teaching:** smart boards, PowerPoint, simulations, educational videos, virtual labs.

A teacher should have **technological competence, digital pedagogy skills, ethical awareness (copyright, privacy), online communication skills** and the ability to evaluate digital resources. ### 13.4 Challenges and Concerns

Infrastructure, connectivity, cost, digital divide, language, teacher training, resistance to change, over-dependence, distraction, screen-time, cyberbullying, plagiarism and AI misuse. ## Chapter 15: Cybersecurity

### 15.1 Cyber Ethics and Netiquette

**Cyber ethics** are the moral principles for using computers and the internet responsibly: respect privacy, do not hack, do not pirate software, do not plagiarise, do not spread false information, give credit and be honest. **Netiquette** (network etiquette) is the set of rules for polite online behaviour: be respectful, avoid ALL CAPS, do not spam, verify before sharing, respect copyright, protect others' privacy and think before posting.

### 15.2 Cybercrime

**Cybercrime** is a crime in which a computer or network is the tool, the target or the place of the crime. Common forms:

- **Hacking:** unauthorised access to systems. **Ethical (white hat)** hackers test security with permission, **black hat** hackers attack, **grey hat** lies between.

- **Phishing:** fake e-mails or sites to steal credentials. **Vishing** is by voice call, **smishing** by SMS, **spear phishing** targets a specific person, and **pharming** redirects users to fake sites.

- **Identity theft, cyberstalking, cyberbullying, online fraud, sextortion.**

- **Spoofing:** disguising as a trusted source. **Spam:** unsolicited bulk messages.

- **Denial of Service (DoS) and Distributed DoS (DDoS):** flooding a service to make it unavailable.

- **Man-in-the-Middle (MITM):** intercepting communication. **SQL injection:** attacking databases through input. **Zero-day:** exploit of an unknown vulnerability.

- **Social engineering:** manipulating people rather than machines.

### 15.3 Malware

**Malware** is malicious software.

| Type             | Behaviour                                                              |
|------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Virus**        | Attaches to files and spreads when the host file is run                |
| **Worm**         | Self-replicates across networks **without a host file or user action** |
| **Trojan horse** | Disguised as useful software, does not self-replicate                  |
| **Ransomware**   | Encrypts data and demands payment (WannaCry, 2017)                     |
| **Spyware**      | Secretly collects information                                          |
| **Adware**       | Shows unwanted adverts                                                 |
| **Keylogger**    | Records keystrokes                                                     |
| **Rootkit**      | Hides deep in the OS                                                   |
| **Botnet**       | Network of infected computers controlled remotely                      |
| **Logic bomb**   | Triggered by a condition or date                                       |

### 15.4 Cyber Security: Principles and Measures

**CIA triad:** **Confidentiality** (only authorised access), **Integrity** (data is not altered), **Availability** (data is accessible when needed). Authentication means verifying identity. Authorisation means granting permissions.

**Measures:** strong passwords and a password manager, **two-factor or multi-factor authentication (2FA/MFA)**, **OTP**, **antivirus and anti-malware**, **firewall**, **encryption** (converts data to unreadable cipher text, **symmetric** uses one key and **asymmetric** uses a public-private key pair), **VPN**, regular updates and patches, backups, **HTTPS** sites, careful use of public Wi-Fi, avoiding unknown links and attachments, and digital literacy training. **Digital signature** uses asymmetric cryptography to give authenticity, integrity and non-repudiation. **Digital certificates** are issued by a **Certifying Authority (CA)**. **Biometrics** are fingerprint, iris and face. **CAPTCHA** distinguishes humans from bots.

### 15.5 Indian Cyber Law

- **Information Technology Act, 2000:** India's primary cyber law, in force from **17 October 2000**, based on the UNCITRAL Model Law on E-Commerce. It gives **legal recognition to electronic records, e-contracts and digital signatures**, and defines cybercrimes and penalties. **Amended in 2008** (added provisions on cyber terrorism, identity theft and data protection).

- **Important sections:** **43** (damage to computer or data without permission, compensation), **66** (computer-related offences, hacking), **66C** (identity theft), **66D** (cheating by personation), **66E** (privacy violation), **66F** (cyber terrorism), **67** (publishing obscene material), **67B** (child sexual abuse material), **69** (government interception and monitoring powers), **70** (protected systems) and **72** (breach of confidentiality). **Section 66A** (offensive messages) was **struck down by the Supreme Court in *Shreya Singhal v Union of India* (2015)**.

- **CERT-In** (Indian Computer Emergency Response Team, 2004, under MeitY) is the national agency for incident response. **I4C** (Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre) and the **National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in)** and **helpline 1930** support reporting.

- **Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023:** India's data-protection law covering consent, rights of data principals and duties of data fiduciaries. Rules under it are being implemented in phases. Check the latest status before the exam.

- **National Cyber Security Policy, 2013.**

### 15.6 Copyright, IPR and Plagiarism

- **Intellectual Property Rights (IPR):** copyright, patent, trademark, design, geographical indication.

- **Copyright Act, 1957** (amended 2012) protects original literary, artistic, musical and software works. In India, copyright lasts for the author's life plus 60 years. **Fair dealing** allows limited use for criticism, review, research and education.

- **Plagiarism:** presenting others' words or ideas as one's own. Forms: copy-paste, paraphrasing without credit, self-plagiarism, mosaic plagiarism. Tools: Turnitin, Ouriginal, iThenticate, and Shodhshuddhi in India.

- **UGC (Promotion of Academic Integrity and Prevention of Plagiarism in HEIs) Regulations, 2018:** similarity up to **10%** is excluded from penalties (Level 0), **10–40%** (Level 1), **40–60%** (Level 2) and **above 60%** (Level 3), with increasing penalties.

- **Creative Commons** and **open licences** allow lawful reuse. **Software piracy** is illegal copying.

- **Digital footprint:** trail of data left online. **Cookies:** small files stored by websites.

## Chapter 17: Exam Pattern, Recent Trends and Question Formats

### 17.1 Paper 1 Structure and Question Styles

**Paper 1 structure:** 50 MCQs, 100 marks, 2 marks each, 60 minutes, no negative marking. ICT is Unit 8 of 10, and it normally contributes about 5 questions (around 10 marks). Attempt it in full, because it is among the most scoring units.

**Question styles seen in recent shifts (2023 to 2025):**

- **Direct factual (about 40%):** full forms, who/which body runs which portal, year of launch.

- **Match the following (about 20%):** abbreviations with expansions, portals with purposes, protocols with functions.

- **Statement I / Statement II (about 15%):** two statements, choose both true, both false, or one true.

- **Arrange in order (about 10%):** generations, DIKW, memory hierarchy, units of data, e-mail steps.

- **Application or analysis (about 15%):** which tool or technology suits a scenario.

### 17.2 Trends to Prepare For

- The **digital initiatives of Indian higher education** (SWAYAM, NDLI, Shodhganga, ABC, APAAR, NAD, DigiLocker, PM e-Vidya, DIKSHA, NIRF, AISHE) are asked almost every year.

- **Cyber safety, cyber law and IT Act 2000** are asked every year.

- **NEP 2020 links** to ICT, such as ABC, multidisciplinary learning and technology-enabled education, are increasingly common.

- **Newer technologies** such as AI, IoT, cloud computing, blockchain, big data, 5G and generative AI appear as definitions or applications.

- **Abbreviations** are a guaranteed source of marks.

- Recent papers use **statement-based and assertion-style** questions, so knowing *why* something is true matters, not only what it is.

### 17.3 Scoring Strategy

**Scoring strategy:** Master abbreviations, digital initiatives (who, what, when) and cyber law first. These give the highest return per hour of study. Then revise the computer fundamentals, which are stable and repeat in pattern.

### 17.4 Match the Following

Start with the item you are **100% sure of**, strike out those options, and the rest fall into place. High-frequency pairs: OMR with answer sheets, MICR with cheques, SMTP with sending mail, POP3 and IMAP with receiving, Shodhganga with theses, NDLI with IIT Kharagpur, NAD with certificates, ABC with credits, SWAYAM Prabha with DTH, GeM with procurement, UPI with NPCI, CERT-In with incident response.

### 17.5 Statement I and Statement II

Check each statement separately and then test the link. Common false statements to watch for: "The internet and WWW are the same", "RAM is non-volatile", "A worm needs a host file", "SWAYAM Prabha is a MOOC platform", "IMAP downloads and deletes mail from the server", "Wi-Fi is wired", "Web 1.0 is read-write" and "A hub sends data only to the intended device". Watch for absolute words such as *always*, *only* and *never*, which often signal a false statement.

### 17.6 Arrange in Order

- **DIKW:** Data → Information → Knowledge → Wisdom.

- **Generations:** Vacuum tube → Transistor → IC → Microprocessor → AI.

- **Memory (fast to slow):** Register → Cache → RAM → SSD/HDD → Optical/Tape.

- **Data units:** Bit → Byte → KB → MB → GB → TB → PB.

- **Network size:** PAN → LAN → MAN → WAN.

- **Mobile generations:** 1G → 2G → 3G → 4G → 5G.

- **Web versions:** 1.0 → 2.0 → 3.0.

- **Timeline:** ARPANET (1969) → TCP/IP (1983) → WWW (1989–1991) → VSNL public internet in India (1995) → IT Act (2000) → SWAYAM (2017) → ABC (2021) → 5G in India (2022).
