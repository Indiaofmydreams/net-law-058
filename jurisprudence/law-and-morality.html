About this question set

This set has 18 multiple-choice questions on Law and Morality from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 1, Jurisprudence. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 18 of the 18 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Jurisprudence notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

18 practice questions with answers

1'The existence of law is one thing; its merit or demerit is another.' This statement is attributed to:

  1. Kelsen
  2. Hart
  3. Fuller
  4. Austin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Austin

This is Austin's classic statement of the separation between law and morality in positivism.

Report this question

2'Lex iniusta non est lex' (An unjust law is not a law) is associated with:

  1. Austin
  2. St. Thomas Aquinas
  3. Hart
  4. Kelsen
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. St. Thomas Aquinas

St. Thomas Aquinas held that an unjust law lacks the character of law — a core Natural Law principle.

Report this question

3The Hart-Fuller debate of 1958 was published in:

  1. Harvard Law Review
  2. Oxford Law Review
  3. Yale Law Journal
  4. Cambridge Law Journal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Harvard Law Review

The famous Hart-Fuller debate on law and morality was published in the Harvard Law Review, 1958.

Report this question

4The Wolfenden Report (1957) recommended:

  1. Criminalising homosexuality
  2. Enforcing moral standards through criminal law
  3. Decriminalising homosexual acts between consenting adults in private
  4. Codifying English customary law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Decriminalising homosexual acts between consenting adults in private

The Wolfenden Report (1957) recommended decriminalisation of consensual adult homosexual acts, triggering the Hart-Devlin debate.

Report this question

5Lord Devlin's argument in the Hart-Devlin debate was:

  1. Law should never enforce morality
  2. Shared morality is the cement of society and law may enforce it
  3. Morality is irrelevant to law
  4. Only harm-causing acts should be criminalised
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Shared morality is the cement of society and law may enforce it

Devlin argued that a shared morality holds society together and its enforcement by law is justified.

Report this question

6Mill's 'Harm Principle' states:

  1. Law should enforce all moral standards
  2. The only legitimate basis for restricting liberty is to prevent harm to others
  3. Law and morality are identical
  4. Sovereign can restrict liberty for any reason
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The only legitimate basis for restricting liberty is to prevent harm to others

Mill's harm principle from 'On Liberty' (1859): only harm to others can justify restricting individual freedom.

Report this question

7Radbruch's Formula was developed in response to:

  1. The Nuremberg Trials
  2. The French Revolution
  3. Atrocities of the Nazi regime
  4. Both the Nuremberg Trials and Nazi atrocities
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Both the Nuremberg Trials and Nazi atrocities

Radbruch's formula argued that extremely unjust laws lose their legal character — a post-WWII natural law revival.

Report this question

8Which of the following best represents the 'separability thesis' in legal theory?

  1. Law and morality are conceptually separate
  2. Law and morality are necessarily connected
  3. Law derives its validity from morality
  4. Natural law is superior to positive law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Law and morality are conceptually separate

The separability thesis (Hart) holds that there is no necessary connection between law as it is and law as it ought to be.

Report this question

9The 'Critical Legal Studies' (CLS) movement argues that:

  1. Law is neutral and objective
  2. Law reflects universal moral values
  3. Law is politics and serves dominant class interests
  4. Law must be based on precedent alone
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Law is politics and serves dominant class interests

CLS holds that law is indeterminate and serves dominant power structures, not neutral principles.

Report this question

10The 'minimum content of natural law' concept was given by:

  1. Fuller
  2. Kelsen
  3. H.L.A. Hart
  4. Dworkin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. H.L.A. Hart

Hart, despite being a positivist, acknowledged a minimum moral content (protecting persons, property, promises) necessary for any legal system.

Report this question

11Who argued that there is a 'necessary connection' between law and morality?

  1. Austin
  2. Kelsen
  3. American Realists
  4. Natural Law theorists
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Natural Law theorists

Natural Law theorists hold that law must conform to morality to be valid law.

Report this question

12The 'open texture' of law is a concept developed by:

  1. Dworkin
  2. Austin
  3. Fuller
  4. H.L.A. Hart
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. H.L.A. Hart

Hart: language is inherently open-textured — rules have a clear core but a penumbra of uncertain cases requiring judicial discretion.

Report this question

13Hart's 'penumbra of doubt' refers to:

  1. Cases at the borderline where the rule's application is uncertain
  2. The clear centre of meaning of a legal rule
  3. Moral principles underlying law
  4. Fundamental rights in the Constitution
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Cases at the borderline where the rule's application is uncertain

Hart: every rule has a core of settled meaning and a penumbra of doubtful cases where interpretation is needed.

Report this question

14Fuller's eight principles of 'inner morality of law' are collectively called:

  1. Procedural natural law / principles of legality
  2. Natural law principles
  3. Constitutional morality
  4. Positive morality
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Procedural natural law / principles of legality

Fuller called his eight requirements procedural natural law or principles of legality.

Report this question

15Which of the following is a Fuller requirement for law to be valid?

  1. Laws must be kept secret
  2. Laws must be consistent with each other
  3. Laws must be complex
  4. Laws must be known only to judges
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Laws must be consistent with each other

Fuller's eight principles include: generality, promulgation, non-retroactivity, clarity, non-contradiction, possibility of compliance, constancy, congruence.

Report this question

16'Constitutional morality' as a concept recently used by the Indian Supreme Court refers to:

  1. Religious morality enshrined in law
  2. The morality inherent in constitutional values and principles
  3. Public morality of the majority
  4. Morality as defined by Parliament
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The morality inherent in constitutional values and principles

Constitutional morality was used in Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India (2018) to strike down Section 377 IPC.

Report this question

17Mill's work in which the 'harm principle' is elaborated is:

  1. Utilitarianism
  2. A System of Logic
  3. Principles of Political Economy
  4. On Liberty (1859)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. On Liberty (1859)

Mill elaborated the harm principle in 'On Liberty' (1859).

Report this question

18Devlin argued that sexual immorality between consenting adults should be criminalised because:

  1. It harms third parties
  2. It violates divine law
  3. Shared morality is the bond holding society together and its dissolution threatens society
  4. The sovereign commands it
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Shared morality is the bond holding society together and its dissolution threatens society

Devlin: morality is the cement of society; its breakdown threatens society's existence; therefore law may enforce it.
