About this question set

This set has 17 multiple-choice questions on Schools of Jurisprudence from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 1, Jurisprudence. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 17 of the 17 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Jurisprudence notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

17 practice questions with answers

1Hart's secondary rules include all of the following EXCEPT:

  1. Rule of Sanction
  2. Rule of Recognition
  3. Rule of Change
  4. Rule of Adjudication
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Rule of Sanction

Hart's three secondary rules: Recognition, Change, Adjudication. 'Rule of Sanction' is not one of them — that belongs to Austin.

2Dworkin's 'Hercules' is a metaphor for:

  1. An ideal sovereign
  2. The strongest legal argument
  3. A hypothetical ideal judge who finds the best answer for every case
  4. The constitution of a democratic state
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A hypothetical ideal judge who finds the best answer for every case

Hercules is Dworkin's ideal judge — omniscient, with infinite time — who can find the uniquely correct answer to every case.

Report this question

3Dworkin's 'Law as Integrity' means:

  1. Law must always be codified
  2. Judges must follow precedent rigidly
  3. Law must be interpreted as a coherent whole expressing consistent principles of justice
  4. Law is identical to morality
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Law must be interpreted as a coherent whole expressing consistent principles of justice

Law as Integrity (Dworkin): judges must interpret law so that it expresses a coherent set of principles of justice and fairness.

Report this question

4Which school of jurisprudence is most closely associated with the term 'jus gentium'?

  1. Historical School
  2. Analytical School
  3. Natural Law School (Roman Law period)
  4. Realist School
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Natural Law School (Roman Law period)

Jus gentium = law of nations/peoples; developed by Roman jurists as universal natural law applicable to all humanity.

Report this question

5'Sociological jurisprudence' in America is most associated with:

  1. Roscoe Pound
  2. Savigny
  3. Eugen Ehrlich
  4. Oliver Wendell Holmes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Roscoe Pound

Roscoe Pound is the dominant figure of American sociological jurisprudence.

Report this question

6Assertion (A): Kelsen's Pure Theory excludes sociology, politics, and morality from the study of law. Reason (R): Kelsen wanted to establish law as an autonomous normative science.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

Kelsen excluded non-legal elements to establish jurisprudence as a pure normative science.

Report this question

7Assertion (A): Savigny opposed codification of German law. Reason (R): Savigny believed law is a product of the Volksgeist and cannot be artificially made.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

Savigny opposed Thibaut's codification proposal — law grows organically from the spirit of the people.

Report this question

8Match the following works with their authors: 'The Province of Jurisprudence Determined' — 'The Concept of Law' — 'The Morality of Law' — 'Taking Rights Seriously'

  1. Hart, Austin, Dworkin, Fuller
  2. Austin, Fuller, Hart, Dworkin
  3. Dworkin, Hart, Austin, Fuller
  4. Austin, Hart, Fuller, Dworkin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Austin, Hart, Fuller, Dworkin

Austin (1832), Hart (1961), Fuller (1964), Dworkin (1977) — standard works of each school.

Report this question

9Match: Social Engineering — Volksgeist — Grundnorm — Rule of Recognition

  1. Savigny, Pound, Hart, Kelsen
  2. Kelsen, Hart, Savigny, Pound
  3. Pound, Savigny, Kelsen, Hart
  4. Hart, Kelsen, Pound, Savigny
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Pound, Savigny, Kelsen, Hart

Pound=Social Engineering, Savigny=Volksgeist, Kelsen=Grundnorm, Hart=Rule of Recognition.

10Hart distinguished between being 'obliged' and being under an 'obligation.' This distinction means:

  1. Both are identical
  2. Being obliged = coerced by force; being under an obligation = accepting an internal point of view toward a rule
  3. Obligation is voluntary; being obliged is contractual
  4. Obligation arises only in criminal law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Being obliged = coerced by force; being under an obligation = accepting an internal point of view toward a rule

Hart: being obliged (gunman scenario) = external coercion. Obligation = internal acceptance of a rule as a standard.

Report this question

11'External point of view' and 'internal point of view' towards law are concepts of:

  1. Kelsen
  2. Dworkin
  3. Fuller
  4. H.L.A. Hart
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. H.L.A. Hart

Hart: internal point of view = accepting rules as binding. External point of view = observing regularities from outside without accepting them.

Report this question

12'Teleological' theory of law means law is judged by:

  1. Its source
  2. Its procedure
  3. Its formal validity
  4. Its purpose/end (telos)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Its purpose/end (telos)

Teleological theory: law must be evaluated by its purpose/end — associated with sociological and natural law schools.

Report this question

13'Deontological' theory of law/ethics means:

  1. Judging acts by their consequences
  2. Judging law by its source
  3. Judging acts by whether they conform to rules/duties, regardless of consequences
  4. Judging law by social utility
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Judging acts by whether they conform to rules/duties, regardless of consequences

Deontology (Kant): acts are right or wrong based on conformity to duty/rules, not consequences.

Report this question

14Bentham's 'principle of utility' means:

  1. The greatest happiness of the greatest number is the measure of right and wrong
  2. Law must be based on custom
  3. Law must be obeyed regardless of its content
  4. Natural law is the basis of all rights
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The greatest happiness of the greatest number is the measure of right and wrong

Bentham's utilitarianism: the rightness of an act is measured by its tendency to produce maximum happiness for the maximum number.

Report this question

15'Felicific calculus' is a concept given by:

  1. Bentham
  2. Austin
  3. Mill
  4. Hart
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Bentham

Bentham's felicific calculus: a method to quantify pleasure and pain to measure the utility of an act.

Report this question

16The concept of 'jus naturale' in Roman law was:

  1. The law of Roman citizens only
  2. The law of all nations based on reason and nature
  3. The Emperor's decrees
  4. Codified statutory law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The law of all nations based on reason and nature

Jus naturale = universal natural law based on reason, applicable to all human beings.

Report this question

17'Social solidarity' as a basis of law was propounded by:

  1. Savigny
  2. Roscoe Pound
  3. Ehrlich
  4. Leon Duguit
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Leon Duguit

Leon Duguit rejected sovereignty-based theories — law is based on social solidarity and interdependence.
