About this question set

This set has 17 multiple-choice questions on Nature and Sources of Law from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 1, Jurisprudence. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 17 of the 17 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Jurisprudence notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

17 practice questions with answers

1Who defined law as 'the body of principles recognised and applied by the State in the administration of justice'?

  1. Austin
  2. Salmond
  3. Holland
  4. Savigny
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Salmond

Salmond's definition focuses on State recognition and administration of justice.

Report this question

2Austin defined law as:

  1. A rule of external human action enforced by sovereign political authority
  2. A set of rules recognised by courts
  3. A moral order enforced by society
  4. A command issued by a sovereign and backed by sanction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A command issued by a sovereign and backed by sanction

Austin's imperative theory defines law as a command of the sovereign backed by sanction.

Report this question

3According to Austin, which of the following is NOT a source of positive law?

  1. Legislation
  2. Precedent
  3. Divine law
  4. Custom (directly)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Divine law

Austin called divine law and morality 'positive morality', not law proper.

Report this question

4The term 'Jurisprudence' is derived from the Latin words:

  1. Jus + prudence
  2. Juris + dictio
  3. Juris + prudentia
  4. Lex + prudentia
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Juris + prudentia

Jurisprudence comes from 'juris' (law) and 'prudentia' (knowledge/skill).

Report this question

5Who is regarded as the 'Father of Jurisprudence'?

  1. Bentham
  2. Savigny
  3. Austin
  4. Kelsen
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Austin

Austin is regarded as the Father of Jurisprudence for systematising the analytical study of law.

Report this question

6Which of the following is a formal source of law according to Salmond?

  1. Custom
  2. Legislation
  3. Precedent
  4. Both Legislation and Precedent
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Both Legislation and Precedent

Salmond distinguished formal sources (legislation, precedent) from material sources (customs, principles of justice).

Report this question

7Custom becomes law when:

  1. It is widely followed
  2. It is recognised and enforced by courts
  3. It has existed for 100 years
  4. It is approved by the legislature
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. It is recognised and enforced by courts

Custom acquires legal force when courts recognise and enforce it.

Report this question

8Which source of law does Savigny give the highest importance to?

  1. Legislation
  2. Custom
  3. Judicial precedent
  4. Equity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Custom

Savigny's Historical School held that law originates from the Volksgeist (spirit of the people), manifested through custom.

Report this question

9'Volksgeist' means:

  1. Will of the sovereign
  2. Voice of the judiciary
  3. Conscience of the legislature
  4. Spirit of the people
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Spirit of the people

Volksgeist = spirit/consciousness of the people, Savigny's foundational concept.

Report this question

10Holland defined jurisprudence as the:

  1. Philosophy of positive law
  2. Formal science of positive law
  3. Science of legislation
  4. Science of rights and duties
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Formal science of positive law

Holland defined jurisprudence as the formal science of positive law.

Report this question

11The doctrine of 'stare decisis' relates to which source of law?

  1. Legislation
  2. Custom
  3. Judicial Precedent
  4. Equity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Judicial Precedent

Stare decisis means 'stand by decided cases' — the principle of binding precedent.

Report this question

12In which of the following is 'ratio decidendi' found?

  1. Judgment of a court
  2. Obiter dicta
  3. Legislature debates
  4. Statutory provisions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Judgment of a court

Ratio decidendi (reason for the decision) is found in the judgment of a court and creates binding precedent.

Report this question

13'Obiter dicta' means:

  1. Remarks made by judge in passing, not binding
  2. Binding principle of a case
  3. Dissenting opinion of a judge
  4. Majority opinion in a case
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Remarks made by judge in passing, not binding

Obiter dicta = things said by the way; not binding but may be persuasive.

Report this question

14According to Paton, which of the following is the most important source of law in modern times?

  1. Custom
  2. Precedent
  3. Equity
  4. Legislation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Legislation

Paton considered legislation the most important source of law in modern times.

Report this question

15'Law is not found but made' — this view is associated with:

  1. Historical School
  2. Natural Law School
  3. Sociological School
  4. Analytical School
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Analytical School

The Analytical School views law as positively enacted/made, not discovered through custom.

Report this question

16'Ubi societas ibi jus' means:

  1. Where there is law, there is society
  2. Law exists beyond society
  3. Where there is society, there is law
  4. Justice is blind
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Where there is society, there is law

This Latin maxim expresses the inseparable link between society and law.

Report this question

17Hugo Grotius is known as the:

  1. Father of International Law
  2. Father of Jurisprudence
  3. Father of Natural Law
  4. Father of Sociology of Law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Father of International Law

Hugo Grotius is the Father of International Law. His work 'De Jure Belli ac Pacis' (1625) founded modern international law.
