About this question set

This set has 16 multiple-choice questions on Nature and Sources of Law from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 1, Jurisprudence. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 16 of the 16 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Jurisprudence notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

16 practice questions with answers

1Assertion (A): Austin excluded international law from the category of positive law. Reason (R): International law lacks a sovereign to enforce it.

  1. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are correct but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is correct but R is wrong
  4. A is wrong but R is correct
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A

Austin correctly excluded international law — it is positive morality, not positive law, because there is no sovereign above states.

Report this question

2Which of the following is described as a 'material source' of law by Salmond?

  1. Legislation
  2. Precedent
  3. Constitution
  4. Custom and principles of justice
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Custom and principles of justice

Salmond: material sources provide the content of law (custom, equity, justice); formal sources give it legal force (legislation, precedent).

Report this question

3'Equity' as a source of law developed in England through:

  1. Parliament
  2. Common Law courts
  3. Court of Chancery
  4. House of Lords
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Court of Chancery

Equity developed in the Court of Chancery, administered by the Lord Chancellor to mitigate common law's harshness.

Report this question

4Which of the following best describes 'conventional law'?

  1. Law made by Parliament
  2. Law based on customs
  3. Law derived from judicial decisions
  4. Law arising from agreements between states (treaties)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Law arising from agreements between states (treaties)

Austin's classification: conventional law = law arising from agreements between parties, e.g., international treaties.

Report this question

5'Positive law' according to Austin consists of:

  1. Laws of God
  2. Commands set by political superiors to political inferiors
  3. Laws of nature
  4. Moral rules enforced by public opinion
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Commands set by political superiors to political inferiors

Positive law = commands set by determinate political superiors (sovereigns) to political inferiors.

Report this question

6Which of the following jurists held that 'legislation is the most democratic source of law'?

  1. Salmond
  2. Bentham
  3. Austin
  4. Savigny
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Bentham

Bentham was a strong advocate of legislation and codification as a rational, democratic source of law.

Report this question

7A custom to be recognised as law must satisfy which of the following conditions according to English common law?

  1. Antiquity, continuity, peacefulness, reasonableness, certainty, obligatory force
  2. Age of 50 years, written form, judicial approval
  3. Parliamentary recognition only
  4. Universal acceptance and divine sanction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Antiquity, continuity, peacefulness, reasonableness, certainty, obligatory force

English common law requires customs to be ancient, continuous, peaceable, reasonable, certain, and compulsory to be legally recognised.

Report this question

8'Judge-made law' or law created through precedent is called:

  1. Common law / case law
  2. Statute law
  3. Customary law
  4. Delegated legislation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Common law / case law

Common law or case law refers to law developed through judicial precedent.

Report this question

9'Delegated legislation' means:

  1. Law made by the courts
  2. Constitutional amendments
  3. International treaties ratified by Parliament
  4. Law made by subordinate authorities under powers conferred by the legislature
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Law made by subordinate authorities under powers conferred by the legislature

Delegated legislation = secondary legislation made by executive/bodies under authority delegated by Parliament.

Report this question

10'Command' in Austin's sense must be issued by:

  1. A determinate human superior (sovereign) not in the habit of obedience to any other
  2. Any official
  3. Parliament alone
  4. The judiciary
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A determinate human superior (sovereign) not in the habit of obedience to any other

Austin's sovereign = determinate person(s) habitually obeyed but not in the habit of obeying any other superior.

Report this question

11The 'sanction' in Austin's theory is:

  1. A reward given for compliance
  2. A moral condemnation
  3. A legislative warning
  4. The evil the sovereign will inflict for non-compliance with a command
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The evil the sovereign will inflict for non-compliance with a command

Sanction = the likelihood of harm/evil that the sovereign will apply for non-compliance.

Report this question

12The term 'jurisprudence' was first used in a systematic sense by:

  1. Ulpian (Roman jurist)
  2. Austin
  3. Bentham
  4. Aristotle
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Ulpian (Roman jurist)

Ulpian defined jurisprudentia as 'the knowledge of things divine and human, the science of the just and unjust.'

Report this question

13Ulpian's three precepts of law are:

  1. 'Command, duty, sanction'
  2. 'Live honestly, harm no one, give every man his due'
  3. 'Custom, legislation, precedent'
  4. 'Reason, nature, justice'
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 'Live honestly, harm no one, give every man his due'

Ulpian: honeste vivere (live honestly), alterum non laedere (harm no one), suum cuique tribuere (give each their due).

Report this question

14'Suum cuique tribuere' means:

  1. Live honestly
  2. Do no harm to others
  3. Give to each person their own (their due)
  4. Respect the sovereign
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Give to each person their own (their due)

Suum cuique tribuere = give to each what is their own/their due — one of Ulpian's three precepts.

Report this question

15'Audi alteram partem' means:

  1. Hear one side only
  2. Hear the other side / Both sides must be heard
  3. The judge decides alone
  4. Precedent must be followed
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Hear the other side / Both sides must be heard

Audi alteram partem = hear the other side — a principle of natural justice requiring that both parties are heard.

Report this question

16'Nemo judex in causa sua' means:

  1. Everyone must follow the law
  2. Judges are infallible
  3. No one should be a judge in their own cause
  4. Law must be codified
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. No one should be a judge in their own cause

Nemo judex in causa sua = no person should be a judge in their own case — rule against bias (natural justice).
