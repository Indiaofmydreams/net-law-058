About this question set

This set has 18 multiple-choice questions on Succession and Inheritance from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 7, Family Law. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 18 of the 18 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Family Law notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

18 practice questions with answers

1The Hindu Succession Act, 1956 was amended to give daughters coparcenary rights in:

  1. 2005
  2. 2015
  3. 2010
  4. 2000
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 2005

The Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 amended Section 6 to give daughters equal coparcenary rights by birth, same as sons.

Report this question

2In Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma (2020), the Supreme Court held that daughters get coparcenary rights:

  1. Only if the father was alive on 9th September 2005
  2. Only if the daughter was born after 2005
  3. Irrespective of whether the father was alive on 9th September 2005
  4. Only for self-acquired property of father
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Irrespective of whether the father was alive on 9th September 2005

The Supreme Court held that the right of daughters as coparceners is by birth and does not depend on the father being alive on the date of the amendment (September 9, 2005).

Report this question

3Under the Hindu Succession Act, 1956, the property of a Hindu male dying intestate devolves first upon:

  1. Class II heirs
  2. Cognates
  3. Agnates
  4. Class I heirs
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Class I heirs

Section 8 HSA establishes the order: Class I heirs first, then Class II heirs, then agnates, then cognates, and finally the Government (escheat).

Report this question

4Under Muslim Law of Inheritance, the Quranic heirs (Sharers) are also known as:

  1. Dhawul Arham
  2. Asabat
  3. Zawil Furud
  4. Uterine heirs
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Zawil Furud

Zawil Furud (also spelt Dhawul Furud) means 'persons with fixed shares' — they are the Quranic heirs whose shares are specified in the Quran.

Report this question

5The rule of 'Doctrine of Aul' (increase) under Muslim Law means:

  1. When shares of heirs exceed the whole estate, each share is proportionally reduced
  2. The estate increases with distant relatives
  3. When residue remains, it goes to the State
  4. When shares do not exhaust the estate, it is distributed proportionally
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. When shares of heirs exceed the whole estate, each share is proportionally reduced

The Doctrine of Aul (increase/proportional reduction) applies when the total shares of Quranic heirs exceed the whole estate — each sharer's fraction is proportionally reduced.

Report this question

6Under Muslim Inheritance Law, the 'Doctrine of Radd' (return) means:

  1. When shares exceed one, proportional reduction
  2. Exclusion of distant relatives
  3. When Quranic heirs' shares are less than one, the residue returns to sharers proportionally
  4. The estate reverts to the State
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. When Quranic heirs' shares are less than one, the residue returns to sharers proportionally

Radd (return) is the opposite of Aul. When the total shares of Quranic heirs do not exhaust the estate, the surplus is returned to them proportionally (except to the spouse who takes only their fixed share).

Report this question

7'Agnates' under Hindu Succession Act are:

  1. Relations through females only
  2. Relations through males only
  3. Both paternal and maternal relations
  4. Adopted relatives
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Relations through males only

Section 3(a) of the HSA defines agnates as persons related by blood or adoption wholly through males.

Report this question

8A Hindu woman's right to her self-acquired property under the Hindu Succession Act, 1956 is:

  1. Absolute ownership
  2. Life interest only
  3. Joint ownership with husband
  4. Limited interest
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Absolute ownership

Section 14(1) of the HSA, 1956 provides that any property possessed by a female Hindu, whether acquired before or after commencement of the Act, shall be held by her as full owner.

Report this question

9Under Sunni Law, a bequest (will/wasiyat) cannot exceed:

  1. The entire estate
  2. 1/3 of the estate after debts
  3. 1/4 of the estate
  4. 1/2 of the estate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1/3 of the estate after debts

Under Sunni Muslim Law, a valid bequest (wasiyat) cannot exceed 1/3 of the estate after payment of debts and funeral expenses.

Report this question

10Under Sunni Law, a bequest in favour of an heir (one who inherits) is:

  1. Valid without consent of other heirs
  2. Invalid unless consented to by all other heirs at the time of the testator's death
  3. Valid up to 1/6 of the estate
  4. Always invalid
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Invalid unless consented to by all other heirs at the time of the testator's death

Under Sunni Muslim Law, a bequest in favour of an heir is invalid unless all other legal heirs consent to it at the time of the testator's death.

Report this question

11The principle 'the nearer excludes the more remote' applies in:

  1. Hindu Succession only
  2. Muslim Succession only
  3. Both Hindu and Muslim Succession
  4. Only under the Indian Succession Act, 1925
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Both Hindu and Muslim Succession

The principle that a nearer heir excludes more remote heirs operates in both Hindu and Muslim succession systems.

Report this question

12Under the 'Doctrine of Parentage' under Muslim Law, a child born within what period after dissolution of marriage is presumed legitimate under Hanafi law?

  1. 6 months after marriage and before 2 years after dissolution
  2. 6 months after marriage and before 1 year after dissolution
  3. 3 months after marriage and before 1 year
  4. Any time after marriage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 6 months after marriage and before 2 years after dissolution

Under Hanafi law, the maximum period of gestation is 2 years. A child born within 6 months of marriage and within 2 years of dissolution is presumed to be legitimate.

Report this question

13After the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005, a daughter of a coparcener:

  1. Becomes a coparcener by birth in her own right, same as a son
  2. Becomes a coparcener only after the father's death
  3. Becomes a coparcener only if unmarried
  4. Gets a share only if the father has no son
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Becomes a coparcener by birth in her own right, same as a son

Section 6 as amended by the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 provides that a daughter of a coparcener shall, by birth, become a coparcener in her own right in the same manner as a son.

Report this question

14The 'Doctrine of Pious Obligation' under Hindu Law was abolished by:

  1. Personal Laws (Amendment) Act, 2010
  2. Hindu Succession Act, 1956
  3. Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005
  4. Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005

Section 6(4) inserted by the 2005 Amendment provides that no court shall recognise any right to proceed against a son, grandson or great-grandson for recovery of any debt due from his father, grandfather or great-grandfather solely on the ground of the pious obligation under the Hindu law.

Report this question

15Under the Hindu Succession Act, 1956, 'Escheat' (Section 29) means:

  1. In default of all heirs, the property devolves upon the Government
  2. Property shared among cognates
  3. Property reverts to the donor
  4. Property distributed among distant relatives
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. In default of all heirs, the property devolves upon the Government

Section 29 of the HSA, 1956 provides for escheat — when a Hindu dies intestate and leaves no heirs, the property devolves upon the Government, which takes it subject to all obligations and liabilities.

Report this question

16Under Section 15 of the Hindu Succession Act, 1956, property of a Hindu female dying intestate devolves FIRST upon:

  1. Husband's heirs
  2. Father's heirs
  3. Mother and father
  4. Sons, daughters and husband
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Sons, daughters and husband

Section 15(1)(a) provides that the property of a Hindu female dying intestate shall devolve firstly upon the sons, daughters (including predeceased children's issue) and the husband.

Report this question

17Class II heirs under the Hindu Succession Act, 1956 are enumerated in:

  1. Section 8
  2. Schedule — Class II of the Schedule
  3. Section 9
  4. Section 11
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Schedule — Class II of the Schedule

The Schedule to the Hindu Succession Act, 1956 enumerates Class I and Class II heirs. Class II heirs in the Schedule are grouped in entries I through XI.

Report this question

18Section 24 of the Hindu Succession Act (which caused a widow to lose inheritance on remarriage) was:

  1. Amended to allow widows to retain inheritance
  2. Retained in its original form
  3. Omitted by the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005
  4. Replaced by Section 15
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Omitted by the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005

The 2005 Amendment omitted Section 24 which had the effect of disqualifying certain widows on remarriage from inheriting.
