About this question set

This set has 20 multiple-choice questions on Will, Gift and Wakf from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 7, Family Law. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 20 of the 20 questions carry a written explanation.

20 practice questions with answers

1Under Muslim Law, a gift (Hiba) is complete only upon:

  1. Registration
  2. Payment of consideration
  3. Written declaration
  4. Oral declaration + delivery of possession
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Oral declaration + delivery of possession

The three essentials of a valid Hiba are: (1) declaration by the donor, (2) acceptance by the donee, and (3) delivery of possession. Registration is not required.

Report this question

2A gift of future property under Muslim Law is:

  1. Voidable
  2. Valid
  3. Void
  4. Valid if witnessed
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Void

Under Muslim Law, a gift of future property is void. The donor must have legal ownership of the property at the time of making the gift.

Report this question

3Hiba-bil-Iwaz under Muslim Law is:

  1. Gift in exchange for consideration
  2. Gift with a condition
  3. Gift to a non-Muslim
  4. Gift revocable at will
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Gift in exchange for consideration

Hiba-bil-Iwaz is a gift made in exchange for a return consideration (iwaz). Once consideration has passed, the gift becomes irrevocable.

Report this question

4Under Muslim Law, which of the following gifts is irrevocable?

  1. Gift to a spouse
  2. Gift to a stranger
  3. Hiba-bil-Iwaz where consideration has passed
  4. Gift made in contemplation of death (Marz-ul-Maut)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Hiba-bil-Iwaz where consideration has passed

Hiba-bil-Iwaz is irrevocable once consideration has passed. Gifts to strangers, spouses (in some cases), and blood relations are generally revocable. Marz-ul-Maut gifts have special rules.

Report this question

5A 'Wakf' under Muslim Law is:

  1. A trust for family only
  2. A permanent dedication of property for religious, pious or charitable purposes
  3. A gift of property
  4. A bequest under a will
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A permanent dedication of property for religious, pious or charitable purposes

Wakf is the permanent dedication by a Muslim of any movable or immovable property for any purpose recognised by Muslim Law as religious, pious or charitable.

Report this question

6Wakf-alal-Aulad is:

  1. Wakf for public religious purposes
  2. A revocable charitable endowment
  3. Wakf for mosques only
  4. Wakf for the benefit of family/descendants (private wakf)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Wakf for the benefit of family/descendants (private wakf)

Wakf-alal-Aulad is a private wakf where the benefit is given first to the family/descendants and ultimately to charitable purposes.

Report this question

7The Wakf Act regulating Wakf properties in India was enacted/amended in:

  1. 1913 and 1954
  2. 1939 and 1986
  3. 1954 and 1995
  4. 1995 (with major amendment in 2013)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 1995 (with major amendment in 2013)

The current Wakf Act was enacted in 1995 and underwent a major amendment in 2013. The Wakf (Amendment) Act, 2025 brought further significant changes.

Report this question

8Under Muslim Law, the mutawalli (manager of wakf) is:

  1. The beneficiary of the wakf
  2. A trustee/manager who manages the property in accordance with the wakf deed
  3. The person who created the wakf
  4. Owner of the wakf property
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A trustee/manager who manages the property in accordance with the wakf deed

The mutawalli is a manager or superintendent of Wakf property. The mutawalli does not own the property but manages it as a trustee.

Report this question

9Under Hindu Law, a will (testament) is governed by:

  1. The Hindu Succession Act, 1956
  2. The Indian Succession Act, 1925
  3. The Transfer of Property Act, 1882
  4. Both (a) and (b)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The Indian Succession Act, 1925

Wills by Hindus are governed by the Indian Succession Act, 1925 (Sections 57 and Schedule III apply to Hindus), not the Hindu Succession Act.

Report this question

10A gift under Muslim Law can be made for a limited period (life interest):

  1. True — called Hiba-bil-Shart
  2. True — called Marz-ul-Maut gift
  3. True — called Ariya
  4. False — a gift under Muslim Law cannot be for a limited period; it must be absolute
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. False — a gift under Muslim Law cannot be for a limited period; it must be absolute

Under Muslim Law, a gift must be absolute. A gift for a limited period or with conditions is void to the extent of the limitation. The gift cannot be conditional or for a life estate.

Report this question

11The essential conditions for a valid Wakf under Muslim Law are:

  1. Court approval is necessary
  2. The Wakif must be a Muslim, property must be specific, and dedication must be permanent for pious/religious/charitable purpose
  3. Registration is mandatory
  4. The Wakif can be of any religion
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The Wakif must be a Muslim, property must be specific, and dedication must be permanent for pious/religious/charitable purpose

A valid Wakf requires: (1) the Wakif must be a Muslim, (2) the property must be specific and in existence, (3) the dedication must be permanent, and (4) it must be for purposes recognised under Muslim Law.

Report this question

12Can a Wakif (person who creates a Wakf) retain benefit for himself under Muslim Law?

  1. Only for the first 10 years
  2. Yes — a Wakif can retain benefit for himself and then pass it to others (Wakf-alal-Aulad)
  3. Only if approved by the Wakf Board
  4. Never — the Wakif must exclude himself entirely
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Yes — a Wakif can retain benefit for himself and then pass it to others (Wakf-alal-Aulad)

In Wakf-alal-Aulad, the Wakif can retain life interest for himself, then for descendants, and ultimately for charitable purposes. This was settled in Abu Fata Mahomed v. Russomoy (1894).

Report this question

13Which of the following is NOT a feature of a valid Wakf?

  1. The property dedicated must be of a permanent nature
  2. It must be permanent
  3. It can be revoked at the will of the Wakif at any time
  4. It must be for purposes recognised as pious, religious or charitable by Muslim Law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. It can be revoked at the will of the Wakif at any time

Once validly constituted, a Wakf is irrevocable and permanent. The Wakif loses ownership and the property vests in God for the benefit of the specified purpose.

Report this question

14Under Muslim Law, Wakf property can be exchanged (Istibdal):

  1. At the discretion of the Mutawalli freely
  2. Only by the original Wakif
  3. Automatically after 99 years
  4. Only with court permission and when it benefits the Wakf
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Only with court permission and when it benefits the Wakf

Istibdal (exchange of Wakf property) is permitted only with prior court permission and only when the exchange will benefit the Wakf — the property fetched must be at least equivalent in value.

Report this question

15The Wakf (Amendment) Act, 2025 is significant because:

  1. It introduced non-Muslim members to Wakf Boards, removed 'Wakf by user' provisions, and required government survey before declaring Wakf property
  2. It merged Wakf Boards with State Governments
  3. It nationalised all Wakf properties
  4. It abolished Wakf Tribunals
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It introduced non-Muslim members to Wakf Boards, removed 'Wakf by user' provisions, and required government survey before declaring Wakf property

The Wakf (Amendment) Act, 2025 made major changes including introducing non-Muslim members to Wakf Boards, removing 'Wakf by user', and requiring government survey before Wakf property can be declared.

Report this question

16Hiba-bil-Shart under Muslim Law refers to:

  1. Gift of usufruct only
  2. Gift made in contemplation of death
  3. Gift made subject to a condition — the condition is void but the gift itself may be valid
  4. Gift in exchange for consideration
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Gift made subject to a condition — the condition is void but the gift itself may be valid

Hiba-bil-Shart is a gift with a condition. Under Muslim Law, conditions cannot be attached to a gift. Generally the condition is void; the gift may stand if the condition does not go to the essence of the transaction.

Report this question

17A Hindu making a will must comply with the requirements of:

  1. The Hindu Succession Act, 1956
  2. Section 63 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925 — signed/marked by testator, attested by two or more witnesses
  3. The Transfer of Property Act, 1882
  4. No formality is required for a Hindu will
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Section 63 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925 — signed/marked by testator, attested by two or more witnesses

Section 63 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925 applies to Hindus for wills — the will must be signed or marked by the testator and attested by two or more witnesses present at the same time.

Report this question

18A 'codicil' under the Indian Succession Act, 1925 is:

  1. A revocation of an earlier will
  2. A separate will entirely
  3. An instrument made in relation to a will explaining, altering or adding to its dispositions — deemed to form part of the will
  4. A gift deed attached to a will
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. An instrument made in relation to a will explaining, altering or adding to its dispositions — deemed to form part of the will

Section 2(b) ISA — codicil = instrument made in reference to a will explaining, altering or adding to its dispositions; it forms part of the will.

Report this question

19Under the Indian Succession Act, 1925, a will is revoked by:

  1. Another will or codicil, or a written declaration, or by burning/tearing/destroying with intention to revoke (also by marriage in case of non-Hindus)
  2. The testator's illness only
  3. Passing of 10 years without a codicil
  4. The death of a beneficiary
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Another will or codicil, or a written declaration, or by burning/tearing/destroying with intention to revoke (also by marriage in case of non-Hindus)

Section 70 ISA — a will is revoked by another will or codicil, by a writing declaring intention to revoke executed like a will, or by burning, tearing or otherwise destroying it with intention to revoke. Under Section 69, marriage revokes the will of non-Hindus; it does not apply to Hindus.

Report this question

20A 'privileged will' under Section 65 of the Indian Succession Act can be made by:

  1. Any person above 18 years
  2. A soldier in actual warfare, an airman in actual warfare, or a mariner at sea — without usual attestation requirements
  3. A judge or magistrate
  4. A terminally ill person
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A soldier in actual warfare, an airman in actual warfare, or a mariner at sea — without usual attestation requirements

Section 65 ISA — privileged wills: soldiers/airmen employed in an expedition or engaged in actual warfare, and mariners at sea can make wills without usual attestation requirements.
