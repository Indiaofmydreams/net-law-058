Paper analysis

What the CUET PG LLM paper has looked like, which subjects carry the marks, what kinds of questions are asked, how the paper has changed, and how +4 and −1 should shape the way you attempt it.

Where these numbers come from. NTA does not publish subject-wise weightage or official papers with topic tags. This page combines OFFICIAL pattern facts, REPORTED candidate counts and difficulty feedback from 2025 and 2026 coverage, and EDITORIAL estimates built from the syllabus structure and published coaching analyses. Treat the estimates as a planning guide, not a promise.

Core, regular, occasional

CORE

Constitution of India is the single largest block. Add Criminal Law (substantive and procedure), Contract, Torts, Jurisprudence and Hindu Law. Together these usually supply well over half of the paper.

REGULAR

Property, Company, Administrative, Muslim Law, Environmental, Public International Law, Human Rights, CPC, Evidence, IPR and Interpretation of Statutes give a few questions each; any one of them can surprise you.

OCCASIONAL

Labour, Banking, Insurance, ADR, RTI, Taxation, Competition, Professional Ethics, Penology, White Collar Crimes, Women and Criminal Law and International Trade give one or two questions or none. Do the basics for these late.

Trends to plan around

The paper is wide, not deep

Thirty-plus subjects in 75 questions means many subjects appear once. Breadth with accurate basics beats deep reading of a few subjects.

Basics dominate

Easy-to-moderate ratings in 2025 and 2026 came from direct questions on standard LL.B. content. Marks are lost on carelessness and half-knowledge, not on obscure cases.

Old and new criminal codes

2026 papers still used IPC terms. With BNS, BNSS and BSA in force since July 2024, expect mixed numbering over the next cycles: know both.

Current developments matter a little

Recent Acts (Labour Codes, Mediation Act 2023, DPDP Act 2023) and landmark judgments appear as one-liners. A short last-month revision is enough.

Competition is regional

About 11,000 candidates sat the LL.M. paper in 2026, but each university has few seats, so the cut-off you need depends on where you apply, not on a national rank.

Break-even is 20 percent accuracy: below it a blind attempt loses marks on average. Narrowing four options to two moves a guess to 50 percent, where the expected gain is +1.5 per question.

Strategy that follows from the trends

  1. Pass 1 (about 45 minutes): answer every question you are sure of. Do not linger past 60 seconds.
  2. Pass 2 (about 30 minutes): return to questions where you can remove two options; answer those.
  3. Pass 3 (rest): check marked answers and leave the pure guesses blank.
  4. Prepare in layers: core subjects to 90 percent, regular to 70 percent, occasional to the basics.
  5. Practise in the real ratio: the Practice Arena scores 1 and −0.25, which is +4 and −1 divided by four.
