UGC NET Paper 1 Unit 2: Research Aptitude Notes and MCQs

Research Aptitude tests whether you understand how a study is planned, carried out and reported. You do not need advanced statistics, only the vocabulary of research and the logic of the steps. These notes follow the official syllabus topic by topic and end with sample MCQs.

What Unit 2 covers

Research Aptitude is mostly definitions and sequences, so short daily revision of terms works better than reading long chapters. The official syllabus lists 6 topics for this unit. Each is explained below with the points examiners most often use.

1Research: meaning, types, characteristics and approaches

  • Research is a systematic, objective search for new knowledge or for better understanding of a problem.
  • Types by purpose: basic (fundamental) research builds theory; applied research solves practical problems; action research improves practice in one's own setting.
  • Characteristics: systematic, empirical, objective, logical, replicable and based on evidence.
  • Approaches: quantitative (numerical data), qualitative (non-numerical, meaning and experience) and mixed methods.

2Research methods: experimental, descriptive, historical, qualitative and quantitative

  • Experimental method: the researcher manipulates an independent variable and observes the effect on a dependent variable, usually with a control group.
  • Descriptive method: describes existing conditions through survey, case study or correlational study.
  • Historical method: studies past events using primary and secondary sources. Ex-post facto research studies causes after events have occurred, without manipulation.
  • Qualitative methods include interviews, observation, case study, ethnography and grounded theory. Quantitative methods use measurement and statistics.

3Steps of research

  • Typical sequence: identify the problem, review the literature, formulate the hypothesis or research questions, design the study, collect data, analyse data, interpret and report.
  • A hypothesis is a testable tentative statement. The null hypothesis states no significant difference or relationship.
  • Sampling: probability methods (simple random, systematic, stratified, cluster) and non-probability methods (purposive, quota, snowball, convenience).
  • Tools must be reliable (consistent results) and valid (measure what they claim to measure). A pilot study helps refine tools before the main study.

4Thesis and article writing: format and styles of referencing

  • A thesis usually has a title page, abstract, contents, introduction, review of literature, methodology, results, discussion, conclusion, references and appendices.
  • An abstract is a short summary of aims, methods and findings. A journal article follows a similar but shorter structure.
  • APA is author-date style, common in social sciences. MLA is author-page, common in literature and humanities. Chicago uses notes and bibliography or author-date. Vancouver uses numbered references, common in medicine.
  • A bibliography lists works consulted; a reference list lists only works cited in the text.

5Application of ICT in research

  • Search and literature: online databases, Google Scholar, Scopus, Web of Science and INFLIBNET resources.
  • Reference managers such as Zotero and Mendeley store and format citations. Statistical software such as SPSS and R supports analysis.
  • Shodhganga is the national repository of Indian electronic theses and dissertations.
  • Plagiarism detection software, online surveys, cloud storage and collaboration tools support the research workflow.

6Research ethics: integrity, plagiarism and responsible conduct

  • Core principles: honesty, informed consent, confidentiality, voluntary participation, avoidance of harm and proper credit to others.
  • Research misconduct includes fabrication, falsification and plagiarism. Plagiarism is presenting another person's work or ideas as one's own.
  • Authorship should reflect real contribution, and conflicts of interest must be declared. Self-plagiarism and duplicate publication are also misconduct.
  • UGC regulations on academic integrity (2018) set graded consequences based on the level of similarity found in a thesis or paper.

Key terms and facts to remember

Independent variable

The variable manipulated by the researcher; the cause or treatment.

Dependent variable

The outcome that is measured; it depends on the treatment.

Null hypothesis

States no significant difference. It is tested and may be rejected; it is never simply proved.

Reliability and validity

Reliability is consistency of results. Validity is measuring what the tool is meant to measure.

Population and sample

The population is the whole group; the sample is the subset actually studied.

APA, MLA, Chicago

Referencing styles: author-date in social sciences, author-page in humanities, and notes or author-date.

Nuremberg Code1947

An early statement of voluntary informed consent in human research.

Declaration of Helsinki1964

Ethical principles for medical research involving human participants.

Belmont Report1979

Respect for persons, beneficence and justice in research ethics.

UGC academic integrity regulations2018

Rules to prevent plagiarism in higher education, with penalties graded by the level of similarity.

Shodhganga

Repository of Indian doctoral theses maintained by INFLIBNET.

How to study this unit

  • Make a one-page chart of sampling methods and sort them into probability and non-probability. This single chart answers many questions.
  • Keep the steps of research in order and know why the literature review comes before the hypothesis.
  • For ethics, remember the short list: consent, confidentiality, honesty, credit. Most options that violate one of these are wrong.
