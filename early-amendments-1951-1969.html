About this question set

This set has 18 multiple-choice questions on Early Amendments (1951-1969) from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 2, Constitutional and Administrative Law. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 18 of the 18 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Constitutional & Administrative Law notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

18 practice questions with answers

1The Ninth Schedule was added to the Constitution by which Amendment?

  1. Seventeenth Amendment, 1964
  2. Fourth Amendment, 1955
  3. Seventh Amendment, 1956
  4. First Amendment, 1951
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. First Amendment, 1951

Along with Arts. 31A and 31B, to protect land reform laws from judicial challenge.

Report this question

2Which of the following was NOT done by the First Amendment Act, 1951?

  1. Added 'public order', 'friendly relations with foreign States' and 'incitement to an offence' as grounds under Art. 19(2)
  2. Inserted Arts. 31A and 31B
  3. Inserted Article 21A
  4. Inserted Art. 15(4) for socially and educationally backward classes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Inserted Article 21A

Art. 21A was inserted by the 86th Amendment (2002).

Report this question

3The First Amendment inserting Art. 15(4) was a response to the decision in:

  1. Bela Banerjee v. State of W.B.
  2. A.K. Gopalan v. State of Madras
  3. State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan (1951)
  4. Re Berubari Union
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan (1951)

The Court struck down communal G.O. for college admissions; the 1st Amendment enabled special provisions for backward classes.

Report this question

4The restriction on freedom of speech in the First Amendment was a reaction to which pair of 1950 cases?

  1. A.K. Gopalan and Sajjan Singh
  2. Bela Banerjee and Champakam
  3. Kesavananda and Golak Nath
  4. Romesh Thappar v. State of Madras and Brij Bhushan v. State of Delhi
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Romesh Thappar v. State of Madras and Brij Bhushan v. State of Delhi

Both cases struck down press-related restrictions on grounds of 'public safety' as unconstitutional.

Report this question

5The Fourth Amendment (1955) made which change to Art. 31(2)?

  1. Adequacy of compensation was made non-justiciable
  2. Right to property was abolished
  3. Compensation was replaced with 'amount'
  4. Right to property was made a legal right under Art. 300A
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Adequacy of compensation was made non-justiciable

Response to State of West Bengal v. Bela Banerjee (1954), which required 'just equivalent' compensation.

Report this question

6The Seventh Amendment (1956) is associated with:

  1. Reorganisation of States and abolition of the Part A, B, C classification
  2. Merger of Sikkim
  3. Anti-defection law
  4. Creation of Panchayati Raj
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Reorganisation of States and abolition of the Part A, B, C classification

It reorganised into 14 States and 6 Union Territories, enabled a common High Court for two or more States (Art. 231) and inserted Arts. 350A-350B.

Report this question

7Article 350A (instruction in mother tongue at primary stage) and Art. 350B (Special Officer for linguistic minorities) were inserted by:

  1. Sixty-first Amendment, 1989
  2. Seventh Amendment, 1956
  3. Third Amendment, 1954
  4. Twenty-first Amendment, 1967
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Seventh Amendment, 1956

Linked to the States Reorganisation Commission recommendations.

Report this question

8The Ninth Amendment (1960) was enacted following the opinion in:

  1. Shankari Prasad
  2. Re Berubari Union and Exchange of Enclaves (1960)
  3. Keshavan Madhava Menon
  4. In re Kerala Education Bill
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Re Berubari Union and Exchange of Enclaves (1960)

The Court held cession of Indian territory requires a constitutional amendment under Art. 368, and that the Preamble is not part of the Constitution (later overruled in Kesavananda).

Report this question

9Which of the following was held in Re Berubari Union (1960), later overruled?

  1. The Preamble is justiciable
  2. Preamble is amendable only once
  3. The Preamble is a part of the basic structure
  4. The Preamble is not a part of the Constitution
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The Preamble is not a part of the Constitution

Kesavananda Bharati (1973) held the Preamble is part of the Constitution; Union of India v. LIC (1995) reaffirmed.

Report this question

10Dadra and Nagar Haveli were incorporated into India as a Union Territory by:

  1. Tenth Amendment, 1961
  2. Fourteenth Amendment, 1962
  3. Twelfth Amendment, 1962
  4. Seventh Amendment, 1956
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Tenth Amendment, 1961

12th (1962) integrated Goa, Daman and Diu; 14th (1962) integrated Puducherry.

Report this question

11Special provisions for Nagaland (Art. 371A) were inserted by:

  1. Twenty-second Amendment, 1969
  2. Thirteenth Amendment, 1962
  3. Thirty-sixth Amendment, 1975
  4. Fifty-third Amendment, 1986
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Thirteenth Amendment, 1962

Nagaland became the 16th State in 1963.

Report this question

12The Fourteenth Amendment (1962) is associated with:

  1. Creation of Meghalaya
  2. Incorporation of Goa
  3. Creation of Nagaland
  4. Incorporation of Puducherry and insertion of Art. 239A
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Incorporation of Puducherry and insertion of Art. 239A

Art. 239A provides for legislatures/councils for some Union Territories.

Report this question

13Which Amendment raised the retirement age of High Court judges from 60 to 62 and extended HC writ jurisdiction under Art. 226?

  1. Thirtieth Amendment, 1972
  2. Fifteenth Amendment, 1963
  3. Fifty-fourth Amendment, 1986
  4. Seventh Amendment, 1956
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Fifteenth Amendment, 1963

Also permitted High Courts to issue writs to persons/authorities outside territorial limits if the cause of action arose within.

Report this question

14Which Amendment inserted 'sovereignty and integrity of India' in the oath/affirmation of candidates and in Art. 19(2)-(4)?

  1. Sixth Amendment
  2. Sixteenth Amendment, 1963
  3. Forty-second Amendment
  4. Eighteenth Amendment
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Sixteenth Amendment, 1963

Enacted following the Sino-Indian conflict.

Report this question

15Sindhi was added to the Eighth Schedule by the:

  1. Ninety-sixth Amendment, 2011
  2. Seventy-first Amendment, 1992
  3. Ninety-second Amendment, 2003
  4. Twenty-first Amendment, 1967
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Twenty-first Amendment, 1967

71st added Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali; 92nd added Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali; 96th replaced 'Oriya' with 'Odia'. The Schedule now lists 22 languages.

Report this question

16The Thirteenth Amendment is related to Nagaland, while which Amendment dealt with the creation of Meghalaya as an autonomous State within Assam?

  1. Twenty-second Amendment, 1969
  2. Twenty-seventh Amendment, 1971
  3. Fifty-first Amendment, 1984
  4. Thirty-first Amendment, 1973
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Twenty-second Amendment, 1969

Inserted Art. 244A. 27th (1971) provided for Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh and others as Union Territories and inserted Art. 239B.

Report this question

17The Seventeenth Amendment (1964) is most notable for:

  1. Expanding the Ninth Schedule and redefining 'estate' in Art. 31A
  2. Introducing Fundamental Duties
  3. Introducing anti-defection law
  4. Introducing Art. 21A
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Expanding the Ninth Schedule and redefining 'estate' in Art. 31A

Challenged in Sajjan Singh v. State of Rajasthan (1965).

Report this question

18The Fifth Amendment (1955) amended which Article?

  1. Article 226
  2. Article 31
  3. Article 368
  4. Article 3 (time limit for State legislatures to express views on bills affecting States)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Article 3 (time limit for State legislatures to express views on bills affecting States)

Proviso to Art. 3 enables the President to specify a period within which the State legislature may express its views.
