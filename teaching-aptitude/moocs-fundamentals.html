24 practice questions with answers

1The first MOOC in history was conducted in:

  1. 2008
  2. 2012
  3. 2005
  4. 2010
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 2008

Concept: The first MOOC was "Connectivism and Connective Knowledge" by George Siemens and Stephen Downes, University of Manitoba, Canada, 2008. 2,300 participants enrolled.

2The year 2012 is called the "Year of the MOOC" because:

  1. UGC made MOOCs compulsory in 2012
  2. India launched SWAYAM in 2012
  3. MOOCs were invented in 2012
  4. Coursera, edX, and Udacity were all launched in 2012, bringing MOOCs to mainstream global attention
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Coursera, edX, and Udacity were all launched in 2012, bringing MOOCs to mainstream global attention

Concept: 2012 = explosive growth of MOOC platforms. New York Times called it "The Year of the MOOC." Coursera (Stanford), edX (MIT+Harvard), Udacity (Stanford) all launched.

3Which of the following is NOT a feature of a MOOC?

  1. Delivered online
  2. Massive enrolment possible
  3. Open access — no entry qualification required
  4. Limited to 30 students per batch
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Limited to 30 students per batch

Concept: Massive = unlimited, no cap. MOOCs have enrolled hundreds of thousands simultaneously. Limitation of 30 = traditional classroom, not MOOC.

Report this question

4cMOOC stands for:

  1. Certified MOOC
  2. Computer-based MOOC
  3. Curriculum-oriented MOOC
  4. Connectivist MOOC
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Connectivist MOOC

Concept: cMOOC = Connectivist MOOC. Based on George Siemens' Connectivism theory. Knowledge is distributed across networks. Learners co-create knowledge through interaction.

Report this question

5xMOOC stands for:

  1. Extended MOOC
  2. External MOOC
  3. Extensivist MOOC — content-delivery model
  4. Experimental MOOC
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Extensivist MOOC — content-delivery model

Concept: xMOOC = content-focused, lecture-based, quiz-assessed. Coursera, edX = xMOOC model. Content flows from expert to learner. Contrast with cMOOC = networked, collaborative knowledge-creation.

Report this question

6The theoretical foundation of cMOOC is:

  1. Connectivism
  2. Constructivism
  3. Behaviourism
  4. Cognitivism
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Connectivism

Concept: Siemens' Connectivism (2004) = learning by connecting nodes in a network. Knowledge exists in the network, not just in the individual. Internet-age learning theory.

Report this question

7Who developed the theory of Connectivism that underlies cMOOCs?

  1. Both George Siemens and Stephen Downes
  2. George Siemens
  3. Stephen Downes
  4. Sebastian Thrun
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both George Siemens and Stephen Downes

Concept: Both co-created connectivism and co-facilitated the first MOOC (CCK08). Siemens wrote "Connectivism: A Learning Theory for the Digital Age" (2004). Downes expanded it.

Report this question

8Coursera was founded by professors from which university?

  1. Stanford University
  2. Harvard University
  3. Oxford University
  4. MIT
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Stanford University

Concept: Coursera = Andrew Ng + Daphne Koller, Stanford, 2012. edX = MIT + Harvard, 2012. Udacity = Sebastian Thrun, Stanford, 2012.

9edX was founded by:

  1. University of Toronto
  2. Stanford University alone
  3. Yale and Princeton jointly
  4. MIT and Harvard University jointly
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. MIT and Harvard University jointly

Concept: edX launched in 2012 as a non-profit. MIT + Harvard = edX. Now owned by 2U Inc. Still offers free audit options.

10Which of the following is India's national MOOC platform?

  1. Udemy
  2. Coursera
  3. SWAYAM
  4. edX
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. SWAYAM

Direct memory: PYQ. SWAYAM = India's own MOOC platform. Free, government-run, covers school to postgraduate level.

11NPTEL primarily offers MOOCs in which domain?

  1. Science, technology, engineering, and management
  2. Agriculture and rural development
  3. Law and governance
  4. Arts and humanities
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Science, technology, engineering, and management

Concept: NPTEL (National Programme on Technology Enhanced Learning) = IITs + IISc joint initiative. STEM-focused courses. These now integrate with SWAYAM.

Report this question

12A typical MOOC assessment includes:

  1. Physical demonstration only
  2. Only written examinations in examination halls
  3. Online quizzes, peer assessment, assignments, and discussion forums
  4. Oral viva only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Online quizzes, peer assessment, assignments, and discussion forums

Concept: MOOCs use online assessment tools. Quizzes = automated. Assignments = submitted online. Peer review = scalable feedback mechanism for large enrolments.

Report this question

13The completion rate of most MOOCs globally is approximately:

  1. 40–50%
  2. 60–70%
  3. 5–15%
  4. 80–90%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 5–15%

Concept: This is one of the most significant criticisms of MOOCs. Despite massive enrolment, very few students complete courses. Research consistently shows 5–15% completion. Low accountability + no social pressure = high dropout.

Report this question

14The low completion rate of MOOCs is attributed to:

  1. Excessive difficulty of content
  2. High enrolment fees
  3. Lack of social interaction, accountability, and personalised support
  4. Poor quality of video lectures
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Lack of social interaction, accountability, and personalised support

Concept: MOOCs lack the social bonding and accountability of traditional classrooms. No teacher watching = easy to abandon. This is the most cited limitation in research.

Report this question

15Which of the following is an ADVANTAGE of MOOCs over traditional education?

  1. Stronger peer relationships
  2. Higher completion rates
  3. Better laboratory facilities
  4. Global accessibility, flexible schedule, and low or zero cost
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Global accessibility, flexible schedule, and low or zero cost

Concept: MOOCs democratise education — anyone with internet can access courses from top universities globally, at any time, for free or very low cost. This is their greatest strength.

Report this question

16Peer assessment in MOOCs is primarily used because:

  1. Regulations require it
  2. It is cheaper and scales with massive enrolment where individual instructor feedback is impossible
  3. Peers are more qualified than instructors
  4. Students prefer peer feedback over instructor feedback
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. It is cheaper and scales with massive enrolment where individual instructor feedback is impossible

Concept: With 50,000 enrolled students, one instructor cannot give individual feedback. Peer assessment = scalable solution. Also develops critical thinking in assessors.

Report this question

17Which of the following best describes a "hybrid MOOC" or "blended MOOC"?

  1. A MOOC that gives both certificate and degree
  2. A MOOC offered in two languages simultaneously
  3. A MOOC that combines online content delivery with face-to-face sessions or local study groups
  4. A MOOC that uses only audio content
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A MOOC that combines online content delivery with face-to-face sessions or local study groups

Concept: Hybrid MOOC = best of both = online flexibility + human interaction and accountability of in-person meetings. Addresses completion rate problem.

Report this question

18MOOCs were first conceptualised to address which global education challenge?

  1. Lack of sports facilities in universities
  2. Shortage of textbooks
  3. Access to quality higher education for millions who cannot attend physical universities
  4. Poor library resources
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Access to quality higher education for millions who cannot attend physical universities

Concept: The vision = democratise access. Rural student in India or Africa can access MIT-quality lectures. Remove geographic, economic, and social barriers to quality education.

Report this question

19Which of the following is a LIMITATION of MOOCs?

  1. MOOCs are not accessible on mobile devices
  2. MOOCs are available only in English
  3. MOOCs charge very high fees
  4. MOOCs cannot provide laboratory-based learning, hands-on skills, and the social dimension of campus education
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. MOOCs cannot provide laboratory-based learning, hands-on skills, and the social dimension of campus education

Concept: MOOC weakness = no physical lab, no campus social life, no sports, no mentoring relationships. Best for theoretical knowledge; limited for psychomotor/professional skills.

Report this question

20Certificate earned from a MOOC is called:

  1. Diploma of honour
  2. Transcript
  3. Degree certificate
  4. Digital badge or verified certificate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Digital badge or verified certificate

Concept: Most MOOCs provide digital certificates (PDF/shareable on LinkedIn). "Verified certificate" = identity verified via webcam/ID. Not equivalent to a formal degree.

Report this question

21The concept of "Open Educational Resources" (OER) is most closely linked to MOOCs through:

  1. Both being restricted to postgraduate level
  2. Their shared commitment to freely accessible, openly licensed educational materials
  3. Both requiring classroom attendance
  4. Their shared commercial nature
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Their shared commitment to freely accessible, openly licensed educational materials

Concept: OER = openly licensed content (Creative Commons). MOOCs often use and distribute OER. UNESCO promotes OER as fundamental to equitable education.

Report this question

22Which of the following types of learning is MOST supported by MOOC discussion forums?

  1. Psychomotor learning
  2. Drill-based procedural learning
  3. Collaborative and social learning (Vygotsky's social constructivism)
  4. Affective domain — values clarification
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Collaborative and social learning (Vygotsky's social constructivism)

Concept: Forums = learners teaching each other, sharing perspectives = social learning. Vygotsky = learning through social interaction. Discussion forums partially compensate for MOOC's isolation.

Report this question

23MOOCs that lead to a formal academic degree upon completion are called:

  1. Distance education degrees
  2. Nano-degrees or MicroMasters or Professional certificates evolving into full degrees
  3. Mini-MOOCs
  4. MOODs (Massive Open Online Degrees)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Nano-degrees or MicroMasters or Professional certificates evolving into full degrees

Concept: edX MicroMasters, Coursera Specialisations, Georgia Tech's online MS degree via Udacity = MOOCs evolving toward formal credentials. The line between MOOC and online degree is blurring.

Report this question

24"Self-paced MOOC" means:

  1. Content is delivered at double speed
  2. The course is completed at the university's fixed pace
  3. Learner progresses through content at their own speed with no fixed deadlines
  4. Pace is set by peer groups
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Learner progresses through content at their own speed with no fixed deadlines

Concept: Self-paced = maximum flexibility. No cohort deadlines. Learner controls schedule entirely. Most suitable for working adult learners (andragogy principle).
