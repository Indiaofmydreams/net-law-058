15 practice questions with answers

1The "grade point" system under CBCS replaced the traditional percentage system primarily to:

  1. Eliminate internal assessment
  2. Reduce the importance of examinations
  3. Enable comparison across institutions with different marking standards, and align with international grading practices
  4. Make it harder for students to calculate their performance
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Enable comparison across institutions with different marking standards, and align with international grading practices

Concept: Percentage marks = not comparable across universities (one gives marks liberally, another strictly). Grade points on 10-point scale = standardised, internationally comparable.

2Under CBCS, which of the following is an example of a "Generic Elective" for a Chemistry student?

  1. Analytical Chemistry
  2. Physical Chemistry
  3. Organic Synthesis
  4. Introduction to Sociology
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Introduction to Sociology

Concept: Generic Elective = from ANOTHER discipline. Chemistry student taking Sociology = GE. Chemistry courses (A, B, D) = Core or DSE.

Report this question

3CBCS promotes "horizontal mobility" which means:

  1. Students can move to higher studies easily
  2. Students can move between programmes, institutions, or disciplines while retaining their credits
  3. Teachers can move between departments
  4. Physical movement of students within campus
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Students can move between programmes, institutions, or disciplines while retaining their credits

Concept: Horizontal = lateral movement at same level. Vertical = upward to higher degree. CBCS enables both.

Report this question

4Which of the following best explains why CBCS aligns with NEP 2020?

  1. Both focus exclusively on technical education
  2. Both reduce the role of universities
  3. Both mandate only traditional examinations
  4. Both emphasise flexibility, multidisciplinarity, credit accumulation, and learner-centred education
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Both emphasise flexibility, multidisciplinarity, credit accumulation, and learner-centred education

Concept: NEP 2020 and CBCS share philosophy: student chooses, credits accumulate, disciplines mix, institutions become permeable. CBCS is the structural mechanism for NEP 2020 goals.

5Under the 4-year undergraduate programme (FYUP) under NEP 2020, credits earned and exits available are:

  1. Only one exit at 4 years
  2. Exit after 1 year = Certificate; 2 years = Diploma; 3 years = Degree; 4 years = Degree with Honours/Research
  3. Exit only after 2 years
  4. No exit option — all 4 years are mandatory
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Exit after 1 year = Certificate; 2 years = Diploma; 3 years = Degree; 4 years = Degree with Honours/Research

Direct memory: PYQ pattern. NEP 2020 multiple exit options.
Mnemonic: 1=C, 2=D, 3=Deg, 4=H → "Certificates Don't Determine Honours"

6"Audit courses" under CBCS are:

  1. Compulsory courses with extra credits
  2. Courses in financial auditing
  3. Courses taken for learning without credit — no grade recorded, only pass/fail attendance
  4. Courses offered online only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Courses taken for learning without credit — no grade recorded, only pass/fail attendance

Concept: Audit = attend and learn, no grade impact. Useful for students wanting exposure to a subject without affecting SGPA.

Report this question

7CBCS is most aligned with which global education framework?

  1. China's Gaokao system
  2. USA's SAT-based system
  3. European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System (ECTS)
  4. UK's O-level system
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System (ECTS)

Concept: CBCS = India's adaptation of ECTS. ECTS = credit framework enabling mobility across European universities. CBCS replicates this for Indian higher education.

Report this question

8A student who fails in one course under CBCS:

  1. Can carry forward the failed course and clear it in a subsequent semester without losing the entire year
  2. Fails the entire semester and must repeat it
  3. Gets zero credits for the entire year
  4. Is automatically expelled
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Can carry forward the failed course and clear it in a subsequent semester without losing the entire year

Concept: CBCS = course-by-course progression. Failure in one = retake that course only. No "year fail" concept. This reduces the devastating impact of one poor performance.

Report this question

9The "continuous assessment" component under CBCS typically contributes what percentage to the final grade?

  1. 20–40% internal + 60–80% end-semester examination
  2. Exactly 50% each
  3. 100% — only internal assessment
  4. 0% — only end-semester exam counts
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 20–40% internal + 60–80% end-semester examination

Concept: UGC CBCS model = blend of continuous internal assessment (assignments, quizzes, mid-term) + end-semester examination. Exact ratio varies by institution but 25–40% internal is typical.

Report this question

10Which of the following is a DISADVANTAGE of CBCS as implemented in Indian universities?

  1. CGPA calculation is impossible
  2. Implementation challenges — lack of faculty, infrastructure, and course variety in smaller colleges to offer genuine choice
  3. Credits are not internationally recognised
  4. Students have too many subject choices
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Implementation challenges — lack of faculty, infrastructure, and course variety in smaller colleges to offer genuine choice

Concept: CBCS works well in large, well-resourced universities. In smaller colleges = few faculty, few courses = "choice" is illusory. This gap between design and implementation is widely cited.

Report this question

11Under CBCS, the Academic Bank of Credits (ABC) functions as:

  1. A database of question papers
  2. A scholarship fund
  3. A digital repository where students' credits from multiple institutions are stored and can be redeemed
  4. A bank that loans money for education
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A digital repository where students' credits from multiple institutions are stored and can be redeemed

Concept: ABC (launched 2021 under NEP 2020) = digital credit locker. Multiple institutions contribute credits → student accumulates → redeems for degree. Complementary to CBCS.

Report this question

12CBCS in higher education was modelled on which existing Indian example before 2015?

  1. CBSE board system
  2. IIT credit system and some progressive universities (like Hyderabad Central University) that already used credit systems
  3. ICSE board system
  4. State board examinations
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. IIT credit system and some progressive universities (like Hyderabad Central University) that already used credit systems

Concept: IITs have used credit systems since their founding. HCU, JNU, and other central universities had credit systems before UGC universalised CBCS. UGC learned from these.

Report this question

13Under CBCS, "Open Electives" are available to:

  1. Students from ANY department across the university
  2. Only foreign students
  3. Only postgraduate students
  4. Only students from the same department
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Students from ANY department across the university

Concept: Open Elective = truly open = cross-disciplinary. Any student, any department can enrol. Maximises interdisciplinary exposure. Example: Philosophy course open to Science students.

Report this question

14A student in a 3-year CBCS programme typically has how many semesters?

  1. 4 semesters
  2. 3 semesters
  3. 6 semesters
  4. 8 semesters
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 6 semesters

Direct memory: 3 years × 2 semesters per year = 6 semesters. Each semester = 15–18 weeks. PYQ level fact.

Report this question

15CBCS was introduced partly to address which persistent problem in Indian higher education?

  1. Rigidity of traditional system — students stuck in fixed programmes with no flexibility, specialisation, or lateral mobility
  2. Excessive focus on practical skills
  3. Too many elective options already available
  4. Too much student autonomy
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Rigidity of traditional system — students stuck in fixed programmes with no flexibility, specialisation, or lateral mobility

Concept: Pre-CBCS = annual system, fixed courses, no choice, no mobility, one bad year = repeat entire year. CBCS = flexibility, choice, credit accumulation, mobility.
