25 practice questions with answers

1"Learners differ not only in how much they learn but in how they learn." This statement refers to:

  1. Physical differences
  2. Learning styles and cognitive styles
  3. Intelligence differences only
  4. Socioeconomic differences
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Learning styles and cognitive styles

Concept: Qualitative differences in learning = style. Quantitative = how much = intelligence/aptitude. Both types of individual differences matter.

Report this question

2The VARK model of learning styles was developed by:

  1. Neil Fleming
  2. Howard Gardner
  3. Carl Jung
  4. David Kolb
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Neil Fleming

Concept: VARK = Visual, Auditory, Read/Write, Kinesthetic — Neil Fleming (1987). Do not confuse with VAK (Visual, Auditory, Kinesthetic) which omits Read/Write.

Report this question

3Howard Gardner's Theory of Multiple Intelligences challenges the traditional concept of intelligence by proposing:

  1. Intelligence is multidimensional — at least 8 distinct types
  2. Intelligence can only be measured by IQ tests
  3. There is only one form of general intelligence (g-factor)
  4. Intelligence is fixed from birth
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Intelligence is multidimensional — at least 8 distinct types

Concept: Gardner (1983) — 8 intelligences: Linguistic, Logical-Mathematical, Spatial, Musical, Bodily-Kinesthetic, Interpersonal, Intrapersonal, Naturalist. Implication: teach to diverse strengths.

Report this question

4The "interpersonal" intelligence in Gardner's theory refers to:

  1. Understanding oneself — self-awareness
  2. Sensitivity to natural environment
  3. Understanding and relating effectively to others
  4. Skill with numbers and logic
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Understanding and relating effectively to others

Concept: Inter = between persons = social intelligence. Intra = within person = self-awareness. This confusion appears in NET questions.

Report this question

5David Kolb's Experiential Learning Theory proposes that effective learning involves a cycle of:

  1. Observe → Imitate → Practise → Master
  2. Lecture → Quiz → Feedback → Revision
  3. Read → Memorise → Reproduce → Forget
  4. Concrete Experience → Reflective Observation → Abstract Conceptualisation → Active Experimentation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Concrete Experience → Reflective Observation → Abstract Conceptualisation → Active Experimentation

Mnemonic: CRAA → "Cycling Round And Around"
Concrete Experience → Reflective Observation → Abstract Conceptualisation → Active Experimentation

Report this question

6A learner who prefers to read and write, takes detailed notes, and learns best through text is classified in VARK as:

  1. Read/Write learner
  2. Auditory learner
  3. Visual learner
  4. Kinesthetic learner
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Read/Write learner

Concept: Read/Write = text-dominant. Visual = diagrams/charts. Auditory = listening/discussion. Kinesthetic = hands-on/experience.

Report this question

7"Field dependent" and "Field independent" cognitive styles were identified by:

  1. Kolb
  2. Witkin
  3. Gardner
  4. Piaget
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Witkin

Concept: Witkin (1954) — Field Independent = analytical, separates figure from background easily, works independently. Field Dependent = holistic, context-dependent, better at social interaction.

Report this question

8A field-independent learner typically:

  1. Depends heavily on social context for learning
  2. Has difficulty separating relevant from irrelevant information
  3. Performs better in cooperative learning than individual tasks
  4. Analyses information independently of its surrounding context
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Analyses information independently of its surrounding context

Concept: Field Independent = analytical, self-directed, good at STEM. Field Dependent = holistic, social, good at humanities and interpersonal tasks.

Report this question

9According to Erik Erikson, the psychosocial crisis of young adulthood (college-age students, 19–40 years) is:

  1. Industry vs. Inferiority
  2. Intimacy vs. Isolation
  3. Identity vs. Role Confusion
  4. Generativity vs. Stagnation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Intimacy vs. Isolation

Concept: Young adulthood = forming close relationships vs. emotional isolation. College students are in this stage. Implication: peer learning, collaborative environments support development.

Report this question

10Which of the following is a cognitive characteristic of adult learners that DIFFERS from adolescent learners?

  1. Adults cannot engage in abstract thinking
  2. Adults have shorter attention spans
  3. Adults integrate new learning with a vast repertoire of prior experience
  4. Adults rely on concrete operations only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Adults integrate new learning with a vast repertoire of prior experience

Concept: Knowles' andragogy principle — adult experience = richest learning resource. New learning is always filtered through what adults already know and have lived.

Report this question

11"Motivation to learn" in adolescents is most strongly influenced by:

  1. Examination schedule alone
  2. Parent instructions
  3. Government policy
  4. Peer group norms and sense of belonging
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Peer group norms and sense of belonging

Concept: Adolescence = peer dominance. Whether peers value studying strongly predicts individual motivation. Social belonging = powerful motivator.

Report this question

12Which of the following is an ACADEMIC characteristic of adolescent learners?

  1. Growing capacity for logical and hypothetical reasoning
  2. Complete emotional stability
  3. Inability to engage in abstract reasoning
  4. Learning exclusively through physical activity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Growing capacity for logical and hypothetical reasoning

Concept: Piaget's Formal Operational stage (12+) = hypothetical-deductive reasoning emerges. This is the defining academic cognitive change of adolescence.

Report this question

13"Self-efficacy" as defined by Albert Bandura refers to:

  1. External evaluation of performance
  2. Academic intelligence score
  3. Actual ability to perform a task
  4. A person's belief in their own capacity to execute behaviours necessary to succeed
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A person's belief in their own capacity to execute behaviours necessary to succeed

Concept: Bandura (1977) — self-efficacy ≠ actual ability. It is PERCEIVED competence. High self-efficacy → greater effort → better performance. Critical learner characteristic.

Report this question

14A student who says "I always fail maths no matter how hard I try" is exhibiting:

  1. High self-efficacy
  2. Growth mindset
  3. Learned helplessness
  4. Intrinsic motivation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Learned helplessness

Concept: Seligman's Learned Helplessness (1967) = repeated failure → belief that outcomes are uncontrollable → giving up. Opposite of self-efficacy.

Report this question

15Carol Dweck's concept of "Growth Mindset" in learners means:

  1. Ability is innate and effort is pointless
  2. Intelligence and abilities can be developed through dedication and hard work
  3. Intelligence is fixed and cannot be changed
  4. Talented students do not need to try
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Intelligence and abilities can be developed through dedication and hard work

Concept: Dweck (2006) — Growth mindset vs. Fixed mindset. Growth = brain as a muscle. Fixed = talent is destiny. Growth mindset learners persist, embrace challenges, learn from failure.

Report this question

16Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs has direct implications for learner characteristics because:

  1. It establishes that unmet basic needs (physiological, safety) prevent effective learning
  2. It proves that all students are equally motivated
  3. It shows that only the most intelligent students can learn
  4. It suggests that motivation is always external
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It establishes that unmet basic needs (physiological, safety) prevent effective learning

Concept: Maslow (1943) — needs hierarchy: Physiological → Safety → Love/Belonging → Esteem → Self-actualisation. A hungry, unsafe, or socially isolated student cannot effectively engage in cognitive learning.

Report this question

17The "emotional" characteristics of adult learners, according to Knowles, include:

  1. High emotional dependence on the teacher
  2. A self-concept as capable, responsible, self-directing persons
  3. Need for teacher approval at every step
  4. Constant emotional turbulence
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A self-concept as capable, responsible, self-directing persons

Concept: Adults have established self-concepts. Being treated as children (directed, controlled) causes resistance. Andragogy = respect adult self-concept.

Report this question

18The principle that "learners construct knowledge by connecting new information to prior knowledge" is the basis of:

  1. Connectionism
  2. Constructivism
  3. Behaviourism
  4. Classical conditioning
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Constructivism

Concept: Constructivism (Piaget, Vygotsky, Ausubel) = prior knowledge + new experience → new understanding. Prior knowledge is the anchor — Ausubel called these "advance organisers."

Report this question

19Which of the following is a social characteristic unique to adult learners compared to adolescent learners?

  1. Orientation toward multiple social roles — parent, employee, citizen — simultaneously
  2. Dependence on family for learning decisions
  3. Avoidance of social interaction
  4. Need for peer acceptance
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Orientation toward multiple social roles — parent, employee, citizen — simultaneously

Concept: Adults juggle multiple roles; learning must fit into complex life contexts. This is why adult education must be flexible, relevant, and immediately applicable.

Report this question

20The concept of "Locus of Control" (Rotter) is relevant to learner characteristics because:

  1. It determines the student's dominant learning style
  2. It measures a student's reading speed
  3. It describes whether a learner believes outcomes are controlled by themselves (internal) or by external forces
  4. It measures academic intelligence
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. It describes whether a learner believes outcomes are controlled by themselves (internal) or by external forces

Concept: Internal locus = "I control my success" = higher achievement. External locus = "luck/teacher decides" = lower persistence. Rotter (1954). Highly relevant to motivation and achievement.

Report this question

21"Emotional Intelligence" (EQ) as a learner characteristic was popularised by:

  1. Alfred Binet
  2. Daniel Goleman
  3. Charles Spearman
  4. Howard Gardner
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Daniel Goleman

Concept: Goleman (1995) — EQ includes self-awareness, self-regulation, empathy, motivation, social skills. Research suggests EQ predicts success better than IQ in many life domains.

Report this question

22Which of the following learner characteristics is MOST directly addressed by cooperative learning strategies?

  1. Physical coordination
  2. Social and interpersonal learning skills
  3. Factual recall speed
  4. Individual memorisation capacity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Social and interpersonal learning skills

Concept: Cooperative learning = social + cognitive. Addresses interpersonal intelligence (Gardner), social characteristics of learners, Vygotsky's social learning.

Report this question

23The "readiness to learn" in adolescent learners is primarily driven by:

  1. Government examination schedule
  2. Teacher's syllabus coverage
  3. Peer group study habits
  4. Biological maturation and developmental tasks of adolescence
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Biological maturation and developmental tasks of adolescence

Concept: Adolescent readiness = maturation of prefrontal cortex + social/emotional developmental tasks (identity, relationships). Contrast with adult readiness = triggered by life situations.

Report this question

24A student who is highly curious, asks many questions, and pursues knowledge beyond the syllabus is demonstrating:

  1. Disruptive behaviour
  2. Examination anxiety
  3. Intrinsic motivation and intellectual curiosity
  4. Poor classroom discipline
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Intrinsic motivation and intellectual curiosity

Concept: Intrinsic motivation (Deci & Ryan's Self-Determination Theory) = curiosity, mastery, personal growth. Best predictor of deep learning and long-term achievement.

Report this question

25According to research on adolescent brain development, the last region of the brain to fully mature is:

  1. The cerebellum
  2. The brainstem
  3. The amygdala
  4. The prefrontal cortex
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The prefrontal cortex

Concept: Prefrontal cortex = judgement, impulse control, planning, decision-making. Fully matures around age 25. This explains why adolescents show risk-taking behaviour and emotional impulsivity — educational implication: structure and guidance remain necessary.
