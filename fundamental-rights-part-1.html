16 practice questions with answers

1The right to property was a Fundamental Right under Article 19(1)(f) and Article 31. It was deleted and made a constitutional/legal right under Article 300-A by which Amendment?

  1. 25th Amendment
  2. 46th Amendment
  3. 42nd Amendment
  4. 44th Amendment
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 44th Amendment

The 44th Amendment Act, 1978 deleted Articles 19(1)(f) and 31, and inserted Article 300-A making the right to property only a legal right, not a Fundamental Right.

2Which Article of the Constitution deals with the Right to Equality before law and equal protection of law?

  1. Article 14
  2. Article 13
  3. Article 16
  4. Article 15
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Article 14

Article 14 provides that the State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or the equal protection of the laws within the territory of India.

3The doctrine of 'Reasonable Classification' under Article 14 requires:

  1. The classification must be based on intelligible differentia only
  2. The differentia must have a rational nexus to the object sought to be achieved
  3. Both A and B
  4. Neither A nor B
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Both A and B

Two cumulative tests under Article 14: (1) intelligible differentia — the basis of classification must be clear and distinct; (2) rational nexus — the classification must be rationally connected to the object of the law. Leading case: State of West Bengal v. Anwar Ali Sarkar (1952). Concept note: Article 14 prohibits class legislation but permits reasonable classification. Both conditions must be satisfied simultaneously — intelligible differentia AND rational nexus to the object of the legislation.

Report this question

4'Arbitrariness' as a ground to strike down state action under Article 14 was evolved in which case?

  1. R.D. Shetty v. International Airport Authority
  2. Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Mujib
  3. E.P. Royappa v. State of Tamil Nadu
  4. Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. E.P. Royappa v. State of Tamil Nadu

In E.P. Royappa (1974), the Supreme Court held that equality is antithetic to arbitrariness and any arbitrary State action violates Article 14. This expanded Article 14 beyond mere 'reasonable classification' to strike down arbitrary executive action.

5Article 15 prohibits discrimination on grounds of:

  1. Religion, Race, Caste, Language, Place of birth
  2. Religion, Race, Caste, Sex, Place of domicile
  3. Religion, Race, Caste, Sex, Place of birth
  4. Religion, Caste, Language, Sex, Place of birth
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Religion, Race, Caste, Sex, Place of birth

Article 15(1) prohibits discrimination ONLY on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth. Language and domicile are NOT included in Article 15.

Report this question

6Which Article enables the State to make special provisions for women and children?

  1. Article 15(3)
  2. Article 15(4)
  3. Article 16(4)
  4. Article 21-A
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Article 15(3)

Article 15(3) provides that nothing in Article 15 shall prevent the State from making any special provision for women and children. Article 15(4) is for backward classes.

Report this question

7The concept of 'substantive due process' under Indian Constitutional Law was evolved through the interpretation of which Article?

  1. Article 14
  2. Article 19
  3. Article 21
  4. Article 32
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Article 21

In Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978), the SC held that the procedure under Article 21 must be just, fair and reasonable — importing substantive due process into Indian Constitutional Law. Concept note: Article 21 Expansion: Originally in A.K. Gopalan v. State of Madras (1950), Article 21 was narrowly interpreted. In Maneka Gandhi (1978), the SC held procedure must be just, fair and reasonable. Post-Maneka, Article 21 has been expanded to include right to livelihood, right to privacy (Puttaswamy, 2017), right to health, right to education (now 21-A), right to dignified death, etc.

8Right to Education as a Fundamental Right was inserted as Article 21-A by which Constitutional Amendment?

  1. 93rd Amendment Act, 2005
  2. 97th Amendment Act, 2011
  3. 44th Amendment Act, 1978
  4. 86th Amendment Act, 2002
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 86th Amendment Act, 2002

The 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002 inserted Article 21-A providing free and compulsory education to children between 6–14 years as a Fundamental Right.

Report this question

9Which case held that 'Right to Privacy' is a Fundamental Right under Article 21?

  1. K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India
  2. Joseph Shine v. Union of India
  3. NALSA v. Union of India
  4. Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India

In K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017), a 9-judge constitutional bench unanimously held that privacy is a fundamental right under Article 21 of the Constitution.

10Which Article of the Constitution provides for the Right against exploitation — prohibition of traffic in human beings and forced labour?

  1. Article 22
  2. Article 21
  3. Article 23
  4. Article 24
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Article 23

Article 23 prohibits traffic in human beings, begar and other similar forms of forced labour. Article 24 prohibits employment of children below 14 years in factories, mines or other hazardous work.

Report this question

11Freedom of religion is guaranteed under Articles:

  1. Articles 23–24
  2. Articles 25–28
  3. Articles 19–22
  4. Articles 29–30
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Articles 25–28

Articles 25–28 deal with freedom of religion. Articles 29–30 deal with cultural and educational rights of minorities.

Report this question

12The 'golden triangle' in Constitutional Law refers to:

  1. Articles 12, 13, and 14
  2. Articles 14, 19, and 21
  3. Articles 19, 20, and 21
  4. Articles 21, 22, and 23
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Articles 14, 19, and 21

The 'golden triangle' of Articles 14, 19, and 21 was evolved in Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978). Any law that affects these rights must pass the test of all three Articles.

Report this question

13Article 13 declares void any law that is inconsistent with or in derogation of Fundamental Rights. The phrase 'law' here includes:

  1. Legislation only
  2. Ordinances only
  3. Laws in force including ordinances, regulations, customs and usages
  4. Constitutional amendments only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Laws in force including ordinances, regulations, customs and usages

Article 13(3)(a) defines 'law' to include any Ordinance, order, bye-law, rule, regulation, notification, custom or usage. Constitutional amendments were held NOT to be 'law' under Article 13 in Kesavananda Bharati.

Report this question

14Which Article gives the Supreme Court power to issue writs for enforcement of Fundamental Rights?

  1. Article 226
  2. Article 136
  3. Article 32
  4. Article 131
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Article 32

Article 32 is the 'heart and soul' of the Constitution (Dr. Ambedkar). It gives the Supreme Court power to issue writs including habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari, and quo warranto for enforcement of Fundamental Rights.

Report this question

15Assertion (A): Article 21 includes the right to a fair trial and speedy justice. Reason (R): In Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar (1979), the SC held speedy trial is a component of Article 21.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

Article 21 has been expansively interpreted to include the right to speedy trial (Hussainara Khatoon, 1979), right to legal aid, and right to fair procedure. R correctly explains why A is true.

Report this question

16Which of the following is CORRECT about the 'Basic Structure Doctrine'? (i) Propounded in Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973); (ii) Limits Parliament's amending power; (iii) Federalism is a basic feature; (iv) Free and fair elections is a basic feature

  1. i and ii only
  2. i, ii and iii only
  3. i, ii, iii and iv — all four correct
  4. i and iv only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. i, ii, iii and iv — all four correct

All four are correct: (i) Kesavananda Bharati (1973) established basic structure; (ii) Parliament cannot amend to destroy basic structure; (iii) Federal character is a basic structure element (Kesavananda); (iv) Free and fair elections recognised as basic structure in Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975).
