8 practice questions with answers

1Which of the following pollutant gases is NOT produced both naturally and as a result of industrial activity?

  1. Nitrous oxide
  2. Methane
  3. Carbon dioxide
  4. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)

CFCs are entirely man-made and have no natural source. CO2, methane, and nitrous oxide all have both natural and anthropogenic sources.

2The dominant source of pollution due to oxides of nitrogen (NOx) in urban areas is:

  1. Power plants
  2. Commercial sector
  3. Energy use in industry
  4. Road transport
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Road transport

In urban areas, road transport (vehicles) is the dominant source of NOx emissions, contributing to smog formation and respiratory problems.

Report this question

3Which of the following pollutants is the major cause of respiratory disease?

  1. Nitrogen oxides
  2. Carbon monoxide
  3. Volatile organic compounds
  4. Suspended fine particles (PM2.5)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Suspended fine particles (PM2.5)

Suspended fine particles (PM2.5) are the primary cause of respiratory diseases as they can penetrate deep into the lungs and even enter the bloodstream.

Report this question

4Among the following air pollutants, which directly impacts human health as well as climate?

  1. Sulphur dioxide
  2. Oxides of nitrogen
  3. Soot (Black Carbon)
  4. Chlorofluorocarbons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Soot (Black Carbon)

Soot (black carbon) is a 'short-lived climate pollutant' that directly impacts both human health (respiratory and cardiovascular disease) and climate (absorbs solar radiation, causes warming).

5Frequent episodes of dense smog in winter season in Delhi NCR can be attributed to: (i) Stubble burning (ii) Biomass burning in rural households (iii) Transport (iv) Transboundary movement of pollutants

  1. (i) and (iii) only
  2. (i), (ii) and (iii) only
  3. (i), (iii) and (iv) only
  4. (i), (ii), (iii) and (iv)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. (i), (ii), (iii) and (iv)

Delhi's winter smog is a result of multiple combined factors: stubble burning in Haryana/Punjab, biomass burning for heating, transport emissions, and transboundary movement of pollutants — all four contribute.

6Assertion (A): In urban areas, smog episodes occur frequently in winters. Reason (R): In winters, a lot of biomass is burnt by people for heating purposes.

  1. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
  2. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
  3. (A) is true and (R) is false
  4. Both (A) and (R) are false
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)

Both statements are true, but biomass burning is only one of several reasons for winter smog. Temperature inversion, low wind speed, and multiple pollution sources also contribute — so R is not the complete/correct explanation of A.

7Photochemical smog is formed when:

  1. Sulphur dioxide reacts with rain water
  2. NOx reacts with hydrocarbons in sunlight to form ground-level ozone
  3. Coal is burned in industrial areas
  4. Carbon dioxide combines with dust particles
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. NOx reacts with hydrocarbons in sunlight to form ground-level ozone

Photochemical smog is a summer phenomenon formed when nitrogen dioxide (from traffic and industry) reacts with hydrocarbons in the presence of sunlight to produce ground-level ozone and other oxidants.

Report this question

8The Great Smog of London (1952) was primarily caused by:

  1. NOx from vehicles reacting with sunlight
  2. Industrial pollution (coal burning) combined with high-pressure anticyclone and windless conditions
  3. Agricultural stubble burning
  4. Volcanic ash from nearby eruptions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Industrial pollution (coal burning) combined with high-pressure anticyclone and windless conditions

The Great London Smog (December 5–9, 1952) was caused by a combination of industrial coal pollution and unusual weather — an anticyclone created still, cold conditions that trapped pollutants over the city for 5 days, killing thousands.
