UGC NET Paper 1 Unit 9: People, Development & Environment

This unit links everyday environmental issues with development goals and the laws and agreements that respond to them. Most questions test recall of goals, gases, treaties and years. These notes keep the essentials of each syllabus topic short so you can revise them quickly, whatever your subject.

What Unit 9 covers

This unit rewards neat tables of goals, treaties and laws, so revise them often rather than reading long chapters. The official syllabus lists 8 topics for this unit. Each is explained below with the points examiners most often use.

1Development and environment: millennium and sustainable development goals

  • The Millennium Development Goals (MDGs) were eight goals set in 2000 for the target year 2015, covering poverty, health, education and gender equality.
  • The Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) are 17 goals adopted by the UN in 2015 under Agenda 2030, to be achieved by 2030.
  • Sustainable development means meeting present needs without harming the ability of future generations to meet theirs (Brundtland Report, 1987).
  • SDG 13 is climate action. Other examples: SDG 1 no poverty, SDG 4 quality education, SDG 6 clean water and sanitation, SDG 7 affordable and clean energy.

2Human-environment interaction; anthropogenic activities and environmental impact

  • Anthropogenic activities are those caused by humans, such as farming, industry, mining, urbanisation and deforestation.
  • Their effects include habitat loss, pollution, loss of biodiversity and climate change.
  • Ecological footprint measures human demand on nature, and carrying capacity is the limit that an environment can support.
  • The three pillars of sustainability are environment, society and economy.

3Environmental issues: local, regional and global; air, water, soil, noise and waste pollution

  • Local issues include waste and sewage. Regional issues include acid rain and river pollution. Global issues include climate change and ozone depletion.
  • Primary air pollutants are emitted directly (carbon monoxide, sulphur dioxide, particulate matter). Secondary pollutants such as ground-level ozone and photochemical smog form in the air.
  • Biochemical oxygen demand (BOD) indicates organic pollution in water. Eutrophication is the nutrient enrichment of water that causes algal blooms and lowers dissolved oxygen.
  • Noise is measured in decibels (dB). Waste may be solid, liquid, hazardous, e-waste or biomedical, and is managed by reduce, reuse and recycle.

4Climate change, global warming, ozone depletion, acid rain and impacts on human health

  • Greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide and water vapour trap heat and cause global warming. Methane traps far more heat per molecule than carbon dioxide.
  • The ozone layer in the stratosphere absorbs ultraviolet radiation. Ozone depletion is caused by chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs).
  • Acid rain is caused mainly by sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides forming acids in the atmosphere.
  • Health impacts include respiratory disease, heat stress, vector-borne disease, skin cancer and cataracts (from UV exposure) and food insecurity.

5Natural and energy resources: solar, wind, soil, hydro, geothermal, biomass and nuclear

  • Renewable resources are replenished naturally: solar, wind, hydro, geothermal, tidal and biomass. Non-renewable resources are coal, petroleum and natural gas.
  • Geothermal energy comes from heat inside the Earth. Biomass energy comes from plant and animal matter and is generally treated as renewable.
  • Nuclear energy uses fission of uranium. It is low in carbon but raises waste and safety concerns.
  • Soil is a natural resource formed over a very long time, and it faces erosion, salinisation and loss of fertility.

6Natural hazards and disasters; mitigation and management

  • Natural hazards include earthquakes, floods, cyclones, droughts, landslides, volcanic eruptions and tsunamis. Industrial and chemical accidents are anthropogenic disasters.
  • Disaster management has stages: mitigation, preparedness, response and recovery.
  • In India, the Disaster Management Act, 2005 created the National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) as the apex body, with state and district authorities.
  • Early warning, community awareness and land-use planning reduce the damage from disasters.

7Environmental protection legislation and international agreements

  • India: Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972, Water Act 1974, Forest (Conservation) Act 1980, Air Act 1981, Environment (Protection) Act 1986 (after the Bhopal gas tragedy), and the National Green Tribunal Act 2010.
  • Stockholm Conference 1972 (first major UN conference on the environment) and Earth Summit, Rio de Janeiro 1992.
  • Montreal Protocol 1987 (ozone-depleting substances), Kyoto Protocol 1997 (cutting greenhouse gas emissions), Paris Agreement 2015 (limit warming to well below 2 degrees C, pursuing 1.5 degrees C).
  • World Environment Day is on 5 June.

8Environmental assessment and education

  • Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) studies the likely effects of a project on the environment before approval. In India it works under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986.
  • An EIA includes screening, scoping, impact analysis, public hearing, review and monitoring.
  • Environmental education builds awareness, knowledge, attitudes and skills for protecting the environment. It was made a compulsory subject at school and college level by a Supreme Court order in 2003 (M.C. Mehta case).
  • The Tbilisi Declaration, 1977 set the goals of environmental education.

Key terms and facts to remember

Sustainable Development Goals2015

17 goals under Agenda 2030.

Millennium Development Goals2000

Eight goals with 2015 as the target year.

Montreal Protocol1987

Phases out ozone-depleting substances such as CFCs.

Kyoto Protocol1997

Binding cuts in greenhouse gas emissions for developed countries.

Paris Agreement2015

Keep warming well below 2 degrees C and pursue 1.5 degrees C.

Environment (Protection) Act1986

Umbrella law in India, passed after the Bhopal gas tragedy.

Stockholm Conference1972

First UN conference on the human environment.

Ozone layer

Lies in the stratosphere and absorbs ultraviolet radiation.

Eutrophication

Nutrient enrichment of water leading to algal blooms and low oxygen.

BOD

Biochemical oxygen demand; a higher value means more organic pollution.

NDMA2005

Apex body for disaster management in India under the Disaster Management Act.

World Environment Day

Observed on 5 June.

How to study this unit

  • Learn the treaty-year-subject triplets in the table. Most agreement questions ask for exactly one of the three.
  • Keep a list of gases and their effects: CFCs and ozone, SO2 and NOx and acid rain, CO2 and methane and warming.
  • Revise the Indian environmental Acts by year, and remember that the Environment Protection Act of 1986 is the umbrella law.
