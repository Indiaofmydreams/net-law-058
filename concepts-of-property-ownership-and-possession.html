About this question set

This set has 22 multiple-choice questions on Concepts of Property, Ownership and Possession from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 1, Jurisprudence. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 22 of the 22 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Jurisprudence notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

22 practice questions with answers

1Savigny's theory of possession requires:

  1. Only corpus possessionis
  2. Only animus domini
  3. Both corpus possessionis and animus domini
  4. Neither corpus nor animus
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Both corpus possessionis and animus domini

Savigny required both corpus (physical control) and animus domini (intention to hold as owner).

Report this question

2Ihering's theory of possession requires:

  1. Only animus domini
  2. Both corpus and animus as equally essential
  3. Primarily corpus possessionis; animus is presumed
  4. Possession in law recognised by a court
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Primarily corpus possessionis; animus is presumed

Ihering: corpus is the main element; animus is presumed unless the law says otherwise.

Report this question

3'Animus domini' means:

  1. Intention to hold a thing as owner
  2. Physical control over a thing
  3. Legal title to property
  4. Transfer of possession
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Intention to hold a thing as owner

Animus domini = intention to hold/possess as owner.

Report this question

4'Corpus possessionis' means:

  1. Physical control over a thing
  2. Intention to possess
  3. Document of title
  4. The owner's right to exclude
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Physical control over a thing

Corpus possessionis = the physical element of possession — actual control over the thing.

Report this question

5Who said ownership is 'a plenary real right'?

  1. Austin
  2. Holland
  3. Salmond
  4. Paton
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Salmond

Salmond described ownership as a plenary (complete) real right — the highest form of real right.

Report this question

6'Nemo dat quod non habet' means:

  1. One cannot give what one does not have
  2. Possession is nine-tenths of the law
  3. The owner is always right
  4. Property cannot be transferred without consent
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. One cannot give what one does not have

This maxim means one cannot transfer a better title than one possesses.

Report this question

7'Possession is nine-tenths of the law' means:

  1. Possession creates a strong presumption of ownership
  2. Ownership is more important than possession
  3. Law protects possession for nine years
  4. Nine types of possession exist in law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Possession creates a strong presumption of ownership

Possession creates a prima facie presumption of ownership, enforceable against everyone except the true owner.

Report this question

8The possessor of a thing is protected even against the true owner in some circumstances. This protection is justified by which theory?

  1. Will theory
  2. Interest theory
  3. Possessory title theory
  4. Absolute right theory
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Possessory title theory

Possessory title theory: possession itself creates a title good against all but the true owner.

Report this question

9'Mediate possession' means:

  1. Direct physical control of a thing
  2. Possession through an intermediary (agent/bailee)
  3. Possession by court order
  4. Joint possession of two persons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Possession through an intermediary (agent/bailee)

Mediate possession = possession exercised through an agent, servant or bailee.

Report this question

10'Incorporeal ownership' relates to:

  1. Ownership of land
  2. Ownership of tangible movables
  3. Ownership of intangible rights like patents, copyrights
  4. Ownership of government property
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Ownership of intangible rights like patents, copyrights

Incorporeal ownership is ownership over intangible things such as intellectual property rights.

Report this question

11When ownership and possession are with different persons, courts generally favour:

  1. The owner always
  2. The possessor always
  3. The owner unless another person is a bona fide purchaser for value
  4. The State
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The owner unless another person is a bona fide purchaser for value

Generally ownership prevails, but a bona fide purchaser for value without notice is an important exception.

Report this question

12'Vested ownership' is different from 'contingent ownership' in that:

  1. Vested ownership depends on a future condition
  2. Vested ownership is immediate and unconditional
  3. Contingent ownership is more secure
  4. Both are the same in law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Vested ownership is immediate and unconditional

Vested ownership = immediate, unconditional. Contingent ownership = depends on a future uncertain event.

Report this question

13Assertion (A): Possession is protected even against the true owner in some cases. Reason (R): Possession creates prima facie evidence of ownership.

  1. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are correct but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is correct but R is wrong
  4. R is correct but A is wrong
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A

Possession is protected because it creates prima facie evidence of ownership and protects social order.

Report this question

14'Possessory lien' is a right to:

  1. Retain goods in one's possession until a debt is paid
  2. Transfer possession of goods
  3. Sell goods without the owner's consent
  4. Register a claim to property
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Retain goods in one's possession until a debt is paid

Possessory lien = right to retain another's property until a debt is discharged.

Report this question

15Which of the following is an example of 'constructive possession'?

  1. Physically carrying goods in one's hands
  2. Bailee holding goods for the bailor
  3. Holding a key to a warehouse containing goods
  4. Thief holding stolen property
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Holding a key to a warehouse containing goods

Constructive possession = legal possession without direct physical control, e.g., holding the key to a warehouse.

Report this question

16'Adverse possession' means:

  1. Possession with the consent of the true owner
  2. Hostile, open, continuous possession for a prescribed period that may extinguish the original owner's title
  3. Possession by government of private land
  4. Temporary possession for a specific purpose
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Hostile, open, continuous possession for a prescribed period that may extinguish the original owner's title

Adverse possession: hostile, open, continuous possession for the statutory period extinguishes the original owner's title.

Report this question

17'Immediate possession' and 'mediate possession' are concepts discussed by:

  1. Savigny
  2. Austin
  3. Salmond
  4. Kelsen
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Salmond

Salmond distinguished immediate possession (direct physical control) from mediate possession (through agent/bailee).

Report this question

18'Jus in re aliena' means:

  1. Right of ownership over one's own property
  2. Right over another's property (lesser rights like easement, mortgage, lease)
  3. Right to inherit property
  4. Right to transfer property
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Right over another's property (lesser rights like easement, mortgage, lease)

Jus in re aliena = encumbrances on another's property; includes easements, mortgages, leases.

Report this question

19'Co-ownership' or joint ownership is characterised by:

  1. One owner with exclusive rights
  2. Sequential ownership
  3. Temporary ownership by contract
  4. Multiple persons each having equal undivided share in the same property
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Multiple persons each having equal undivided share in the same property

Co-ownership = multiple persons holding undivided shares in the same property simultaneously.

Report this question

20Austin's definition of ownership includes the following rights EXCEPT:

  1. Right to create obligations on third parties without consent
  2. Right to use
  3. Right to exclude others
  4. Right to destroy
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Right to create obligations on third parties without consent

Austin's incidents of ownership: right to use indefinitely, manage, exclude others, alienate. Creating obligations on third parties is not included.

Report this question

21The Roman law maxim 'superficies solo cedit' means:

  1. Water rights belong to the State
  2. The possessor has better title than the stranger
  3. Whatever is affixed to the soil belongs to the soil/owner of land
  4. Property follows the person
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Whatever is affixed to the soil belongs to the soil/owner of land

Superficies solo cedit: whatever is built on land belongs to the landowner — the fixture follows the land.

Report this question

22Assertion (A): Ihering's possession theory requires both corpus and animus with equal emphasis. Reason (R): Ihering treated animus as a social/legal presumption rather than a subjective mental element.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation
  3. A is false but R is true
  4. Both A and R are false
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A is false but R is true

A is false — Ihering did NOT require equal emphasis on both. R is true — he treated animus as presumed.
