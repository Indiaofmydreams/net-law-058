This set has 23 multiple-choice questions on Understanding the Structure of Arguments; Deductive and Inductive Reasoning from the UGC NET Paper 1 syllabus, unit 6, Logical Reasoning. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key. This set is an answer-key drill: check each answer, then revisit the notes for any you miss.

1If one premise is negative, the conclusion must be:

  1. Universal
  2. Affirmative
  3. Negative
  4. Particular
Show answer

Answer: C. Negative

2From two particular premises:

  1. A universal conclusion follows
  2. An A conclusion follows
  3. A valid conclusion always follows
  4. No valid conclusion follows

Answer: D. No valid conclusion follows

  1. Appeal to force
  2. Undistributed middle
  3. Ad hominem

16Post hoc ergo propter hoc assumes that:

  1. The speaker is unreliable
  2. The majority is right

  1. Ad hominem
