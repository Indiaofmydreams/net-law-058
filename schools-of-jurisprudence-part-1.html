About this question set

This set has 19 multiple-choice questions on Schools of Jurisprudence from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 1, Jurisprudence. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 19 of the 19 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Jurisprudence notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

19 practice questions with answers

1The Analytical School of Jurisprudence is also known as:

  1. Natural Law School
  2. Historical School
  3. Sociological School
  4. Imperative School
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Imperative School

The Analytical School is called the Imperative School because law is seen as a command (imperative) of the sovereign.

Report this question

2Austin's three elements of positive law are:

  1. Command, Sovereign, Sanction
  2. Duty, Right, Sanction
  3. Custom, Precedent, Legislation
  4. Reason, Nature, Justice
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Command, Sovereign, Sanction

Austin: law = command + issued by sovereign + backed by sanction.

Report this question

3Who propounded the 'Pure Theory of Law'?

  1. Austin
  2. Hart
  3. Kelsen
  4. Roscoe Pound
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Kelsen

Hans Kelsen propounded the Pure Theory of Law.

Report this question

4According to Kelsen, 'Grundnorm' means:

  1. The basic/fundamental norm from which all other norms derive validity
  2. Supreme court judgment
  3. Constitutional provision
  4. Sovereign command
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The basic/fundamental norm from which all other norms derive validity

The Grundnorm is the presupposed basic norm that validates all other legal norms in the hierarchy.

Report this question

5Kelsen's Pure Theory of Law is called 'pure' because:

  1. It is based on natural justice
  2. It is free from politics, ethics, and sociology
  3. It relies only on customs
  4. It is derived from divine law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. It is free from politics, ethics, and sociology

Kelsen wanted to establish law as an autonomous normative science, free from all non-legal elements.

Report this question

6'Law is a command of the sovereign' — this definition is given by:

  1. Kelsen
  2. Austin
  3. Hart
  4. Salmond
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Austin

This is Austin's classic definition from his command/imperative theory.

Report this question

7Hart's 'Rule of Recognition' is a:

  1. Primary rule
  2. Grundnorm
  3. Customary rule
  4. Secondary rule
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Secondary rule

Hart's secondary rules include: Rule of Recognition, Rule of Change, and Rule of Adjudication.

Report this question

8Which jurist criticised Austin's command theory by pointing out it cannot apply to constitutional law, customary law, and international law?

  1. Kelsen
  2. H.L.A. Hart
  3. Roscoe Pound
  4. Savigny
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. H.L.A. Hart

Hart's critique of Austin showed that command theory fails for power-conferring rules and other types of law.

Report this question

9Savigny's Historical School developed in reaction to:

  1. The Natural Law revival
  2. Bentham's utilitarianism
  3. The codification movement in Germany
  4. Roman Law system
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The codification movement in Germany

Savigny opposed Thibaut's proposal for German codification, arguing law must grow organically from the people.

Report this question

10Henry Maine's famous work 'Ancient Law' (1861) introduced the idea of movement of progressive societies from:

  1. Status to Contract
  2. Contract to Status
  3. Custom to Legislation
  4. Morality to Law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Status to Contract

Maine's famous thesis: progressive societies move from status (fixed by birth) to contract (freely negotiated).

Report this question

11Roscoe Pound's concept of 'social engineering' means:

  1. Using law to build infrastructure
  2. Balancing competing social interests through law
  3. Enforcing sovereign commands
  4. Codifying customs into statute
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Balancing competing social interests through law

Pound viewed law as a tool for social engineering — balancing and reconciling competing interests.

Report this question

12Roscoe Pound classified interests into how many categories?

  1. Three
  2. Two
  3. Four
  4. Five
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Three

Pound's three categories: Individual interests, Public interests, and Social interests.

Report this question

13'Law is a living thing and it mirrors the society' — this view best reflects:

  1. Analytical School
  2. Natural Law School
  3. Realist School
  4. Sociological School
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Sociological School

The Sociological School views law as a social phenomenon that must reflect and serve social needs.

Report this question

14Oliver Wendell Holmes said: 'The prophecies of what courts will do in fact, and nothing more pretentious, are what I mean by the law.' This statement belongs to:

  1. Realist School
  2. Natural Law School
  3. Analytical School
  4. Historical School
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Realist School

Holmes' statement is the classic expression of American Legal Realism.

Report this question

15The Realist School is divided into American Realism and Scandinavian Realism. Who among the following belongs to Scandinavian Realism?

  1. Jerome Frank
  2. Karl Llewellyn
  3. Axel Hägerström
  4. Oliver Wendell Holmes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Axel Hägerström

Axel Hägerström founded Scandinavian Realism, which focused on the psychological reality of law.

Report this question

16Lon Fuller's 'Inner Morality of Law' (Procedural Natural Law) requires eight principles of legality. Which of the following is NOT one of them?

  1. Laws must be general
  2. Laws must be promulgated
  3. Laws must be congruent with official action
  4. Laws must be retrospective
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Laws must be retrospective

Fuller requires laws to be prospective (not retrospective). Retrospective laws violate the inner morality of law.

Report this question

17Which school of jurisprudence treats law as a 'social fact'?

  1. Natural Law
  2. Sociological
  3. Analytical
  4. Historical
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Sociological

Sociological jurisprudence treats law as a social fact embedded in social relations.

Report this question

18Match the following jurists with their schools: Austin — Savigny — Pound — Holmes

  1. Analytical, Historical, Sociological, Realist
  2. Historical, Analytical, Realist, Sociological
  3. Analytical, Sociological, Historical, Realist
  4. Realist, Analytical, Sociological, Historical
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Analytical, Historical, Sociological, Realist

Austin=Analytical, Savigny=Historical, Pound=Sociological, Holmes=Realist.

Report this question

19Dworkin's critique of Hart argued that law also includes:

  1. Only rules
  2. Principles and policies in addition to rules
  3. Only sovereign commands
  4. Only customs
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Principles and policies in addition to rules

Dworkin argued in 'Taking Rights Seriously' that law includes principles (binding) and policies, not just rules.
