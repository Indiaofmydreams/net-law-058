§Overview#

A letter series follows the same logic as a number series, but the terms are letters. The one skill you need is converting letters to their positions in the alphabet (A = 1 to Z = 26) and then treating the problem like a number series.

3.2Memory aids (EJOTY and friends)#

In brief

Covers EJOTY, FKPUZ, ADGJMPSVY, Vowels A, E, I, O, U.

  • EJOTY
  • FKPUZ
  • ADGJMPSVY
  • Vowels A, E, I, O, U

  • EJOTY = E 5, J 10, O 15, T 20, Y 25. These are the multiples of 5, so you can find any nearby letter in two steps (N is one before O, so N = 14).

  • FKPUZ = 6, 11, 16, 21, 26.

  • ADGJMPSVY = every third letter from A: positions 1, 4, 7, 10, 13, 16, 19, 22, 25.

  • Vowels A, E, I, O, U sit at positions 1, 5, 9, 15, 21.

  • The first half of the alphabet is A to M (1 to 13) and the second half is N to Z (14 to 26).

3.3Opposite (mirror) letters#

In brief

In the mirror alphabet A pairs with Z, B with Y, and so on.

  • Position formulas

In the mirror alphabet A pairs with Z, B with Y, and so on. The positions of every opposite pair add up to 27.

A-Z B-Y C-X D-W E-V F-U G-T H-S I-R J-Q K-P L-O M-N
1+26 2+25 3+24 4+23 5+22 6+21 7+20 8+19 9+18 10+17 11+16 12+15 13+14

Position formulas

  • Opposite of a letter at position p = 27 - p.

  • Position from the right end = 27 - position from the left end. So the 7th letter from the right is 27 - 7 = 20th from the left, which is T.

  • Wrap-around: after Z comes A again. If a calculation goes above 26, subtract 26; if it goes below 1, add 26. Example: X (24) + 5 = 29, minus 26 = 3 = C.

3.5Position and arrangement questions#

In brief

Covers Alphabetical rearrangement, Pairs with equal gaps, Letters between two letters.

  • Alphabetical rearrangement
  • Pairs with equal gaps
  • Letters between two letters

  • Letter to the right or left of a given letter: convert to a number, add or subtract, then convert back. The 5th letter to the right of the 13th letter from the left is the 18th letter, R.

  • Alphabetical rearrangement: arrange the letters of the word alphabetically and count how many stay in the same place. For DREAM the alphabetical order is A, D, E, M, R, and only E keeps its position, so the answer is 1.

  • Pairs with equal gaps: for each pair of letters in a word, compare the gap in the word with the gap in the alphabet, which means list the pairs systematically instead of guessing.

  • Letters between two letters: the number of letters between positions p and q is |p - q| - 1.

3.6Worked examples#

In brief

Example 1.

Example 1. B, D, G, K, P, ?

  • Positions: 2, 4, 7, 11, 16. Differences: 2, 3, 4, 5, so the next difference is 6.

  • 16 + 6 = 22, which is V.

Example 2. Find the opposite letter of K, then the letter 4 places after it.

  • K is 11, so its opposite is 27 - 11 = 16 = P.

  • Four places after P gives 20 = T.

Example 3. AB, CD, EF, GH, ?

  • Each pair is two consecutive letters and the pairs continue in order: IJ.

3.7Tricks#

In brief

Always write the position numbers under the letters before looking for a pattern. If there are two jumps alternating (for example +1, +3, +1, +3), treat them as one…

  • Always write the position numbers under the letters before looking for a pattern.

  • If there are two jumps alternating (for example +1, +3, +1, +3), treat them as one repeating cycle.

  • For long letter strings check whether the pattern repeats after a fixed number of letters (cyclic pattern) and fill the blanks by repetition.

  • For "opposite letter" questions use the pairs in 4.3; memorising the 13 pairs takes five minutes.

  • Forgetting wrap-around after Z.

  • Using the number of letters between two letters as the position difference. The difference is one more than the number of letters between them.
