15.1Plane figures#

In brief

Figure Area Perimeter or other Rectangle (l, b) l x b 2(l + b); diagonal = square root of (l squared + b squared) Square (side a) a squared 4a; diagonal = a x square root of…

Figure Area Perimeter or other
Rectangle (l, b) l x b 2(l + b); diagonal = square root of (l squared + b squared)
Square (side a) a squared 4a; diagonal = a x square root of 2
Triangle (base b, height h) 1/2 x b x h Heron: square root of [s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)], s = (a + b + c)/2
Equilateral triangle (side a) (square root of 3/4) x a squared 3a
Right triangle 1/2 x base x height hypotenuse squared = base squared + height squared
Parallelogram base x height 2(sum of adjacent sides)
Rhombus 1/2 x d1 x d2 4 x side
Trapezium 1/2 x (sum of parallel sides) x height sum of all sides
Circle (radius r) pi x r squared circumference 2 x pi x r
Sector (angle theta) (theta/360) x pi x r squared arc = (theta/360) x 2 x pi x r
Semicircle 1/2 x pi x r squared pi x r + 2r

Use pi = 22/7 when the radius or diameter is a multiple of 7, otherwise use 3.14 or 3.1416 as the question says.

Pythagorean triples to memorise: (3, 4, 5), (5, 12, 13), (8, 15, 17), (7, 24, 25), (20, 21, 29), (9, 40, 41), and their multiples (6, 8, 10), (9, 12, 15), (10, 24, 26).

15.2Solids#

  • Scaling rule
  • Polygon angles
Solid Volume Surface area
Cube (edge a) a cubed total 6a squared; diagonal a x square root of 3
Cuboid (l, b, h) l x b x h total 2(lb + bh + hl); diagonal = square root of (l squared + b squared + h squared)
Cylinder (r, h) pi x r squared x h curved 2 x pi x r x h; total 2 x pi x r (r + h)
Cone (r, h, slant l) 1/3 x pi x r squared x h curved pi x r x l; total pi x r (r + l); l = square root of (r squared + h squared)
Sphere (r) 4/3 x pi x r cubed 4 x pi x r squared
Hemisphere (r) 2/3 x pi x r cubed curved 2 x pi x r squared; total 3 x pi x r squared

Scaling rule: if every length of a figure is multiplied by k, areas are multiplied by k squared and volumes by k cubed. Doubling the radius of a circle makes the area 4 times as large; doubling the radius of a sphere makes the volume 8 times as large.

Polygon angles: sum of interior angles of an n-sided polygon = (n - 2) x 180 degrees. Each interior angle of a regular polygon = (n - 2) x 180/n degrees. The sum of exterior angles is 360 degrees.

15.3Mensuration examples#

In brief

Covers 1540, 15 pi, 38,808.

  • 1540
  • 15 pi
  • 12 pi
  • 38,808
  • 5,544

  • A rectangle of 12 by 5 has diagonal = square root of (144 + 25) = 13.

  • A cylinder with r = 7 and h = 10 has volume = 22/7 x 49 x 10 = 1540.

  • A cone with r = 3 and h = 4 has slant height 5, curved surface = pi x 3 x 5 = 15 pi, and volume = 1/3 x pi x 9 x 4 = 12 pi.

  • A sphere of radius 21 has volume = 4/3 x 22/7 x 9261 = 38,808 and surface area = 4 x 22/7 x 441 = 5,544.

15.4Permutations and combinations#

In brief

Covers 34,650.

  • Examples
  • 34,650
Concept Formula or rule
Fundamental principle "And" (one task after another) multiplies the ways; "or" (alternative tasks) adds the ways
Factorial n! = n x (n - 1) x ... x 1, with 0! = 1
Permutation (order matters) nPr = n!/(n - r)!
Combination (order does not matter) nCr = n!/[r!(n - r)!]
Symmetry and identities nCr = nC(n - r); nC0 = nCn = 1; nCr + nC(r - 1) = (n + 1)Cr
Arrangements with repeated items n!/(p! x q! x r!) for p, q, r identical items
Circular arrangements (n - 1)! (or (n - 1)!/2 for a necklace or garland)
Items that must stay together Treat them as one block, then multiply by the arrangements inside the block
Handshakes among n people nC2
Diagonals of an n-sided polygon n(n - 3)/2

Examples

  • Number of 3-digit numbers from the digits 1 to 5 without repetition = 5P3 = 60.

  • Ways to choose 3 men from 5 and 2 women from 4 = 5C3 x 4C2 = 10 x 6 = 60.

  • Arrangements of the letters of LEVEL = 5!/(2! x 2!) = 30.

  • Arrangements of the letters of MISSISSIPPI = 11!/(4! x 4! x 2!) = 34,650.

  • 5 people in a row with two particular people always together: 4! x 2! = 48.

  • Handshakes among 10 people = 10C2 = 45. Diagonals of a hexagon = 6 x 3/2 = 9.

15.5Probability#

In brief

Covers 7/8, 5/36, 7/13, 5/14.

  • Standard sample spaces to know
  • Examples
  • 7/8
  • 5/36
  • 7/13
  • 5/14
Concept Formula or rule
Probability of an event P(E) = favourable outcomes/total outcomes, always between 0 and 1
Complement P(not E) = 1 - P(E)
Addition rule P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B)
Mutually exclusive events P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B)
Independent events P(A and B) = P(A) x P(B)
Conditional probability P(A given B) = P(A and B)/P(B)
Odds in favour of E Favourable : unfavourable

Standard sample spaces to know

  • One coin has 2 outcomes; n coins have 2 to the power n. One die has 6 outcomes; two dice have 36.

  • A deck has 52 cards: 4 suits of 13, 26 red and 26 black, 12 face cards (J, Q, K), and 4 aces.

  • Two dice sum to 7 in 6 ways (probability 1/6) and to 12 in 1 way (probability 1/36).

Examples

  • At least one head in 3 tosses = 1 - 1/8 = 7/8.

  • Sum of 8 on two dice = (2,6), (3,5), (4,4), (5,3), (6,2) = 5/36.

  • A red card or a king = 26/52 + 4/52 - 2/52 = 7/13.

  • A bag has 5 red and 3 blue balls, and 2 are drawn without replacement. Both are red with probability 5C2/8C2 = 10/28 = 5/14.

15.6Traps#

In brief

Using permutation when order does not matter, or combination when it does. Forgetting the complement shortcut for "at least one" problems. Forgetting to subtract the overlap…

  • Using permutation when order does not matter, or combination when it does.

  • Forgetting the complement shortcut for "at least one" problems.

  • Forgetting to subtract the overlap in addition-rule problems (a red king counted twice).

  • Using 22/7 when the radius is not a multiple of 7.
