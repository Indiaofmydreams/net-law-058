§Overview#

The average (arithmetic mean) is the sum of all observations divided by the number of observations. Average questions almost always reduce to one idea: sum = average x number of items. Age problems use the same idea along with the fact that the gap between two ages never changes.

12.2Properties#

In brief

The average always lies between the smallest and the largest value. If every item rises by k, the average rises by k. If every item is multiplied by k, the average is…

  • The average always lies between the smallest and the largest value.

  • If every item rises by k, the average rises by k. If every item is multiplied by k, the average is multiplied by k.

  • The sum of deviations from the average is always zero.

Example 1. The average of 5 numbers is 27. One number is excluded and the average of the rest becomes 25. Find the excluded number.

  • Total = 5 x 27 = 135. New total = 4 x 25 = 100.

  • Excluded number = 135 - 100 = 35.

Example 2. 30 students have an average mark of 40 and 20 students have an average mark of 60. Find the combined average.

  • (30 x 40 + 20 x 60)/50 = (1200 + 1200)/50 = 48.

Example 3. Ravi's average over 5 tests is 72, and after the 6th test it is 75. Find his 6th score.

  • Total after 6 tests = 6 x 75 = 450. Total after 5 tests = 5 x 72 = 360.

  • 6th score = 90.

Example 4. The average of 10 numbers is 15. One number was read as 26 instead of 36. Find the correct average.

  • Correct sum = 150 + 10 = 160. Correct average = 16.

Example 5. The average of 8 persons rises by 2 when one person of weight 65 is replaced by a new person. Find the new person's weight.

  • New person = 65 + 8 x 2 = 81.

Example 6. The average of 10 numbers is 20. Another number is added and the average becomes 21. Find the new number.

  • New number = 20 + 11 x 1 = 31. (Check: (200 + 31)/11 = 21.)

Example 7. A car goes uphill at 40 km/h and returns downhill over the same distance at 60 km/h. Find the average speed.

  • 2 x 40 x 60/(40 + 60) = 48 km/h.

12.4Ages: key ideas#

In brief

Covers difference.

  • difference

  • The difference between two people's ages stays the same for ever.

  • After t years every person's age increases by t, so the total for n people rises by n x t.

  • Express present ages as ratio parts (3k and 5k), form an equation with the condition given, and solve for k.

  • A person's age t years ago is present age - t. A person who is not yet born (for example a newborn) has age 0 at birth.

Example 8. The present ages of A and B are in the ratio 3 : 5. After 6 years the ratio becomes 9 : 13. Find their present ages.

  • (3k + 6)/(5k + 6) = 9/13, so 39k + 78 = 45k + 54, which gives 6k = 24 and k = 4.

  • A = 12 years, B = 20 years. (After 6 years: 18 and 26, which is 9 : 13.)

Example 9. A father is four times as old as his son. After 5 years he will be three times as old. Find their present ages.

  • Let the son be x and the father 4x. Then 4x + 5 = 3(x + 5), so x = 10.

  • Son = 10 years, father = 40 years.

Example 10. The average age of 5 family members is 24 years. The youngest, aged 8, leaves. Find the average age of the remaining four.

  • Total = 120. After the youngest leaves: 112. Average = 28 years.

12.6Tricks#

In brief

Covers Assumed mean (deviation method), Use the change-in-total idea, Use equal steps for consecutive numbers, Age gap is fixed.

  • Use the change-in-total idea
  • Age gap is fixed

  • Assumed mean (deviation method): pick a convenient value near the data, add the deviations, divide by the count and adjust. Marks 78, 82, 85, 79, 76 with an assumed mean of 80 have deviations -2, +2, +5, -1, -4 (sum 0), so the average is exactly 80.

  • Use the change-in-total idea: the change in total = (number of items) x (change in average).

  • Use equal steps for consecutive numbers: the middle number is the average, so five consecutive numbers with an average of 15 are 13, 14, 15, 16, 17.

  • Age gap is fixed: use the gap, which does not change, to simplify problems.
