§Overview#

Work problems become easy once you think in terms of rates. If someone completes a job in x days, that person does 1/x of the job per day, and rates (not times) are what you add.

Concept Formula
One day's work of a person who needs x days 1/x
Work = rate x time Work = efficiency x time
Efficiency and time Inversely proportional for the same work
Two persons together (x and y days) xy/(x + y) days
Three persons together (x, y, z days) 1/(1/x + 1/y + 1/z) days
A and B together take x days, A alone takes y days B alone takes xy/(y - x) days
If A is n times as efficient as B A takes 1/n of the time B takes
Men-days (compound work) (M1 x D1 x H1)/W1 = (M2 x D2 x H2)/W2
Sharing wages In the ratio of the work done by each (efficiency x time)

13.2The LCM (efficiency) method#

In brief

  • Example
  • 6 days

  1. Take the total work as the LCM of the days given.

  2. Divide the total work by each person's days to get that person's efficiency (units per day).

  3. Add efficiencies for people working together, and subtract for those who undo the work.

  4. Time = total work / combined efficiency.

Example. A takes 10 days and B takes 15 days. LCM = 30 units. A does 3 units a day, B does 2 units a day, together 5 units a day, so the job takes 30/5 = 6 days.

13.3Pipes and cisterns#

In brief

Covers inlet pipe.

  • inlet pipe
  • outlet pipe
  • leak

  • An inlet pipe fills the tank (positive work). An outlet pipe or leak empties it (negative work).

  • Net rate = sum of the inlet rates - sum of the outlet rates.

  • If the net rate is negative, the tank empties, and the time to empty is 1/|net rate|.

  • A leak: if a tank fills in x hours normally but y hours with a leak (y larger than x), the leak alone empties a full tank in xy/(y - x) hours.

13.4Worked examples#

In brief

Example 1.

  • Example 2
  • 10 days

  • 5 more days
  • Example 4 (alternate days)

Example 1. A can do a job in 12 days. A and B together do it in 8 days. How long does B alone take?

  • xy/(y - x) = 8 x 12/(12 - 8) = 24 days. (LCM method: with a total of 24, A does 2 and the pair does 3 a day, so B does 1 a day.)

Example 2. A and B can finish a job in 12 days, B and C in 15 days, and C and A in 20 days. How long will A, B and C take together?

  • 1/12 + 1/15 + 1/20 = (5 + 4 + 3)/60 = 12/60 = 1/5. This is twice the combined rate of the three.

  • A + B + C do 1/10 per day, so they take 10 days.

Example 3. A takes 10 days and B takes 15 days. They start together, but B leaves after 3 days. How long does A take to finish the rest?

  • Together they do 1/10 + 1/15 = 1/6 per day, so in 3 days they finish 1/2.

  • The remaining 1/2 takes A alone 5 days. Total time is 3 + 5 = 8 days, and A works 5 more days.

Example 4 (alternate days). A takes 10 days and B takes 15 days, working on alternate days and starting with A. How long does the job take?

  • In every two days they finish 1/10 + 1/15 = 1/6.

  • Six such pairs finish exactly 1 job, so it takes 12 days.

Example 5 (wages). A takes 10 days and B takes 15 days, and together they earn 5000. How should it be split?

  • Efficiency ratio = 3 : 2. A gets 3000 and B gets 2000.

Example 6 (pipes). Pipe A fills a tank in 12 hours, pipe B in 18 hours, and pipe C empties it in 36 hours. All are opened together. When is the tank full?

  • Net rate = 1/12 + 1/18 - 1/36 = (3 + 2 - 1)/36 = 4/36 = 1/9. The tank fills in 9 hours.

Example 7 (men-days). 10 men finish a job in 12 days. How many men are needed to finish it in 8 days?

  • Men x days is constant: 10 x 12/8 = 15 men.

Example 8 (efficiency). A is twice as good a worker as B, and together they finish a job in 12 days. How long does B alone take?

  • Let B take x days, so A takes x/2. Then 1/x + 2/x = 3/x = 1/12, so x = 36. B takes 36 days and A takes 18 days.

13.5Tricks#

In brief

Covers Choose the total work as the LCM, "A is 25% more efficient than B", Alternate days, Dividing money, Pipes.

  • Alternate days
  • Dividing money
  • Pipes

  • Choose the total work as the LCM to avoid fractions.

  • "A is 25% more efficient than B" means the efficiency ratio is 5 : 4, so the time ratio is 4 : 5.

  • Alternate days: compute the work done in one full cycle first, then see how many cycles are needed and how the last partial cycle ends.

  • Dividing money: the wage share is proportional to work done, not simply to days worked.

  • Pipes: convert hours and minutes to the same unit and treat every pipe as a signed rate.
