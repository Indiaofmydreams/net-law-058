10.2Memory aids#

In brief

Covers Data to wisdom ladder, NOIR, Histogram, Line chart, Questionnaire and more.

  • Data to wisdom ladder
  • NOIR
  • Histogram
  • Bar chart
  • Line chart
  • Pie chart
  • Scatter plot
  • Ogive crossing point

  • Data to wisdom ladder: Data, Information, Knowledge, Wisdom.

  • NOIR: Nominal, Ordinal, Interval, Ratio, in rising order of power.

  • Parameter and Population, Statistic and Sample: P goes with P, S goes with S.

  • Histogram: continuous data, no gaps. Bar chart: categories, gaps.

  • Line chart: time trend. Pie chart: share of a whole. Scatter plot: relationship. Ogive crossing point: median.

  • Questionnaire: the respondent fills. Schedule: the investigator fills.

  • Census: whole population. Sample: a part, with sampling error.

  • 5 Vs of big data: Volume, Velocity, Variety, Veracity, Value.

  • G2C, G2B, G2G, G2E: government to citizen, business, government, employee.

  • FAIR data: Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable.

  • Percentage change always divides by the old value.

10.3Pairs the examiner likes to contrast#

In brief

Pair Key difference Primary and secondary data First-hand and original, against previously collected Quantitative and qualitative data Numbers and measurement, against…

Pair Key difference
Primary and secondary data First-hand and original, against previously collected
Quantitative and qualitative data Numbers and measurement, against descriptions and categories
Discrete and continuous variable Separate whole values, against any value in a range
Questionnaire and schedule Self-filled, against interviewer-filled
Census and sample Complete enumeration, against a selected part
Probability and non-probability sampling Known chance of selection, against no known chance
Bar chart and histogram Gaps and categories, against no gaps and continuous classes
Exclusive and inclusive classes Upper limit excluded, against both limits included
Cross-sectional and time series data Many units at one time, against one unit over time
Parameter and statistic Population measure, against sample measure
Percentage and percentage point Relative change, against simple difference
Data and information Raw facts, against processed meaning
Open data and big data Freely shared data, against very large and varied data
Digital divide and digital literacy Gap in access and use, against skill to use ICT

From the pattern of how these topics are usually tested, the following are the areas where a direct concept question is most likely. This is a judgement based on the syllabus wording and typical questions, not a count from a particular year.

10.5Rapid-fire true and false#

In brief

Covers False, False, False, False, False and more.

  • False
  • True

  • Mean is not affected by extreme values. False: it is affected; the median is not.

  • A histogram has gaps between bars. False: the bars touch.

  • A pie chart suits time series. False: use a line chart.

  • Secondary data are collected first-hand. False: that is primary data.

  • Temperature in Celsius is a ratio scale. False: it is an interval scale.

  • Mode can be absent in a data set. True.

  • A rise from 40 per cent to 50 per cent is a 10 per cent increase. False: it is 10 percentage points and a 25 per cent increase.

  • In a symmetric distribution, mean, median and mode are equal. True.

  • A questionnaire needs a literate respondent. True.

  • A census always has sampling error. False: sampling error belongs to sample surveys (non-sampling errors can still occur).
