2.1The big picture#

In brief

Independence brought three tasks for higher education: expand access beyond a small elite, build institutions for science, technology and national development, and reorient…

  • expand
  • build
  • reorient
  • 1950s
  • 1960s
  • 1970s and 1980s
  • 1990s
  • 2000s

Independence brought three tasks for higher education: expand access beyond a small elite, build institutions for science, technology and national development, and reorient a colonial system towards Indian needs. The story unfolds in phases. The 1950s were the period of institution-building and the first commissions. The 1960s brought the Kothari Commission and the national policy framework. The 1970s and 1980s saw expansion, the 42nd Amendment (education moved to the Concurrent List in 1976), the open university movement and NPE 1986. The 1990s brought liberalisation, funding constraints and private participation. The 2000s focused on quality, the Knowledge Commission and the Yash Pal Committee. The 2010s and 2020s brought accreditation reforms, NEP 2020, credit-based flexibility, foreign campuses and the move towards a single regulator.

2.3UGC: the central regulator of the post-independence era#

In brief

The University Grants Commission began as an interim body in 1953 (inaugurated by Maulana Azad) and became a statutory body under the UGC Act, 1956 .

  • University Grants Commission
  • statutory body
  • UGC Act, 1956
  • Its three functions
  • Key sections to remember

The University Grants Commission began as an interim body in 1953 (inaugurated by Maulana Azad) and became a statutory body under the UGC Act, 1956. The original University Grants Committee of 1945 had been limited to Aligarh, Banaras and Delhi. The Radhakrishnan Commission's recommendation led to the present form. The UGC has a Chairperson, a Vice-Chairperson and ten other members. It is headquartered in New Delhi, with regional centres at places such as Pune, Bhopal, Kolkata, Hyderabad, Guwahati and Bengaluru.

Its three functions. (1) Funding: grants to eligible universities and colleges (through Sections 2(f) and 12(B)). (2) Coordination: among Centre, States and institutions. (3) Maintenance of standards: setting regulations on minimum qualifications, curricula frameworks, degrees and quality.

Key sections to remember. Section 2(f): definition of a university. Section 3: declaration of an institution as a "deemed to be university" (by the Central Government on UGC advice). Section 12(B): fitness for central grants. Section 22: only a university can confer degrees. A college must be listed under 2(f) and 12(B) to receive UGC development grants.

Related bodies created over time. NAAC (1994, Bengaluru; accreditation), NCERT (1961), NIEPA (planning and administration), INFLIBNET (1991; library networking), CEC (media, television), IUCAA and other inter-university centres, and various fellowship schemes (JRF and NET were introduced in the 1980s).

2.4Expansion and institutions#

In brief

Covers Centres of excellence, Agricultural universities, Open and distance learning, Private and deemed universities, Recent expansion schemes and more.

  • Centres of excellence
  • Agricultural universities
  • Open and distance learning
  • Recent expansion schemes
  • Scale

  • Centres of excellence. IIT Kharagpur (1951) was the first IIT; the IIT Act came in 1961. IIMs at Calcutta and Ahmedabad were founded in 1961. AIIMS Act 1956. IISc Bengaluru (1909) predates independence. IISERs began in 2006. NITs were created under the NIT Act 2007. IIITs (PPP) Act 2017. National law universities started with NLSIU Bengaluru (1987).

  • Agricultural universities. G. B. Pant University of Agriculture and Technology at Pantnagar (1960) was the first agricultural university; the idea came from the US land-grant model.

  • Open and distance learning. Correspondence courses began at Delhi University in 1962; Dr. B. R. Ambedkar Open University (1982) was the first open university; IGNOU followed in 1985.

  • Private and deemed universities. Growth accelerated after the 1990s and after the Supreme Court's Yash Pal v. State of Chhattisgarh (2005) and TMA Pai (2002) decisions.

  • Recent expansion schemes. RUSA (2013) for state institutions, merged into PM-USHA in 2023; Institutions of Eminence (2017).

  • Scale. According to the All India Survey on Higher Education for 2021 to 22, India had roughly 1,100 to 1,200 universities and over 45,000 colleges, with a Gross Enrolment Ratio of about 28 per cent. These figures change each year, so check the latest AISHE report.

Policy landmarks. Scientific Policy Resolution (1958), Technology Policy Statement (1983), Science, Technology and Innovation Policy (2013), Prime Minister's Research Fellowship (2018), IMPRINT (2015), GIAN (2015), SPARC (2018). The research system in India is concentrated in national labs and elite institutes, and universities contribute a smaller share. A recurring policy concern is that this separation weakens teaching-research integration, which NEP 2020 tries to address with research-intensive universities and the National Research Foundation.

★Memory Capsule: Evolution of Higher Learning and Research in Post-Independence India

Memorise

  • Commissions by the decade: 48 Radhakrishnan, 52 Mudaliar, 64 Kothari, 86 NPE, 2005 Knowledge Commission, 2009 Yash Pal, 2019 Kasturirangan.
  • Kothari = 10+2+3 and 6 per cent. The 42nd Amendment (1976) made education a Concurrent subject.
  • IIT 1951 (Kharagpur), IIT Act 1961, IIM 1961, AIIMS Act 1956, UGC Act 1956. Many items from the 1950s and 1960s.
  • UGC Act sections: 2(f) define, 3 deemed, 12(B) grants, 22 degrees.

★Analysis Angle: Evolution of Higher Learning and Research in Post-Independence India

Analyse / traps

  • Continuity. Every commission since 1948 has said the same few things: more funding, more autonomy, more access for disadvantaged groups, better teacher quality, and linking education with work. The differences are of emphasis. Radhakrishnan stressed quality and values; Kothari stressed national development and a system structure; NPE 1986 stressed equity and open learning; NKC and Yash Pal stressed regulation reform and autonomy; NEP 2020 stresses flexibility, multidisciplinarity and a single regulator.

  • Regulation story. UGC (1956) combined funding and regulation. Later bodies split specialised domains (AICTE, NCTE, NMC, BCI), creating overlap. The NKC (IRAHE), Yash Pal (NCHER), the 2018 draft HECI Act and NEP 2020 (HECI) all attempted to unify them, and the 2025 VBSA Bill continues that arc.

  • Funding. Kothari's 6 per cent target is still repeated in NPE 1986 and NEP 2020 but actual public spending has stayed well below it.
