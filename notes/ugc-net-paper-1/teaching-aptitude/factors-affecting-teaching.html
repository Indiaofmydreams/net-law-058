5.1The Big Picture#

In brief

Teaching effectiveness is not decided by the teacher alone; it emerges from the dynamic interaction of four sets of factors: teacher-related, learner-related, content and…

Teaching effectiveness is not decided by the teacher alone; it emerges from the dynamic interaction of four sets of factors: teacher-related, learner-related, content and method-related, and institutional or environmental. Questions in this area usually reward the option that sees teaching as a system and reject options such as “teacher factors alone determine quality” or “institutional factors override all others.”

5.2Teacher-Related Factors#

In brief

Subject knowledge and pedagogical skill: a teacher must know the content and how to teach it.

  • Communication ability
  • Personality and attitude
  • Teacher expectations
  • Pygmalion (Rosenthal) effect
  • Rosenthal and Jacobson
  • Adaptive teaching

Subject knowledge and pedagogical skill: a teacher must know the content and how to teach it. Communication ability: clarity, voice, language, questioning and listening. Personality and attitude: enthusiasm, warmth, patience, fairness and sense of humour create a climate of trust. Professional commitment and motivation: a motivated teacher motivates learners. Experience and reflective practice: the teacher who reflects on lessons improves. Teacher expectations: high expectations tend to raise performance, low ones lower it. This is the Pygmalion (Rosenthal) effect: in the 1968 study by Rosenthal and Jacobson at an elementary school, teachers were told that certain randomly chosen children were “bloomers” about to show intellectual gains; those children actually improved more, showing that expectation changes teacher behaviour and thereby student outcomes. The reverse is the Golem effect. Adaptive teaching, the ability to adjust methods to learners’ needs, is a major teacher quality.

5.3Learner-Related Factors#

In brief

Prior knowledge, intelligence, readiness (physical, mental, emotional), motivation (intrinsic vs extrinsic), interest, attention, learning style, self-efficacy, health…

  • readiness
  • motivation

Prior knowledge, intelligence, readiness (physical, mental, emotional), motivation (intrinsic vs extrinsic), interest, attention, learning style, self-efficacy, health, emotional state, family background, language proficiency, and peer influence. The principle is that the learner is an active partner: teaching can only create conditions for learning, not force it.

5.4Content, Method and Material Factors#

In brief

The nature and difficulty of the content (abstract vs concrete), the logical organisation of material, the match between the method and the objective, availability of…

The nature and difficulty of the content (abstract vs concrete), the logical organisation of material, the match between the method and the objective, availability of teaching aids and technology, appropriate evaluation and sufficient time. A method that suits a psychomotor objective (demonstration) may be useless for a reflective objective (discussion or project).

5.5Institutional, Social and Environmental Factors#

In brief

Infrastructure (classroom, library, laboratory, internet), class size (very large classes reduce interaction), school climate and leadership, administrative support…

  • class size
  • classroom climate

Infrastructure (classroom, library, laboratory, internet), class size (very large classes reduce interaction), school climate and leadership, administrative support, curriculum flexibility, examination pressure, policy and regulation, community and parental involvement, and socio-economic conditions. The classroom climate (emotionally safe, democratic, respectful) strongly affects participation and learning. Studies of classroom climate include Lippitt and White’s experiment on authoritarian, democratic and laissez-faire leadership, which found democratic climates the most productive in the long run.

5.6Effective Teaching and Teacher Roles#

In brief

Modern thought views the teacher as a facilitator, guide, mentor, motivator, evaluator, researcher and reflective practitioner , not a mere transmitter.

  • Reflective teaching
  • action research

Modern thought views the teacher as a facilitator, guide, mentor, motivator, evaluator, researcher and reflective practitioner, not a mere transmitter. Characteristics of effective teaching: clear objectives, organised content, variety of methods, active involvement, regular feedback, high expectations, positive climate and efficient use of time. Reflective teaching (Donald Schon: reflection-in-action and reflection-on-action) and action research are tools for improving practice.

★Memory Capsule: Factors Affecting Teaching

Memorise

  • Four groups: teacher, learner, content and method, institutional and environmental.
  • Pygmalion effect = Rosenthal and Jacobson (1968), “bloomers”: teacher expectation changes student performance.
  • Adaptive teaching = a key teacher-related quality.
  • Climate study: Lippitt and White (authoritarian, democratic, laissez-faire).
  • Teacher roles: facilitator, guide, mentor, motivator, reflective practitioner.

★Analysis Angle: Factors Affecting Teaching

Analyse / traps

The correct answer in “factor” questions is generally the interactive one. If a question describes a teacher who behaves differently towards students believed to be bright and the results follow the expectation, the concept is the Pygmalion effect (a teacher-related factor that works through the learner). If a question asks what makes a class improve, look for options that combine teacher quality, learner readiness and a supportive environment. Beware of absolute words such as “alone”, “only”, “never” and “overtide all”: these usually mark wrong options.
