10.3Look-Alike Pairs and How to Separate Them#

In brief

Pair How to tell them apart Kyoto and Paris Kyoto: 1997, binding targets for developed countries only, top-down. Paris: 2015, every country submits NDCs, bottom-up Vienna…

Pair How to tell them apart
Kyoto and Paris Kyoto: 1997, binding targets for developed countries only, top-down. Paris: 2015, every country submits NDCs, bottom-up
Vienna and Montreal Vienna 1985 is the framework; Montreal 1987 is the protocol with phase-out schedules
Montreal and Kigali Montreal controls ozone-depleting substances; Kigali phases down HFCs, which are greenhouse gases
Cartagena and Nagoya Cartagena: biosafety and LMOs. Nagoya: access and benefit sharing of genetic resources
Rio and Rio+20 Rio 1992: Agenda 21, UNFCCC, CBD. Rio+20 2012: The Future We Want, SDG process
MDGs and SDGs 8 goals for mainly developing countries (2000 to 2015); 17 universal goals (2016 to 2030)
Water Act and Air Act Water 1974 created the boards; Air 1981 extended board powers to air; EP Act 1986 is the umbrella law
Richter and Mercalli Richter: magnitude (energy, logarithmic). Mercalli: intensity (observed effects)
Mitigation and preparedness Mitigation reduces risk beforehand; preparedness readies the response
Structural and non-structural Structural: embankments, shelters. Non-structural: zoning, warning, awareness
Bioaccumulation and biomagnification Within one organism over time; up the food chain across organisms
BOD and COD BOD: oxygen used by microbes. COD: oxygen for chemical oxidation
Primary and secondary pollutants Primary emitted directly (SO2, CO); secondary formed by reaction (ozone, PAN)
Weak and strong sustainability Weak: capital substitutable. Strong: some natural capital irreplaceable
PM10 and PM2.5 10 and 2.5 micrometre diameters; PM2.5 penetrates deeper
Chipko, Appiko, Silent Valley, Narmada Uttarakhand (trees), Karnataka (trees), Kerala (hydro project), Narmada valley (dams)
Jhum and Jal Jeevan Jhum is shifting cultivation in the north-east; Jal Jeevan is the tap water mission linked to SDG 6

10.4Cross-Topic Chains for Analysis Questions#

In brief

Fossil fuels: burning coal and oil gives CO2 (climate change, ocean acidification, sea level rise), SO2 and NOx (acid rain, respiratory disease), particulates (heart and…

  • Fossil fuels: burning coal and oil gives CO2 (climate change, ocean acidification, sea level rise), SO2 and NOx (acid rain, respiratory disease), particulates (heart and lung disease), and fly ash (soil and water). The response sits in SDG 7 and 13, the Paris Agreement, renewable energy, ISA and Bharat Stage norms.

  • Agriculture: fertilizers give nitrous oxide and nitrate runoff (eutrophication, blue baby syndrome), paddies and livestock give methane, irrigation gives salinity and groundwater loss, pesticides give biomagnification. The response is soil health cards, organic farming, zero tillage and efficient irrigation.

  • Forests: deforestation reduces carbon storage, increases erosion, floods and landslides, destroys habitat, and affects tribal livelihoods; the response is the Forest Conservation Act, Forest Rights Act, JFM, REDD+ and SDG 15.

  • Cities: impervious surfaces cause floods, vehicles and construction cause smog, waste causes landfill methane and leachate, and noise adds stress; the response is the Solid Waste Rules, NCAP, GRAP and SDG 11.

  • Hazards: vulnerability, not just the hazard, causes disasters; mitigation, early warning, building codes and community preparedness reduce risk under the DM Act and the Sendai Framework.

  • Law and treaties: the EP Act implements Stockholm and creates the rule-making framework; the CBD led to the Biological Diversity Act; Basel connects to the Hazardous Waste Rules; Montreal leads to the ODS Rules, and Paris connects with the NAPCC and India's NDC.

10.5Mnemonics and Memory Hooks#

In brief

Covers Environmental SDGs, Indian environmental laws in order, Sendai priorities, Metal and disease pairs, Seismic and tsunami and more.

  • Environmental SDGs
  • Sendai priorities
  • Metal and disease pairs
  • Seismic and tsunami
  • EP Act sections
  • Kyoto flexibility mechanisms

  • Environmental SDGs: 6, 7, 12, 13, 14, 15 (water, energy, consumption, climate, ocean, land)

  • Rio gave three documents (Rio Declaration, Agenda 21, Forest Principles) and opened two treaties (UNFCCC, CBD)

  • Indian environmental laws in order: Water 1974, Forest 1980, Air 1981, EP 1986

  • Sendai priorities: Understand, Govern, Invest, Prepare

  • Metal and disease pairs: mercury with Minamata, cadmium with Itai-itai, arsenic with arsenicosis and blackfoot disease, fluoride with fluorosis, nitrate with blue baby syndrome, lead with plumbism, silica with silicosis

  • Seismic and tsunami: Richter for magnitude, Mercalli for intensity, INCOIS for tsunami warning

  • EP Act sections: 3 powers, 5 directions, 6 rules, 7 standards, 8 hazardous handling, 15 penalty, 16 companies, 17 government departments

  • Kyoto flexibility mechanisms: ET, JI, CDM; Paris tools: NDC, Global Stocktake, Article 6
