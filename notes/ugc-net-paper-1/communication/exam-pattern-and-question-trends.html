0.1Exam pattern and where Communication fits#

In brief

Overall UGC NET pattern (current, as followed through the 2026 cycles):

  • Paper 1
  • 50 MCQs, 100 marks
  • Paper 2
  • There is no negative marking
  • 10 units
  • 5 questions per unit
  • Unit 4

Overall UGC NET pattern (current, as followed through the 2026 cycles):

  • The exam is a Computer-Based Test (CBT) with two papers taken in one continuous sitting of 3 hours (180 minutes) with no break between them.

  • Paper 1 (General Teaching and Research Aptitude, common for all subjects): 50 MCQs, 100 marks, i.e. 2 marks per question.

  • Paper 2 (your chosen subject): 100 MCQs, 200 marks.

  • Total: 150 questions, 300 marks. There is no negative marking. Attempt every question.

  • Paper 1 has 10 units with roughly 5 questions per unit. Communication is Unit 4; the others are Teaching Aptitude, Research Aptitude, Comprehension, Mathematical Reasoning, Logical Reasoning, Data Interpretation, ICT, People Development and Environment, and Higher Education System.

  • Always re-check the latest NTA information bulletin before applying, because NTA can alter pattern details between cycles.

Official syllabus of Unit 4 – Communication (the topic blocks you must cover):

  1. Communication: meaning, types and characteristics of communication

  2. Effective communication: verbal and non-verbal, inter-cultural and group communications, classroom communication

  3. Barriers to effective communication

  4. Mass media and society

Everything below is organised to cover each of these blocks plus the theory background (models, theorists, laws, institutions) that MCQs commonly draw from.

0.2Question trends: how the unit is actually tested#

In brief

Caution: the points below are patterns observed across past papers and coaching analyses, not official NTA statistics.

  • Caution
  • Number of questions
  • 5 per paper
  • Difficulty
  • Question formats you will see
  • Typical traps
  • Study priority

Caution: the points below are patterns observed across past papers and coaching analyses, not official NTA statistics. Use them as a guide to priorities, and verify against the last 8 to 10 years of previous-year papers.

  • Number of questions: usually about 5 per paper (sometimes 4 to 5). It is one of the more scoring units because it is theory-heavy and not calculation-heavy.

  • Difficulty: generally easy to moderate. Paper 1 in the June 2026 cycle was reported by coaching analyses as easy to moderate overall.

  • Highest-frequency areas (in rough order):

    1. Barriers to communication (types, examples, remedies)

    2. Classroom communication (teacher behaviour, feedback, interaction, Flanders)

    3. Verbal and non-verbal communication (kinesics, proxemics, paralanguage)

    4. Mass media and society (theories, media types, effects, Indian media institutions)

    5. Types and levels of communication (formal/informal, grapevine, directions)

    6. Models and elements of the communication process

    7. Inter-cultural and group communication (less frequent but recurring)

  • Question formats you will see:

    • Memory-based: "Who proposed X?", "Which is NOT an element of communication?", "Proxemics deals with...", "Grapevine is a type of..."

    • Concept-based: identify the barrier or type from a short scenario; match a definition to a term.

    • Analysis-based: statement I / statement II (true/false), assertion–reason, match-the-following (List I with List II), arrange in correct sequence, and "which of the following statements are correct" with combination options.

  • Typical traps: options that mix up similar terms (hypodermic needle vs two-step flow; agenda setting vs cultivation; encoding vs decoding; semantic vs psychological barrier; downward vs horizontal communication), and "NOT / EXCEPT" questions.

  • Study priority: master definitions + theorist names + one-line examples. For analysis questions, practise explaining why an option is wrong.
