1972UN Conference on the Human Environment, Stockholm (5–16 June); Stockholm Declaration (26 principles) and Action Plan (109 recommendations); creation of UNEP (HQ Nairobi); World Environment Day (5 June)
1982Nairobi (Stockholm +10); UN World Charter for Nature (UNGA Res. 37/7); UNCLOS signed at Montego Bay; Review of Stockholm; Charter recognised nature’s intrinsic value; UNCLOS Part XII deals with protection of the marine environment
1987Brundtland Commission report Our Common Future; Montreal Protocol; Defined sustainable development; ozone-depleting substances phased out
1992UN Conference on Environment and Development (UNCED), Rio de Janeiro (3–14 June), the “Earth Summit”; Rio Declaration (27 principles), Agenda 21, Forest Principles, UNFCCC and CBD opened for signature; Commission on Sustainable Development created
1997Rio +5; Kyoto Protocol adopted (COP3); Binding emission targets for developed countries
2002World Summit on Sustainable Development (WSSD), Johannesburg; Johannesburg Declaration and Plan of Implementation; three pillars of sustainable development: economic development, social development, environmental protection
2012UN Conference on Sustainable Development (Rio +20); Outcome document The Future We Want; green economy in the context of sustainable development; process leading to the SDGs; High-Level Political Forum replaced CSD
2015UN Sustainable Development Summit (September); COP21 Paris (December); 2030 Agenda with 17 SDGs and 169 targets; Paris Agreement
2022Stockholm +50 (June); UNEA-5.2 (March); Reaffirmed Stockholm; mandate to negotiate a plastics treaty
Ramsar Convention 1971Wetlands; In force 1975; “wise use” of wetlands; Montreux Record lists sites under threat
World Heritage Convention 1972Cultural and natural heritage; UNESCO
CITES 1973Trade in endangered species; In force 1975; Appendices I, II, III
Bonn Convention (CMS) 1979Migratory species
Vienna Convention for the Protection of the Ozone Layer 1985Ozone; Framework treaty
Montreal Protocol 1987Ozone-depleting substances; In force 1989; Kigali Amendment 2016 on HFCs (in force 2019)
Basel Convention 1989Transboundary movement of hazardous wastes; In force 1992; Prior Informed Consent; Ban Amendment 1995
UNFCCC 1992Climate change; In force 1994; objective is stabilisation of greenhouse gas concentrations; CBDR; COPs held annually
CBD 1992Biodiversity; Three objectives: conservation, sustainable use, fair and equitable sharing of benefits from genetic resources
UNCCD 1994Desertification; In force 1996
Kyoto Protocol 1997Climate; Entered into force 16 Feb 2005; binding targets for Annex I parties (about 5.2% below 1990 levels in 2008–12); flexibility mechanisms: Emissions Trading, Joint Implementation, Clean Development Mechanism
Rotterdam Convention 1998Hazardous chemicals and pesticides in trade; Prior Informed Consent procedure
Cartagena Protocol 2000Biosafety (living modified organisms); Under the CBD; in force 2003
Stockholm Convention 2001Persistent Organic Pollutants; In force 2004
Nagoya Protocol 2010Access and benefit sharing; In force 2014
Minamata Convention 2013Mercury; In force 2017
Paris Agreement 2015Climate; In force 4 Nov 2016; temperature goal “well below 2°C” and efforts towards 1.5°C; Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs); five-yearly global stocktake; Article 6 on market and non-market approaches; Article 8 loss and damage
Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework 2022Biodiversity; COP15 of the CBD; “30 by 30” target
BBNJ Agreement 2023Biodiversity beyond national jurisdiction (high seas); Adopted June 2023; reported to have entered into force in January 2026
Article 21Protection of life and personal liberty; Includes the right to a wholesome environment, pollution-free air and water, and sustainable development
Article 48A (DPSP)The State shall endeavour to protect and improve the environment and to safeguard the forests and wildlife of the country; Inserted by the 42nd Amendment 1976
Article 51A(g) (Fundamental Duty)Duty of every citizen to protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers and wildlife, and to have compassion for living creatures; Inserted by the 42nd Amendment 1976; courts use it with Art. 48A to interpret Art. 21
Article 47Duty of the State to raise the level of nutrition and standard of living and to improve public health; Used in water and pollution cases
Article 14Equality and non-arbitrariness; Used to challenge arbitrary clearances; M.K. Ranjitsinh (2024) links climate rights to Arts. 14 and 21
Article 19(1)(g) with 19(6)Freedom to carry on trade or business, subject to reasonable restrictions; Pollution-causing industries have no fundamental right to pollute
Articles 32 and 226Writ jurisdiction of Supreme Court and High Courts; Public interest litigation and compensation as a public law remedy
Article 39(b) and (c)Distribution of material resources of the community to subserve the common good
Article 51(c) and Article 253Respect for international law; Parliament’s power to legislate for treaty implementation; Basis of the EP Act 1986
Article 252Parliament may legislate for States by consent; Used for the Water Act 1974
Seventh ScheduleList I: Entry 52 (industries), 53 (atomic energy), 56 (inter-State rivers), 57 (fishing beyond territorial waters); List II: Entry 6 (public health and sanitation), 14 (agriculture), 17 (water), 18 (land); List III: Entry 17A (forests), 17B (protection of wild animals and birds), 20 (economic and social planning), 29 (prevention of the extension of infectious or contagious diseases); Forests and wild animals/birds moved from State List to Concurrent List by the 42nd Amendment 1976
Eleventh Schedule (Panchayats)Includes social forestry and farm forestry, minor forest produce, drinking water; Articles 243G
Twelfth Schedule (Municipalities)Public health, sanitation, conservancy and solid waste management; urban forestry, protection of the environment and promotion of ecological aspects; Article 243W
Wild Life (Protection) Act 1972National Board for Wild Life (s.5A); Central Zoo Authority (s.38A); National Tiger Conservation Authority and tiger reserves (2006 amendment); protected areas: sanctuaries (s.18), national parks (s.35), conservation reserves (s.36A), community reserves (s.36C); hunting prohibited (s.9); schedules reduced and CITES species schedule added by the Wild Life (Protection) Amendment Act 2022
Forest (Conservation) Act 1980, renamed Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam by the 2023 amendments.2 restricts de-reservation of reserved forests and use of forest land for non-forest purposes without prior approval of the Central Government; the 2023 amendment narrowed the Act’s coverage and exempted certain categories (for example linear projects near borders and security-related infrastructure)
Indian Forest Act 1927Reserved, protected and village forests
Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act 2006Recognises individual and community forest rights; Gram Sabha role (Orissa Mining Corporation v. MoEF, Niyamgiri, 2013)
Compensatory Afforestation Fund Act 2016CAMPA; funds for compensatory afforestation
Biological Diversity Act 2002 (amended 2023)National Biodiversity Authority (Chennai), State Biodiversity Boards, Biodiversity Management Committees; access and benefit sharing; implements the CBD
Public Liability Insurance Act 1991No-fault liability for accidents while handling hazardous substances; compulsory insurance; Environmental Relief Fund (s.7A); claims before the Collector
National Green Tribunal Act 2010See section 5
Energy Conservation Act 2001 (amended 2022)Bureau of Energy Efficiency; amendments enable a carbon market
Factories Act 1948Chapter IVA on hazardous processes (inserted after Bhopal in 1987)
Motor Vehicles Act 1988Pollution under control certificates and emission norms
Insecticides Act 1968; Atomic Energy Act 1962; Civil Liability for Nuclear Damage Act 2010Regulation of pesticides; radiation safety; nuclear damage liability (check recent nuclear law reforms)
Penal and procedural lawPublic nuisance in the IPC (ss.268, 277, 278, 290) with parallel provisions in the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita 2023; s.133 CrPC (corresponding Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita provision) for removal of public nuisance, as in Ratlam Municipality v. Vardhichand (1980)
Ratlam Municipality v. Vardhichand (1980)Municipalities cannot plead lack of funds to avoid statutory duty to provide drains and sanitation; public nuisance remedy
Rural Litigation and Entitlement Kendra (1985)Doon Valley mining halted; ecological balance under Art. 21
M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Oleum Gas Leak, 1986/87)Rule of absolute liability for hazardous industries; Rylands v. Fletcher exceptions rejected; compensation proportional to the enterprise’s capacity
M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Ganga Pollution, 1988)Tanneries ordered to treat effluents; locus standi for public-spirited persons
Union Carbide Corporation v. Union of India (1989)Bhopal settlement of US\$470 million; later partly revisited
Subhash Kumar v. State of Bihar (1991)Right to pollution-free water and air under Art. 21; limits on PIL for private interest
Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action v. Union of India (1996)Bichhri chemical sludge case; polluter pays; absolute liability applied
Vellore Citizens’ Welfare Forum (1996)Precautionary and polluter pays are part of Indian law
T.N. Godavarman Thirumulpad v. Union of India (1996 onwards)“Forest” given its dictionary meaning, covering all areas recorded as forest; continuing mandamus; Central Empowered Committee
M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Taj Trapezium, 1997)Industries around the Taj Mahal ordered to switch to natural gas or relocate
S. Jagannath v. Union of India (1997)Shrimp aquaculture in coastal zones restricted; polluter pays and precaution applied
M.C. Mehta v. Kamal Nath (1997)Public trust doctrine
A.P. Pollution Control Board v. M.V. Nayudu (1999; 2001)Precautionary principle with burden of proof on the developer; need for environmental courts with technical members
M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Delhi vehicular pollution, 1998–2001)CNG for public transport in Delhi
Almitra H. Patel v. Union of India (2000)Municipal solid waste and a citizen’s right to clean living
Karnataka Industrial Areas Development Board v. C. Kenchappa (2006)Sustainable development balances the economy and ecology
Orissa Mining Corporation v. MoEF (2013)Gram Sabhas decide cultural and religious rights of tribals in forest-rights claims (Niyamgiri)
Hanuman Laxman Aroskar v. Union of India (2019)EIA process must be meaningful; Mopa airport clearance quashed and remanded
Vanashakti v. Union of India (2025)In May 2025 the Supreme Court held ex post facto environmental clearance illegal; on 18 November 2025 a three-judge bench recalled it by a 2:1 majority and sent the matter for fresh consideration. Treat as unsettled
M.K. Ranjitsinh v. Union of India (2024)Right against the adverse effects of climate change under Arts. 14 and 21 (Great Indian Bustard)
