§Overview#

Unit VIII is one of the most predictable units of UGC NET Law (code 058): the syllabus has exactly ten topics, the questions are mostly factual, and the same facts (years, Articles, section numbers, composition of bodies, landmark cases) are recycled every cycle. These notes cover every listed topic and sub-topic in detail.

0.1Paper pattern (verify against the current NTA bulletin)#

In brief

Paper 2 (Law) has 100 objective questions of 2 marks each (200 marks); Paper 1 has 50 questions (100 marks). Both are taken in a single 3-hour sitting. The official notices…

  • Paper 2 (Law) has 100 objective questions of 2 marks each (200 marks); Paper 1 has 50 questions (100 marks). Both are taken in a single 3-hour sitting. The official notices state that there is no negative marking.

  • The paper is divided into 10 units, so Unit VIII typically contributes roughly 8 to 12 questions. The split is not fixed, but it is always a mix of environment law and human rights law.

  • Question formats seen in recent cycles: (a) direct one-line factual questions; (b) Statement I / Statement II (true-false pairs); (c) Match List I with List II (case with principle, convention with year, Article with subject, commission with statute); (d) "which of the following is/are correct" multi-statement questions; (e) chronological ordering of conferences or instruments; (f) occasional Assertion-Reason.

  • Public question banks for this unit show that questions on the UN Human Rights Council, the UN Decade for Human Rights Education (1995–2004) and the body that constitutes the NHRC have been asked. Expect the same style of fact-recall again.

0.2Trends you can plan around#

In brief

The human rights half is steadier and more factual (UDHR, ICCPR, ICESCR, Optional Protocols, Vienna Declaration 1993, the Protection of Human Rights Act 1993 and its…

  1. The human rights half is steadier and more factual (UDHR, ICCPR, ICESCR, Optional Protocols, Vienna Declaration 1993, the Protection of Human Rights Act 1993 and its amendments of 2006 and 2019). Questions on NHRC composition, appointment and removal recur almost every cycle.

  2. The environment half rewards case law and dates. Vellore Citizens, M.C. Mehta (several), Rural Litigation, Subhash Kumar, Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action, A.P. Pollution Control Board v. Nayudu, T.N. Godavarman and the Kamal Nath public trust case are the usual match-the-following material.

  3. The NGT is the single most examined environmental topic because it is compact: Act of 2010, Sections 14 to 22, composition, jurisdiction, limitation, appeal to the Supreme Court within 90 days, the seven scheduled Acts.

  4. International conferences are a perennial chronological question: Stockholm 1972, Nairobi 1982, Brundtland 1987, Rio 1992, Kyoto 1997, Johannesburg 2002, Rio+20 2012, Paris 2015, Glasgow 2021.

  5. Group rights questions are statute-driven: Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act 2016, Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act 2007, Juvenile Justice Act 2015, POCSO 2012, Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act 2005, POSH Act 2013, the minorities commission statute of 1992, and Articles 338, 338A and 338B for the SC, ST and backward classes commissions.

  6. Recent developments are a small but real slice. Examples covered below: the Jan Vishwas amendments to the Environment (Protection) Act 1986 (penalty regime from 1 April 2024), the Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam, India's 2026–28 UN Human Rights Council term, and the Supreme Court's 2025 Vanashakti judgment and its recall.
