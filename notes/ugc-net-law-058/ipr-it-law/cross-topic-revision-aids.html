11.5Common traps to avoid in MCQs#

In brief

Covers 3(k) (software per se), Section 38B, Section 3A is technology neutral, Section 66A is not law, India is not a member of and more.

  • 3(k) (software per se)
  • Section 38B
  • Section 66A is not law
  • Section 69A is valid
  • Section 79(3)(b)
  • India is not a member of
  • IPAB no longer exists
  • CAT is merged into TDSAT

  1. Berne minimum is life + 50; India gives life + 60. Do not mix them.

  2. Section 3(d) (new form of known substance) is different from Section 3(e) (mere admixture) and from 3(k) (software per se).

  3. Pre-grant opposition (Section 25(1)) can be filed by any person; post-grant (25(2)) by a person interested within 12 months of publication of grant.

  4. Compulsory licence under Section 84 is available only after 3 years, whereas Section 92 can be invoked at any time.

  5. GI rights cannot be assigned or licensed (Section 24); trademarks can. GI: association of producers; no individual applicant.

  6. Moral rights (Section 57) survive assignment; performer's moral rights are in Section 38B.

  7. Digital signature is a type of electronic signature under the IT Act; Section 3A is technology neutral.

  8. Section 43 is civil (compensation); Section 66 is criminal and requires dishonest or fraudulent intention.

  9. Section 66A is not law (struck down); Section 69A is valid; Section 79(3)(b) requires court/government order.

  10. India is not a member of the Rome Convention, UPOV, Budapest Convention on Cybercrime, Patent Law Treaty (check each before answering).

  11. IPAB no longer exists: appeals go to the High Court; CAT is merged into TDSAT.

  12. Biodiversity approvals: Section 3 (foreigners/foreign-controlled entities, NBA), Section 7 (Indian users, SBB intimation). Do not swap NBA and SBB.

  13. The Information Technology Act does not apply to negotiable instruments (other than cheques), powers of attorney, trusts, wills, and contracts for the sale/conveyance of immovable property.

  14. TRIPS vs Berne: TRIPS incorporates Berne except moral rights (Art. 6bis).

11.6A practical revision plan#

In brief

Covers Week 1, Week 2, Week 3, Final days.

  • Week 1
  • Week 2
  • Section 3 list
  • Section 9/11 grounds
  • Week 3
  • Final days

  • Week 1: Topics 1–3 and Copyright (4); make flashcards for numbers, sections and cases.

  • Week 2: Patents (5) and Trademarks (6), with the Section 3 list, Section 9/11 grounds and procedures written out from memory.

  • Week 3: GI (7), Biodiversity/TK (8) and IT law (9–10): tables of sections and penalties.

  • Final days: the master tables in Section 11, the recent-amendment list in Section 0, and previous years' papers (focus on option elimination: wrong time periods and swapped section numbers).

Disclaimer: these notes are a study aid compiled from the Acts and leading cases; recent items marked "verify" (rules, commencement dates, ratification counts and pending litigation) can change, so confirm them from the bare Acts, India Code, official gazette notifications and WIPO/WTO sources before the exam.
