12.2Composition and Appointment#

In brief

CEC + such number of other ECs as President may fix (Article 324(2)).

CEC + such number of other ECs as President may fix (Article 324(2)). Appointed by President. The Chief Election Commissioner Act, 2023 changed the appointment process — selection committee (PM + Leader of Opposition + Cabinet Minister). This change (removing CJI from selection) is under judicial challenge.

12.3Tenure and Removal#

In brief

Term: 6 years or 65 years — whichever is earlier.

Term: 6 years or 65 years — whichever is earlier. The CEC can be removed ONLY in the same manner as a SC judge — by impeachment process (Article 324(5)). Other ECs can be removed by President on recommendation of CEC. T.N. Seshan v. Union of India (1995): CEC and other ECs have equal powers; CEC has no veto.

12.4Powers of Election Commission#

In brief

  • Preparation and revision of electoral rolls

  • Recognition of political parties; allotment of election symbols

  • Model Code of Conduct enforcement (not statutory — based on Art. 324 plenary power)

  • Setting election schedules; monitoring of elections

  • Disqualification of candidates for corrupt practices (on reference from President/Governor)

  • Advice on disqualification of MPs/MLAs under Articles 103 and 192

12.5Key Cases#

In brief

T.N. Seshan v. Union of India (1995): Equal powers of CEC and ECs; CEC has no veto Mohinder Singh Gill v. CEC (1978): Election Commission's superintendence powers are…

  • T.N. Seshan v. Union of India (1995): Equal powers of CEC and ECs; CEC has no veto

  • Mohinder Singh Gill v. CEC (1978): Election Commission's superintendence powers are plenary

  • Association for Democratic Reforms v. Union of India (2002): Candidates must disclose criminal antecedents, assets, educational qualifications — right to information is part of Art. 19(1)(a)

  • Lily Thomas v. Union of India (2013): Convicted MPs/MLAs disqualified from date of conviction — Section 8(4) RPA struck down
