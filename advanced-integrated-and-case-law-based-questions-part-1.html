19 practice questions with answers

1Which of the following pairs of IP rights does NOT require registration in India for the right to subsist?

  1. Patent and Trademark
  2. Geographical Indication and Copyright
  3. Copyright and Performer's Rights
  4. Trademark and Industrial Design
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Copyright and Performer's Rights

Copyright (Section 13) and Performer's Rights (Section 38) arise automatically upon creation/performance without any requirement of registration. Patents, trademarks, GIs, and industrial designs all require registration.

2The 'work for hire' doctrine in copyright, as reflected in Section 17 of the Copyright Act, 1957, provides that when an author creates a work under a contract of service or apprenticeship, the first owner of copyright is the:

  1. Author/employee
  2. Government
  3. Employer, in the absence of a contrary agreement
  4. Publisher
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Employer, in the absence of a contrary agreement

Section 17 provides that in the absence of any agreement to the contrary, where a work is made by an author in the course of his employment under a contract of service or apprenticeship, the employer shall be the first owner of the copyright therein.

3Which landmark case established that the 'sweat of the brow' doctrine does NOT apply in India and a higher threshold of creativity is required for copyright in compilations?

  1. R.G. Anand v. M/s Deluxe Films (1978)
  2. Zee Telefilms v. Sundial Communications (2003)
  3. Eastern Book Company v. D.B. Modak (2008)
  4. Amar Nath Sehgal v. Union of India (2005)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Eastern Book Company v. D.B. Modak (2008)

The Supreme Court in Eastern Book Company v. D.B. Modak specifically rejected the 'sweat of the brow' doctrine (mere labour is insufficient) and held that a 'modicum of creativity' through the exercise of skill and judgment is required for copyright in compilations.

4Which of the following is TRUE regarding copyright protection for computer programs in India?

  1. Computer programs are protected as literary works under the Copyright Act, 1957
  2. Computer programs require patent registration
  3. Computer programs are protected as artistic works
  4. Computer programs have no IP protection in India
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Computer programs are protected as literary works under the Copyright Act, 1957

Section 2(o) of the Copyright Act defines 'literary work' to include computer programmes, tables and compilations including computer databases. Computer programs therefore receive copyright protection as literary works.

5The Indian Patents Act, 1970 does NOT grant patent protection to:

  1. Computer programs per se
  2. Biotechnology inventions
  3. New chemical processes
  4. Pharmaceutical product inventions (post-2005)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Computer programs per se

Section 3(k) of the Patents Act specifically excludes computer programmes per se from patentable subject matter. Computer programs may be protected as literary works under copyright law.

Report this question

6The 'exhaustion of rights' doctrine (first-sale doctrine) in IP law means:

  1. IP rights expire after a fixed period of use
  2. IP rights are exhausted if not enforced within a limitation period
  3. An IP owner must licence to all who request
  4. Once the IP owner sells a product, they cannot control its subsequent resale
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Once the IP owner sells a product, they cannot control its subsequent resale

The exhaustion doctrine provides that once an IP owner sells a product, their IP rights in that specific product are 'exhausted' — they cannot prevent the buyer from reselling or using that particular item.

7Assertion (A): Copyright in India does not require registration for subsistence of protection. Reason (R): India follows the Berne Convention principle of automatic protection upon creation.

  1. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are correct but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is correct but R is incorrect
  4. A is incorrect but R is correct
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A

Both are correct. India follows the Berne Convention's automatic protection principle — copyright subsists as soon as the original work is created and fixed in a material form, without requiring any formality such as registration.

8Assertion (A): Section 3(d) of the Patents Act 1970 prevents grant of patent to new forms of known substances unless enhanced efficacy is demonstrated. Reason (R): This provision is aimed at preventing 'evergreening' of pharmaceutical patents.

  1. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are correct but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is correct but R is incorrect
  4. Both A and R are incorrect
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A

Both are correct and the reason correctly explains the purpose of Section 3(d). By requiring enhanced efficacy, the provision prevents pharmaceutical companies from obtaining new patents on minor modifications of existing drugs (evergreening) to artificially extend their monopoly.

9Assertion (A): Traditional Knowledge (TK) cannot be protected as a patent in India. Reason (R): Section 3(p) of the Patents Act, 1970 excludes inventions which are traditional knowledge or aggregation/duplication of known properties of traditionally known components.

  1. Both A and R are correct but R is not the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A
  3. A is correct but R is incorrect
  4. Both A and R are incorrect
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A

Both are correct. Section 3(p) specifically excludes traditional knowledge from patentability, thereby providing a statutory basis for the protection of India's traditional knowledge against biopiracy.

10Under the Berne Convention, which principle removes the need for any formalities (registration, deposit) as a condition for copyright protection?

  1. National Treatment
  2. Minimum Standards
  3. Reciprocity
  4. Automatic Protection / No Formalities principle
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Automatic Protection / No Formalities principle

Article 5(2) of the Berne Convention establishes the principle that the enjoyment and the exercise of copyright shall not be subject to any formality — protection is automatic upon creation of the work.

11Which of the following correctly reflects the hierarchy of IP appeal authorities in India for patent matters (post-2021)?

  1. Patent Office → IPAB → Supreme Court
  2. Patent Office → District Court → High Court → Supreme Court
  3. Controller General → Cyber Appellate Tribunal → High Court
  4. Patent Office → High Court → Supreme Court
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Patent Office → High Court → Supreme Court

The Intellectual Property Appellate Board (IPAB) was abolished by the Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021. Appeals from orders of the Controller (Patent Office) now lie directly to the High Court, and further appeals lie to the Supreme Court.

12Assertion (A): India's TKDL (Traditional Knowledge Digital Library) acts as a 'defensive' form of traditional knowledge protection. Reason (R): TKDL does not grant exclusive IP rights to TK holders; instead, it creates prior art databases to prevent third parties from obtaining patents over traditional knowledge.

  1. Both A and R are correct but R is not the correct explanation of A
  2. A is correct but R is incorrect
  3. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A
  4. Both A and R are incorrect
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A

Both are correct. TKDL is a defensive protection mechanism — it does not create any new IP rights in traditional knowledge; instead it serves as a prior art database accessible to patent examiners worldwide, preventing third parties from obtaining patents on traditional Indian knowledge.

13Which of the following statements about the TRIPS Agreement is INCORRECT?

  1. It is administered exclusively by WIPO
  2. It sets minimum standards of IP protection for WTO members
  3. It provides for enforcement of IP rights
  4. It was signed in 1994
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It is administered exclusively by WIPO

TRIPS is administered by the WTO (World Trade Organization), not by WIPO. While WIPO administers most IP conventions (Berne, Paris, PCT etc.), TRIPS is an agreement under the WTO framework.

14Assertion (A): India's sui generis plant variety protection system under the PPV&FR Act, 2001 is more balanced than the UPOV model. Reason (R): Unlike UPOV, the Indian Act incorporates farmers' rights, allowing farmers to save, use, exchange, and sell farm-saved seed (but not branded seed) of protected varieties, thereby protecting food security and traditional agricultural practices.

  1. Both A and R are correct but R is not the correct explanation of A
  2. A is correct but R is incorrect
  3. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A
  4. Both A and R are incorrect
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A

Both are correct. The PPV&FR Act is widely regarded as more balanced than UPOV because it explicitly protects farmers' rights — including the right to save, use, sow, resow, exchange, share or sell farm produce of protected varieties — a dimension absent from UPOV.

15Consider the following statements about patentability under the Patents Act, 1970, and identify which are CORRECT: (i) A new use of a known substance is patentable without showing enhanced efficacy. (ii) Methods of agriculture are not patentable. (iii) Mathematical methods are not patentable. (iv) Living micro-organisms produced by human intervention can be patentable.

  1. i and ii only
  2. i, iii and iv only
  3. All four statements are correct
  4. ii, iii and iv only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. ii, iii and iv only

Statement (i) is incorrect — Section 3(d) prevents patenting new uses of known substances without proof of enhanced efficacy. Statements (ii), (iii), and (iv) are correct: Section 3(h) excludes agriculture/horticulture; Section 3(k) excludes mathematical methods; and micro-organisms with technical application may be patentable (unlike plants/animals under Section 3(j)).

16Which of the following correctly describes the relationship between copyright and the Internet in India?

  1. The IT Act, 2000 entirely governs copyright on the Internet
  2. The Copyright Act, 1957 (as amended in 2012) applies to digital content; the IT Act governs electronic records and cyber offences; both operate concurrently
  3. Neither the Copyright Act nor the IT Act applies to Internet content
  4. WIPO directly governs copyright disputes on Indian websites
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The Copyright Act, 1957 (as amended in 2012) applies to digital content; the IT Act governs electronic records and cyber offences; both operate concurrently

In India, copyright in digital content is governed by the Copyright Act, 1957 (aligned with WIPO Internet Treaties through the 2012 amendment), while cyber offences relating to IP (digital piracy as a cyber crime, circumvention of technical protection measures, etc.) are also addressed under the IT Act, 2000. Both statutes operate concurrently.

17Consider the following statements about the Designs Act, 2000 and the Copyright Act, 1957 and identify the CORRECT statement:

  1. A design registered under the Designs Act continues to enjoy copyright protection after the design's registration term expires
  2. A design capable of being registered under the Designs Act but which has NOT been registered does not lose copyright protection until industrially reproduced more than 50 times
  3. Designs Act and Copyright Act operate in completely separate spheres with no overlap
  4. Any artistic work automatically gets design protection without registration
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A design capable of being registered under the Designs Act but which has NOT been registered does not lose copyright protection until industrially reproduced more than 50 times

Section 15(2) of the Copyright Act provides that copyright in an artistic work (which is capable of being registered as a design but has not been so registered) ceases once the article to which the design has been applied has been reproduced more than fifty times by an industrial process. Until that threshold, copyright is retained.

18Which of the following correctly states the three objectives of the Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD, 1992)?

  1. Conservation of biological diversity, sustainable use of its components, and fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from genetic resources
  2. Patenting of biological resources, sustainable use, and commercialisation
  3. Conservation, prohibition on use, and regulation of GMOs
  4. Biodiversity conservation, prevention of biopiracy, and free access to genetic resources
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Conservation of biological diversity, sustainable use of its components, and fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from genetic resources

Article 1 of the CBD states its three objectives: (1) conservation of biological diversity; (2) the sustainable use of its components; and (3) the fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising out of the utilisation of genetic resources.

19The 'Digital piracy' in the context of copyright means:

  1. Unauthorised copying, distribution, or downloading of copyrighted works in digital form
  2. Hacking of digital payment systems
  3. Violation of digital signature laws
  4. Breach of data privacy laws
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Unauthorised copying, distribution, or downloading of copyrighted works in digital form

Digital piracy refers to the unauthorised reproduction, distribution, or use of copyrighted material in digital format, including movies, music, software, and books, through the internet or other digital means.
