What Unit 5 covers

Mathematical Reasoning is a speed game on simple arithmetic, so timed daily practice matters more than learning new methods. The official syllabus lists 4 topics for this unit. Each is explained below with the points examiners most often use.

1Types of reasoning

  • Deductive reasoning moves from general rules to a specific conclusion that must be true if the premises are true.
  • Inductive reasoning moves from specific observations to a general conclusion that is probable, not certain.
  • Other types: analogical reasoning (by comparison), verbal reasoning (words) and non-verbal reasoning (figures and patterns).
  • Quantitative reasoning solves problems with numbers, and logical reasoning tests the structure of arguments.

2Number series, letter series, codes and relationships

  • Number series follow patterns: constant difference, constant ratio, squares, cubes, primes or differences that themselves form a pattern.
  • Letter series use alphabet positions (A=1 to Z=26) with fixed jumps or alternating steps. Opposite letters add up to 27.
  • Coding-decoding applies a rule such as shifting each letter by a fixed number of places or replacing letters with numbers.
  • Blood relation questions need a clear family diagram: for example, a mother's brother is a maternal uncle and his child is a cousin.

3Basic mathematical aptitude: fractions, time and distance, ratio, proportion and percentage

  • Percentage: x% of N is N times x over 100. Convert fractions to percentages for quick comparison.
  • Speed = distance divided by time. To convert km/h to m/s multiply by 5/18.
  • Ratio compares two quantities of the same kind. In A:B and B:C, make the B terms equal to combine them into A:B:C.
  • Proportion states that two ratios are equal. In direct proportion both quantities rise together; in inverse proportion one rises as the other falls.

4Profit and loss, interest, averages, discounts and simple calculations

  • Profit = SP minus CP and profit percent = profit divided by CP times 100. Loss percent is calculated the same way on CP.
  • Simple interest = P x R x T divided by 100. Compound interest amount = P (1 + R/100) to the power T.
  • Average = sum of values divided by the number of values. So sum = average times number of values.
  • Discount is a reduction on the marked price, and the selling price = marked price minus discount.

Key terms and facts to remember

Deductive reasoning

General to specific; the conclusion follows necessarily from the premises.

Inductive reasoning

Specific to general; the conclusion is probable.

Alphabet positions

A=1, B=2 up to Z=26. The letter opposite to a given letter has position 27 minus the original.

Percentage

Fraction out of 100; for example 25% equals 1/4 and 20% equals 1/5.

Speed conversion

1 km/h = 5/18 m/s; 1 m/s = 18/5 km/h.

Profit and loss

Always calculated on the cost price unless stated otherwise.

Simple interest

Interest calculated only on the original principal.

Compound interest

Interest calculated on principal plus interest already earned.

Average

Sum divided by number of items; useful to remember sum equals average times count.

Prime numbers

Numbers with exactly two factors; 2 is the only even prime and 1 is not prime.

Clock angles

The minute hand moves 6 degrees per minute and the hour hand 0.5 degrees per minute.

  2. 8:15
  3. 6:10

3An article sold for ₹360 at 10% loss had a cost price of:

  1. ₹380
  2. ₹400
  3. ₹420
  4. ₹450

Answer: B. ₹400

How to study this unit

  • Learn the formulas table until you can write it in two minutes, then practise ten quick questions a day with a timer.
  • For series questions, write the differences and the ratios first. One of the two usually reveals the pattern.
  • Check each answer by substitution or an estimate. Many wrong options are the result of a common slip, such as dividing instead of multiplying.
