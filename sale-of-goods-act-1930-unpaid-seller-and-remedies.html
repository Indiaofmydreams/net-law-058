8 practice questions with answers

1Under Section 45, a seller is an 'unpaid seller' where:

  1. The goods have been delivered
  2. The price has been paid in part only, and no more is due
  3. The buyer has accepted the goods
  4. The whole of the price has not been paid or tendered, or a bill of exchange received as conditional payment has been dishonoured
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The whole of the price has not been paid or tendered, or a bill of exchange received as conditional payment has been dishonoured

The definition also covers the seller's agent in certain cases.

2Under Section 47, the unpaid seller in possession may exercise his lien in all of the following cases EXCEPT where:

  1. The goods were sold without any stipulation as to credit
  2. The term of credit has expired
  3. The goods were sold on credit, the term has not expired, and the buyer is solvent
  4. The buyer becomes insolvent
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The goods were sold on credit, the term has not expired, and the buyer is solvent

Lien does not arise while credit is still running.

Report this question

3Under Section 49(2), an unpaid seller who has obtained a decree for the price:

  1. Does not lose his lien merely by reason of the decree
  2. Loses his lien
  3. Loses his right of stoppage in transit
  4. Must hand over the goods
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Does not lose his lien merely by reason of the decree

Lien ends only on the events listed in s.49(1).

Report this question

4The right of stoppage in transit under Section 50 is available to an unpaid seller who:

  1. Still has the goods in his own warehouse
  2. Has been paid in full
  3. Has parted with possession of the goods, when the buyer becomes insolvent
  4. Has no contract with the buyer
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Has parted with possession of the goods, when the buyer becomes insolvent

Lets the seller resume possession while goods are in transit.

Report this question

5Under Section 55, where property has not passed to the buyer and the price is not payable on a day certain irrespective of delivery, the unpaid seller's remedy for the buyer's wrongful refusal to accept is:

  1. A suit for the price
  2. Specific performance only
  3. No remedy
  4. A suit for damages for non-acceptance (s.56)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A suit for damages for non-acceptance (s.56)

A suit for the price lies only if property has passed, or price payable on a day certain.

Report this question

6Under Section 57(3), where there is an available market for the goods, the prima facie measure of damages for non-delivery is:

  1. The difference between the contract price and the market price when the goods ought to have been delivered
  2. The cost of the goods to the seller
  3. The seller's profit
  4. The full contract price
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The difference between the contract price and the market price when the goods ought to have been delivered

Follows the measure in Section 73 of the Contract Act.

Report this question

7Under Section 59, where there is breach of warranty by the seller, the buyer:

  1. May reject the goods and repudiate the contract
  2. May only forfeit the goods
  3. May claim no remedy
  4. Is not by reason only of the breach entitled to reject the goods, but may set up the breach in diminution or extinction of the price, or sue for damages
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Is not by reason only of the breach entitled to reject the goods, but may set up the breach in diminution or extinction of the price, or sue for damages

Contrast with the right to reject for breach of a condition.

Report this question

8In a sale by auction, if the seller uses pretended bidding to raise the price, the sale is:

  1. Voidable at the option of the buyer
  2. Void
  3. Valid
  4. Illegal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Voidable at the option of the buyer

Part of the auction rules in s.64.
