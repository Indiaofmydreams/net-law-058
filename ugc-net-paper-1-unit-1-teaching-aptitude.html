UGC NET Paper 1 Unit 1: Teaching Aptitude Notes and MCQs

Teaching Aptitude is the first unit of UGC NET Paper 1 and is common to every candidate, whatever your subject. Questions are mostly direct: who proposed what, which method is learner-centred, which type of evaluation fits a situation. These notes cover each syllabus topic briefly, list the terms worth memorising and give you sample MCQs to try.

What Unit 1 covers

Teaching Aptitude rewards clear definitions, so revise the lists in these notes until you can say each one from memory. The official syllabus lists 6 topics for this unit. Each is explained below with the points examiners most often use.

1Teaching: concept, objectives, levels (memory, understanding and reflective) and characteristics

  • Teaching is an interactive, purposeful process involving the teacher, the learner and the curriculum. It is more than transmitting information.
  • Levels of teaching: memory level (Herbart), understanding level (Morrison) and reflective level (Hunt). The reflective level is the highest and is problem-centred.
  • Objectives are best written as observable learner behaviour. Bloom's cognitive taxonomy (1956) runs from knowledge to evaluation; the 2001 revision uses remember, understand, apply, analyse, evaluate and create.
  • Good teaching is planned, interactive, responsive to feedback and aimed at learning outcomes that can be measured.

2Learner characteristics: adolescent and adult learners; individual differences

  • Adolescents are in a stage of rapid physical, emotional and social change. They need guidance, peer interaction and a sense of identity.
  • Adult learners (andragogy, Knowles) are self-directed, bring experience, want relevance and are motivated by internal goals.
  • Individual differences arise from heredity and environment and show up in pace, ability, interest, learning style and motivation.
  • Teachers handle differences through varied methods, flexible pacing, remedial and enrichment work.

3Factors affecting teaching: teacher, learner, support material, facilities, environment and institution

  • Teacher factors: subject knowledge, communication skill, attitude, preparation and ability to motivate.
  • Learner factors: prior knowledge, motivation, ability, health and attention.
  • Support material and facilities: books, ICT tools, laboratories, library, classroom size and infrastructure.
  • Environment and institution: climate of the class, discipline, leadership, institutional policy and community support.

4Methods of teaching in institutions of higher learning: teacher-centred and learner-centred; offline and online

  • Teacher-centred methods: lecture, demonstration and dictation. The teacher is the main source of information.
  • Learner-centred methods: discussion, seminar, tutorial, project, problem-based learning, heuristic (discovery) method and cooperative learning.
  • Online learning can be synchronous (live, same time) or asynchronous (anytime access). Blended and flipped classrooms combine both modes.
  • Micro-teaching builds specific teaching skills through short, focused practice sessions with feedback.

5Teaching support systems: traditional, modern and ICT-based

  • Traditional aids: chalkboard, charts, models, maps and textbooks.
  • Modern aids: projector, audio-visual material, language labs and educational radio and television.
  • ICT-based support: learning management systems, e-content, MOOCs, virtual classrooms and open educational resources. Indian platforms include SWAYAM and NPTEL.
  • Dale's Cone of Experience (Edgar Dale, 1946) arranges experiences from concrete (direct, purposeful) to abstract (verbal symbols).

6Evaluation systems: elements and types of evaluation; CBCS; computer-based testing; innovations

  • Evaluation is broader than testing. It judges learning against objectives using measurement plus value judgement.
  • Types: formative (during instruction), summative (end of course), diagnostic (locates learning difficulties) and placement (before instruction).
  • CBCS (Choice Based Credit System) is a UGC framework that lets students choose courses and earn credits. NEP 2020 adds an Academic Bank of Credits and flexible entry and exit.
  • Innovations: continuous and comprehensive assessment, grading, open-book tests, online and computer-based testing, and question banks.

Key terms and facts to remember

Bloom's taxonomy1956

Cognitive domain, lowest level is knowledge. Revised in 2001 by Anderson and Krathwohl with create at the top.

Dale's Cone of Experience1946

Moves from direct experience to verbal symbols. It is not a hierarchy of difficulty.

Kolb's experiential learning1984

Cycle of concrete experience, reflective observation, abstract conceptualisation and active experimentation.

Knowles and andragogy1970s

Theory of adult learning: self-directed, experience-based and problem-centred.

Flanders Interaction Analysis1960s

Classroom observation tool with 10 categories: 7 for teacher talk, 2 for student talk and 1 for silence or confusion.

Micro-teaching1963

Developed at Stanford University (Dwight Allen). A small class, a short lesson and one skill at a time.

Pedagogy, andragogy, heutagogy

Teaching children, teaching adults and self-determined learning.

Synchronous and asynchronous

Synchronous means live and at the same time; asynchronous means learner-paced access at any time.

CBCS

Choice Based Credit System, a UGC framework for flexibility in choosing courses.

NEP 20202020

National Education Policy that proposes a four-year undergraduate programme, multiple exit points and the Academic Bank of Credits.

  1. Herbart
  2. Hunt
  3. Bloom
  4. Morrison

Memory level (Herbart), understanding level (Morrison), reflective level (Hunt).

  1. A-2, B-3, C-1
  2. A-1, B-2, C-3
  3. A-2, B-1, C-3

3In Bloom's original cognitive taxonomy, the lowest level is:

  1. Knowledge
  2. Synthesis
  3. Evaluation
  4. Analysis

Answer: A. Knowledge

Knowledge (remembering) is the base level.

4Adult learners (andragogy) are typically:

  1. Self-directed and experience-based
  2. Only memory-driven
  3. Fully dependent
  4. Disinterested

Knowles's assumptions about adult learning.

5A student repeatedly scores low in one topic. The teacher should first conduct:

  1. Diagnostic assessment
  2. Ranking
  3. Annual exam
  4. Summative test

Answer: A. Diagnostic assessment

It locates the exact learning difficulty.

  1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true, but R is false
  4. A is false, but R is true

Answer: A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A

R explains why A is desirable.

How to study this unit

  • Learn the pairs in this unit as name and idea: Herbart-memory, Morrison-understanding, Hunt-reflective. Matching questions repeat them.
  • Always sort a method into teacher-centred or learner-centred before reading the options. Lecture and demonstration are the common teacher-centred answers.
  • Practise assertion and reason questions: decide whether each statement is true first, then whether R explains A.
