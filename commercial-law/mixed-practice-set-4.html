About this question set

This set has 22 multiple-choice questions on Commercial Law from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 6, Commercial Law. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 7 of the 22 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Commercial Law notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

22 practice questions with answers

1In Khan Gul v. Lakha Singh, the court held that a minor who obtained money by falsely representing himself to be an adult:

  1. Was bound by the contract
  2. Could be compelled to restore the benefit, on the principle of restitution rather than enforcement of the contract
  3. Was bound only if he had ratified it
  4. Was free of all liability
Show answer

Answer: B. Could be compelled to restore the benefit, on the principle of restitution rather than enforcement of the contract

Report this question

2Under Section 12, a sane person who is delirious from fever, or so drunk that he cannot understand the contract's terms and form a rational judgment on its effect:

  1. Cannot contract while such delirium or drunkenness lasts
  2. Can contract at any time
  3. Can contract only through a guardian
  4. Makes a contract that is always void
Show answer

Answer: A. Cannot contract while such delirium or drunkenness lasts

Report this question

3Under Section 16(2), a person is deemed to be in a position to dominate the will of another where he:

  1. Is a blood relative only
  2. Is a spiritual guide only
  3. Holds a real or apparent authority over the other, or stands in a fiduciary relation to him
  4. Is a creditor only
Show answer

Answer: C. Holds a real or apparent authority over the other, or stands in a fiduciary relation to him

Report this question

4In Raffles v. Wichelhaus (the ship 'Peerless' case), the contract failed because:

  1. The price was inadequate
  2. There was no consensus ad idem, as the parties meant different ships
  3. The consideration was past
  4. The contract was a wager
Show answer

Answer: B. There was no consensus ad idem, as the parties meant different ships

Report this question

5Which of the following does NOT vitiate free consent under Section 14?

  1. Fraud
  2. Misrepresentation
  3. Mistake (subject to Sections 20-22)
  4. Persuasion without unfair pressure or dominance
Show answer

Answer: D. Persuasion without unfair pressure or dominance

6Assertion (A): Under Section 19A, the court may set aside a contract induced by undue influence either absolutely or upon terms. Reason (R): If the party who got the contract set aside has received a benefit under it, the court may require its restoration on such terms as seem just.

  1. Both A and R are true, and R explains A
  2. Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A
  3. A is true, R is false
  4. A is false, R is true
Show answer

Answer: A. Both A and R are true, and R explains A

7The proviso to Section 30 saves a contribution towards a prize for a horse race of the value of:

  1. Rs. 100 or upwards
  2. Rs. 500 or upwards
  3. Rs. 1,000 or upwards
  4. Any amount
Show answer

Answer: B. Rs. 500 or upwards

Report this question

8Under Section 35, a contract contingent on a specified uncertain event happening within a fixed time becomes void:

  1. If the time expires without the event happening, or the event becomes impossible before then
  2. Only when a court declares it so
  3. Only on the promisor's refusal
  4. Never
Show answer

Answer: A. If the time expires without the event happening, or the event becomes impossible before then

Report this question

9Under Section 150, where goods are bailed for hire, the bailor is responsible for damage from faults in the goods:

  1. Only if he knew of them
  2. Whether or not he was aware of them
  3. Never
  4. Only if the bailee asks
Show answer

Answer: B. Whether or not he was aware of them

Report this question

10Under Section 153, a contract of bailment is:

  1. Voidable at the option of the bailor if the bailee does any act inconsistent with the conditions of bailment
  2. Void
  3. Illegal
  4. Terminated only by the court
Show answer

Answer: A. Voidable at the option of the bailor if the bailee does any act inconsistent with the conditions of bailment

11Under Section 157, where the bailee, without the bailor's consent, mixes the bailor's goods with his own so that they cannot be separated:

  1. The bailor is entitled to be compensated by the bailee for the loss of the goods
  2. The bailor loses the goods
  3. The bailee pays only the expense of separation
  4. The bailment continues unchanged
Show answer

Answer: A. The bailor is entitled to be compensated by the bailee for the loss of the goods

12Under Section 169, a finder of lost goods may sell them where the lawful charges of the finder amount to:

  1. One-half of the value
  2. Two-thirds of the value
  3. One-third of the value
  4. The whole value
Show answer

Answer: B. Two-thirds of the value

13A tailor's right to retain a customer's cloth until stitching charges are paid is:

  1. A general lien
  2. A particular lien under Section 170
  3. A pledge
  4. A mortgage
Show answer

Answer: B. A particular lien under Section 170

14Under Section 174, absent a contract to the contrary, the pawnee:

  1. May retain them only for the debt for which they were pledged, though a contract is presumed for subsequent advances
  2. May retain the goods for any debt owed by the pawnor
  3. May retain them for no debt
  4. May retain them for unrelated future debts
Show answer

Answer: A. May retain them only for the debt for which they were pledged, though a contract is presumed for subsequent advances

15Under Section 178A, a pledge by a person who obtained the goods under a contract voidable under Section 19 or 19A, not yet rescinded, gives the pawnee a good title if he acts:

  1. For consideration only
  2. With notice of the defect
  3. In good faith and without notice of the pawnor's defect of title
  4. Through an agent
Show answer

Answer: C. In good faith and without notice of the pawnor's defect of title

Report this question

16Which of the following is NOT an exception to nemo dat quod non habet?

  1. Sale by a thief to a bona fide purchaser
  2. Sale by a mercantile agent in possession with the owner's consent
  3. Sale by estoppel
  4. Sale by a buyer in possession with the seller's consent
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Sale by a thief to a bona fide purchaser

A thief has no title and cannot pass any.

Report this question

17A obtains goods from B by fraud, so the contract is voidable. Before B rescinds, A sells the goods to C, who buys in good faith without notice. Under Section 29, C:

  1. Gets no title
  2. Gets a good title
  3. Is liable to return the goods to B
  4. Holds only as bailee
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Gets a good title

Depends on B not having rescinded before the sale.

Report this question

18A sells goods to B but remains in possession, then sells and delivers the same goods to C, who acts in good faith without notice of the first sale. Under Section 30(1), C:

  1. Gets no title
  2. Must return the goods to B
  3. Gets a good title, as if the delivery was expressly authorised by the owner
  4. Gets only a lien
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Gets a good title, as if the delivery was expressly authorised by the owner

Seller in possession treated as the owner's authorised agent for this purpose.

Report this question

19Under Section 30(2), a buyer in possession with the seller's consent delivers the goods under a sale or pledge to a person who takes in good faith without notice of the original seller's lien or other right. The transferee:

  1. Gets a good title, free from the seller's lien or right
  2. Gets no title
  3. Takes subject to the seller's lien
  4. Must return the goods
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Gets a good title, free from the seller's lien or right

Innocent transferee is protected.

Report this question

20Match List I with List II. List I: A. Section 27; B. Section 28; C. Section 29; D. Section 30. List II: 1. Voidable contract not yet rescinded; 2. Joint owner in sole possession; 3. Rule that a non-owner cannot pass title, with estoppel and mercantile-agent exceptions; 4. Seller or buyer remaining or obtaining possession after sale.

  1. A-2, B-3, C-4, D-1
  2. A-3, B-2, C-1, D-4
  3. A-1, B-4, C-3, D-2
  4. A-4, B-1, C-2, D-3
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A-3, B-2, C-1, D-4

The four heads of the Act's title provisions.

Report this question

21Under Section 32, unless otherwise agreed, delivery of the goods and payment of the price are:

  1. Independent obligations
  2. Payment first, then delivery
  3. Delivery first, then payment
  4. Concurrent conditions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Concurrent conditions

Each party must be ready and willing to give the other what is due.

Report this question

22Under Section 36(3), where the goods are in the possession of a third person at the time of sale, there is no delivery to the buyer unless:

  1. The seller gives a written notice
  2. The third person acknowledges to the buyer that he holds the goods on the buyer's behalf
  3. The goods are returned to the seller
  4. The buyer pays the third person
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The third person acknowledges to the buyer that he holds the goods on the buyer's behalf

Attornment, a form of constructive delivery.
