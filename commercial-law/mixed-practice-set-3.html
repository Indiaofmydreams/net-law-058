1A person who is usually of unsound mind but occasionally of sound mind may contract:

  1. Never
  2. Only through a guardian
  3. While he is of sound mind
  4. At any time
Show answer

Answer: C. While he is of sound mind

2Under Section 31, a contingent contract depends on an event that is:

  1. Part of the contract
  2. Collateral to the contract
  3. Within the promisor's control
  4. Certain to occur
Show answer

Answer: B. Collateral to the contract

3Under Section 32, a contract contingent on an event happening:

  1. Is enforceable immediately
  2. Is void
  3. Is voidable
  4. Cannot be enforced until the event happens
Show answer

Answer: D. Cannot be enforced until the event happens

4Under Section 36, contingent agreements dependent on an impossible event are:

  1. Void
  2. Voidable
  3. Valid
  4. Enforceable
Show answer

Answer: A. Void

5The rule on remoteness of damages under Section 73 derives from:

  1. Felthouse v. Bindley
  2. Carlill v. Carbolic Smoke Ball
  3. Hyde v. Wrench
  4. Hadley v. Baxendale

6Under Section 73, compensation is NOT awarded for:

  1. Direct loss
  2. Natural loss
  3. Remote and indirect loss
  4. Loss the parties knew was likely
Show answer

Answer: C. Remote and indirect loss

7Fateh Chand v. Balkishan Dass held that under Section 74:

  1. Only reasonable compensation not exceeding the stipulated amount can be awarded
  2. Penalty is always enforceable
  3. Liquidated damages must always be awarded in full
  4. Section 74 applies only to liquidated damages

Answer: A. Only reasonable compensation not exceeding the stipulated amount can be awarded

8In Kailash Nath Associates v. DDA, the Supreme Court held that under Section 74:

  1. Proof of loss is never required
  2. Penalty clauses are void
  3. Compensation is awarded on proof of loss, unless loss is difficult or impossible to prove

  1. Gets no compensation
  2. Must pay compensation

  2. Entitled to nothing

  1. Reciprocal promises
  2. Collateral promises
  3. Contingent promises

17Under Section 3, communication of a proposal, acceptance or revocation is deemed to be made by:

  1. Any act or omission by which the party intends to communicate it, or which has the effect of communicating it
  2. Written words only
  3. Spoken words only
  4. Registered post only

Answer: A. Any act or omission by which the party intends to communicate it, or which has the effect of communicating it

18Under Section 6, a proposal is revoked by: I. Communication of notice of revocation by the proposer; II. Lapse of the time prescribed (or a reasonable time); III. Failure of the acceptor to fulfil a condition precedent to acceptance; IV. Death or insanity of the proposer, if the fact comes to the acceptor's knowledge before acceptance.

  1. I and II only
  2. II and III only
  3. I, II and III only
  4. I, II, III and IV
Show answer

Answer: D. I, II, III and IV

19Under Section 8, performing the conditions of a proposal, or accepting any consideration for a reciprocal promise offered with the proposal, is:

  1. An invitation to treat
  2. A counter-offer
  3. Acceptance of the proposal
  4. Revocation of the proposal

Answer: C. Acceptance of the proposal

20Balfour v. Balfour is authority for the proposition that:

  1. Wagers are void
  2. Consent obtained by undue influence is voidable
  3. Restraints of trade are void
  4. Domestic or social agreements are presumed not to create legal relations

Answer: D. Domestic or social agreements are presumed not to create legal relations

  3. Promissory estoppel
