About this question set

This set has 22 multiple-choice questions on Commercial Law from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 6, Commercial Law. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 22 of the 22 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Commercial Law notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

  • General concepts (22)

22 practice questions with answers

1The Indian Contract Act was enacted in:

  1. 1882
  2. 1872
  3. 1932
  4. 1930
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1872

Indian Contract Act, 1872.

Report this question

2General offer to the world was upheld in:

  1. Carlill v. Carbolic Smoke Ball Co.
  2. Taylor v. Caldwell
  3. Balfour v. Balfour
  4. Hadley v. Baxendale
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Carlill v. Carbolic Smoke Ball Co.

An offer to the public can be accepted by performing the condition.

Report this question

3A minor's agreement is void ab initio per:

  1. Felthouse v. Bindley
  2. Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose
  3. Hyde v. Wrench
  4. Carlill
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose

Privy Council, 1903.

Report this question

4The remoteness rule in contract damages comes from:

  1. Hadley v. Baxendale
  2. Donoghue
  3. Salomon
  4. Wagon Mound
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Hadley v. Baxendale

Reflected in Section 73 of the Contract Act.

Report this question

5Doctrine of frustration/impossibility in India is under:

  1. Section 73
  2. Section 10
  3. Section 124
  4. Section 56
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Section 56

Satyabrata Ghose v. Mugneeram Bangur explains it.

Report this question

6Quasi-contract rests on the principle of:

  1. Free consent
  2. Indemnity
  3. Estoppel
  4. Unjust enrichment
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Unjust enrichment

A person should not unjustly enrich themselves at another's expense.

Report this question

7Unconscionable standard-form contracts were considered in:

  1. Carlill
  2. Mohori Bibee
  3. Central Inland Water Transport v. Brojo Nath Ganguly
  4. Bhagwandas Goverdhandas Kedia
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Central Inland Water Transport v. Brojo Nath Ganguly

The Court struck down an unfair clause.

Report this question

8A contract of guarantee involves how many parties?

  1. Two
  2. Three
  3. Four
  4. Five
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Three

Surety, principal debtor and creditor.

Report this question

9Pledge is a bailment for:

  1. Security for debt or promise
  2. Sale
  3. Gratuitous carriage
  4. Hire
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Security for debt or promise

Goods are bailed as security.

Report this question

10An agent's relationship to the principal is:

  1. Customary
  2. Contractual only
  3. Fiduciary
  4. Adverse
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Fiduciary

An agent owes duties of loyalty and disclosure.

Report this question

11The Sale of Goods Act was enacted in:

  1. 1881
  2. 1930
  3. 1932
  4. 1872
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1930

Sale of Goods Act, 1930.

Report this question

12Breach of a condition in sale entitles the buyer to:

  1. Only specific performance
  2. Nothing
  3. Repudiate the contract
  4. Only damages
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Repudiate the contract

Breach of warranty gives only damages.

Report this question

13A limited liability partnership is governed by the:

  1. Partnership Act, 1932
  2. Contract Act
  3. Companies Act, 2013
  4. LLP Act, 2008
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. LLP Act, 2008

An LLP is a separate legal entity.

Report this question

14Dishonour of a cheque for insufficient funds is an offence under Section:

  1. 138 NI Act
  2. 73 Contract Act
  3. 420 IPC
  4. 27 Companies Act
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 138 NI Act

Section 138, Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881.

Report this question

15Separate legal personality of a company was established in:

  1. Ashbury
  2. Foss v. Harbottle
  3. Salomon v. Salomon & Co.
  4. Lee v. Lee's Air Farming
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Salomon v. Salomon & Co.

House of Lords, 1897.

Report this question

16The rule of majority rule and proper plaintiff comes from:

  1. Daimler
  2. Foss v. Harbottle
  3. Salomon
  4. Macaura
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Foss v. Harbottle

Courts do not interfere in internal management where the majority can ratify.

Report this question

17Under Section 135 Companies Act, CSR spend is of average net profits of preceding three years:

  1. 1%
  2. 2%
  3. 3%
  4. 5%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 2%

Two per cent for companies meeting thresholds.

Report this question

18A prospectus is an invitation to:

  1. Courts
  2. The public to subscribe to securities
  3. Banks
  4. Existing shareholders only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The public to subscribe to securities

A document inviting offers from the public.

Report this question

19The doctrine of ultra vires in company law is illustrated by:

  1. Ashbury Railway Carriage v. Riche
  2. Foss v. Harbottle
  3. Royal British Bank v. Turquand
  4. Salomon
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Ashbury Railway Carriage v. Riche

Acts beyond the objects clause are void.

Report this question

20Validity of contracts formed electronically is recognised under:

  1. Section 10, Contract Act
  2. Section 43 IT Act
  3. Section 65B Evidence Act
  4. Section 10A, IT Act, 2000
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Section 10A, IT Act, 2000

Section 10A IT Act.

Report this question

21Consideration need not be adequate but must be:

  1. In money
  2. Real and lawful
  3. Equal
  4. In writing
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Real and lawful

Value is for the parties to decide.

Report this question

22A voidable contract is enforceable:

  1. By the court only
  2. Never
  3. At the option of the aggrieved party
  4. By either party
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. At the option of the aggrieved party

E.g. contracts induced by coercion.

Report this question
