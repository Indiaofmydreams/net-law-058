14 practice questions with answers

1Under Section 2(h), a contract is:

  1. An agreement enforceable by law
  2. Any agreement
  3. A promise supported by consideration
  4. A proposal accepted in writing
Show answer

Answer: A. An agreement enforceable by law

Report this question

2An agreement enforceable at the option of one or more parties, but not of the other(s), is:

  1. Void
  2. Illegal
  3. Unenforceable
  4. Voidable
Show answer

Answer: D. Voidable

Report this question

3A 'void contract' under Section 2(j) is one that:

  1. Is void from the beginning
  2. Ceases to be enforceable by law
  3. Is enforceable at the option of one party
  4. Is opposed to public policy
Show answer

Answer: B. Ceases to be enforceable by law

Report this question

4In Lalman Shukla v. Gauri Dutt, it was held that:

  1. Performing the conditions of an offer without knowing of it is not acceptance
  2. Acceptance can be inferred from silence
  3. Rewards can never be claimed
  4. Communication of acceptance is unnecessary
Show answer

Answer: A. Performing the conditions of an offer without knowing of it is not acceptance

Report this question

5Carlill v. Carbolic Smoke Ball Co. established that:

  1. An advertisement is only an invitation to treat
  2. The contract was a wager
  3. A general offer can be accepted by performing its conditions
  4. Offers must be addressed to specific persons
Show answer

Answer: C. A general offer can be accepted by performing its conditions

Report this question

6In Harvey v. Facey, the telegram quoting the 'lowest price' was held to be:

  1. An offer
  2. An acceptance
  3. A mere statement of information, not an offer
  4. A counter-offer
Show answer

Answer: C. A mere statement of information, not an offer

Report this question

7Display of goods with prices in a self-service shop is:

  1. An offer
  2. An acceptance
  3. An invitation to treat
  4. A promise
Show answer

Answer: C. An invitation to treat

Report this question

8Under Section 4, communication of acceptance is complete as against the proposer when:

  1. It is put in a course of transmission to him, so as to be out of the acceptor's power to withdraw it
  2. It comes to the proposer's knowledge
  3. The proposer replies to it
  4. The proposer reads it
Show answer

Answer: A. It is put in a course of transmission to him, so as to be out of the acceptor's power to withdraw it

Report this question

9Under Section 5, a proposal may be revoked:

  1. At any time before communication of acceptance is complete as against the proposer
  2. At any time before the acceptor reads the proposal
  3. Only with the acceptor's consent
  4. Only within a reasonable time
Show answer

Answer: A. At any time before communication of acceptance is complete as against the proposer

Report this question

10In Bhagwandas Goverdhandas Kedia v. Girdharilal Parshottamdas, the Supreme Court held that for contracts by telephone:

  1. The contract is formed where the offer is made
  2. Postal rule applies
  3. The contract is formed where the acceptance is received
  4. No contract can be formed
Show answer

Answer: C. The contract is formed where the acceptance is received

Report this question

11Under Section 7, acceptance must be:

  1. Partial
  2. Conditional
  3. Absolute and unqualified
  4. Impliedly silent
Show answer

Answer: C. Absolute and unqualified

Report this question

12In Hyde v. Wrench, a counter-offer was held to:

  1. Reject the original offer
  2. Keep the original offer alive
  3. Amount to acceptance
  4. Be a mere inquiry
Show answer

Answer: A. Reject the original offer

Report this question

13In Felthouse v. Bindley, it was held that:

  1. Silence can be imposed as acceptance by the offeror
  2. Offers are irrevocable
  3. The offeror cannot impose acceptance by silence on the offeree
  4. Acceptance must be in writing
Show answer

Answer: C. The offeror cannot impose acceptance by silence on the offeree

14Which is NOT an essential of a valid contract under Section 10?

  1. Free consent
  2. Lawful object
  3. Competent parties
  4. Writing and registration in every case
