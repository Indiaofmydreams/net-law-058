THE EXAM EXPLAINED

What is AIBE?

The All India Bar Examination is the qualifying test every law graduate takes to practise as an advocate in India. This guide covers what it is for, the pattern, marking, pass marks, eligibility and the subjects.

Read this first. The Bar Council of India can change the pattern, the syllabus and the rules for each edition, and third-party sites sometimes disagree with each other. Everything here is marked Reported because it comes from published coverage of recent editions, not from a current official notice. Confirm each point in the latest Bar Council of India notification before relying on it.

AIBE at a glance

Full formAll India Bar Examination
Conducted byBar Council of India (BCI)
PurposeA qualifying test for law graduates. Passing it earns the Certificate of Practice, which advocates need to practise before the courts (Reported)
NaturePass or fail. It is not a ranking exam: there are no ranks, no seats and no cut-off against other candidates (Reported)
ModeOffline, with an OMR answer sheet at test centres (Reported)
LanguageEnglish and a number of Indian languages; check the notification for the list (Reported)
Official websitesBar Council of India; the AIBE portal linked from it

Why the exam matters

  • Advocates enrolled after the rule came into force need to pass AIBE to receive the Certificate of Practice. Advocates enrolled earlier have been reported as exempt (Reported).
  • Without the certificate an advocate cannot practise independently in the courts. Appearing as an Advocate-on-Record in the Supreme Court is a separate qualification.
  • There has been no cap on the number of attempts in recent editions, so a failed attempt is a setback, not an end (Reported).

Dates, fees and registration

These change with every edition and are announced by the Bar Council of India, often with short notice. This section deliberately does not list them. Check the official AIBE portal and the BCI notices for the current exam date, application window, fee, admit card release and result date.

Exam pattern

Question typeMultiple-choice questions only
Number of questions100
Marks1 mark per question, 100 marks in total
Negative markingNone in recent editions, so a wrong answer costs nothing (Reported)
Duration3 hours 30 minutes, with no sectional time limits (Reported)
Subjects19, listed below with the usual number of questions
What you may carry inRecent editions have been described as open book, with unannotated Bare Acts allowed and notes or commentaries not. The rules on this have changed before: read the admit card and the instructions for your edition (Reported)
The consequence of no negative marking. You should answer all 100 questions. A guess costs nothing; a blank can only lose you a mark. Work through the paper once for questions you know, then return and make your best choice on the rest.

Pass marks and result

CategoryQualifying marks
General and OBC45 out of 100 (45%) (Reported)
SC, ST and persons with disabilities40 out of 100 (40%) (Reported)

The thresholds have been revised in the past, and older sources quote lower figures. Confirm the current values in the notification. The mock-test results on this site compare your score with the pass marks set in the page configuration.

Who can appear

  • Qualification (Reported): an LL.B. degree (three-year, or five-year integrated) from a university or college recognised by the Bar Council of India.
  • Final-year students (Reported): recent editions have allowed students in the final year or semester of the LL.B., without backlogs, to sit the exam. The Certificate of Practice itself is issued after passing, to advocates enrolled with a State Bar Council.
  • Age and attempts (Reported): no upper age limit and no cap on attempts.

Where the marks are. Constitutional Law, Criminal Procedure and Civil Procedure carry 10 questions each. Together with the Penal Code, Evidence and the contract-and-property group, about two-thirds of the paper comes from six areas. Professional Ethics, Family Law and Public Interest Litigation are small but very predictable.

The new criminal laws

The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita and the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam have replaced the Penal Code, the Code of Criminal Procedure and the Evidence Act for the criminal law subjects, and recent AIBE editions were reported to be testing them. Learn the new provisions with their old equivalents, because questions can be framed either way. Confirm in the notification which version of each Act your edition expects.

What the questions are like

  • Direct and provision-based. Most questions test a specific rule, a definition, a time limit or the effect of a section, not long analysis.
  • Moderate difficulty, wide syllabus. The challenge is coverage across 19 subjects, not depth in one.
  • About two minutes per question. 210 minutes for 100 questions gives you time to look provisions up if your edition allows Bare Acts, so knowing where things are matters as much as memory.

How to prepare

  1. Start with the heavy six. Constitutional Law, CrPC/BNSS, CPC and Limitation, IPC/BNS, Evidence/BSA, and the contract-and-property group make up most of the marks.
  2. Learn the new codes with their old numbers. Make a two-column table of the old and new provision for the sections you are likely to meet.
  3. Bank the easy marks. Professional Ethics (the Advocates Act and the Bar Council Rules), Family Law and Public Interest Litigation are short, repetitive and close to the text.
  4. Practise untimed, then add pace. Use the Practice Arena subject by subject and read every explanation. Later, time yourself off-site at about two minutes per question.
  5. Practise looking things up. If your edition allows Bare Acts, train yourself to find a provision in under half a minute, and never rely on a marked-up copy unless the rules say you may.
  6. Take a mock, then work the notebook. Submit a full paper, compare your score with the pass mark, then clear your revision notebook before the next one.
