Designs Act 2000, Geographical Indications Act 1999 and Other IP Statutes

iOverview

Shorter, definition- and number-based questions: design definition and term, GI definition and term, PPVFR rights.

★Must know

  1. DESIGNS ACT 2000 - Sec 2(d) 'design': only the features of shape, configuration, pattern, ornament or composition of lines or colours applied to any article, whether in two dimensional or three dimensional or in both forms, by any industrial process or means, whether manual, mechanical or chemical, separate or combined, which in the finished article appeal to and are judged solely by the eye; but does not include any mode or principle of construction or anything which is in substance a mere mechanical device, and does not include any trade mark, property mark or artistic work under the Copyright Act. Registrable if NEW or ORIGINAL, not previously published or used anywhere in India or elsewhere before the filing date (priority), significantly distinguishable from known designs and not contrary to public order or morality (Secs 4, 6). Sec 11 - copyright in a registered design: TEN years from the date of registration, extendable by a further FIVE years (total fifteen). Sec 19 - cancellation on grounds including prior registration, prior publication, not a design, and so on. Sec 22 - PIRACY of a registered design: applying the design for sale to any article, importing, publishing - liable to pay to the proprietor a sum not exceeding Rs 25,000 as a contract debt for any one contravention (total not exceeding Rs 50,000 recoverable in respect of any one design) or the proprietor may sue for damages and injunction (verify the monetary caps in the bare Act). Interface with the Copyright Act: Sec 15(2) Copyright Act - copyright in a design registrable under the Designs Act but not registered ceases as soon as any article to which the design has been applied has been reproduced more than fifty times by an industrial process (Microfibres Inc v Girdhar & Co, 2006, Delhi HC - designs applied to articles: the line between copyright in artistic works and design). Dissimilarity: a design is for aesthetic features judged by the eye; a patent is for functional invention.
  2. GEOGRAPHICAL INDICATIONS OF GOODS (REGISTRATION AND PROTECTION) ACT 1999 (in force 15 September 2003): Sec 2(1)(e) 'geographical indication' in relation to goods means an indication which identifies such goods as agricultural goods, natural goods or manufactured goods as originating, or manufactured in the territory of a country, or a region or locality in that territory, where a given quality, reputation or other characteristic of such goods is essentially attributable to its geographical origin and in case where such goods are manufactured goods one of the activities of either the production or of processing or preparation of the goods concerned takes place in such territory, region or locality. Registration: GI Registry, Chennai; Register in Part A (the GI) and Part B (authorised users); applicants - association of persons/ producers/ organisation/ authority established by law (not individuals); registration is for TEN years, renewable indefinitely (Sec 18); the registration of GI cannot be assigned, transmitted, licensed or pledged (Sec 24); authorised users must register (Sec 17). First GI registered: Darjeeling Tea (2004-05). Infringement (Sec 22): use of a GI by an unauthorised person which misleads the public, constitutes unfair competition, or falsely represents; penalties (Sec 39: imprisonment 6 months to 3 years and fine Rs 50,000 to Rs 2 lakh). India's GIs include Basmati rice, Kanchipuram silk, Banarasi sarees, Alphonso mango, Tirupati laddu. TRIPS Arts 22-24: additional protection for wines and spirits.
  3. PROTECTION OF PLANT VARIETIES AND FARMERS' RIGHTS ACT 2001 (PPVFR): PPV&FR Authority (New Delhi); registrable plant varieties must be new, distinct, uniform and stable (DUS); breeder's rights - exclusive right to produce, sell, market, distribute, import or export the variety; term - 18 years for trees and vines; 15 years for other crops (and extant varieties 15 years from notification); FARMERS' RIGHTS: farmers can save, use, sow, resow, exchange, share or sell farm produce including seed of a protected variety (but not sell branded seed); right to reward and recognition for conservation; benefit sharing; researcher's exemption. A landmark dispute: PepsiCo India Holdings v Kavitha Kuruganti (Delhi HC 2024-25) - challenges to the registration of the potato variety FL-2027 (check status). Semiconductor Integrated Circuits Layout-Design Act 2000: 10 years registered layout-design protection. Biological Diversity Act 2002: Sec 6 - applicants for IPR based on biological resources from India must obtain prior approval of the National Biodiversity Authority; benefit sharing; and the Patents Act Sec 10(4)(d)/ 25 require disclosure of source and geographical origin of biological material. Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL) - prevents misappropriation of Indian traditional knowledge by providing prior art to patent offices (neem, turmeric, basmati disputes).

⚖Landmark cases

Microfibres Inc v Girdhar & Co

2006

Copyright in a design applied to articles ceases after 50 reproductions industrially (Sec 15 Copyright Act).

!Exam traps

  • TrapDesign term: 10 years + 5 years (15 total).
  • TrapGI: 10 years, renewable; not assignable; Registry at Chennai.
  • TrapCopyright in unregistered design ends after 50 industrial reproductions.
  • TrapPPVFR: 18 years (trees and vines); 15 years (others).

?Likely questions

  1. Term of registered design: ten years, extendable by five.
  2. First GI: Darjeeling Tea.
  3. GI registration term: 10 years renewable.
  4. Farmers' rights under PPVFR: save, use, sow, exchange, share or sell farm produce (not branded seed).
