Trade Marks Act 1999 and Passing Off

iOverview

Registrability (Secs 9, 11), infringement (Sec 29), passing off tests, well-known marks, and remedies. Cadila and Amritdhara are favourites.

★Must know

  1. Sec 2(1)(zb) TRADE MARK: a mark capable of being represented graphically and capable of distinguishing the goods or services of one person from those of others, and may include the shape of goods, their packaging and combination of colours. 'Mark' includes a device, brand, heading, label, ticket, name, signature, word, letter, numeral, shape of goods, packaging or combination of colours. Sound marks and other non-traditional marks are registrable when they can be represented (Trade Marks Rules 2017). Service marks, certification trade marks (Sec 2(1)(e)) and collective marks (Sec 2(1)(g)) are protected. Sec 2(1)(h) 'deceptively similar': a mark so nearly resembling another as to be likely to deceive or cause confusion.
  2. Sec 9 ABSOLUTE GROUNDS FOR REFUSAL: trade marks (a) devoid of any distinctive character; (b) which consist exclusively of marks or indications which may serve in trade to designate the kind, quality, quantity, intended purpose, values, geographical origin or time of production of the goods or services (descriptive); (c) which consist exclusively of marks or indications which have become customary in the current language or in the bona fide and established practices of the trade (generic) - unless, before the date of application, it has acquired a distinctive character as a result of the use made of it or is a well-known trade mark (acquired distinctiveness/ secondary meaning). Sec 9(2): not registrable if likely to deceive the public or cause confusion; contains matter likely to hurt religious susceptibilities; scandalous or obscene; use prohibited under the Emblems and Names (Prevention of Improper Use) Act 1950. Sec 9(3): a mark shall not be registered if it consists exclusively of the shape of goods which results from the nature of the goods themselves, or the shape of goods which is necessary to obtain a technical result, or the shape which gives substantial value to the goods.
  3. Sec 11 RELATIVE GROUNDS: a mark shall not be registered if, because of (a) its identity with an earlier trade mark and similarity of goods/ services, or (b) its similarity to an earlier trade mark and the identity or similarity of the goods/ services, there exists a likelihood of confusion on the part of the public, which includes the likelihood of association with the earlier trade mark. Sec 11(2): a mark which is identical with or similar to an earlier trade mark and is to be registered for goods/ services not similar shall not be registered if the earlier mark is a WELL-KNOWN mark in India and the use of the later mark without due cause would take unfair advantage of or be detrimental to the distinctive character or repute of the earlier mark (dilution). Sec 11(3): prior user rights (passing off/ copyright). Sec 11(6)-(9): the Registrar's factors for determining a well-known trade mark (knowledge or recognition in the relevant section of the public; duration, extent and geographical area of use; promotion; records of successful enforcement). Sec 2(1)(zg) defines 'well-known trade mark'. Sec 12: honest concurrent use. Sec 13: names of chemical elements/ INNs not registrable.
  4. Registration: application (Sec 18) -> examination (Sec 19) -> acceptance and advertisement (Sec 20) -> opposition within FOUR months of advertisement (Sec 21) -> registration (Sec 23). Duration (Sec 25): TEN years from the date of application, renewable for successive periods of ten years (renewal within six months before expiry; a grace period of six months with a surcharge; restoration). Sec 28: rights conferred by registration - exclusive right to use the trade mark in relation to the goods/ services and to obtain relief for infringement; Sec 31: registration is prima facie evidence of validity; Sec 32: protection of registration on the ground of honest user; Sec 34: vested rights of a prior user prevail against a registered proprietor (continuous prior use); Sec 38: assignment and transmission; Sec 45-46: registered users; Sec 47: REMOVAL for non-use - the mark is liable to be removed if it was registered without bona fide intention to use and not used, or has not been used for a continuous period of FIVE years and THREE months from the date on which it was actually entered in the register (the period of five years commencing from the date of registration plus three months); Sec 57: rectification/ cancellation; Sec 59: correction.
  5. Sec 27: no infringement action for an unregistered trade mark, but the right of action against any person for passing off is preserved (Sec 27(2)). Sec 29 INFRINGEMENT: (1) a registered trade mark is infringed by a person who, not being a registered proprietor or a permitted user, uses in the course of trade a mark which is identical with or deceptively similar to the trade mark in relation to the goods/ services in respect of which it is registered and in such manner as to render the use of the mark likely to be taken as being used as a trade mark; (2) use of a similar mark for identical/ similar goods likely to cause confusion; (4) use of an identical/ similar mark on DISSIMILAR goods where the registered mark has a REPUTATION in India and the use takes unfair advantage of or is detrimental to the distinctive character or repute (dilution); (5) use as trade name; (6) acts of use (affixing, selling, importing, advertising); (8) advertising which takes unfair advantage/ is contrary to honest practices. Sec 30: limits on the effect - use in accordance with honest practices; descriptive use; exhaustion of rights (goods put in the market by the proprietor); Sec 35: use of a person's own name/ place of business (bona fide). Sec 142: remedy for groundless threats; Sec 124: stay of infringement suit pending rectification proceedings.
  6. PASSING OFF (common law tort protecting goodwill): the 'classical trinity' - (1) GOODWILL or reputation attached to the goods or services; (2) MISREPRESENTATION by the defendant (intentional or not) leading or likely to lead the public to believe that the goods/ services are those of the plaintiff; (3) DAMAGE or likelihood of damage to the plaintiff (Reckitt & Colman v Borden, 'Jif Lemon' case; accepted in India - Laxmikant V. Patel v Chetanbhat Shah, 2002). Passing off applies to unregistered marks, trade dress, get-up, domain names (Satyam Infoway v Sifynet Solutions, 2004: domain names are protected as trade marks/ passing off), personality/ celebrity names (Titan Industries v Ramkumar Jewellers, 2012). Transborder reputation: N.R. Dongre v Whirlpool Corporation (1996); Toyota Jidosha Kabushiki Kaisha v Prius Auto Industries (2018, SC - Toyota's reputation in 'Prius' did not extend into India's territory; prior user and territoriality). Prior user vs registered proprietor: S. Syed Mohideen v P. Sulochana Bai (2016) - a prior user of a mark has a better right than a subsequent registrant.
  7. TESTS FOR DECEPTIVE SIMILARITY: (a) Amritdhara Pharmacy v Satya Deo Gupta (1963, SC): ordinary purchaser of average intelligence and imperfect recollection; overall structural and phonetic similarity ('Amritdhara' vs 'Lakshmandhara'); (b) Parle Products v J.P. & Co (1972): the overall impression, 'a man of average intelligence and imperfect recollection', not by side-by-side comparison; (c) Cadila Healthcare v Cadila Pharmaceuticals (2001, SC): factors - nature of the marks, degree of resemblance, nature of goods, similarity in nature, character and performance, class of purchasers, mode of purchase, other surrounding circumstances; a STRICTER test applies to pharmaceuticals (risk of life/ health; medicines with look-alike names 'Falcigo' and 'Falcitab'); (d) Ruston & Hornsby v Zamindara Engineering (1969): infringement vs passing off - in infringement, the question is the resemblance and the likelihood of confusion; the defendant's get-up is not a defence; in passing off, the added matter/ trade dress is relevant; (e) Kaviraj Pandit Durga Dutt Sharma v Navaratna Pharmaceutical Laboratories (1965): infringement action is statutory, passing off is common law.
  8. REMEDIES: Sec 134 - the suit shall not be instituted in a court inferior to a District Court; jurisdiction where the plaintiff actually and voluntarily resides, carries on business or personally works for gain (Sec 134(2)); Sec 135 - relief: injunction (including Anton Piller orders, Mareva injunctions, John Doe orders, orders for discovery), damages or account of profits, delivery up of infringing labels and marks for destruction; punitive damages in egregious cases (Time Incorporated v Lokesh Srivastava, 2005). Criminal: Sec 103 - applying false trade marks/ false trade descriptions: imprisonment of not less than six months up to three years and fine of Rs 50,000 to Rs 2 lakh; Sec 104 - selling goods with false trade marks; Sec 105 - enhanced penalty on subsequent conviction; Sec 115 - cognizance and investigation by an officer not below the rank of Deputy Superintendent of Police; Sec 107 - falsely representing a trade mark as registered. Appeals: from the Registrar/ Controller to the High Court (after the abolition of IPAB). Madrid Protocol system: India designated; international registrations through WIPO.

✎Memory aids

  • Passing off = G-M-D: Goodwill, Misrepresentation, Damage.

!Exam traps

  • TrapRegistered mark: 10 years, renewable; removal for non-use: 5 years 3 months.
  • TrapUnregistered mark: no infringement action but passing off (Sec 27(2)).
  • TrapSec 29(4) dilution applies to dissimilar goods and needs reputation in India.
  • TrapPrior user prevails over a registered proprietor (Sec 34; Syed Mohideen).
  • TrapOpposition period: 4 months from advertisement.

?Likely questions

  1. Duration of trade mark registration: 10 years.
  2. Test of deceptive similarity: Amritdhara/ Cadila.
  3. Classical trinity of passing off: goodwill, misrepresentation, damage.
  4. Sec 134 TM Act: jurisdiction where plaintiff resides/ carries on business.
