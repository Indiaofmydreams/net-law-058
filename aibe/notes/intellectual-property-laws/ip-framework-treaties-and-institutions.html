IP Framework: Types, International Treaties, Institutions and Courts

iOverview

Orientation questions: which statute protects what, treaty names and years, and where IP disputes are heard.

★Must know

  1. Intellectual property = creations of the mind protected by law. Indian statutes: Copyright Act 1957; Patents Act 1970; Trade Marks Act 1999; Designs Act 2000; Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act 1999; Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act 2001; Semiconductor Integrated Circuits Layout-Design Act 2000; Biological Diversity Act 2002 (benefit sharing). Trade secrets/ confidential information have no dedicated statute: protected by contract and equity (common law breach of confidence).
  2. International framework: Paris Convention (1883, industrial property; national treatment, priority right of 12 months for patents/ 6 months for designs and trade marks); Berne Convention (1886, copyright; automatic protection, no formalities; India a member since 1928); TRIPS Agreement (1995, WTO; minimum standards; India amended its laws to comply - product patents for pharmaceuticals/ agrochemicals from 1 January 2005); Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT, 1970; India acceded 1998); Madrid Protocol (international registration of trade marks; India acceded 2013); Budapest Treaty (microorganism deposit; India joined 2001); WIPO Copyright Treaty and WIPO Performances and Phonograms Treaty (1996; India acceded 2018); Marrakesh Treaty (accessible formats for persons with print disabilities; India ratified 2014); Doha Declaration on TRIPS and Public Health (2001) - basis for compulsory licensing flexibilities.
  3. Institutions: Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT) is the nodal department; Office of the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks (CGPDTM, Mumbai) with offices at Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai, Mumbai; Copyright Office (Delhi); GI Registry (Chennai); Cell for IPR Promotion and Management (CIPAM). National IPR Policy 2016: 'Creative India; Innovative India'; seven objectives; DPIIT nodal.
  4. Forum: IP suits are 'commercial disputes' under the Commercial Courts Act 2015, so they go to Commercial Courts/ Commercial Divisions of High Courts (specified value Rs 3 lakh). Pre-institution mediation under Sec 12A of the Commercial Courts Act is mandatory unless urgent interim relief is sought (Patil Automation v Rakheja Engineers, 2022). The Intellectual Property Appellate Board (IPAB) was abolished by the Tribunals Reforms Act 2021; its appellate and rectification functions moved to the High Courts; the Delhi High Court has a dedicated IP Division (2021). Jurisdiction for copyright and trade mark infringement suits: Sec 62(2) Copyright Act and Sec 134(2) Trade Marks Act allow filing where the plaintiff resides, carries on business or works for gain (Indian Performing Right Society v Sanjay Dalia, 2015).

!Exam traps

  • TrapIPAB no longer exists (abolished 2021); appeals go to High Courts.
  • TrapProduct patents for pharmaceuticals in India: 1 January 2005 (Patents Amendment Act 2005).
  • TrapCopyright is automatic; trade marks and patents require registration for statutory rights (unregistered marks protected by passing off).

?Likely questions

  1. Which treaty provides the minimum standards of IP protection linked to the WTO? TRIPS.
  2. Madrid Protocol relates to: international registration of trade marks.
  3. Appeals under IP statutes after 2021 lie to: High Court.
