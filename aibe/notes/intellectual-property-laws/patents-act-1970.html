Patents Act 1970

iOverview

Patentability, Section 3 exclusions (3(d), 3(k)), procedure and opposition, term, compulsory licensing and remedies. Novartis and Natco are must-know cases.

★Must know

  1. Sec 2(1)(j) 'INVENTION' = a new product or process involving an inventive step and capable of industrial application. Sec 2(1)(l) 'new invention' = no prior publication or use anywhere before the date of filing (novelty). Sec 2(1)(ja) 'inventive step' = a feature of an invention that involves technical advance as compared to existing knowledge or having economic significance or both and that makes the invention not obvious to a person skilled in the art. Sec 2(1)(ac) 'capable of industrial application' = capable of being made or used in an industry. Three tests: NOVELTY, INVENTIVE STEP, INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY.
  2. Sec 3 - WHAT ARE NOT INVENTIONS: (a) frivolous inventions or claims contrary to well-established natural laws; (b) inventions the primary or intended use of which would be contrary to law or morality or injurious to public health; (c) mere discovery of a scientific principle or abstract theory or discovery of any living or non-living substance occurring in nature; (d) mere discovery of a new form of a known substance which does not result in the enhancement of the known efficacy of that substance, or mere discovery of any new property or new use for a known substance or mere use of a known process, machine or apparatus unless such known process results in a new product or employs at least one new reactant (explanation: salts, esters, polymorphs, metabolites etc. are the same substance unless they differ significantly in properties with regard to efficacy); (e) a substance obtained by mere admixture resulting only in the aggregation of properties; (f) mere arrangement or re-arrangement or duplication of known devices functioning independently; (h) a method of agriculture or horticulture; (i) any process for the medicinal, surgical, curative, prophylactic, diagnostic, therapeutic or other treatment of human beings or animals; (j) plants and animals in whole or in any part thereof other than microorganisms, including seeds, varieties and species and essentially biological processes for production or propagation; (k) a mathematical or business method or a computer programme per se or algorithms; (l) a literary, dramatic, musical or artistic work or any other aesthetic creation; (m) a mere scheme or rule or method of performing mental act or method of playing game; (n) presentation of information; (o) topography of integrated circuits; (p) an invention which in effect is traditional knowledge or an aggregation or duplication of known properties of traditionally known component(s). Sec 4: inventions relating to atomic energy are not patentable.
  3. NOVARTIS AG v UNION OF INDIA (2013, Supreme Court) - Glivec (beta-crystalline form of imatinib mesylate): patent refused under Sec 3(d); 'efficacy' in Sec 3(d) means therapeutic efficacy (for medicines); the beta crystalline form did not show enhanced therapeutic efficacy over imatinib free base; Sec 3(d) is designed to prevent 'evergreening'; the Court also held that the coverage and the disclosure requirements are distinct. COMPUTER-RELATED INVENTIONS: Sec 3(k) excludes 'computer programme per se'; Ferid Allani v Union of India (2019, Delhi HC): inventions that show a technical effect or technical contribution are patentable even if they use computer programs; Guidelines for Examination of Computer Related Inventions 2017 (Patent Office); later decisions follow the technical contribution approach (e.g., Microsoft Technology Licensing v Assistant Controller, 2023).
  4. PROCEDURE: Sec 6 - who may apply (true and first inventor, assignee, legal representative); Sec 7 - form (application with specification); Sec 8 - applicant to furnish information regarding foreign applications (Form 3); Sec 9 - provisional specification then complete specification within 12 months (priority date = filing of provisional); Sec 10 - contents of specification (title, field, description, claims; best method; sufficiency/ enablement); Sec 11 - priority dates; Sec 11A - PUBLICATION of the application after 18 months from the priority date (or earlier on request); Sec 11B - request for examination (the Patents Rules prescribe the period; the Patents (Amendment) Rules 2024 changed some timelines - verify the current period, formerly 48 months from the priority date); Sec 12 - examination by examiner; Sec 13 - search for anticipation; Sec 15 - refusal/ requirement of amendments; Sec 16 - division of applications; Sec 21 - the application is deemed abandoned if not put in order for grant within the prescribed period; Sec 25 - OPPOSITION: PRE-GRANT opposition (Sec 25(1)) by any person after publication but before grant (grounds include prior publication, prior claiming, public knowledge or use, obviousness, not an invention/ patentable under Sec 3 or 4, insufficiency, non-disclosure of foreign filing information, anticipation by traditional knowledge); POST-GRANT opposition (Sec 25(2)) by any person interested within ONE YEAR from the date of publication of the grant; Sec 43 - grant and sealing; Sec 39 - residents of India require prior written permission of the Controller to file abroad (security); Sec 146 - working statement (Form 27 - the 2024 Rules relaxed the frequency to every three years - verify).
  5. TERM AND RIGHTS: Sec 53 - term of a patent is TWENTY YEARS from the date of filing of the application (for PCT national phase, the international filing date); renewal fees annually (Sec 53(2)); Sec 48 - rights of patentee: for a product patent - prevent third parties from making, using, offering for sale, selling or importing the product; for a process patent - prevent use of the process and the product directly obtained; Sec 47 - Government may use patent for its own purposes; Sec 50 - co-owners; Sec 70 - surrender; Sec 64 - REVOCATION on grounds such as not patentable, prior claim, wrongful obtaining, non-disclosure, false suggestion; by the High Court on petition or in a counter-claim; Sec 66 - revocation by the Central Government in public interest. Bolar exemption: Sec 107A(a) - use for experiments/ research or for development and submission of information required under any law regulating the manufacture, use, sale or import of any product; Sec 107A(b) - importation by any person from a person duly authorised under the law to produce and sell or distribute the product (parallel importation permitted - international exhaustion).
  6. COMPULSORY LICENCE - Sec 84: at any time after THREE YEARS from the date of grant of a patent, any person interested may apply for a compulsory licence on any of the grounds: (a) the reasonable requirements of the public with respect to the patented invention have not been satisfied; (b) the patented invention is not available to the public at a reasonably affordable price; (c) the patented invention is not worked in the territory of India. Sec 92 - compulsory licences in circumstances of national emergency, extreme urgency or public non-commercial use (no waiting period); Sec 92A - compulsory licence for export of medicines to countries with insufficient manufacturing capacity (Doha/ Paragraph 6). NATCO PHARMA v BAYER CORPORATION (2012): the first compulsory licence in India, granted by the Controller of Patents (March 2012) for the cancer drug Nexavar (sorafenib tosylate); upheld by the IPAB (2013) and the Bombay High Court (2014); the Supreme Court dismissed Bayer's special leave petition (December 2014). Government use: Sec 100.
  7. INFRINGEMENT AND REMEDIES: Sec 104 - suit for infringement is instituted in a District Court having jurisdiction (not inferior); if the defendant counter-claims for revocation, the suit is transferred to the High Court; Sec 104A - burden of proof in process patent suits (the court may order the defendant to prove that his process is different where the product is new or there's a substantial likelihood that it's made by the patented process); Sec 106 - groundless threats of infringement; Sec 107 - defences (all grounds of revocation); Sec 108 - reliefs: injunction, damages or account of profits; Secs 109-110: exclusive licensee/ patentee. Standards: Bajaj Auto v TVS Motor (2009, Supreme Court) - patent suits to be decided expeditiously; interim injunction in patent cases; Roche v Cipla (2015, Delhi HC DB) - Tarceva (erlotinib): interim injunction refused; public interest and affordability; Monsanto Technology v Nuziveedu Seeds (2019, Supreme Court) - Bt cotton: the Delhi High Court Division Bench had held that the patented gene sequence was unpatentable under Sec 3(j); the Supreme Court remitted for trial and set aside the finding at the interim stage. SEPs (standard essential patents) litigation: Ericsson v Lava (Delhi HC, 2024 - damages of about Rs 244 crore for infringement, FRAND principles); Delhi High Court has also directed 'pro-tem' deposits by implementers in recent SEP cases (2025-26).
  8. Patent of addition (Sec 54), divisional (Sec 16), convention applications (Sec 135 - priority from foreign application within 12 months), PCT national phase (Sec 138 - within 31 months from priority date), working of patents (Sec 83 - general principles: patents granted to encourage inventions and to secure that they are worked on a commercial scale in India without undue delay and not merely to enable patentees to enjoy a monopoly for the importation of the patented article), Patent Agents (Chapter XVI; Sec 126 - registered agents), Controller's decisions appealable to High Court (Sec 117A, after the abolition of IPAB). Fees: reduced for start-ups, small entities and educational institutions (Patents Rules).

⚖Landmark cases

Novartis AG v Union of India

2013

Sec 3(d): enhanced therapeutic efficacy required for new forms; no evergreening.

Natco Pharma v Bayer Corporation

2012

First compulsory licence in India (Nexavar).

F. Hoffmann-La Roche v Cipla

2015

Interim injunction refused in a Tarceva patent case; public interest.

Ferid Allani v Union of India

2019

Computer-related inventions with technical effect/ contribution are patentable.

Monsanto Technology v Nuziveedu Seeds

2019

Patentability of Bt cotton gene under Sec 3(j) to be decided at trial.

Bajaj Auto v TVS Motor

2009

Expeditious trial of patent suits.

SEP infringement; damages.

✎Memory aids

  • Patentability = N-I-I: Novelty, Inventive step, Industrial application.

!Exam traps

  • TrapTerm: 20 years from FILING date (not grant).
  • TrapPublication: 18 months from priority date; pre-grant opposition: any person; post-grant: within 1 year of publication of grant.
  • TrapCompulsory licence under Sec 84: after 3 years from grant.
  • TrapSec 3(d): known substance + no enhanced efficacy = not an invention.
  • TrapSec 3(k): computer programme PER SE is excluded - not all software-related inventions.

?Likely questions

  1. Patent term in India: 20 years.
  2. Novartis v UoI: Sec 3(d).
  3. First compulsory licence: Natco v Bayer.
  4. Pre-grant opposition: Sec 25(1); post-grant: Sec 25(2).
  5. Sec 107A: Bolar provision.
