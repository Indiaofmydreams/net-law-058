IP Framework: Types, International Treaties, Institutions and Courts

Medium-high yieldRead the full chapter →

Exam traps

  • TrapIPAB no longer exists (abolished 2021); appeals go to High Courts.
  • TrapProduct patents for pharmaceuticals in India: 1 January 2005 (Patents Amendment Act 2005).
  • TrapCopyright is automatic; trade marks and patents require registration for statutory rights (unregistered marks protected by passing off).
2

Copyright Act 1957 (as amended in 2012)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • R.G. Anand v Delux Films 1978 No copyright in ideas; test of substantial similarity from the viewpoint of an average viewer.
  • Eastern Book Company v D.B. Modak 2008 Modicum of creativity standard - rejects 'sweat of the brow'.
  • Amar Nath Sehgal v Union of India 2005 Moral rights of authors survive assignment.
  • University of Oxford v Rameshwari Photocopy Services 2016 Educational use of course packs as fair dealing.
  • Super Cassettes Industries v MySpace Inc 2011/ 2016 Intermediary liability and copyright.
  • Indian Performing Right Society v Sanjay Dalia 2015 Plaintiff may sue where he resides or carries on business.

Exam traps

  • TrapTerm: life + 60 years (author works); 60 years from publication (films, sound recordings, anonymous works).
  • TrapEmployer is first owner under a contract OF service; commissioner (photo/ painting for consideration) owns absent agreement.
  • TrapAssignment: writing; default 5 years and India; lapses if not exercised within 1 year.
  • TrapCopyright protects expression, not ideas; registration is optional.
  • TrapSec 63 minimum jail: 6 months; fine Rs 50,000 to Rs 2 lakh.
3

Patents Act 1970

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Novartis AG v Union of India 2013 Sec 3(d): enhanced therapeutic efficacy required for new forms; no evergreening.
  • Natco Pharma v Bayer Corporation 2012 First compulsory licence in India (Nexavar).
  • F. Hoffmann-La Roche v Cipla 2015 Interim injunction refused in a Tarceva patent case; public interest.
  • Ferid Allani v Union of India 2019 Computer-related inventions with technical effect/ contribution are patentable.
  • Monsanto Technology v Nuziveedu Seeds 2019 Patentability of Bt cotton gene under Sec 3(j) to be decided at trial.
  • Bajaj Auto v TVS Motor 2009 Expeditious trial of patent suits.
  • Ericsson v Lava International 2024 SEP infringement; damages.

Memory aids

  • Patentability = N-I-I: Novelty, Inventive step, Industrial application.

Exam traps

  • TrapTerm: 20 years from FILING date (not grant).
  • TrapPublication: 18 months from priority date; pre-grant opposition: any person; post-grant: within 1 year of publication of grant.
  • TrapCompulsory licence under Sec 84: after 3 years from grant.
  • TrapSec 3(d): known substance + no enhanced efficacy = not an invention.
  • TrapSec 3(k): computer programme PER SE is excluded - not all software-related inventions.
4

Trade Marks Act 1999 and Passing Off

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Cadila Healthcare v Cadila Pharmaceuticals 2001 Factors for deceptive similarity; stricter test for medicines.
  • Amritdhara Pharmacy v Satya Deo Gupta 1963 Ordinary purchaser of imperfect recollection.
  • Parle Products v J.P. & Co 1972 Overall impression test; no side-by-side comparison.
  • Laxmikant V. Patel v Chetanbhat Shah 2002 Passing off - goodwill; honest adoption no defence.
  • Satyam Infoway v Sifynet Solutions 2004 Domain names are protected; passing off applies.
  • S. Syed Mohideen v P. Sulochana Bai 2016 Prior user prevails over a subsequent registered proprietor.
  • Toyota Jidosha v Prius Auto Industries 2018 Territoriality and transborder reputation.
  • Kaviraj Pandit Durga Dutt Sharma v Navaratna Pharmaceutical 1965 Distinction between infringement and passing off.

Memory aids

  • Passing off = G-M-D: Goodwill, Misrepresentation, Damage.

Exam traps

  • TrapRegistered mark: 10 years, renewable; removal for non-use: 5 years 3 months.
  • TrapUnregistered mark: no infringement action but passing off (Sec 27(2)).
  • TrapSec 29(4) dilution applies to dissimilar goods and needs reputation in India.
  • TrapPrior user prevails over a registered proprietor (Sec 34; Syed Mohideen).
  • TrapOpposition period: 4 months from advertisement.
5

Designs Act 2000, Geographical Indications Act 1999 and Other IP Statutes

Medium-high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Microfibres Inc v Girdhar & Co 2006 Copyright in a design applied to articles ceases after 50 reproductions industrially (Sec 15 Copyright Act).

Exam traps

  • TrapDesign term: 10 years + 5 years (15 total).
  • TrapGI: 10 years, renewable; not assignable; Registry at Chennai.
  • TrapCopyright in unregistered design ends after 50 industrial reproductions.
  • TrapPPVFR: 18 years (trees and vines); 15 years (others).
