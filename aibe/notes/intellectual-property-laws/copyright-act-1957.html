Copyright Act 1957 (as amended in 2012)

iOverview

Subject-matter, ownership, assignment, term, moral rights, fair dealing, infringement and remedies. Expect at least one question.

★Must know

  1. Sec 13: copyright subsists in (a) original literary, dramatic, musical and artistic works; (b) cinematograph films; (c) sound recordings. Copyright protects EXPRESSION, not ideas (idea-expression dichotomy): R.G. Anand v Delux Films (1978) - no copyright in an idea; infringement judged by substantial similarity as seen by the average viewer/ reader. Originality: Eastern Book Company v D.B. Modak (2008) rejected the 'sweat of the brow' approach and adopted the 'modicum of creativity' standard (copyedited law reports protected for the creative input). Computer programmes and databases are 'literary works' (Sec 2(o)). Titles and short phrases are usually not protected.
  2. Sec 14 EXCLUSIVE RIGHTS: for literary/ dramatic/ musical works - reproduction, issue of copies to the public, performance in public, communication to the public, making cinematograph films or sound recordings, translations and adaptations; computer programmes - also the right to sell or give on commercial rental. Artistic works - reproduction, publication, communication, adaptation. Films - making copy, selling/ renting, communication. Sound recordings - similar.
  3. Sec 17 FIRST OWNER: the author is the first owner, except: (a) literary/ dramatic/ artistic works made by an author in the course of employment of a proprietor of a newspaper/ magazine - the proprietor owns for publication purposes; (b) photograph, painting, portrait, drawing or cinematograph film made for valuable consideration at the instance of another person - that person (in the absence of a contrary agreement); (c) a work made in the course of the author's employment under a CONTRACT OF SERVICE (employee) - the employer (in the absence of a contrary agreement); contrast contract FOR service (independent contractor) - author owns; (d) Government works - the Government. Cinematograph film: the producer is the author (Sec 2(d)(v)). Sec 2(d) 'author': writer, composer, artist, photographer, producer of film/ sound recording, computer-generated work - the person who causes the work to be created.
  4. ASSIGNMENT AND LICENCE (Secs 18-19A, 30-32): Sec 18 - copyright may be assigned (including future works - the assignment takes effect when the work comes into existence). Sec 19: the assignment must be in WRITING signed by the assignor; it must identify the work, rights, duration, territorial extent and royalty; if the period is not stated, it is deemed FIVE YEARS; if territory is not stated, it is presumed to extend WITHIN INDIA; if the assignee does not exercise the rights within ONE YEAR, the assignment lapses unless otherwise specified. Sec 19(4), 19(5), 19(6). Sec 19A: dispute resolution; Sec 18 provisos (2012 amendment): authors of literary/ musical works in films cannot assign the right to receive royalties for the use of their works other than in the cinema hall (except to legal heirs/ copyright societies) - Sec 18(1) 2nd and 3rd provisos. Compulsory licences: Sec 31 (works withheld from public), Sec 31A (unpublished/ anonymous), Sec 31B (persons with disabilities), Sec 31C (cover versions - after five years of the first recording), Sec 31D (statutory licence for broadcasting of literary and musical works and sound recordings - on payment of royalty). Copyright societies: Secs 33-36 (IPRS, PPL, Novex).
  5. TERM (Secs 22-29): literary, dramatic, musical, artistic works - lifetime of the author + SIXTY years (counted from the beginning of the calendar year following the author's death); anonymous/ pseudonymous works, posthumous works, photographs, cinematograph films, sound recordings, Government works, works of public undertakings and international organisations - SIXTY years from the beginning of the calendar year following publication. Broadcast reproduction right - 25 years (Sec 37); performer's right - 50 years (Sec 38).
  6. MORAL RIGHTS - Sec 57 (author's special rights): independently of the author's copyright and even after assignment, the author has the right to claim authorship (paternity) and to restrain or claim damages in respect of any distortion, mutilation, modification or other act in relation to the said work which is done before the expiration of the term of copyright if such distortion, mutilation or modification would be prejudicial to his honour or reputation. Amar Nath Sehgal v Union of India (2005, Delhi HC): destruction of a bronze mural amounted to violation of moral rights. Performers also have moral rights (Sec 38B, 2012).
  7. Sec 52 FAIR DEALING AND OTHER EXCEPTIONS: not an infringement - (a) fair dealing with a literary, dramatic, musical or artistic work (not a computer programme) for (i) private or personal use including research, (ii) criticism or review, (iii) reporting of current events and current affairs including the reporting of a lecture delivered in public; (aa) making of copies/ adaptation of a computer programme by a lawful possessor for backup/ lawful use; (ab) observing, studying and testing functioning for interoperability; (b)-(c) reproduction for judicial proceedings; (h) reproduction for educational purposes - use by teachers/ pupils in the course of instruction; (i) performance in the course of activities of an educational institution; (za) performance of a literary, dramatic or musical work in a religious ceremony; (zb) accessible formats for persons with disabilities (2012). The Delhi High Court in University of Oxford v Rameshwari Photocopy Services (2016, single judge; the Division Bench in 2016 remanded the matter) held that photocopying of course packs for students falls within the educational exception. Fair dealing is an open-ended standard in the common-law tradition: in the AI era, the Delhi High Court in ANI v OpenAI (2026) considered whether training generative AI on copyrighted news content is fair dealing.
  8. INFRINGEMENT (Sec 51): doing without licence any act which is exclusive right of the owner, or permitting for profit any place to be used for communication of the work to the public, or importing infringing copies. Test of infringement: substantial copying - R.G. Anand. Secondary infringement: sale/ hire/ distribution/ import of infringing copies. CIVIL REMEDIES (Secs 55-62): injunction, damages/ account of profits, delivery up of infringing copies (Sec 58); Anton Piller and John Doe orders recognised by courts (Taj Television v Rajan Mandal, 2003; John Doe orders in sports broadcasting cases); innocent infringer is not liable for damages (Sec 55(1) proviso); suit not to be instituted in a court inferior to the District Court (Sec 62(1)). CRIMINAL REMEDIES (Secs 63-70): Sec 63 - imprisonment of not less than SIX MONTHS up to THREE YEARS and fine of Rs 50,000 to Rs 2 lakh (lesser sentence for non-profit use); Sec 63A - second and subsequent conviction: minimum one year up to three years and fine Rs 1 lakh to Rs 2 lakh; Sec 63B - knowing use of an infringing copy of a computer programme: imprisonment of not less than 7 days up to three years and fine Rs 50,000 to Rs 2 lakh; Sec 64 - police may seize infringing copies without warrant; Sec 65A-65B (2012) - protection of technological protection measures and rights management information. Registration (Secs 44-50): optional; registration is prima facie evidence (Sec 48); not a condition for protection; Registrar of Copyrights.
  9. PERFORMERS' RIGHTS (Sec 38-39, 2012): performers' rights of 50 years; moral rights; Sec 39 exceptions. BROADCASTING: ESPN Star Sports v Global Broadcast News (2008) - live broadcast; Eastern Book Company v Navin J. Desai (2001); Super Cassettes Industries v MySpace Inc (2011; Division Bench 2016): intermediary liability under Sec 51(a)(ii) read with IT Act safe harbour (Sec 79); actual knowledge is needed; Indian Performing Right Society v Eastern India Motion Pictures Association (1977): rights of film producers vs composers; Entertainment Network (India) v Super Cassette Industries (2008): compulsory licences for FM radio/ Sec 31.

⚖Landmark cases

R.G. Anand v Delux Films

1978

No copyright in ideas; test of substantial similarity from the viewpoint of an average viewer.

Eastern Book Company v D.B. Modak

2008

Modicum of creativity standard - rejects 'sweat of the brow'.

Amar Nath Sehgal v Union of India

2005

Moral rights of authors survive assignment.

University of Oxford v Rameshwari Photocopy Services

2016

Educational use of course packs as fair dealing.

Super Cassettes Industries v MySpace Inc

2011/ 2016

Intermediary liability and copyright.

Indian Performing Right Society v Sanjay Dalia

2015

Plaintiff may sue where he resides or carries on business.

!Exam traps

  • TrapTerm: life + 60 years (author works); 60 years from publication (films, sound recordings, anonymous works).
  • TrapEmployer is first owner under a contract OF service; commissioner (photo/ painting for consideration) owns absent agreement.
  • TrapAssignment: writing; default 5 years and India; lapses if not exercised within 1 year.
  • TrapCopyright protects expression, not ideas; registration is optional.
  • TrapSec 63 minimum jail: 6 months; fine Rs 50,000 to Rs 2 lakh.

?Likely questions

  1. Moral rights: Sec 57.
  2. Term of copyright in literary work: author's life + 60 years.
  3. Modicum of creativity: Eastern Book Company v D.B. Modak.
  4. Default assignment period when not specified: five years.
  5. Sec 52 deals with: fair dealing/ exceptions.
