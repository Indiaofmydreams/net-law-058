1

Nature, Incorporation and Types of Companies (Companies Act, 2013)

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Salomon v Salomon & Co Ltd 1897 Separate corporate personality; the company is not an agent or trustee of its members.
  • Lee v Lee's Air Farming Ltd 1960 A sole shareholder-director can also be an employee of his own company.
  • Macaura v Northern Assurance Co 1925 A shareholder has no insurable interest in the property of the company.
  • Tata Engineering and Locomotive Co v State of Bihar 1964 Company is a juristic person separate from its shareholders; veil lifted only in exceptional cases.
  • Erlanger v New Sombrero Phosphate Co 1878 Promoters owe a fiduciary duty and cannot make secret profits.
2

Doctrines: Lifting the Corporate Veil, Ultra Vires, Constructive Notice, Indoor Management, Majority Rule

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Foss v Harbottle 1843 Proper plaintiff rule and majority rule; exceptions protect minorities.
  • Royal British Bank v Turquand 1856 Indoor management: outsiders can assume compliance with internal procedures.
  • Ashbury Railway Carriage v Riche 1875 Ultra vires acts are void and incapable of ratification.
  • Gilford Motor Co v Horne 1933 The company was a sham to evade a covenant; veil lifted.
  • Cyrus Investments Ltd v Tata Sons Ltd 2021 Supreme Court reversed NCLAT; limits on NCLT/NCLAT interference in oppression and mismanagement cases.
  • LIC v Escorts Ltd 1986 Corporate veil may be lifted where justice and public interest require; the Court recognised exceptions.
  • State of UP v Renusagar Power Co 1988 Court may lift the veil to see the economic reality, particularly in tax and welfare contexts.

Exam traps

  • TrapUltra vires acts cannot be ratified even by unanimous vote; irregular acts of directors that are intra vires the company can.
  • TrapIndoor management does NOT protect an outsider with actual or constructive suspicion or in cases of forgery.
  • TrapFoss v Harbottle: the company is the proper plaintiff; the individual shareholder cannot sue for corporate wrongs, subject to exceptions.
3

Directors, Meetings, Resolutions, Audit, CSR, Fraud and Winding Up / Insolvency (IBC)

Medium yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Swiss Ribbons Pvt Ltd v Union of India 2019 IBC upheld; classification between financial and operational creditors is valid.
  • Mobilox Innovations v Kirusa Software 2017 Pre-existing dispute test for operational creditors.
  • Innoventive Industries v ICICI Bank 2017 On default, the adjudicating authority must admit the s.7 application.
  • Essar Steel India v Satish Kumar Gupta 2019 CoC's commercial wisdom is paramount and not subject to judicial review on merits.
  • Jaypee Kensington Boulevard Apartments Welfare Association v NBCC 2021 Homebuyers are financial creditors; balance of interests.
  • Ghanashyam Mishra & Sons v Edelweiss ARC 2021 Approved resolution plan extinguishes all claims not part of the plan; clean slate principle.
  • Lalit Kumar Jain v Union of India 2021 Personal guarantors to corporate debtors are brought within IBC.

Exam traps

  • TrapIBC minimum default threshold is Rs 1 crore (not Rs 1 lakh, which was the pre-2020 figure).
  • TrapCIRP: 180 days + 90 days extension; 330-day outer limit.
  • TrapAGM: within 6 months of year-end; max gap 15 months between AGMs.
  • TrapBoard meetings: 4 per year, maximum gap 120 days.
  • TrapSpecial resolution requires at least 75% of votes cast in favour.
4

Members, Shareholders, Share Capital, Transfer of Shares, Debentures and Charges

Medium yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Illingworth v Houldsworth 1904 Floating charge is an equitable charge on a fluctuating class of assets that crystallises on default or winding up.
  • Re Yorkshire Woolcombers Association 1903 Characteristics of floating charge: a class of assets, present and future; the company can deal with them in the ordinary course.
  • Ruben v Great Fingall Consolidated 1906 Forged share certificate is a nullity; the company is not estopped by the secretary's forgery.
  • Bahia and San Francisco Railway 1868 A company issuing a share certificate is estopped from denying the title of a bona fide purchaser relying on it.
  • Vodafone International Holdings v UoI 2012 Look-through approach rejected; separate entity principle respected in cross-border share transfers (tax context).

Exam traps

  • TrapDebenture holders are creditors, not members.
  • TrapAn unregistered charge is void against the liquidator and the creditors, but the debt itself is not extinguished.
  • TrapA share certificate must be issued within two months of allotment and within one month of the receipt of the transfer application Verify.
5

Directors' Duties, Powers, Remuneration, Related Party and Governance (ss.166-197, 177-179)

Medium yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Regal (Hastings) Ltd v Gulliver 1942 Directors who profit from their fiduciary position must account for the profit, even if the company could not have taken the opportunity.
  • Cook v Deeks 1916 Directors cannot divert a company's business opportunity to themselves; the majority cannot ratify such fraud on the minority.
  • Re City Equitable Fire Insurance Co 1925 A director need not exhibit a greater degree of skill than reasonably expected from a person of his knowledge and experience; not an insurer of the company.
  • Percival v Wright 1902 Directors owe duties to the company and not to individual shareholders.

Exam traps

  • TrapDisposal of substantially the whole of the undertaking (20% or more) needs a special resolution (s.180(1)(a)).
  • TrapIndependent directors cannot get stock options.
  • TrapA director's duties are owed to the company and extend to stakeholders as listed in s.166(2).
6

Mergers, Amalgamation, Compromise and Arrangement; Winding Up; NCLT/NCLAT; Registrar and Striking Off

Medium yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Ebrahimi v Westbourne Galleries Ltd 1973 Just and equitable winding up for quasi-partnership companies where mutual confidence breaks down.
  • Union of India v R. Gandhi, President, Madras Bar Association 2010 NCLT/NCLAT constitutional if staffed with judicial competence and independence.
  • Innoventive Industries Ltd v ICICI Bank 2017 IBC overrides other laws (s.238); a single default triggers CIRP and the moratorium.
  • Vijay Madanlal Choudhary v Union of India 2022 PMLA provisions, including the twin bail conditions, upheld; a 'scheduled offence' includes ss.420, 467, 471 IPC (now BNS equivalents) Verify.

Exam traps

  • TrapScheme of arrangement needs majority in number and three-fourths in value of the creditors or members present and voting.
  • TrapFast-track merger requires 90% consent in value of shareholders and creditors (s.233).
  • TrapNCLT to NCLAT: 45 days; NCLAT to Supreme Court: 60 days (Companies Act). Under IBC: 30 (+15) and 45 days.
7

Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016: CIRP, Moratorium, Waterfall, Avoidance Transactions, Landmark Cases

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Innoventive Industries v ICICI Bank 2017 Default is the sole trigger for CIRP under s.7; the NCLT must admit where default and completeness are shown.
  • Mobilox Innovations v Kirusa Software 2017 A pre-existing dispute that is not spurious or hypothetical bars an operational creditor's application.
  • Swiss Ribbons v Union of India 2019 IBC constitutionally valid; financial and operational creditors are different classes; s.29A is valid.
  • K. Sashidhar v Indian Overseas Bank 2019 CoC's commercial wisdom is paramount; the NCLT cannot examine the merits of the CoC's decision.
  • Committee of Creditors of Essar Steel v Satish Kumar Gupta 2019 Distribution under the resolution plan is a matter of CoC's commercial wisdom; secured creditors' priority protected.
  • B.K. Educational Services v Parag Gupta 2018 Limitation Act applies to applications under ss.7 and 9; three years from default.
  • Ghanashyam Mishra & Sons v Edelweiss ARC 2021 A resolution plan extinguishes all claims not included; clean slate for the successful applicant.
  • Anuj Jain v Axis Bank 2020 Undervalued and preferential transactions of the corporate debtor can be avoided; ss.43-51.
  • Lalit Kumar Jain v Union of India 2021 Personal guarantors can be proceeded against under IBC; the resolution plan does not discharge them.
  • Gujarat Urja Vikas Nigam v Amit Gupta 2021 NCLT cannot go into contractual disputes arising from termination of a contract unrelated to insolvency.
  • Vidarbha Industries Power v Axis Bank 2022 Admission under s.7 is not mandatory even on default; the Adjudicating Authority has discretion.

Exam traps

  • TrapThreshold of default: Rs 1 crore. Financial creditors have voting rights in the CoC; operational creditors do not.
  • TrapCoC approval: 66% of voting share for a resolution plan; 90% for withdrawal under s.12A.
  • Trap180 + 90 days, outer limit of 330 days. Fast track: 90 days (+45).
  • TrapIBC overrides other laws through s.238; the Limitation Act applies.
8

Other Business Forms and Corporate Governance: LLP Act 2008, Producer Companies, SEBI and Listed Companies

Low yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Sahara India Real Estate Corp Ltd v SEBI 2012 OFCDs issued to the public are securities; requirement to comply with SEBI regulations; refund directions.
  • Excel Crop Care Ltd v Competition Commission of India 2017 Penalty under the Competition Act is to be computed on relevant turnover and not total turnover.

Exam traps

  • TrapAn LLP is a separate legal entity (body corporate); a partnership firm is not.
  • TrapProducer companies need a minimum of 10 individual producers or 2 producer institutions.
  • TrapLLP: no partner is liable for another partner's wrongful acts, except for fraud (s.30).
