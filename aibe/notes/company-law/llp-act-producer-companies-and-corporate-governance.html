Other Business Forms and Corporate Governance: LLP Act 2008, Producer Companies, SEBI and Listed Companies

iOverview

Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008: an LLP is a body corporate with a separate legal entity from its partners, perpetual succession, the ability to own property and to sue and be sued.

★Must know

  1. Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008: an LLP is a body corporate with a separate legal entity from its partners, perpetual succession, the ability to own property and to sue and be sued. Minimum two partners; no maximum; at least two designated partners who are individuals, of whom at least one must be a resident in India (stayed for at least 120 days in the preceding year) Verify. Partners' liability is limited to their agreed contribution; no partner is liable for the wrongful acts or omissions of any other partner (s.28); but liability for fraud: the LLP and the partners who acted with intent to defraud are liable without limit (s.30). Every partner is an agent of the LLP, not of other partners (s.26). Registration with the Registrar through the incorporation document (s.11); the LLP agreement governs mutual rights and duties; in the absence of an agreement, the First Schedule applies (equal sharing of profits, no remuneration, etc.). Conversion of a partnership firm, private company or unlisted public company into an LLP (ss.55-58). The LLP Act (s.67) provides that the provisions of the Partnership Act, 1932 do not apply; the Central Government may apply provisions of the Companies Act to LLPs by notification.
  2. Producer companies (Part IXA of the Companies Act, 1956, continued): body corporate of primary producers (farmers) with a minimum of ten individuals or two producer institutions; members' liability limited by shares; at least five and not more than fifteen directors; one member one vote in principle; restrictions on transfer of shares; profits distributed through patronage bonus and limited dividend. Nidhi companies (s.406): Nidhi Rules 2014: mutual benefit societies accepting deposits from and lending to members only, with 200 members and Rs 10 lakh net-owned funds at least Verify. Section 8 companies as described above. Sole proprietorship and partnership (Indian Partnership Act, 1932) are not corporate bodies: the partnership firm has no separate legal personality.
  3. Corporate governance and SEBI: Securities and Exchange Board of India Act, 1992 (SEBI as a statutory regulator since 1992): functions include protecting investors, regulating the securities market and promoting its development; SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015 (LODR) require listed companies to ensure a minimum of one-third independent directors (if the chairman is non-executive and a promoter, one-half), at least one woman director, a separate meeting of independent directors, a minimum of 4 board meetings a year, an audit committee, a nomination and remuneration committee, a stakeholders relationship committee, a risk management committee for the top 1,000 listed entities, a whistle-blower policy, and related-party transaction approvals. Insider trading is regulated by the SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015: trading by an insider while in possession of unpublished price sensitive information (UPSI); the code of conduct for fair disclosure. Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956 regulates stock exchanges and contracts in securities. Depositories Act, 1996 provides for dematerialisation.
  4. Sahara India Real Estate Corp Ltd v SEBI (2012): Optionally Fully Convertible Debentures (OFCDs) issued to millions of investors were 'securities' and public issue; Sahara was ordered to refund the money. National Stock Exchange (co-location) cases: SEBI's penal orders against NSE and its officers. For AIBE, understand SEBI's three functions (quasi-legislative, quasi-judicial, quasi-executive), appeals to the Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT) within 45 days and then to the Supreme Court (s.15Z of the SEBI Act).
  5. Competition Act, 2002: the Competition Commission of India (CCI) regulates anti-competitive agreements (s.3), abuse of dominant position (s.4) and combinations (ss.5-6); the 2023 amendment introduced settlement and commitment mechanisms, leniency plus, and the deal value threshold of Rs 2,000 crore (where the target has substantial business operations in India). Competition Appellate Tribunal: NCLAT hears appeals from the CCI (since 2017). Case: CCI v Coordination Committee of Artistes and Technicians of West Bengal Film and Television (2017); Excel Crop Care v CCI (2017) on penalty and turnover (relevant turnover).

⚖Landmark cases

Sahara India Real Estate Corp Ltd v SEBI

2012

OFCDs issued to the public are securities; requirement to comply with SEBI regulations; refund directions.

Excel Crop Care Ltd v Competition Commission of India

2017

Penalty under the Competition Act is to be computed on relevant turnover and not total turnover.

!Exam traps

  • TrapAn LLP is a separate legal entity (body corporate); a partnership firm is not.
  • TrapProducer companies need a minimum of 10 individual producers or 2 producer institutions.
  • TrapLLP: no partner is liable for another partner's wrongful acts, except for fraud (s.30).
