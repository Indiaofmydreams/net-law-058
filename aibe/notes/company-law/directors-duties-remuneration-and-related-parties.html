iOverview

Fiduciary character of directors: they are agents, trustees (of the company's property) and managing partners.

★Must know

  1. Fiduciary character of directors: they are agents, trustees (of the company's property) and managing partners. Duties under s.166 (statutory codification): (1) act in accordance with the articles; (2) act in good faith to promote the objects of the company for the benefit of its members as a whole and in the best interests of the company, its employees, the shareholders, the community and for the protection of the environment; (3) exercise duties with due and reasonable care, skill and diligence, and independent judgment; (4) not to be involved in a situation in which he may have a direct or indirect interest that conflicts, or possibly may conflict, with the interest of the company; (5) not to achieve or attempt to achieve any undue gain or advantage either to himself or to his relatives, partners or associates (if found guilty, he is liable to pay an amount equal to the gain to the company); (6) not to assign his office, and any such assignment is void. Penalty for contravention: fine from Rs 1 lakh to Rs 5 lakh (s.166(7)).
  2. Case law on duties: Regal (Hastings) Ltd v Gulliver (1942): directors who made profit from an opportunity arising out of their position must account for it; Cook v Deeks (1916): directors cannot divert a business opportunity from the company; Percival v Wright (1902): directors owe duties to the company, not individual shareholders (but a fiduciary duty may arise in special circumstances); Re City Equitable Fire Insurance Co (1925): duty of care and skill is judged by the director's knowledge and experience, with no guarantee of mistakes; Foss v Harbottle: breach of duty is a wrong to the company.
  3. Powers of the Board (s.179): the Board is entitled to exercise all such powers and to do all such acts as the company is authorised to exercise, except those that the Act requires to be exercised in general meeting. Certain powers can be exercised only by Board resolution at its meeting (not by circulation): to make calls on shareholders, issue securities including debentures, borrow money, invest funds, grant loans or give guarantee or provide security, approve financial statements and the Board's report, diversify the business, approve amalgamation, merger or reconstruction, take over a company or acquire a controlling or substantial stake in another, and make political contributions.
  4. Restrictions on Board powers (s.180) - the following require a special resolution: (a) selling, leasing or otherwise disposing of the whole or substantially the whole of an undertaking of the company (substantially the whole means 20% or more of the value of the undertaking); (b) investing the compensation received on compulsory acquisition; (c) borrowing money in excess of the aggregate of paid-up share capital, free reserves and securities premium (temporary loans from bankers in the ordinary course are excluded); (d) remitting or giving time for repayment of any debt due from a director. The company's ultra vires borrowing is void; lenders with notice of the lack of authority cannot enforce it.
  5. Managerial remuneration (s.197, Schedule V): total managerial remuneration to the directors, including managing director, whole-time directors and manager, must not exceed 11% of the net profits of the company; if no or inadequate profits, remuneration is payable as per Schedule V with the approval of the shareholders and, where required, the Central Government (now delegated by the 2017 amendments to shareholders through a special resolution). Independent directors may receive only sitting fees, profit-related commission as approved, and reimbursement of expenses; they cannot receive stock options.
  6. Contracts with related parties (s.188): a company cannot enter into specified transactions with a related party (sale, purchase or supply of goods or materials, selling or disposing of property, leasing property, availing or rendering of services, appointment of any agent for purchase or sale, appointment to an office or place of profit, underwriting the subscription of securities) except with the consent of the Board by a resolution at a meeting; for transactions exceeding the prescribed thresholds, prior approval of the company by a resolution (an ordinary resolution after the 2017 amendment) is required, with the related parties not voting. Transactions between a holding company and its wholly owned subsidiary are exempt from the shareholder approval; arm's length transactions in the ordinary course of business are exempt from the Board and shareholder approval requirements of s.188(1) (but not for listed companies under SEBI LODR). Contravention can be avoided by the company if the contract is entered into with a director or his relative who has not obtained the consent; the director concerned is liable to indemnify the company.
  7. Disclosure of interest (s.184): every director must disclose his concern or interest in any company or companies, bodies corporate, firms or other associations of individuals at the first meeting of the Board in which he participates as a director and thereafter at the first meeting of the Board in every financial year or whenever there is a change in the disclosures. An interested director must not participate in the discussion or vote on the matter (except in a private company where the articles do not forbid it, and subject to certain conditions). A contract in which a director is interested and which is not disclosed can be avoided by the company.
  8. Loans to directors and others (ss.185-186): s.185 prohibits a company from advancing any loan, including any loan represented by a book debt, or giving any guarantee or providing any security in connection with a loan taken by a director or by any other person in whom the director is interested, subject to exceptions (e.g., loans to a managing or whole-time director as part of the conditions of service, loans to a wholly owned subsidiary, loans to a body corporate in which the director is interested, if approved by a special resolution and used by the borrower for its principal business activities, at a rate of interest not less than the prevailing yield on government securities of comparable tenure). A contravention is punishable with a fine of Rs 5 lakh to Rs 25 lakh and the director or other person to whom the loan is given with imprisonment up to six months or a fine of Rs 5 lakh to Rs 25 lakh. Section 186 limits loans, guarantees and investments to 60% of paid-up share capital, free reserves and securities premium or 100% of free reserves and securities premium, whichever is more, unless the special resolution is passed; no loan can be given at a rate of interest lower than the prevailing yield of government securities.
  9. Committees: Audit Committee (s.177): mandatory for listed companies and public companies meeting the prescribed thresholds; at least three directors with independent directors forming a majority; the chairperson and majority of the members should be able to read and understand financial statements; functions include recommending appointment and remuneration of auditors, review of financial statements and related party transactions, scrutiny of inter-corporate loans, valuation of undertakings, and evaluation of internal financial controls; a vigil mechanism (whistle-blower policy) is mandatory for listed companies and companies accepting deposits or borrowing above prescribed amounts (s.177(9)). Nomination and Remuneration Committee (s.178): three or more non-executive directors, at least half independent; Stakeholders Relationship Committee for companies having more than 1,000 shareholders, debenture holders, deposit holders and other security holders; CSR Committee (s.135) as above.
  10. Officer in default (s.2(60)): any whole-time director, key managerial personnel, or where there are none, such director as specified by the Board (and where the Board specifies none, all directors), any person in accordance with whose directions or instructions the Board is accustomed to act, and certain other persons (company secretary, share transfer agents, etc.) who are in default of statutory requirements. Section 447 and other penal sections impose liability on officers in default. Independent and non-executive directors are liable only for acts or omissions that occurred with their knowledge, attributable through Board processes, and with their consent or connivance or where they have not acted diligently (s.149(12)).
  11. Secretarial audit and corporate social responsibility: secretarial audit (s.204) applies to listed companies and other prescribed companies, and a practising company secretary conducts it; the Board's Report must include the secretarial audit report, CSR report, risk management, declaration by independent directors and the directors' responsibility statement (s.134(5)). Maintenance of registers, annual return (s.92) within 60 days of the AGM, and financial statements (s.129; Schedule III) laid before the AGM and filed within 30 days of the AGM (s.137). A defaulting company and each officer in default are liable to penalty. The Companies Act has a graded penalty structure with in-house adjudication under s.454 for civil penalties and compounding (s.441).

⚖Landmark cases

Regal (Hastings) Ltd v Gulliver

1942

Directors who profit from their fiduciary position must account for the profit, even if the company could not have taken the opportunity.

Cook v Deeks

1916

Directors cannot divert a company's business opportunity to themselves; the majority cannot ratify such fraud on the minority.

Re City Equitable Fire Insurance Co

1925

A director need not exhibit a greater degree of skill than reasonably expected from a person of his knowledge and experience; not an insurer of the company.

Percival v Wright

1902

Directors owe duties to the company and not to individual shareholders.

!Exam traps

  • TrapDisposal of substantially the whole of the undertaking (20% or more) needs a special resolution (s.180(1)(a)).
  • TrapIndependent directors cannot get stock options.
  • TrapA director's duties are owed to the company and extend to stakeholders as listed in s.166(2).
