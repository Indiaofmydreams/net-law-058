Members, Shareholders, Share Capital, Transfer of Shares, Debentures and Charges

iOverview

★Must know

  1. Member vs shareholder: a 'member' (s.2(55)) is a subscriber to the memorandum, a person who agrees in writing to become a member and whose name is entered in the register of members, or a holder of shares whose name appears as a beneficial owner in the records of a depository. All shareholders are members; in a company limited by guarantee, there can be members without shares. Rights of members include the right to vote, receive dividends, inspect statutory registers (s.94, s.171), receive notices of meetings, requisition an EGM, apply for relief against oppression, and share in surplus assets on winding up.
  2. Kinds of share capital (s.43): equity share capital (with voting rights, or with differential rights as to dividend, voting or otherwise per rules) and preference share capital (preferential right to dividend and return of capital on winding up). Preference shares must be redeemed within a maximum of 20 years (s.55); infrastructure companies may issue for longer than 20 years in specified cases. Irredeemable preference shares are prohibited. Cumulative, participating and convertible preference are the common varieties.
  3. Types of capital: authorised (nominal) capital - maximum amount stated in the memorandum; issued capital; subscribed capital; called-up capital; paid-up capital; reserve capital (the uncalled amount that can be called only on winding up). Alteration of share capital (s.61) can be done by an ordinary resolution if the articles permit: increase of authorised capital, consolidation, sub-division, conversion of shares into stock, cancellation of unissued shares. Reduction of capital (s.66) requires a special resolution and NCLT confirmation (after notice to the Central Government, SEBI and creditors). Buy-back (s.68) is described in 4.11.
  4. Allotment (s.39): the minimum subscription condition (90% under SEBI rules now) is applicable to public offers; shares must be allotted within the period permitted. Allotment of shares can be made only after the application money is received in a separate bank account; Return of allotment (PAS-3) within 30 days; share certificate to be issued within two months after allotment (s.56). An allotment is voidable at the option of the allottee if the prospectus is misleading. Irregular allotment may be set aside by the NCLT.
  5. Calls on shares and forfeiture: the Articles (Table F) provide for calls; the Board makes calls with at least 14 days' notice; failure to pay allows forfeiture after notice (not less than 14 days); forfeited shares may be re-issued as fully paid for the amount paid; forfeiture must be in good faith and for the purpose for which it is authorised (Re Esparto Trading). A lien on partly paid shares is allowed in the articles for the company; for fully paid shares, a lien is allowed only in private companies by the articles; a public company's articles cannot give a lien over fully paid shares (Table F permits lien on partly paid shares only).
  6. Transfer and transmission (ss.56, 58, 59): transfer requires an executed instrument of transfer (SH-4) accompanied by the share certificate and delivered to the company within 60 days of the date of execution; the company must register or refuse within 30 days. Refusal to register a transfer: in a private company, the articles may restrict transfer; in a public company, refusal is permitted only on the grounds set out in the Act and SEBI regulations; the aggrieved person may appeal to the NCLT within 30 days of refusal (s.58(3)). Transmission occurs by operation of law, for example death or insolvency of a member; the transmittee gets the same rights as the transferor. Listed securities are freely transferable (s.58(2) provision), and the securities of listed companies must be held in dematerialised form for transfer (from April 2019).
  7. Rectification of register (s.59): the NCLT may order rectification where the name of a person is wrongly entered or omitted; an application can be made by the person aggrieved, any member, the company or the depository; the register of members is prima facie evidence of the matters directed or authorised to be inserted (s.44: shares are movable property transferable in the manner provided in the articles).
  8. Share certificate and its estoppel effect: a certificate under the common seal (now sealed or signed by two directors and the company secretary) is prima facie evidence of title (s.46); the company is estopped from denying the statements in the certificate as against a bona fide purchaser who relied on it: Re Bahia and San Francisco Railway (1868); Ruben v Great Fingall Consolidated (1906) (forged certificate not binding).
  9. Debentures (s.2(30), s.71): include debenture stock, bonds and other securities of a company whether constituting a charge on the assets of the company or not. They are debt instruments carrying interest; debenture-holders are creditors and not members, and have no voting rights. Secured debentures are those secured by a charge; debenture trust deed and a debenture trustee (SEBI-registered) must be appointed for an issue to the public, with a minimum number of subscribers; debentures with voting rights are prohibited (s.71(2)); the creation of a Debenture Redemption Reserve (DRR) and the deposit/investment of an amount in specified instruments (the 2019 rules exempted listed companies, with some conditions) Verify. A company may issue convertible debentures only if approved by special resolution (s.62(3)). Remedies of debenture holders: sue for principal and interest, appoint a receiver, enforce the charge, apply for winding up.
  10. Charges (ss.77-87): a charge is an interest or lien created on the property or assets of a company or any of its undertakings as security, and includes a mortgage. Types: fixed (specific) charge; floating charge (on a class of assets present and future, which crystallises on default or winding up; Illingworth v Houldsworth (1904); Re Yorkshire Woolcombers Association (1903): characteristics); the floating charge allows the company to deal with the assets in the ordinary course of business until crystallisation. Registration: every charge created by a company must be registered with the Registrar within 30 days of creation (extendable by another 30 days on payment of additional fees, and thereafter by up to 300 days with the Registrar's condonation as per the 2019 amendment) Verify; an unregistered charge is void against the liquidator and any creditor of the company (s.77, s.78), though the debt remains payable; the company must also maintain a register of charges (s.85) and the charge-holder can file for registration (s.78). Satisfaction of charge must be filed (s.82). Registration of charges is also now available on the CERSAI (Central Registry of Securitisation Asset Reconstruction and Security Interest of India) for banks and financial institutions under the SARFAESI Act, 2002.
  11. Preferential allotment, rights issue, bonus, ESOP and sweat equity: further issue of shares (s.62): a rights issue offers the shares to existing shareholders in proportion; the offer remains open for 15 days (reduced to 7 days by the 2020 SEBI/MCA relaxation for rights issues) Verify; a preferential allotment requires a special resolution and compliance with pricing rules (private placement offer letter s.42 and Rule 14). Bonus shares are issued out of free reserves, securities premium or capital redemption reserve (s.63) and must be authorised by the articles; they cannot be issued in lieu of dividend. Employee stock options require special resolution (s.62(1)(b)). Sweat equity shares (s.54): issued at a discount or for consideration other than cash to directors or employees for know-how or intellectual property rights; to be issued after one year of commencement of business and by special resolution (start-ups: 10 years).
  12. Securities premium account (s.52): premium amount must be credited and may be applied only for purposes such as issuing fully paid bonus shares, writing off preliminary expenses, writing off commission or discount on issue of shares or debentures, providing for the premium payable on redemption of redeemable preference shares or debentures, and buy-back of shares. Capital redemption reserve account (s.69): on buy-back out of free reserves or securities premium, an amount equal to the nominal value of the shares bought must be transferred to the CRR.
  13. Prohibition on financial assistance for purchase of own shares (s.67): a public company cannot give financial assistance for the purchase of its own shares or those of its holding company, except for employee share schemes (in trust for employees) and loans to employees (up to six months' salary). Prohibition on holding of own shares through a subsidiary (s.19): a subsidiary company cannot hold shares in its holding company; allotment or transfer to a subsidiary is void; Cross-holding rules. Preference share dividend must be paid before equity dividend.
  14. Subscribers and 'inspection': the register of members (s.88), index of members, register of debenture-holders, and register of other security holders; Annual return (s.92) is to be filed within 60 days of the AGM; the register of significant beneficial owners (s.90): an individual who holds, directly or indirectly, at least 10% of shares or voting rights (or exercises significant influence or control) must file a declaration within 90 days; Rule in the Companies (Significant Beneficial Owners) Rules, 2018.

⚖Landmark cases

Illingworth v Houldsworth

1904

Floating charge is an equitable charge on a fluctuating class of assets that crystallises on default or winding up.

Re Yorkshire Woolcombers Association

1903

Characteristics of floating charge: a class of assets, present and future; the company can deal with them in the ordinary course.

Ruben v Great Fingall Consolidated

1906

Forged share certificate is a nullity; the company is not estopped by the secretary's forgery.

Bahia and San Francisco Railway

1868

A company issuing a share certificate is estopped from denying the title of a bona fide purchaser relying on it.

Vodafone International Holdings v UoI

2012

Look-through approach rejected; separate entity principle respected in cross-border share transfers (tax context).

!Exam traps

  • TrapDebenture holders are creditors, not members.
  • TrapAn unregistered charge is void against the liquidator and the creditors, but the debt itself is not extinguished.
  • TrapA share certificate must be issued within two months of allotment and within one month of the receipt of the transfer application Verify.
