iOverview

The case-by-case canvas. AIBE asks 'which case dealt with X?'. Learn each case with its ratio and the legislation it led to.

★Must know

  1. Prisoners and criminal justice: Hussainara Khatoon (1979) - undertrials; speedy trial; free legal aid; Sunil Batra (1978/1980) - prison conditions; solitary confinement; letter-petition; Sheela Barse v. State of Maharashtra (1983) - women prisoners; Sheela Barse v. Union of India (1986) - children in jail; Veena Sethi v. State of Bihar (1982) - forgotten prisoners; Kadra Pahadiya v. State of Bihar (1981) - blinding of prisoners (Bhagalpur); Prem Shankar Shukla v. Delhi Administration (1980) - handcuffing; D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal (1997) - custodial violence; Common Cause v. Union of India (1996) - speedy trial for petty offences; Re: Inhuman Conditions in 1382 Prisons (2016-; 2024 orders on s.479 BNSS); Lalita Kumari (2014) - FIR.
  2. Labour and weaker sections: PUDR v. Union of India (1982) - Asiad; minimum wage; Art 23; Bandhua Mukti Morcha (1984) - bonded labour; Neeraja Chaudhary v. State of M.P. (1984) - rehabilitation; Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corporation (1985) - pavement dwellers; livelihood; M.C. Mehta v. State of Tamil Nadu (1996) - child labour (Sivakasi); Bachpan Bachao Andolan v. Union of India (2011) - child trafficking; Vishaka (1997) -> POSH Act 2013; Medha Kotwal Lele v. Union of India (2013) - implementation; NALSA v. Union of India (2014) - transgender rights; Laxmi v. Union of India (2014) - acid attack; Shakti Vahini v. Union of India (2018) - honour killings; Tehseen Poonawalla v. Union of India (2018) - mob lynching; Navtej Singh Johar (2018).
  3. Environment: Rural Litigation and Entitlement Kendra v. State of U.P. (1985) - Doon Valley limestone quarrying; first major environmental PIL; M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Oleum Gas Leak, 1986/87) - absolute liability; M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Ganga Pollution, 1987/88) - tanneries; M.C. Mehta (Taj Trapezium, 1997) - industries around Taj; M.C. Mehta v. Kamal Nath (1997) - public trust doctrine; T.N. Godavarman Thirumulpad v. Union of India (1996-) - forests; Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum v. Union of India (1996) - precautionary principle and polluter pays are part of Indian law; Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action v. Union of India (1996) - polluter pays; Subhash Kumar v. State of Bihar (1991) - right to pollution-free water and air; S. Jagannath v. Union of India (1997) - shrimp farming; A.P. Pollution Control Board v. M.V. Nayudu (1999) - precautionary principle; M.K. Ranjitsinh v. Union of India (2024) - Great Indian Bustard; right against climate change harms; Vanashakti v. Union of India (2025) - ex post facto environmental clearance (later recalled/modified in 2025 Verify). Legislation traced to PIL: Environment Protection Act 1986 (post-Bhopal), National Green Tribunal Act 2010 (influence of PIL in Bhopal; A.P. Pollution Control Board).
  4. Governance, corruption and police: Vineet Narain v. Union of India (1998) - hawala case; CBI autonomy; Central Vigilance Commission; Prakash Singh v. Union of India (2006) - police reforms (State Security Commission, DGP selection and fixed tenure, separation of investigation from law and order, Police Establishment Board, Police Complaints Authority); Centre for PIL v. Union of India (2012) - 2G spectrum allocation: licences cancelled; Manohar Lal Sharma v. Union of India (2014) - coal block allocations declared illegal; Common Cause v. Union of India (2017) - allocation of coal blocks; Subramanian Swamy v. Manmohan Singh (2012) - sanction for prosecution; Anita Kushwaha v. Pushap Sudan (2016) - access to justice; Dr. Subramanian Swamy v. Union of India (2016) - criminal defamation upheld; Lok Prahari v. Union of India (2018) - disqualification of convicts.
  5. Right to information and elections: Union of India v. Association for Democratic Reforms (2002) and PUCL v. Union of India (2003) - voters' right to know candidates' antecedents (Art 19(1)(a)); NOTA (2013, PUCL); Association for Democratic Reforms v. Union of India (2024) - Electoral Bonds scheme struck down; Anoop Baranwal v. Union of India (2023) - appointment of Election Commissioners; Lily Thomas v. Union of India (2013) - disqualification on conviction; Public Interest Foundation v. Union of India (2019).
  6. Health, food and social welfare: Parmanand Katara v. Union of India (1989) - emergency medical aid is a duty; Paschim Banga Khet Mazdoor Samity v. State of W.B. (1996) - right to emergency treatment; Consumer Education and Research Centre v. Union of India (1995) - workers' health (asbestos); People's Union for Civil Liberties v. Union of India (2001-) - right to food, mid-day meals, public distribution; led to the National Food Security Act 2013; Common Cause v. Union of India (2018) - living will and passive euthanasia; Unni Krishnan v. State of A.P. (1993) - right to education; Mohini Jain (1992); Society for Unaided Private Schools of Rajasthan (2012) - RTE quota; Laxmi (2014).
  7. Constitutional questions via PIL: Kesavananda (no); Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association v. Union of India (2015) - NJAC; S.R. Bommai (not PIL); Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015) - s.66A IT Act; Puttaswamy (2017) - privacy and Aadhaar; Joseph Shine (2018) - adultery; Kaushal Kishor (2023); Supriyo (2023); In re: Art 370 (2023); Special Reference on Governors (2025). The Supreme Court has also taken suo motu cognisance in matters like Aravalli definition (2025), stray dogs (2025), prison overcrowding, and sexual violence (Nirbhaya-type cases).

⚖Landmark cases

M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Oleum Gas Leak)

1987

Absolute liability for hazardous industries; Art 21; compensation based on capacity to pay.

Rural Litigation and Entitlement Kendra v. State of U.P. (Doon Valley)

1985

First major environmental PIL; limestone quarrying stopped; Art 21 and ecological balance.

Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum v. Union of India

1996

Precautionary principle and polluter-pays principle are part of Indian environmental law; sustainable development.

Prakash Singh v. Union of India

2006

Seven-point directives for police reforms.

Centre for PIL v. Union of India (2G case)

2012

122 telecom licences cancelled; natural resources must be allocated fairly through transparent auctions as per Art 14 (the Presidential Reference on this in 2012 limited the auction rule).

Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corporation

1985

Right to livelihood; pavement dwellers cannot be evicted without notice.

Parmanand Katara v. Union of India

1989

Every doctor and hospital must provide emergency medical aid; Art 21.

M.C. Mehta v. State of Tamil Nadu

1996

Child labour; rehabilitation fund; compensation of Rs 20,000 per child.

Vineet Narain v. Union of India

1998

Hawala case; autonomy of CBI; continuing mandamus.

T.N. Godavarman Thirumulpad v. Union of India

1996

Forest conservation; 'forest' defined by dictionary meaning; continuing mandamus; restructured in 2026.

✎Memory aids

  • Environment PILs: Doon Valley (1985) -> Oleum (1987) -> Vellore (1996) -> Godavarman (1996) -> Taj (1997).

!Exam traps

  • TrapAbsolute liability (Oleum Gas) vs strict liability (Rylands v. Fletcher).
  • TrapVishaka (1997) guidelines preceded the POSH Act 2013.
  • TrapThe polluter-pays and precautionary principles were adopted in Vellore Citizens (1996).

?Likely questions

  1. In which case were police reforms directed by the Supreme Court? (Prakash Singh v. Union of India)
  2. Which case introduced the precautionary principle in India? (Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum)
  3. Which PIL concerned the right to livelihood of pavement dwellers? (Olga Tellis)
  4. Which case established absolute liability? (M.C. Mehta - Oleum Gas Leak)
