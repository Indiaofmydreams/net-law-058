1

PIL: Meaning, Evolution, Constitutional Basis, Types and Judicial Techniques

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Art 32Right to move the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights - 'heart and soul' (Ambedkar).
Art 226High Court writ jurisdiction for Fundamental Rights and 'any other purpose' - broader than Art 32.
Art 39AEqual justice and free legal aid.
Art 142Supreme Court's power to do complete justice - used in PIL directions.

Cases in one line

  • Hussainara Khatoon v. Home Secretary, State of Bihar 1979 First reported PIL; speedy trial and free legal aid are part of Art 21; release of undertrials.
  • S.P. Gupta v. Union of India (Judges' Transfer case) 1981 Liberalised locus standi: any public-spirited person can seek redress for public injury.
  • People's Union for Democratic Rights v. Union of India (Asiad Workers) 1982 PIL maintainable; non-payment of minimum wages is forced labour (Art 23).
  • Bandhua Mukti Morcha v. Union of India 1984 Epistolary jurisdiction; bonded labour; commissions; Art 21.
  • Fertilizer Corporation Kamgar Union v. Union of India 1981 Standing of a union/workers to challenge State action; expanded locus.
  • Municipal Council, Ratlam v. Vardhichand 1980 Community can compel the municipality to discharge its statutory duty; public nuisance and Art 21.
  • Sunil Batra v. Delhi Administration 1980 Letter from a prisoner treated as a writ petition; prison reforms.
  • Vineet Narain v. Union of India 1998 Continuing mandamus; CBI independence; guidelines for investigation of high officials.
  • Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan 1997 Guidelines against sexual harassment at workplace; international conventions read into Art 21.

Memory aids

  • PIL tools: 'L-C-A-C-M' - Letter petition, Commission, Amicus, Continuing mandamus, Monitoring committee.

Exam traps

  • TrapPIL is a judicial creation; there is no statute on PIL.
  • TrapHussainara Khatoon (1979) is the first reported PIL; S.P. Gupta (1981) is the case on locus standi.
  • TrapPIL can be filed under Art 32 and Art 226 but not under Art 136 as such.
  • TrapA High Court can issue directions in PIL 'for any other purpose' (Art 226), unlike Art 32 (enforcement of FRs).
2

PIL Procedure: Who Can File, Where, Supreme Court Guidelines (1988) on Letter-Petitions, Categories Entertained and Excluded, Costs and Interim Relief

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Supreme Court PIL guidelines (1988)List of 12 categories ordinarily entertained (bonded labour, neglected children, prisoners, police, women, SC/ST atrocities, environment...).
Excluded categoriesLandlord-tenant; service/pension/gratuity; complaints against Government/local bodies (except listed); admissions to colleges; early-hearing requests.
Art 226High Court PIL under Rules; affidavits and costs.

Cases in one line

  • Dr. Duryodhan Sahu v. Jitendra Kumar Mishra 1998 PIL not maintainable in service matters; Administrative Tribunals have jurisdiction.
  • Janata Dal v. H.S. Chowdhary 1992 Strangers cannot interfere in criminal prosecutions; locus standi limited to bona fide PIL.
  • Raunaq International Ltd. v. I.V.R. Construction Ltd. 1999 Courts must be cautious in tender-related PIL; costs may be imposed.
  • Narmada Bachao Andolan v. Union of India 2000 Court reluctant to interfere in policy decisions in PIL; rehabilitation to be monitored.
  • Holicow Pictures Pvt. Ltd. v. Prem Chandra Mishra 2007 PIL must be genuine; exemplary costs for misuse.
  • Dattaraj Nathuji Thaware v. State of Maharashtra 2005 PIL is not a panacea; courts must filter abuse.

Memory aids

  • Not PIL: 'L-S-G-A-E' - Landlord-tenant, Service/pension, Government complaints, Admissions, Early hearing.

Exam traps

  • TrapBonded labour, neglected children, jail-harassment, police inaction, women atrocities and environmental pollution are entertained; service matters and landlord-tenant are not.
  • TrapPIL by a stranger in a criminal case is not ordinarily allowed.
  • TrapCourts impose costs for abuse; PIL is not a 'matter of right'.
3

Landmark PILs by Theme: Prisoners, Labour, Women, Environment, Governance, Health, Education, Elections

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Arts 21, 23, 24, 32Rights frequently enforced through PIL.
Environment (Protection) Act 1986; NGT Act 2010Statutes shaped by environmental PIL.
POSH Act 2013Enacted after Vishaka guidelines.
National Food Security Act 2013Influenced by the PUCL right to food PIL.

Cases in one line

  • M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Oleum Gas Leak) 1987 Absolute liability for hazardous industries; Art 21; compensation based on capacity to pay.
  • Rural Litigation and Entitlement Kendra v. State of U.P. (Doon Valley) 1985 First major environmental PIL; limestone quarrying stopped; Art 21 and ecological balance.
  • Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum v. Union of India 1996 Precautionary principle and polluter-pays principle are part of Indian environmental law; sustainable development.
  • Prakash Singh v. Union of India 2006 Seven-point directives for police reforms.
  • Centre for PIL v. Union of India (2G case) 2012 122 telecom licences cancelled; natural resources must be allocated fairly through transparent auctions as per Art 14 (the Presidential Reference on this in 2012 limited the auction rule).
  • Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corporation 1985 Right to livelihood; pavement dwellers cannot be evicted without notice.
  • Parmanand Katara v. Union of India 1989 Every doctor and hospital must provide emergency medical aid; Art 21.
  • M.C. Mehta v. State of Tamil Nadu 1996 Child labour; rehabilitation fund; compensation of Rs 20,000 per child.
  • Vineet Narain v. Union of India 1998 Hawala case; autonomy of CBI; continuing mandamus.
  • T.N. Godavarman Thirumulpad v. Union of India 1996 Forest conservation; 'forest' defined by dictionary meaning; continuing mandamus; restructured in 2026.

Memory aids

  • Environment PILs: Doon Valley (1985) -> Oleum (1987) -> Vellore (1996) -> Godavarman (1996) -> Taj (1997).

Exam traps

  • TrapAbsolute liability (Oleum Gas) vs strict liability (Rylands v. Fletcher).
  • TrapVishaka (1997) guidelines preceded the POSH Act 2013.
  • TrapThe polluter-pays and precautionary principles were adopted in Vellore Citizens (1996).
4

Limits, Abuse and Regulation of PIL: Balwant Singh Chaufal Guidelines, Judicial Overreach, Docket Restructuring and 2025-26 Developments

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

State of Uttaranchal v. Balwant Singh Chaufal (2010)Guidelines: bona fide PIL only; credentials; costs; High Court rules.
Constitution Bench remarks (2026)PIL is not to become Private, Publicity, Paisa or Political Interest Litigation.
Continuing mandamusUsed in forests, pollution, police, prisons; being restructured in 2026.

Cases in one line

  • State of Uttaranchal v. Balwant Singh Chaufal 2010 Guidelines to curb abuse; credentials, bona fides, costs.
  • Ashok Kumar Pandey v. State of West Bengal 2004 PIL not to be used for private grievances; courts must verify credentials.
  • Guruvayur Devaswom Managing Committee v. C.K. Rajan 2003 Guidelines on PIL, exercising jurisdiction cautiously.
  • Janata Dal v. H.S. Chowdhary 1992 PIL by strangers in criminal matters restrained; 'meddlesome interlopers'.
  • Divisional Manager, Aravali Golf Club v. Chander Hass 2008 Judicial restraint; judges must not overstep into executive domain.
  • In re: T.N. Godavarman Thirumulpad (restructuring order) 2026 Forest PIL restructured on 13 April 2026 Verify.

Memory aids

  • Balwant Singh Chaufal: 'B-C-C-C' - Bona fide, Credentials, Costs, Concrete public interest.

Exam traps

  • TrapCosts are the standard penalty for frivolous PIL.
  • TrapCourts will not substitute their opinion in policy matters but will enforce fundamental rights.
  • TrapA PIL cannot be used to seek political mileage or publicity.
