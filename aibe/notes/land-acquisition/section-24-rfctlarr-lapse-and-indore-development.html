Section 24 - Transitional Provisions and Lapse (Indore Development Authority)

iOverview

The most litigated provision and the most likely 'case-law' question in the Land Acquisition segment. Know the text, the 5-judge Bench holding (IDA v Manoharlal, 2020), and the 2026 reaffirmations.

★Must know

  1. Sec 24(1): notwithstanding anything contained in this Act, in any case of land acquisition proceedings initiated under the Land Acquisition Act 1894, (a) where no award under Sec 11 of the said Land Acquisition Act has been made, then, all provisions of this Act relating to the determination of compensation shall apply; or (b) where an award under Sec 11 of the said Land Acquisition Act has been made, then such proceedings shall continue under the provisions of the said Land Acquisition Act, as if the said Act has not been repealed.
  2. Sec 24(2): notwithstanding anything contained in sub-section (1), in case of land acquisition proceedings initiated under the Land Acquisition Act 1894, where an award under the said Sec 11 has been made five years or more prior to the commencement of this Act but the physical possession of the land has not been taken or the compensation has not been paid, the said proceedings shall be deemed to have lapsed and the appropriate Government, if it so chooses, shall initiate the proceedings of such land acquisition afresh in accordance with the provisions of this Act. Proviso: where an award has been made and compensation in respect of a majority of land holdings has not been deposited in the account of the beneficiaries, then, all beneficiaries specified in the notification for acquisition under Sec 4 of the said Land Acquisition Act, shall be entitled to compensation in accordance with the provisions of this Act.
  3. Pune Municipal Corporation v Harakchand Misirimal Solanki (2014) held that 'paid' means actual payment to the landowner and that deposit in court is not payment; Indore Development Authority v Shailendra (2018) (Arun Mishra J.) disagreed and referred the issue to a larger Bench; INDORE DEVELOPMENT AUTHORITY v MANOHARLAL (2020) 8 SCC 129, five-judge Constitution Bench (Arun Mishra J.), overruled Pune Municipal Corporation. Holdings: (1) the word 'or' between 'possession' and 'compensation' in Sec 24(2) must be read as 'nor' or 'and' - a deemed lapse arises only when BOTH possession has not been taken AND compensation has not been paid, due to inaction of the authorities for five years or more prior to 1 January 2014; (2) 'paid' in Sec 24(2) does not include deposit of compensation in court; but if compensation was tendered and the owner refused or failed to accept it, there is no lapse (the obligation to pay is satisfied by tender; non-deposit in court does not lead to lapse; the landowner can't take advantage of his own refusal); (3) the five-year period excludes periods covered by an interim order/ stay of the court (the time during stay is excluded); (4) possession is taken in the manner prescribed by the law, i.e. by drawing a panchnama/ inquest report (the standard mode in the 1894 Act); once possession is taken, the land vests in the State free from encumbrances (Sec 16) and there is no divesting; (5) Sec 24(2) does not give rise to a new cause of action to question the legality of concluded proceedings of land acquisition; it applies to pending proceedings in which possession has not been taken or compensation has not been paid for five years (the 'cut-off' of 1 January 2014); (6) Sec 24(1)(a): if no award was made as on 1 January 2014, there is no lapse and compensation is determined under the 2013 Act; (7) the 2013 Act Sec 24 does not apply to concluded acquisitions.
  4. Subsequent purchasers: Shiv Kumar v Union of India (2019) and later cases - subsequent purchasers have no locus standi to challenge the land acquisition or to claim lapse under Sec 24(2), because after notification under Sec 4 (and Sec 6) any transfer is void against the State (the land is encumbered by the acquisition). The Supreme Court reiterated this in a 2026 decision.
  5. Recent Supreme Court (and High Court) decisions, including 2026 rulings, continue to apply IDA v Manoharlal: (a) acquisitions do not lapse if either possession has been taken or compensation has been tendered; (b) delay in taking possession because of litigation does not entitle the owner to the benefit of the lapse; (c) the Supreme Court has allowed delayed appeals by the Delhi Development Authority against High Court judgments that had declared DDA acquisitions lapsed under Sec 24(2) in the public interest; (d) a valid tender satisfies the obligation to pay; refusal to accept doesn't cause lapse. A person who claims lapse must prove inaction for five years prior to the commencement date.
  6. Sec 24(1)(b) read with Sec 24(2) is the transitory regime: 'where an award has been made - the 1894 Act continues; where the award has been made five years or more before and neither possession nor compensation has been given - lapse'. The 'five years' is counted back from 1 January 2014 (i.e., the award on or before 1 January 2009).

⚖Landmark cases

Indore Development Authority v Manoharlal

2020, 5-judge Bench

Sec 24(2) - lapse only on cumulative failure to take possession AND pay compensation for 5 years; 'paid' excludes court deposit but tender suffices; periods of stay excluded; no fresh cause of action.

Pune Municipal Corporation v Harakchand Misirimal Solanki

2014

Overruled by IDA v Manoharlal.

Shiv Kumar v Union of India

2019

Subsequent purchasers cannot challenge acquisition on the ground of lapse.

!Exam traps

  • TrapThe 'five-year' period is counted from 1 January 2014 backwards; it excludes stay periods.
  • Trap'Or' read as 'nor/and': lapse needs BOTH non-possession AND non-payment.
  • TrapDeposit in court is not 'payment' but tender is enough to prevent lapse if owner refuses.
  • TrapSec 24(2) does not revive concluded acquisitions; subsequent purchasers have no locus.

?Likely questions

  1. Which Constitution Bench decision interprets Sec 24(2)? Indore Development Authority v Manoharlal.
  2. Under Sec 24(2), the acquisition lapses if: neither physical possession was taken nor compensation paid for 5 years or more before 1 Jan 2014.
  3. Section 24(1)(a): no award as on 1 January 2014 - compensation per 2013 Act.
