Compensation and R&R under the 2013 Act (Chapter IV, V and VI)

iOverview

Market value, multiplier, solatium, interest and the R&R entitlements are favourite numerical questions.

★Must know

  1. Sec 26 MARKET VALUE: the Collector shall adopt the following criteria in assessing and determining the market value of the land: (a) the market value, if any, specified in the Indian Stamp Act 1899 for the registration of sale deeds in the area where the land is situated; or (b) the average sale price for similar type of land situated in the nearest village or nearest vicinity area; or (c) the consented amount of compensation as agreed upon under Sec 2(2) in case of acquisition of lands for private companies or for public private partnership projects - WHICHEVER IS HIGHER. For (b), the average sale price is determined from the highest 50% of the sale deeds registered during the preceding three years in the nearest village. The date for determining the market value is the date of publication of the preliminary notification (Sec 11 notification). Sec 26(2): the market value is multiplied by a factor (1 for urban areas; between 1 and 2 for rural areas based on distance from urban area - First Schedule, as notified by the State).
  2. Secs 27-29: buildings, trees, standing crops and other assets attached to land, and damages sustained, are valued and added. Sec 30: SOLATIUM - the Collector shall, in addition to the market value of the land as provided in Sec 26, in every case, award an amount calculated at the rate of 100% of the compensation amount (market value x multiplier + value of assets attached) in consideration of the compulsory nature of the acquisition. In addition, the Collector shall award interest at 12% per annum on the market value for the period commencing on the date of publication of the Sec 11 notification until the date of award or the date of taking possession, whichever is earlier. Total effect: compensation can be up to 2 x (market value x multiplier + assets) + interest, i.e. in rural areas up to four times the market value (in the original scheme: 'up to 4 times in rural areas and 2 times in urban areas').
  3. SECOND SCHEDULE (R&R ENTITLEMENTS) - illustrative: provision of land for land in irrigation projects (where the acquired land is for irrigation, to the extent possible, each affected family to be allotted 1 acre of land in the command area); offer for developed land (20% of developed land for the land owners whose land is acquired for urban development - 'Land owners' compulsory acquisition for urban development projects - 20% of the developed land); houses (constructed house to affected families displaced, in rural areas of 150 sq.m plinth area, urban areas as per State rules); subsistence allowance (Rs 3,000 per month per family for a period of 12 months); one-time transport allowance (Rs 50,000); one-time resettlement allowance of Rs 50,000; jobs (employment to at least one member of the family, or lump sum of Rs 5 lakh, or annuity of Rs 2,000 per month per family for 20 years, indexed); special provisions for SC/ST families (additional benefits, e.g. one-time allowance of Rs 50,000 additional, free land for community, etc.). Third Schedule: infrastructural amenities (roads, drainage, schools, health centres, post offices, anganwadis, etc.). The figures are indicative; State rules differ.
  4. R&R institutions: the Administrator for R&R, the Commissioner for R&R, the R&R Committee at project level, the National Monitoring Committee, and the Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Authority (Sec 51) which decides references. Sec 64: a person interested who has not accepted the award may require the Collector to refer the matter to the Authority. Sec 74: appeal to the High Court within sixty days of the Authority's award or order. In Deputy Commissioner and Special Land Acquisition Officer v M/s SV Global Mill Ltd (SC, February 2026), the Court held that Sec 74 does not exclude Sec 5 of the Limitation Act 1963, so delay in appeals can be condoned for sufficient cause, including in cases begun under the 1894 Act where compensation is determined under the 2013 Act by virtue of Sec 24(1)(a).
  5. Payment and deposit: compensation is to be paid to the persons entitled; where a person refuses to accept or there is a dispute as to title or apportionment, the amount is deposited with the Authority or court. Delay in payment attracts interest; verify the exact rate and section in the bare Act before answering a numerical question on it.
  6. Tarsem Singh: Union of India v Tarsem Singh (2019) - the Court held that Sec 3J of the National Highways Act 1956 (which excluded the applicability of the Land Acquisition Act 1894 provisions relating to solatium and interest) is arbitrary and violative of Art 14 to the extent that it denies solatium and interest to the land owners whose land was acquired under the NH Act; accordingly, solatium and interest are payable in NH acquisitions. National Highways Act 1956: Sec 3A (notification), 3D (declaration; vesting), 3G (compensation determined by the competent authority; arbitration for disputes - Sec 3G(5)), Sec 3J. Metro Railways, Railways Act also in the Fourth Schedule; their compensation to be on the 2013 Act basis after the 2015 notification.
  7. Compensation principles from case law: (i) market value is what a willing purchaser would pay to a willing seller; comparable sales (Sec 26(1)(b)); deduction for development cost for large tracts; (ii) a residential plot sale deed cannot be used to determine compensation for industrial land (Supreme Court, recent); (iii) compensation is to be calculated as on the date of the preliminary notification; (iv) acquisition of a part of a land; (v) tax: compensation for compulsory acquisition of agricultural land is exempt from income tax (Sec 96 of the 2013 Act; Sec 10(37) Income-tax Act for urban agricultural land compensation).

⚖Landmark cases

Union of India v Tarsem Singh

2019

Solatium and interest apply to National Highways acquisitions; Sec 3J NH Act struck down to that extent.

Deputy Commissioner & SLAO v M/s SV Global Mill Ltd (SC, Feb 2026)

-

Sec 74 of the 2013 Act does not bar Sec 5 Limitation Act; delay can be condoned in appeals.

!Exam traps

  • TrapSolatium under 2013 Act: 100% (vs 30% under 1894 Act). Interest: 12% p.a. from Sec 11 notification (vs 12% from Sec 4 notification in the 1894 Act).
  • TrapMarket value is whichever is HIGHER of the stamp-duty value and the average of highest 50% of sale deeds in the preceding 3 years.
  • TrapMultiplier: 1 for urban; 1-2 for rural, per State and distance from urban area.
  • TrapAppeal under Sec 74 - High Court, within 60 days.

?Likely questions

  1. Solatium under the 2013 Act is: 100% of the compensation.
  2. Market value is determined as: higher of (a) stamp value, (b) average sale price of similar land (highest 50% in last three years), (c) consented amount.
  3. Appeals from the Land Acquisition Authority lie to: High Court.
  4. Tarsem Singh case: solatium & interest apply to NH acquisitions.
