The Land Acquisition Act 1894 (what still matters)

iOverview

Because pending cases and Sec 24 of the 2013 Act refer to 1894 Act sections, AIBE may ask one or two section-level questions. Memorise the process chain.

★Must know

  1. Process chain under the 1894 Act: Sec 4 preliminary notification (notice that land is likely to be needed for public purpose; officers may enter and survey) -> Sec 5A hearing of objections (to the Collector within 30 days; a substantive right - the only opportunity to object) -> Sec 6 declaration (conclusive evidence that the land is needed for a public purpose; to be made within three years of Sec 4 notification until 1984 and ONE YEAR after the 1984 Amendment) -> Sec 7-8 directions to the Collector to take order for acquisition; land marked out and measured -> Sec 9 public notice to persons interested to appear and state their claims -> Sec 11 enquiry and award by the Collector -> Sec 11A (inserted 1984): award must be made within TWO YEARS of the Sec 6 declaration, else the proceedings lapse -> Sec 12: award is final and conclusive evidence as between the Collector and persons interested -> Sec 16: Collector takes possession and the land vests ABSOLUTELY in the Government, free from all encumbrances -> Sec 18: reference to Court (civil court) by a person interested who has not accepted the award - within six weeks from the date of the Collector's award if present or represented, or within six weeks of receipt of notice under Sec 12(2), or within six months of the Collector's award in other cases -> Sec 54: appeal to the High Court from the Civil Court's award.
  2. Sec 17 URGENCY: (1) in cases of urgency the Collector may, on the expiration of 15 days from the publication of the Sec 9(1) notice, take possession of waste or arable land needed for public purpose; (4) the appropriate Government may direct that the provisions of Sec 5A shall not apply (dispensing with the hearing of objections) in cases of urgency. Misuse of Sec 17(4) was a major complaint: courts have held that dispensing with Sec 5A needs real urgency (Union of India v Mukesh Hans, 2004; Radhy Shyam v State of UP, 2011).
  3. Sec 23: matters to be considered in determining compensation - (1) market value of the land at the date of publication of the Sec 4(1) notification; damage sustained due to taking standing crops, severing, injury to other property, change of residence/ place of business, reduction in profits; (1A) an additional amount of 12% per annum on the market value from the date of Sec 4(1) notification until the date of the award or taking of possession (added in 1984); (2) SOLATIUM - 30% of the market value in consideration of the compulsory nature of the acquisition. Sec 24: matters neglected - speculative potential, increase due to the project, disinclination of the owner to part, etc. Sec 28: interest on excess compensation awarded by Court (9% for the first year, 15% thereafter); Sec 34: interest payable by the Collector for delay in payment/ deposit of compensation (9% for the first year, then 15%). Sec 28A: right of re-determination for persons who did not seek reference but are similarly situated, if the Court has enhanced compensation for others (within three months of the Court's award).
  4. Sec 48: the Government is at liberty to withdraw from the acquisition of any land of which possession has not been taken (and the owner is entitled to compensation for damage caused by the withdrawal). Part VII (Secs 38-44B): acquisition for companies - consent required; agreement under Sec 41; Sec 40 requirement of previous consent of the Government. Sec 3: definitions - 'land' includes benefits arising out of land and things attached to the earth. Sec 31: payment of compensation (tender; deposit in court if the owner refuses to receive or there's a dispute).

!Exam traps

  • TrapSec 11A: award within TWO YEARS from the Sec 6 declaration (1894 Act) - not 12 months (that is Sec 25 of the 2013 Act).
  • TrapSec 5A objections can be dispensed with only through Sec 17(4).
  • TrapSec 16 vests title ABSOLUTELY free from encumbrances (Sec 17(2) for urgency).

?Likely questions

  1. Hearing of objections under the 1894 Act: Sec 5A.
  2. Land vests absolutely in Government under: Sec 16.
  3. Solatium under the 1894 Act: 30%.
  4. Reference to Court: Sec 18.
