iOverview

Background questions: Art 300A, eminent domain, the shift from the 1894 Act to the 2013 Act. Recent 7-judge Bench ruling on Art 300A is a hot topic.

★Must know

  1. Eminent domain = the sovereign power of the State to take private property for public purpose upon payment of compensation. Original Constitution: Art 31 (right to property as a Fundamental Right) with compensation; Art 19(1)(f) right to acquire, hold and dispose of property. The 44th Constitutional Amendment (1978) deleted Art 19(1)(f) and Art 31 and inserted ARTICLE 300A in Part XII (Chapter IV): 'No person shall be deprived of his property save by authority of law.' Right to property is now a constitutional right (not a fundamental right) and a statutory/ legal right (Jilubhai Nanbhai Khachar v State of Gujarat, 1994; Vidya Devi v State of Himachal Pradesh, 2020 - right to property is also a human right; the State cannot claim title by adverse possession against a citizen it has dispossessed without following due process of law).
  2. Entry 42, List III (Concurrent List): 'Acquisition and requisitioning of property' - both Parliament and State Legislatures can legislate. Hence State amendments to the 2013 Act (with Presidential assent under Art 254(2)) in several States.
  3. The Land Acquisition Act 1894 (colonial) - broad powers; low compensation; no R&R; 'urgency clause' misuse. It was amended in 1984. The Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act 2013 (RFCTLARR Act or 'LARR Act') - enacted on 26 September 2013 and brought into force on 1 January 2014; it repealed the 1894 Act (Sec 114) subject to savings. The 2015 Ordinances (to dilute consent and SIA requirements) lapsed; a Bill to the same effect lapsed in 2015. Several States (e.g., Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana) enacted State amendments to exempt particular projects from consent/ SIA requirements.
  4. Kolkata Municipal Corporation v Bimal Kumar Shaw (SC, 8 January 2024, 7-judge Bench): right to property under Art 300A is more than a bare right; the State's power to deprive a person of property must follow a procedure encompassing seven sub-rights: (i) the right to notice; (ii) the right to be heard (objections); (iii) the right to a reasoned decision; (iv) the duty to acquire only for public purpose; (v) the right of restitution or fair compensation; (vi) the right to an efficient and expeditious process; (vii) the right of conclusion (the final conclusion of the proceeding). Non-compliance can vitiate the deprivation. Expect a direct question on this.
  5. K.T. Plantation v State of Karnataka (2011): Art 300A requires a law, public purpose is implicit, and compensation is not necessarily a requirement of the Article but arbitrary or illusory compensation can be challenged; Tarsem Singh (2019) and others emphasise that fair compensation is a statutory entitlement. State of Haryana v Mukesh Kumar (2011): right to property is a human right.

2024
