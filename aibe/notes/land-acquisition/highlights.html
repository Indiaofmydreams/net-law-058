Constitutional Framework and Evolution

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Kolkata Municipal Corporation v Bimal Kumar Shaw 2024 Seven procedural sub-rights under Art 300A.
  • Vidya Devi v State of HP 2020 Right to property is a human right; no adverse possession by the State.
  • Jilubhai Nanbhai Khachar v State of Gujarat 1994 Art 300A post 44th Amendment; right to property as a constitutional right.

Exam traps

  • TrapRight to property is NOT a fundamental right but a constitutional right under Art 300A.
  • TrapLand acquisition is in the CONCURRENT List (Entry 42, List III).

The Land Acquisition Act 1894 (what still matters)

Medium yieldRead the full chapter →

Exam traps

  • TrapSec 11A: award within TWO YEARS from the Sec 6 declaration (1894 Act) - not 12 months (that is Sec 25 of the 2013 Act).
  • TrapSec 5A objections can be dispensed with only through Sec 17(4).
  • TrapSec 16 vests title ABSOLUTELY free from encumbrances (Sec 17(2) for urgency).
3

RFCTLARR Act 2013 - Scheme and Process

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Exam traps

  • TrapConsent: 80% for acquisition for private companies; 70% for PPP projects (Sec 2(2)); not required for the government's own use.
  • TrapSec 19(7): declaration within 12 months from Sec 11 notification; Sec 25: award within 12 months from Sec 19 declaration; Sec 38: payment within 3 months; monetary R&R within 6 months; infrastructural R&R 18 months.
  • TrapReturn of unutilised land: 5 years (Sec 101).
  • TrapUrgency clause (Sec 40) - only for defence/ national security/ natural calamities; additional 75% compensation.
  • TrapFourth Schedule enactments (e.g., National Highways Act, Railways Act, Atomic Energy Act, Metro Railways Act, Land Acquisition (Mines) Act) are exempt from the main Act, but compensation and R&R provisions were extended to them by the 2015 Order.
4

Compensation and R&R under the 2013 Act (Chapter IV, V and VI)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Union of India v Tarsem Singh 2019 Solatium and interest apply to National Highways acquisitions; Sec 3J NH Act struck down to that extent.
  • Deputy Commissioner & SLAO v M/s SV Global Mill Ltd (SC, Feb 2026) - Sec 74 of the 2013 Act does not bar Sec 5 Limitation Act; delay can be condoned in appeals.

Exam traps

  • TrapSolatium under 2013 Act: 100% (vs 30% under 1894 Act). Interest: 12% p.a. from Sec 11 notification (vs 12% from Sec 4 notification in the 1894 Act).
  • TrapMarket value is whichever is HIGHER of the stamp-duty value and the average of highest 50% of sale deeds in the preceding 3 years.
  • TrapMultiplier: 1 for urban; 1-2 for rural, per State and distance from urban area.
  • TrapAppeal under Sec 74 - High Court, within 60 days.
5

Section 24 - Transitional Provisions and Lapse (Indore Development Authority)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Indore Development Authority v Manoharlal 2020, 5-judge Bench Sec 24(2) - lapse only on cumulative failure to take possession AND pay compensation for 5 years; 'paid' excludes court deposit but tender suffices; periods of stay excluded; no fresh cause of action.
  • Pune Municipal Corporation v Harakchand Misirimal Solanki 2014 Overruled by IDA v Manoharlal.
  • Shiv Kumar v Union of India 2019 Subsequent purchasers cannot challenge acquisition on the ground of lapse.

Exam traps

  • TrapThe 'five-year' period is counted from 1 January 2014 backwards; it excludes stay periods.
  • Trap'Or' read as 'nor/and': lapse needs BOTH non-possession AND non-payment.
  • TrapDeposit in court is not 'payment' but tender is enough to prevent lapse if owner refuses.
  • TrapSec 24(2) does not revive concluded acquisitions; subsequent purchasers have no locus.
