RFCTLARR Act 2013 - Scheme and Process

iOverview

The Act itself: objectives, applicability, consent, SIA, food security, notifications, timelines, award, payment and possession.

★Must know

  1. Objects: (i) fair compensation and transparency; (ii) rehabilitation and resettlement (R&R) of affected families; (iii) informed consultation and participation of affected persons (Gram Sabha, Panchayat, Municipal bodies); (iv) minimise displacement; (v) ensure that the interests of the owners of the land and other affected families are protected, and that development is sustainable.
  2. Sec 2(1) APPLICABILITY: the Act applies when the appropriate Government acquires land for its own use, hold and control, including for Public Sector Undertakings and for public purpose, and includes the following purposes: (a) for strategic purposes relating to naval, military, air force, and armed forces of the Union, including central paramilitary forces or any work vital to national security or defence of India or State police, safety of the people; (b) for infrastructure projects (as listed, including roads, ports, power, railways, irrigation, water supply, sanitation, health, education, sports, etc.); (c) project for project affected families; (d) for planned development or improvement of village sites or any site in the urban areas or provision of land for residential purposes for the weaker sections; (e) for residential purpose to the poor or landless or to persons residing in areas affected by natural calamities; etc. Sec 2(2): the Act applies also when the appropriate Government acquires land for (a) public private partnership projects where the ownership of the land continues to vest with the Government (for public purpose) - requires consent of at least 70% of the affected families; (b) private companies for public purpose - consent of at least 80% of the affected families. 'Affected family' includes land owners and persons whose livelihood is dependent on the land (tenants, sharecroppers, agricultural labourers, artisans), forest dwellers, etc.
  3. Sec 3: key definitions include 'appropriate Government', 'Collector', 'affected family', 'displaced family', 'land owner' (includes persons entitled to compensation, tenants and holders of forest rights), 'market value' and 'public purpose'. Consent is not required where the Government acquires land for its own use for the listed purposes; it is required for private-company and PPP acquisitions as above.
  4. Chapter II (Secs 4-9): Determination of social impact and public purpose. Sec 4: SIA - whenever the appropriate Government intends to acquire land for a public purpose, it shall consult the concerned Panchayat, Municipality or Municipal Corporation at village level or ward level, and carry out a Social Impact Assessment study in consultation with them, in such manner and within such time as may be prescribed (within six months from the date of its commencement). Sec 5: public hearing of SIA; Sec 6: publication of SIA report in local languages and on the website; Sec 7: appraisal of the SIA report by an independent multi-disciplinary Expert Group; Sec 8: examination of proposals for land acquisition and SIA report by the appropriate Government (the Government must be satisfied that the public purpose is served, the proposed acquisition is the minimum required, and the benefits outweigh the social costs); Sec 9: exemption from SIA in the case of urgency (Sec 9 - in case of urgency, the appropriate Government may exempt the SIA, subject to the provisions of Sec 40).
  5. Sec 10: SPECIAL PROVISION TO SAFEGUARD FOOD SECURITY - no irrigated multi-cropped land shall be acquired under this Act (except as a demonstrable last resort); wastelands to be identified for use (the total area of the land acquired in the State must not exceed the limit prescribed). Sec 11: preliminary notification - after the Expert Group's recommendation, a notification giving details of land to be acquired, the nature of public purpose, reasons for acquisition, summary of SIA, particulars of the Administrator for R&R, and place where the SIA report is available; Sec 12-15: Sec 15 - hearing of objections - any person interested may, within 60 days from the date of publication of preliminary notification, file objections to the Collector; the Collector hears and submits the report to the appropriate Government. Sec 16: R&R scheme - preparation by the Administrator; Sec 19: PUBLICATION OF DECLARATION (declaration that land is required for a public purpose) - the declaration must be made within TWELVE MONTHS from the date of publication of the preliminary notification under Sec 11 (otherwise the notification lapses - Sec 19(7)); the declaration is conclusive evidence that the land is needed for public purpose; Sec 20: land to be marked out; Sec 21: notice to persons interested; Sec 23: inquiry and award by the Collector (Sec 23: after hearing the persons interested, the Collector prepares the award of compensation and R&R); Sec 25: PERIOD WITHIN WHICH AN AWARD SHALL BE MADE - the Collector shall make an award within a period of TWELVE MONTHS from the date of publication of the declaration under Sec 19, and if no award is made within that period, the entire proceedings for the acquisition of the land shall lapse (Government may initiate fresh proceedings - the exception that where the Government extends the period for 'reasons recorded', which the court considers for validity).
  6. Sec 38: payment of compensation and taking possession - the Collector shall take possession only after ensuring that full payment of compensation as well as rehabilitation and resettlement entitlements are paid or tendered to the entitled persons within a period of THREE MONTHS for the compensation and a period of SIX MONTHS for the monetary part of rehabilitation and resettlement entitlements listed in the Second Schedule, commencing from the date of the award made under Sec 30; infrastructural entitlements to be provided within 18 months from the date of the award (Sec 38(2)). Sec 40: SPECIAL POWERS IN CASE OF URGENCY - in case of urgency, whenever the appropriate Government so directs, the Collector, though no such award has been made, may on the expiration of 48 hours from the publication of the notice to the persons interested, take possession of any land needed for the purposes of defence of India or national security or for any emergencies arising out of natural calamities or any other emergency with the approval of the Government; compensation includes an ADDITIONAL 75% of the compensation as urgency charges (Sec 40(3)); Sec 40(2): no urgency can be invoked for any purpose other than defence/ national security or calamity; the urgency clause cannot be used for private companies or PPP.
  7. Sec 41: SPECIAL PROVISIONS FOR SCHEDULED CASTES AND SCHEDULED TRIBES - as far as possible, no acquisition in Scheduled Areas shall be made (acquisition only as a last resort); in the case of acquisition in Scheduled Areas, prior consent of the concerned Gram Sabha or Panchayats or autonomous District Councils must be obtained; the affected SC/ST families are to be resettled in the same Scheduled Area (one-third additional R&R benefits - extra benefits under Second Schedule); (Sec 41(3) - consent of Gram Sabha at the stage of SIA).
  8. Sec 96: exemption from stamp duty and fees for transfers under the Act. Sec 101 RETURN OF UNUTILISED LAND: when any land acquired remains unutilised for a period of FIVE YEARS from the date of taking possession, it shall be returned to the original owner(s) or legal heirs, or to the Land Bank of the appropriate Government. Sec 105: the Act does not apply to the 13 enactments in the Fourth Schedule (e.g., National Highways Act, Railways Act, Atomic Energy Act, Metro Railways Acts), but the Central Government extended the compensation and R&R provisions to them by notification (28 August 2015). Sec 107: States may enact laws giving higher compensation or additional benefits. Sec 108: affected families may opt for a more beneficial State law or policy. Sec 114: repeal of the 1894 Act with savings.

!Exam traps

  • TrapConsent: 80% for acquisition for private companies; 70% for PPP projects (Sec 2(2)); not required for the government's own use.
  • TrapSec 19(7): declaration within 12 months from Sec 11 notification; Sec 25: award within 12 months from Sec 19 declaration; Sec 38: payment within 3 months; monetary R&R within 6 months; infrastructural R&R 18 months.
  • TrapReturn of unutilised land: 5 years (Sec 101).
  • TrapUrgency clause (Sec 40) - only for defence/ national security/ natural calamities; additional 75% compensation.
  • TrapFourth Schedule enactments (e.g., National Highways Act, Railways Act, Atomic Energy Act, Metro Railways Act, Land Acquisition (Mines) Act) are exempt from the main Act, but compensation and R&R provisions were extended to them by the 2015 Order.

?Likely questions

  1. Consent of 80% of affected families for: private companies.
  2. SIA is dealt with under: Chapter II (Sec 4-9).
  3. Land unutilised for five years must be returned: Sec 101.
  4. Award must be made within: 12 months from declaration (Sec 25).
  5. Urgency clause additional compensation: 75%.
