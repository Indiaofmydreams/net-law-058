Discharge, Breach, Damages and Quasi-Contracts (Secs 62-75)

iOverview

Damages (Secs 73-74) is the highest-yield topic of the Contract Act, along with novation (Sec 62), restitution (Secs 64-65) and quasi-contract (Secs 68-72).

★Must know

  1. Modes of discharge: performance; mutual agreement (novation, rescission, alteration - Sec 62; remission - Sec 63); impossibility (Sec 56); breach; operation of law (insolvency, merger, death in personal contracts); lapse of time (Limitation).
  2. Sec 62 NOVATION: if parties substitute a new contract for the old, or rescind or alter it, the original contract need not be performed. Novation requires the consent of all parties including the incoming party. Sec 63: a promisee may dispense with or remit, wholly or in part, the performance of the promise, or extend the time, or accept any satisfaction (accord and satisfaction) - without fresh consideration. Sec 64: a party who rescinds a voidable contract must restore any benefit he has received. Sec 65: when an agreement is discovered to be void, or a contract becomes void, any person who has received an advantage under it must restore it or make compensation. Sec 66: rescission of voidable contract: may be done by notice. Sec 67: neglect of promisee to afford promisor reasonable facilities for performance excuses the promisor.
  3. Sec 73 DAMAGES: when a contract has been broken, the party who suffers is entitled to compensation for any loss or damage caused to him naturally in the usual course of things from such breach, or which the parties knew, when they made the contract, to be likely to result from the breach. Compensation is NOT given for remote and indirect loss. Explanation: the means of remedying inconvenience must be considered (duty to mitigate). Rule in Hadley v Baxendale (1854): two limbs - natural/ordinary consequences, and special circumstances known to both parties. Damages: compensatory, never punitive. Exemplary damages are generally not awarded for breach of contract, with limited exceptions (breach of promise to marry; wrongful dishonour of a customer's cheque by a bank). Sec 73 also applies to obligations resembling contracts (quasi-contract).
  4. Sec 74 LIQUIDATED DAMAGES & PENALTY: when a contract has been broken, if a sum is named as the amount to be paid in case of such breach, or if the contract contains any other stipulation by way of penalty, the party complaining of the breach is entitled, whether or not actual damage or loss is proved, to receive from the party who has broken the contract reasonable compensation not exceeding the amount so named or, as the case may be, the penalty stipulated for. Indian law does not distinguish strictly between liquidated damages and penalty - the court awards reasonable compensation (Fateh Chand v Balkishan Das, 1963). The expression 'whether or not actual damage or loss is proved' is subject to: where it is impossible to assess damages, the sum named is a genuine pre-estimate and can be given (ONGC v Saw Pipes, 2003); but where damage is capable of proof, the claimant must prove actual loss (Kailash Nath Associates v DDA, 2015 - forfeiture of earnest money must be reasonable, and if the contract doesn't specify earnest money as security for damages, only the loss proved can be recovered). Forfeiture of earnest money is subject to Sec 74. Sec 74 exception: a stipulation for bail-bond/ recognizance/ similar instrument to the Government (the exception covers bail bonds, bonds to the Government).
  5. Sec 75: a person who rightfully rescinds a contract is entitled to compensation for damage sustained through non-fulfilment.
  6. QUASI-CONTRACTS (Secs 68-72) - 'obligations resembling those created by contract': Sec 68: claim for necessaries supplied to a person incapable of contracting (minor, lunatic) or to his dependants - reimbursement from the property of such person (not personal liability). Sec 69: reimbursement of person paying money due by another in payment of which he is interested. Sec 70: a person lawfully doing anything for another, or delivering anything to him, not intending to act gratuitously, and the other enjoys the benefit, is bound to compensate (State of West Bengal v B.K. Mondal, 1962). Conditions: lawful act; not intended to be gratuitous; the other person enjoys the benefit; the other must have the option to accept or reject (Mulamchand v State of MP, 1968 - Sec 70 applies where the contract fails for non-compliance with Art 299). Sec 71: finder of goods - same responsibility as a bailee. Sec 72: a person to whom money has been paid, or anything delivered, by mistake or under coercion must repay or return it (Sales Tax Officer v Kanhaiya Lal Mukund Lal, 1959: refund of tax illegally collected).
  7. Remedies for breach: damages; specific performance; injunction; quantum meruit (Sec 65/70, remuneration for work done where contract is discharged by the other's breach or the contract is void); rescission. Quantum meruit: claim by a party who has partly performed, where the other party prevented completion.

⚖Landmark cases

Hadley v Baxendale

1854

Remoteness of damage: natural consequence or special knowledge.

Fateh Chand v Balkishan Das

1963

Sec 74: reasonable compensation; the party cannot forfeit more than reasonable compensation; the Court can grant relief even if no loss proved up to the named amount.

ONGC v Saw Pipes

2003

Liquidated damages enforceable where loss is difficult to assess; Sec 74 and arbitral awards.

Kailash Nath Associates v DDA

2015

Forfeiture of earnest money must be proved reasonable; party must show actual loss when quantifiable.

State of West Bengal v B.K. Mondal

1962

Sec 70 - benefits enjoyed, no gratuitous intent.

Sales Tax Officer v Kanhaiya Lal Mukund Lal

1959

Sec 72 - tax paid under mistake/ coercion refundable.

!Exam traps

  • TrapSec 74 is about reasonable compensation 'not exceeding' the named sum - the sum is a ceiling.
  • TrapSec 70 requires the benefit to be ENJOYED - not merely conferred.
  • TrapQuasi-contract is NOT based on consent; it is based on equity and justice.
  • TrapSec 65 applies to void agreements discovered and contracts that become void; Sec 64 to voidable contracts rescinded.
  • TrapDamages for mental agony: not usually granted in breach of contract (exceptions: marriage promise, hotel booking cases in consumer law).

?Likely questions

  1. Sec 73 - remote damages not recoverable; Hadley v Baxendale.
  2. Sec 74 deals with: liquidated damages and penalty.
  3. Sec 72 - money paid by mistake or coercion must be repaid.
  4. Substitution of a new contract for an old one is: novation.
