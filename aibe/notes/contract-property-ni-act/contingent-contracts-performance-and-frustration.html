Contingent Contracts, Performance, Time and Impossibility (Secs 31-58)

iOverview

Doctrine of frustration (Sec 56), time as essence (Sec 55), tender (Sec 38) and contingent contracts (Secs 31-36) are the usual favourites.

★Must know

  1. Sec 31: contingent contract = a contract to do or not to do something if some event, collateral to such contract, does or does not happen (insurance, guarantee, indemnity). Sec 32: contingent on an event happening cannot be enforced until the event has happened; if it becomes impossible, void. Sec 33: contingent on event NOT happening: enforceable when happening becomes impossible. Sec 34: event is the future conduct of a living person - deemed impossible when he does anything which makes it impossible within a definite time. Sec 35: contracts contingent on specified event happening within a fixed time become void if the event does not happen in that time (or becomes impossible). Sec 36: contingent agreements to do or not to do anything if an impossible event happens are void.
  2. Sec 37: parties must perform or offer to perform their promises, unless performance is dispensed with or excused under this Act or any other law. Promisors' representatives are bound unless a contrary intention appears.
  3. Sec 38: TENDER (offer of performance) - must be unconditional; at proper time and place; allow the promisee a reasonable opportunity to ascertain that the thing offered is the thing promised. Valid tender of performance, if refused, relieves the promisor of liability for non-performance and does not forfeit his rights. Tender of money must be of the exact amount (not obligatory to be accepted otherwise), and tender of money does not discharge the debt (the debt continues) but protects from costs/interest.
  4. Sec 39: when a party refuses to perform, or disables himself from performing, his promise in its entirety, the promisee may put an end to the contract unless he has signified his acquiescence in its continuance (anticipatory breach: Hochster v De la Tour; Frost v Knight).
  5. Sec 40: contracts involving personal skill - the promisor must perform personally; Sec 41: acceptance of performance by a third person discharges the promisor even though the promisee is not bound to accept; Sec 42-45: devolution of joint liabilities and rights.
  6. Secs 46-50: time and place: where no time is specified, performance within a reasonable time; where the promise is to be performed on a certain day without application by the promisee, the promisor may perform at any time during usual business hours on that day and at the place where the promise ought to be performed.
  7. Sec 51-54: reciprocal promises. Sec 51: promisor not bound to perform unless the promisee is ready and willing to perform his reciprocal promise; Sec 52: order of performance; Sec 53: prevention by one party makes the contract voidable at the option of the other, who is entitled to compensation; Sec 54: when one party cannot perform the first part, the other need not perform.
  8. Sec 55: TIME AS ESSENCE. If time is of the essence and a party fails to perform by the fixed time, the contract becomes voidable at the option of the promisee. If time is not of the essence, the contract is not voidable, but the promisee is entitled to compensation for loss caused by the delay. In sale of immovable property, time is NOT presumed to be of the essence (Chand Rani v Kamal Rani, 1993), though in rising markets courts take a strict view (K.S. Vidyanadam v Vairavan, 1997; Saradamani Kandappan v S. Rajalakshmi, 2011). Acceptance of late performance without notice of intention to claim compensation forfeits the right to compensation (Sec 55 last para).
  9. Sec 56 FRUSTRATION: (1) an agreement to do an impossible act is void; (2) a contract to do an act which, after the contract is made, becomes impossible or, by reason of some event which the promisor could not prevent, unlawful, becomes void when the act becomes impossible or unlawful; (3) where one person has promised to do something which he knew, or with reasonable diligence might have known, and which the promisee did not know, to be impossible or unlawful, such promisor must compensate the promisee for loss sustained through non-performance.
  10. Indian courts apply Sec 56 as a positive law (not on implied-term theory): Satyabrata Ghose v Mugneeram Bangur (1954). Frustration is not available merely because performance becomes more onerous or expensive (Energy Watchdog v CERC 2017; Alopi Parshad v Union of India 1960). Doctrine applies to leases only in exceptional cases; it cannot apply where the contract contains a force majeure clause (the clause governs - Sec 32 route). Examples: Taylor v Caldwell (music hall burnt); Krell v Henry (coronation procession cancelled - frustration of purpose); Herne Bay Steam Boat v Hutton (distinguished).
  11. Sec 57-58: reciprocal promises in alternative; Secs 59-61: appropriation of payments - the debtor's intimation prevails (Sec 59), failing which the creditor's right of appropriation (Sec 60), failing which payments are applied in discharge of debts in order of time (Sec 61).

⚖Landmark cases

Satyabrata Ghose v Mugneeram Bangur

1954

Sec 56 is exhaustive of frustration in India; 'impossible' includes practical impossibility; no implied-term theory.

Taylor v Caldwell

1863

Destruction of subject-matter frustrates the contract.

Krell v Henry

1903

Frustration of purpose.

Energy Watchdog v CERC

2017

Rise in coal prices does not frustrate a contract; commercial hardship is no excuse.

Hochster v De la Tour

1853

Anticipatory breach.

Chand Rani v Kamal Rani

1993

Time not of essence for immovable property sale unless expressly stated or implied.

!Exam traps

  • TrapSec 56 paragraph 2: contract becomes void on impossibility; the loss lies where it falls, but restitution is allowed under Sec 65.
  • TrapMere increase in cost or difficulty is NOT impossibility.
  • TrapTime of essence for immovable property: not presumed; in commercial/mercantile contracts: presumed.
  • TrapSec 38 tender: tender of goods vs tender of money (money tender does not discharge debt).

?Likely questions

  1. Doctrine of frustration is contained in which section? (56)
  2. Sec 55 time of essence: contract becomes voidable at option of the promisee.
  3. A contingent contract is: collateral event, e.g., insurance contract.
