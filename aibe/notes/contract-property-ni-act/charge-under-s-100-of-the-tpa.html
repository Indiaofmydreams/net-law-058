Transfer of Property Act: Charge (Sec 100)

iOverview

A short but frequently tested concept: charge vs mortgage.

★Must know

  1. Sec 100: where immovable property of one person is by act of parties or operation of law made security for the payment of money to another, and the transaction does not amount to a mortgage, the latter person is said to have a charge on the property; and all the provisions hereinbefore contained which apply to a simple mortgage shall, so far as may be, apply to such charge. A charge is created by (a) act of parties - e.g., a document creating a charge for maintenance, marriage expenses; or (b) operation of law - e.g., seller's charge (Sec 55(4)(b)), buyer's charge (Sec 55(6)(b)), charge of the mortgagor on insurance proceeds, Sec 73, partner's charge, etc.
  2. A charge does not transfer an interest in the property; a mortgage does. A charge is enforceable against any person who takes the property with notice (except a transferee for consideration without notice, Sec 100 proviso: 'no charge shall be enforced against any property in the hands of a person to whom such property has been transferred for consideration and without notice of the charge'). A charge created by act of parties needs registration if the amount is Rs 100 or more. A charge may be created by will (a charge on the testator's property for legacies).
  3. Distinction: a mortgage = transfer of interest; a charge = security without transfer of interest; mortgage can only be created by act of parties; a charge by act of parties or by operation of law; mortgage can be of immovable property only; charge can be of immovable or movable property in other statutes (floating charge under the Companies Act); mortgage confers a right to foreclose in some cases (not charge); charge is enforceable only by sale (as simple mortgage).

!Exam traps

  • TrapCharge is NOT a transfer of interest in property; mortgage is.
  • TrapCharge cannot be enforced against a bona fide transferee for value without notice.

?Likely questions

  1. A charge may be created by: act of parties or operation of law.
  2. Seller's charge is a charge by operation of law.
