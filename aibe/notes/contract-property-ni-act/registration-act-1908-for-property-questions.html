Registration Act 1908 (essentials for property questions)

High yield1 Qs expected6 min readSec 17(1)(a)-(e), 17(1A), 17(2); Sec 18; Sec 23-25; Sec 47; Sec 49

iOverview

Sec 17 (compulsory registration), Secs 23-25 (time), Sec 47 (relation back), Sec 49 (effect of non-registration) are frequent AIBE targets.

★Must know

  1. Sec 17(1) COMPULSORILY REGISTRABLE documents (if relating to immovable property): (a) instruments of gift of immovable property; (b) other non-testamentary instruments which purport or operate to create, declare, assign, limit or extinguish, whether in present or in future, any right, title or interest, whether vested or contingent, of the value of one hundred rupees and upwards, to or in immovable property; (c) non-testamentary instruments which acknowledge the receipt or payment of any consideration on account of the creation, declaration, assignment, limitation or extinction of any such right, title or interest; (d) leases of immovable property from year to year, or for any term exceeding one year, or reserving a yearly rent; (e) non-testamentary instruments transferring or assigning any decree or order of a court or any award when such decree or order or award purports or operates to create, declare, assign, limit or extinguish, whether in present or in future, any right, title or interest, whether vested or contingent, of the value of one hundred rupees and upwards, to or in immovable property. Sec 17(1A) (inserted 2001): documents containing contracts to transfer for consideration any immovable property for the purpose of Sec 53A of the TPA must be registered if they have been executed on or after the commencement of the Registration and Other Related Laws (Amendment) Act 2001; otherwise they shall have no effect for the purposes of Sec 53A.
  2. Sec 17(2) EXCEPTIONS (documents that need not be registered even if they relate to immovable property): composition deeds; instruments relating to shares in a joint stock company not creating rights in immovable property; debentures and their endorsement/transfer; documents merely creating a right to obtain another document (but see Sec 17(1A)); receipts for consideration and assignments of money decrees; Government grants; partition instruments made by a Revenue Officer; certain loan orders under the Land Improvement Loans Act and Agriculturists' Loans Act; and certificates of sale granted to purchasers at public auction by a Civil or Revenue Officer. Wills are optionally registrable (Sec 18(e)); an unregistered will is valid.
  3. Sec 18: optional registration (e.g., wills, instruments not requiring registration under Sec 17, instruments relating to movable property). Sec 23: TIME for presentation - a document (other than a will) must be presented for registration within FOUR MONTHS from the date of its execution; if the document is a decree or order, within 4 months from the day on which the decree or order was made (or becomes final in appeal). Sec 24: documents executed by several persons at different times - within 4 months of each execution. Sec 25: delay - where, owing to urgent necessity or unavoidable accident, a document is not presented within the time, the Registrar may, in cases where the delay in presentation does not exceed four months, direct that, on payment of a fine not exceeding ten times the amount of the proper registration-fee, such document shall be accepted for registration. Sec 32: persons who may present (the executant, claimant, or their agents/representatives). Sec 34: enquiry before registration (admission of execution; identity). Sec 35: procedure on admission and denial of execution. Sec 40: wills - may be presented by the testator or, after his death, by any person claiming as executor or otherwise under the will.
  4. Sec 47: a registered document shall operate from the time from which it would have commenced to operate if no registration thereof had been required or made, and not from the time of its registration (relation back to date of execution). Sec 48: registered documents relating to property, when duly registered, take effect against oral agreements or declarations (unless accompanied by delivery of possession and Sec 53A applicable, Sec 48 - the 'exception' for oral agreements accompanied by delivery of possession which constitute a valid transfer under law). Sec 49: EFFECT OF NON-REGISTRATION: no document required by Sec 17 or by any provision of the TPA to be registered shall (a) affect any immovable property comprised therein, or (b) confer any power to adopt, or (c) be received as evidence of any transaction affecting such property or conferring such power, unless it has been registered. Proviso: an unregistered document affecting immovable property and required by this Act or the TPA to be registered may be received as evidence of a contract in a suit for specific performance or as evidence of part performance of a contract for the purposes of Sec 53A of the TPA, or as evidence of any collateral transaction not required to be effected by registered instrument. (K.B. Saha & Sons v Development Consultant Ltd (2008): collateral-purpose principle). Sec 50: registered document relating to property has priority over unregistered ones.
  5. Registration is constructive notice of the contents (Explanation 1 to Sec 3 TPA). A document registered in an office not having jurisdiction is still valid if registered by an office within the district. Registration does not validate a void document (it merely gives notice and satisfies the formal requirement). The Registrar can refuse registration on prescribed grounds (Sec 71 etc. - in the discharge of ministerial functions; the Registrar cannot adjudicate on title - Park View Enterprises v State of Tamil Nadu, 1990 - quasi-judicial functions not available).
  6. Recent trend: online registration and e-stamping (State portals), Aadhaar-based identification, 'digital' presentation; the Registration Act being re-considered in the Draft Registration Bill 2013, not yet enacted. Real estate: RERA requires registered agreements for sale (Sec 13 RERA).

⚖Landmark cases

K.B. Saha & Sons v Development Consultant Ltd

2008

Unregistered documents can be used for collateral purposes (e.g., to prove the character of possession), not to prove the transaction itself.

Suraj Lamp & Industries v State of Haryana

2012

Only registered conveyance can transfer title to immovable property.

Park View Enterprises v State of Tamil Nadu

1990

Registering authority cannot decide the validity of title.

!Exam traps

  • TrapGift of immovable property: registration is compulsory REGARDLESS of value; sale/ mortgage/ lease - value threshold Rs 100 (lease: 1 year).
  • TrapTime for presentation: 4 months from execution; further 4 months with fine up to ten times the registration fee (so the maximum is 8 months).
  • TrapA registered document operates from the date of EXECUTION, not registration (Sec 47).
  • TrapWills: registration optional.

?Likely questions

  1. Time limit for presenting a document for registration: 4 months (Sec 23).
  2. Fine for delay under Sec 25: up to ten times the registration fee.
  3. Effect of non-registration: Sec 49.
  4. Registered document operates from: date of execution (Sec 47).
