Easements and Licences (Indian Easements Act 1882)

Medium yield1 Qs expected5 min readSec 4-6; Sec 13; Sec 15; Sec 18; Sec 52; Sec 60; Sec 62-63

In this chapter

  1. Overview
  2. Must know (7 points)
  3. Exam traps
  4. Likely questions

Chapter highlights →My highlights →

Tip: select any text to highlight it or ask a doubt.

iOverview

Definition (Sec 4), kinds, acquisition by prescription (Sec 15), extinction and licence (Secs 52-63).

★Must know

  1. Sec 4: an easement is a right which the owner or occupier of certain land possesses, as such, for the beneficial enjoyment of that land, to do and continue to do something, or to prevent and continue to prevent something being done, in or upon, or in respect of, certain other land not his own. Dominant heritage = land for whose beneficial enjoyment the right exists; dominant owner = owner of dominant heritage; servient heritage and owner = land on which the liability is imposed. Easement is an incorporeal right attached to land; it is not a personal right; it cannot exist apart from the dominant heritage (Sec 6(c) TPA).
  2. Characteristics: (1) imposed by dominant on servient heritage; (2) the right must be for the beneficial enjoyment of the dominant heritage; (3) the two heritages must be owned by different persons; (4) it is a right without profit (no right to take anything - profit a prendre is a different right); (5) it is a right of 'user' not 'possession'. Examples: right of way, right to light and air, right to the flow of water, right of support, right of drainage.
  3. Kinds: continuous (enjoyment without act of man - e.g., light and air) and discontinuous (needs an act of man - right of way); apparent (visible sign) and non-apparent; positive (the right to do something) and negative (prevent something). Also easements of necessity, quasi-easements, customary easements, and acquired by prescription.
  4. ACQUISITION: by grant (express or implied - Sec 8), by necessity (Sec 13) and quasi-easements, by prescription (Sec 15), by custom (Sec 18), and by transfer. Sec 15 PRESCRIPTION: where the access and use of light or air to and for any building have been peaceably enjoyed therewith as an easement, and as of right, without interruption, and for twenty years, and where support from one person's land or things affixed thereto has been peaceably received by another person's land subjected to or things affixed thereto as an easement, and as of right, without interruption, for twenty years, and where a right of way or any other easement has been peaceably and openly enjoyed by any person claiming title thereto, as an easement, and as of right, without interruption, and for twenty years, the right to such access and use of light or air, support, or other easement, shall be absolute. Each of the said periods of twenty years shall be taken to be a period ending within two years next before the institution of the suit wherein the claim to which such period relates is contested. Where the property over which a right is claimed belongs to Government, the period is thirty years. Enjoyment must be nec vi, nec clam, nec precario (without force, secrecy or permission).
  5. Sec 13: easements of necessity (a person transferring or bequeathing immovable property is entitled to a way of necessity); Sec 14: quasi-easements arising on a severance of tenements; Sec 16: exclusion of days of disability; Sec 12: who may impose easements (owner of the heritage); Sec 17: easements acquired by a person with no title; Secs 24-25: exercise of an easement - the extent of user must not be altered; Sec 32: no liability for interruption. Extinction (Secs 37-47): by dissolution of right of servient owner; by expiry of limited period; by release; by revocation; by unity of ownership (merger - Sec 45 related); by permanent change in the dominant heritage; by necessity ceasing; by non-user for twenty years (Sec 46: easement of necessity extinguished when necessity ends); and abandonment.
  6. Sec 52 LICENCE: where one person grants to another, or to a definite number of other persons, a right to do, or continue to do, in or upon the immovable property of the grantor, something which would, in the absence of such right, be unlawful, and such right does not amount to an easement or an interest in the property, the right is called a licence. Sec 53: who may grant; Sec 54: licence may be express or implied; Sec 56: licence is not transferable, and not assignable; Sec 60: A licence may be revoked by the grantor, unless (a) it is coupled with a transfer of property and such transfer is in force; (b) the licensee, acting upon the licence, has executed a work of a permanent character and incurred expenses in the execution. Sec 62: a licence is deemed to be revoked (a) when, from a cause preceding the grant of it, the grantor ceases to have any interest in the property affected by the licence; (b) when the licensee dies or ceases to occupy the property; (c) when the licensee becomes insolvent; (d) when the licence is granted for a term and the term expires; (e) in case of licence for a particular purpose, when the purpose is attained or abandoned; (f) when the licensee assigns the licence. Sec 63: where a licence is revoked, the licensee is entitled to a reasonable time to leave the property and remove his goods. Sec 64: licensee's right to compensation if revoked wrongly.
  7. Remedies: suit for injunction/ damages (Sec 33 Easements Act: suit for compensation for obstruction of easement); the Specific Relief Act Secs 38-42 apply. Easement by prescription cannot be acquired against a co-owner's property by a co-owner, and a tenant can acquire an easement for his landlord's benefit only.

!Exam traps

  • TrapEasement vs licence: easement is an interest; licence is a personal permission, revocable unless Sec 60 exceptions.
  • TrapPrescription: 20 years (private land); 30 years (Government land).
  • TrapEasement cannot be separated from the dominant heritage; it is not transferable apart from it.

?Likely questions

  1. Period for acquiring easement by prescription: 20 years (30 years against Government).
  2. Licence is defined in Sec 52 of the Easements Act.
  3. A licence coupled with a transfer of property: irrevocable (Sec 60(a)).
