Transfer of Property Act: Sale of Immovable Property (Secs 54-57)

Very high yield1 Qs expected6 min readSec 54; Sec 55(1)-(6); Sec 56; Sec 57

  3. Exam traps
  4. Likely questions

iOverview

Mode of sale, rights and liabilities of seller and buyer, seller's charge. Nearly guaranteed.

★Must know

  1. Sec 54: 'sale' is a transfer of ownership in exchange for a price paid or promised or part-paid and part-promised. Such transfer, in the case of tangible immovable property of the value of one hundred rupees and upwards, or in the case of a reversion or other intangible thing, can be made only by a registered instrument. In the case of tangible immovable property of a value less than one hundred rupees, such transfer may be made either by a registered instrument or by delivery of the property. Delivery of tangible immovable property takes place when the seller places the buyer, or such person as he directs, in possession of the property.
  2. Contract for sale (agreement to sell): a contract that a sale of such property shall take place on terms settled between the parties. It does NOT, of itself, create any interest in or charge on such property (Sec 54, last para). Hence a purchaser under an agreement to sell has no title; he has only a right to sue for specific performance (and Sec 53A protection if the conditions are satisfied).
  3. Sec 55(1) SELLER'S DUTIES: (a) to disclose to the buyer any material defect in the property or in the seller's title of which the seller is, and the buyer is not, aware, and which the buyer could not with ordinary care discover; (b) to produce to the buyer on his request for examination all documents of title relating to the property which are in the seller's possession or power; (c) to answer to the best of his information all relevant questions put to him by the buyer in respect to the property or the title thereto; (d) on payment or tender of the amount due in respect of the price, to execute a proper conveyance of the property when the buyer tenders it to him for execution at a proper time and place; (e) between the date of the contract of sale and the delivery of the property, to take as much care of the property and all documents of title relating thereto which are in his possession as an owner of ordinary prudence would take of such property and documents; (f) to give, on being so required, the buyer, or such person as he directs, such possession of the property as its nature admits; (g) to pay all public charges and rents accrued due in respect of the property up to the date of the sale, the interest on all encumbrances on such property due on such date, and, except where the property is sold subject to encumbrances, to discharge all encumbrances on the property then existing.
  4. Sec 55(2): the seller is deemed to contract with the buyer that the interest which the seller professes to transfer subsists and that he has power to transfer the same (implied covenant for title); in a sale of a mortgage, etc., different. Sec 55(3): where the whole or any part of the purchase-money has been paid to the seller by the buyer before the sale is complete (i.e., on or before execution) the buyer is entitled to a charge. Sec 55(4): SELLER'S CHARGE - the seller is entitled to a charge upon the property, as against the buyer and all persons claiming under him, for the amount of the purchase-money, or any part thereof remaining unpaid, and for interest on such amount or part from the date on which possession is delivered. Seller's charge is lost if the seller has given up possession or by agreement; it is enforceable against transferees with notice.
  5. Sec 55(5) BUYER'S DUTIES: (a) to disclose to the seller any fact as to the nature or extent of the seller's interest in the property of which the buyer is aware, but of which he has reason to believe that the seller is not aware, and which materially increases the value of such interest; (b) to pay or tender, at the time and place of completing the sale, the purchase-money to the seller or such person as he directs - the buyer's tender of the price at the proper time; (c) where the ownership of the property has passed to the buyer before payment of the whole of the purchase-money, to pay interest on the unpaid purchase-money from the date of passing ownership; (d) as between the seller and buyer, after the ownership of the property has passed to the buyer, to bear any loss arising from the destruction, injury or decrease in value of the property not caused by the seller, and to pay all public charges and rents which become payable in respect of the property after the date of the sale.
  6. Sec 55(6)(b): the buyer is entitled to the benefit of any improvement in, or increase in value of, the property, and the rents and profits thereof, from the date on which the ownership is passed to him. Sec 55(6)(b) buyer's charge: a charge on the property, as against the seller and all persons claiming under him, to the extent of the seller's interest in the property, for the amount of any purchase-money properly paid by the buyer in anticipation of the delivery of possession and for interest on such amount; and, when he properly declines to accept the delivery of the property for any improper cause, for the earnest (if any) and costs awarded to him of a suit to compel specific performance. The charge arises by operation of law.
  7. Sec 56: marshalling by subsequent purchaser - if the owner of two or more properties mortgages them to one person and then sells one or more to another person, the buyer is, in the absence of a contract to the contrary, entitled to have the mortgage debt satisfied out of the property or properties not sold to him, so far as the same will extend, but not so as to prejudice the rights of the mortgagee or persons claiming under him. Sec 57: sale of mortgaged property by court - rights of buyer on a Court sale.
  8. Registration: sale of immovable property requires registration under the Registration Act 1908 (Sec 17(1)(b)); the document is also required to be stamped. An unregistered sale deed cannot convey title (except for tangible property < Rs 100 with delivery of possession); it is inadmissible in evidence to prove the transaction except for the limited collateral purposes (Registration Act Sec 49).
  9. Sale of property in execution of a decree: the Civil Procedure Code (Order XXI) and Sec 54 do not apply to court sales for mode of transfer. A sale certificate is deemed a transfer of title at the date of the confirmation of sale (Order XXI Rule 94).

!Exam traps

  • TrapSale of tangible immovable property worth Rs 100 and above: ONLY by registered instrument; below Rs 100 - registered instrument OR delivery of possession.
  • TrapAgreement to sell does NOT create any interest in or charge on the property.
  • TrapSeller's charge (Sec 55(4)) arises for unpaid price with interest from date of delivery of possession.
  • TrapBuyer bears the loss after ownership has passed (Sec 55(5)(d)).

?Likely questions

  1. Sale of tangible immovable property of Rs 100 and above requires: registered instrument.
  2. Contract for sale: does not by itself create any interest in the property.
  3. Seller's charge for unpaid purchase money: Sec 55(4).
