Negotiable Instruments: Definitions, Types and Parties (Secs 1-17)

iOverview

Definitions of promissory note, bill of exchange, cheque; negotiability; holder vs holder in due course. Expect 1 conceptual question alongside the Sec 138 question.

★Must know

  1. Sec 13: a negotiable instrument means a promissory note, bill of exchange or cheque payable either to order or to bearer. Characteristics: free transferability by delivery (bearer) or endorsement and delivery (order); the holder in due course gets good title free from defects of the transferor's title; the holder can sue in his own name; presumptions apply (Sec 118). Statutory negotiable instruments: only these three; others (hundis, bank drafts, dividend warrants, share warrants, railway receipts, bills of lading) are negotiable by custom or are not negotiable (documents of title).
  2. Sec 4 PROMISSORY NOTE: an instrument in writing (not being a bank-note or a currency-note) containing an unconditional undertaking, signed by the maker, to pay a certain sum of money only to, or to the order of, a certain person, or to the bearer of the instrument. Essentials: in writing; promise to pay (not mere acknowledgment - 'I.O.U. Rs 500' is not a note); unconditional; signed by maker; certain parties; certain sum; payable in money only; stamped. A note payable to bearer on demand cannot be issued (Sec 31 RBI Act - only RBI/Government can issue bearer demand notes).
  3. Sec 5 BILL OF EXCHANGE: an instrument in writing containing an unconditional order, signed by the maker, directing a certain person to pay a certain sum of money only to, or to the order of, a certain person or to the bearer of the instrument. Three parties: drawer (maker), drawee (the person directed to pay), payee. The drawee becomes ACCEPTOR on accepting the bill by signing. Sec 7: drawer, drawee, drawee in case of need, acceptor, acceptor for honour, payee. Sec 6 CHEQUE: a bill of exchange drawn on a specified banker and not expressed to be payable otherwise than on demand, and it includes the electronic image of a truncated cheque and a cheque in the electronic form (Explanation I - added in 2002). Cheque = a bill of exchange drawn on a banker, payable on demand. A cheque does not require acceptance and has no days of grace. Cheque validity: three months from the date of drawing (RBI Circular, effective 1 April 2012).
  4. Sec 8: HOLDER - the person entitled in his own name to the possession of the instrument and to receive or recover the amount due thereon from the parties thereto. A mere custodian (agent) is not a holder. Sec 9 HOLDER IN DUE COURSE (HDC): any person who for consideration became the possessor of a promissory note, bill of exchange or cheque if payable to bearer, or the payee or indorsee thereof, if payable to order, before the amount mentioned in it became payable, and without having sufficient cause to believe that any defect existed in the title of the person from whom he derived his title. Essentials: (1) for consideration; (2) became possessor before maturity; (3) in good faith, without notice of defect; (4) payee or endorsee/ bearer. Privileges: (Sec 53) good title; free from defects of the previous holder's title (fraud, no consideration) and defences available against prior parties; (Sec 118) presumption of HDC status (but see Sec 58 - if obtained by fraud/ unlawful means, the burden of proof is on the holder). A payee can be HDC (Sec 9), though for some purposes the payee cannot be a holder in due course against the drawer in a defence of lack of consideration between those two.
  5. Sec 10: PAYMENT IN DUE COURSE - payment in accordance with the apparent tenor of the instrument, in good faith and without negligence, to any person in possession thereof under circumstances which do not afford a reasonable ground for believing that he is not entitled to receive payment of the amount therein mentioned. Sec 14: NEGOTIATION - when a promissory note, bill or cheque is transferred to any person so as to constitute that person the holder thereof, the instrument is said to be negotiated. Sec 15: ENDORSEMENT - when the maker or holder of a negotiable instrument signs the same, otherwise than as such maker, for the purpose of negotiating the same, on the back or face thereof, or on a slip of paper annexed thereto, or so signs for the same purpose a stamped paper intended to be completed as a negotiable instrument, he is said to endorse the same, and is called the endorser. Sec 16: endorsement in blank vs in full - if the endorser signs his name only, the endorsement is in blank (instrument becomes payable to bearer); if he adds a direction to pay to a specified person, it is in full. Sec 17: effect of endorsement - the endorsement of a negotiable instrument followed by delivery transfers to the endorsee the property therein with the right of further negotiation; but the endorsement may, by express words, restrict or exclude such right (restrictive endorsement: 'pay A only'; 'sans recours'; conditional endorsement).
  6. Sec 18-20: ambiguous instruments - holder may treat as either promissory note or bill; Sec 20: INCHOATE STAMPED INSTRUMENTS - where one person signs and delivers to another a paper stamped in accordance with the law relating to negotiable instruments then in force in India, and either wholly blank or having written thereon an incomplete negotiable instrument, he thereby gives prima facie authority to the holder thereof to make or complete, as the case may be, upon it a negotiable instrument, for any amount specified therein and not exceeding the amount covered by the stamp. The person so signing shall be liable upon such instrument, in the capacity in which he signed the same, to any holder in due course for such amount. (Bir Singh v Mukesh Kumar, 2019: even a blank signed cheque voluntarily handed over attracts presumption under Sec 139.)
  7. Sec 21-25: At sight / on demand / after sight; Sec 22 MATURITY - the maturity of a promissory note or bill of exchange is the date at which it falls due. Every promissory note or bill of exchange which is not expressed to be payable on demand, at sight or on presentment is at maturity on the third day after the day on which it is expressed to be payable (three days of grace). Sec 23: calculating maturity of bill or note payable so many months after date or sight; Sec 24: so many days after date or sight (the day of presentation/date excluded); Sec 25: when the day of maturity is a public holiday, the instrument shall be deemed due on the NEXT PRECEDING BUSINESS DAY (this is the opposite of the Contract Act/ General Clauses Act approach).
  8. Sec 26-30: capacity - every person capable of contracting can bind himself and be bound by making, drawing, acceptance, endorsement, delivery and negotiation; a minor may draw, endorse, deliver and negotiate so as to bind all parties except himself (Sec 26). Sec 27: agency - may sign through an agent; Sec 28: liability of agent signing - where the agent signs in his own name, he is personally liable unless he indicates that he is signing as agent (Sec 28: an agent who signs his name 'without indicating that he is signing as agent' is personally liable; Sec 27 for the principal). Sec 29: liability of legal representative. Sec 30: LIABILITY OF DRAWER - the drawer of a bill of exchange or cheque is bound, in case of dishonour by the drawee or acceptor thereof, to compensate the holder, provided due notice of dishonour has been given to, or received by, the drawer. Sec 31: LIABILITY OF DRAWEE OF CHEQUE - the drawee of a cheque having sufficient funds of the drawer in his hands properly applicable to the payment of such cheque must pay the cheque when duly required so to do, and, in default of such payment, must compensate the drawer for any loss or damage caused by such default. Sec 32: liability of maker of note and acceptor of bill - in the absence of a contract to the contrary, the maker of a promissory note and the acceptor before maturity of a bill of exchange are bound to pay the amount thereof at maturity according to the apparent tenor of the note or acceptance respectively (principal debtors). Sec 35: liability of endorser - in the absence of a contract to the contrary, whoever endorses and delivers a negotiable instrument before maturity, without in such endorsement expressly excluding or making conditional his own liability, is bound thereby to every subsequent holder, in case of dishonour by the drawee, acceptor or maker, to compensate such holder for any loss or damage caused to him by such dishonour, provided due notice of dishonour has been given. Sec 37: maker, drawer and acceptor principals; the others sureties. Sec 43: a negotiable instrument made, drawn, accepted, endorsed or transferred without consideration, or for a consideration which fails, creates no obligation of payment between the parties to the transaction (but if any such party has transferred the instrument with or without endorsement to a holder for consideration, such holder, and every subsequent holder deriving title from him, may recover the amount due on such instrument from the transferor for consideration or any prior party thereto). Sec 44: partial failure of consideration. Sec 46-48: delivery; negotiation by delivery (bearer instruments) and by endorsement and delivery (order instruments).
  9. DISHONOUR (Secs 91-98): dishonour by non-acceptance (Sec 91) and dishonour by non-payment (Sec 92); Sec 93: notice of dishonour must be given by the holder to all prior parties who he wishes to make liable; Sec 94: mode; Sec 98: when notice of dishonour is unnecessary (e.g., when the party charged has dispensed with notice, when the drawer has countermanded payment, the drawer could not suffer damage from the want of notice); Secs 99-104: noting and protest (protest: foreign bills; protest is a formal certificate by a notary public). Sec 82: discharge of parties from liability - by cancellation, release, payment; Sec 87: MATERIAL ALTERATION - any material alteration of a negotiable instrument renders the same void as against any one who is a party thereto at the time of making such alteration and does not consent thereto, unless it was made in order to carry out the common intention of the original parties. Alterations that are material: date, sum payable, time of payment, place of payment, rate of interest, addition of a party, crossing, striking out words; (Sec 89: payment in due course of altered cheque - the banker is protected if the alteration was not apparent). In the 2026 Supreme Court ruling (decided 17 July 2026), conviction under Sec 138 was set aside where the cheque bore visible tampering - the Court held no extra proof was needed where the alteration was patent.
  10. CROSSING OF CHEQUES (Secs 123-131A): Sec 123 - general crossing: the cheque bears across its face an addition of the words 'and company' or any abbreviation thereof, between two parallel transverse lines, or two parallel transverse lines simply, either with or without the words 'not negotiable' - the banker shall not pay it otherwise than to a banker. Sec 124 - special crossing: the name of a banker is added across its face - payable only to that banker. Sec 125: crossing after issue - the holder may cross a cheque generally or specially, and add 'not negotiable'; Sec 126: payment of crossed cheque; Sec 129: protection of banker; Sec 130: cheque bearing 'not negotiable' - the transferee of such a cheque cannot have, and cannot give, a better title than the transferor had (it remains negotiable/ transferable, but loses its characteristic of conferring better title); Sec 131: non-liability of banker receiving payment of crossed cheque (COLLECTING BANKER) - a banker who has in good faith and without negligence received payment for a customer of a cheque crossed generally or specially to himself shall not, in case the title to the cheque proves defective, incur any liability to the true owner of the cheque by reason only of having received such payment. Sec 131A: the provisions apply to drafts.
  11. PRESUMPTIONS (Sec 118): until the contrary is proved, it shall be presumed (a) of consideration - that every negotiable instrument was made or drawn for consideration, and that every such instrument, when it has been accepted, indorsed, negotiated or transferred, was accepted, indorsed, negotiated or transferred for consideration; (b) as to date - that every negotiable instrument bearing a date was made or drawn on such date; (c) as to time of acceptance; (d) as to time of transfer; (e) as to order of endorsements; (f) as to stamps - that a lost promissory note, bill of exchange or cheque was duly stamped; (g) that the holder of a negotiable instrument is a holder in due course (except in the case of Sec 58). Sec 119: Sec 119 - presumption on proof of protest; Sec 120: estoppel against denying the original validity of the instrument - no maker of a promissory note, and no drawer of a bill of exchange or cheque, and no acceptor of a bill for the honour of the drawer shall, in a suit thereon by a holder in due course, be permitted to deny the validity of the instrument as originally made or drawn; Sec 121: estoppel against denying capacity of payee to endorse; Sec 122: estoppel against denying signature or capacity of prior party.

!Exam traps

  • TrapCheque is payable ON DEMAND only; days of grace do not apply to cheques/ demand instruments.
  • TrapHolder in due course must have taken BEFORE maturity and for CONSIDERATION and in good faith.
  • TrapSec 25: if the maturity day is a public holiday, due on the preceding business day.
  • TrapCrossing 'not negotiable' does not make the cheque non-transferable; it only removes the ability to confer better title (Sec 130).
  • TrapUnder Sec 6, electronic image of truncated cheque (CTS) and cheque in electronic form are cheques.
  • TrapSec 118 presumptions are rebuttable.

?Likely questions

  1. Which of these is not a negotiable instrument? (bank draft? bill of lading / share warrant / hundis...)
  2. Cheque is a bill of exchange drawn on a specified banker and payable on demand: Sec 6.
  3. Presumption of consideration: Sec 118(a). Presumption in favour of holder: Sec 139.
  4. Holder in due course - Sec 9.
