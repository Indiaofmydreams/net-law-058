Lawful Object, Void Agreements and Restraints (Sections 23-30)

iOverview

Sec 23, 26, 27, 28 and 30 appear repeatedly, often with the exceptions and cases.

★Must know

  1. Sec 23: consideration or object is lawful unless: (1) forbidden by law; (2) of such a nature that if permitted it would defeat the provisions of any law; (3) fraudulent; (4) involves or implies injury to the person or property of another; (5) the Court regards it as immoral; (6) opposed to public policy. Every agreement of which the object or consideration is unlawful is void.
  2. Public policy heads: trading with enemy, stifling prosecution, champerty/maintenance (in India, champerty is not per se void if bona fide - Ram Coomar Coondoo v Chunder Canto Mookerjee), marriage brokerage, sale of public offices, interference with administration of justice, agreements tending to create monopoly, unconscionable terms in standard form contracts (Central Inland Water Transport Corp v Brojo Nath Ganguly 1986).
  3. Sec 24: if any part of a single consideration for one or more objects, or any one or any part of any one of several considerations for a single object, is unlawful, the agreement is void (subject to severability - if lawful part can be separated, it is enforceable).
  4. Sec 25: see 1.2. Sec 26: agreement in restraint of marriage of any person other than a minor is void.
  5. Sec 27: agreement by which anyone is restrained from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business of any kind is, to that extent, void. Single statutory exception: sale of goodwill (a seller may agree with the buyer to refrain from carrying on a similar business within specified local limits, so long as the buyer carries on like business, provided the limits appear to the Court reasonable). Also Sec 54 Partnership Act (partners).
  6. Indian law has no 'doctrine of reasonable restraint' for trade: any restraint is void, but restraint DURING the term of employment is valid (Niranjan Shankar Golikari v Century Spinning, 1967), and negative covenants operative during subsistence of contract can be enforced by injunction. Post-employment restraints are void (Superintendence Co of India v Krishan Murgai, 1981). Restraints on distributors/ exclusive dealing during contract are generally valid (Gujarat Bottling Co v Coca-Cola, 1995).
  7. Sec 28 (as substituted in 1997): agreements void if they (a) restrict absolutely a party from enforcing his rights under or in respect of a contract by usual legal proceedings in ordinary tribunals, or limit the time within which he may enforce his rights; (b) extinguish the rights of a party or discharge any party from liability under the contract on the expiry of a specified period so as to restrict any party from enforcing his rights. Exceptions: agreements to refer future disputes to arbitration, and agreements to refer questions already arisen to arbitration. Exclusive jurisdiction clauses are valid if the chosen forum has jurisdiction and is not ousting all courts (ABC Laminart v A.P. Agencies, 1989; Hakam Singh v Gammon (India) Ltd, 1971).
  8. Sec 29: agreements whose meaning is not certain or capable of being made certain are void. Sec 30: agreements by way of wager are void; no suit for recovering anything alleged to be won on a wager. Exception: horse-race prizes of Rs 500 or more (subscription/ contribution to a plate or prize). Collateral transactions to wagers are enforceable in most States (not in Maharashtra, where Bombay Act makes them void). Insurance and lottery are not wagers in the legal sense; Gherulal Parakh v Mahadeodas Maiya (1959): speculative contracts of forward sale/purchase are not wagers unless there is a common intention not to deliver goods and only pay differences.
  9. Sec 56 (first paragraph): an agreement to do an act impossible in itself is void.
  10. Immoral agreements: e.g., concubinage contracts, prostitution (Gherulal v Mahadeodas). Agreements to stifle prosecution (Sec 23): void if the offence is non-compoundable.

⚖Landmark cases

Niranjan Shankar Golikari v Century Spinning

1967

Restraint during employment term is valid; not hit by Sec 27.

Superintendence Co of India v Krishan Murgai

1981

Post-termination restraint is void under Sec 27.

Gherulal Parakh v Mahadeodas Maiya

1959

Wager vs speculation; immoral objects.

Central Inland Water Transport v Brojo Nath Ganguly

1986

Unconscionable terms in standard-form (service) contracts are void under Sec 23 and Art 14.

Hakam Singh v Gammon (India) Ltd

1971

Parties may choose between courts having jurisdiction; ouster of all courts is void.

Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball

-

Not a wager - Sec 30 distinction.

!Exam traps

  • TrapSec 27 has only ONE statutory exception in the Contract Act: sale of goodwill. Others come from Partnership Act or case law.
  • TrapArbitration clauses are an exception to Sec 28.
  • TrapA wager is void, NOT illegal/forbidden (except in certain States); collateral transactions are enforceable.
  • TrapRestraint on marriage: only of a MINOR is excepted.

?Likely questions

  1. Agreement in restraint of trade is void under which section? (27)
  2. Which of the following is an exception to Sec 27? (sale of goodwill)
  3. Agreement by way of wager is: void.
  4. Niranjan Shankar Golikari: restraint during the continuance of employment valid.
