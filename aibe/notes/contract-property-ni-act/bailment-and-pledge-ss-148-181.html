Bailment and Pledge (Secs 148-181)

Medium-high yield1 Qs expected4 min readSec 148-171; Sec 172-181

  2. Must know (7 points)
  3. Exam traps
  4. Likely questions

Chapter highlights →My highlights →

iOverview

Definition, duties of bailor/bailee, lien (Sec 170-171), finder (Sec 168-169), pledge and pledgee's right of sale (Sec 176).

★Must know

  1. Sec 148: BAILMENT - delivery of goods by one person to another for some purpose, upon a contract that they shall, when the purpose is accomplished, be returned or otherwise disposed of according to the directions of the person delivering them. Person delivering = bailor; person receiving = bailee. Delivery may be actual or constructive (Sec 149). Bailment is based on possession, not ownership; ownership does not pass.
  2. Sec 150: bailor must disclose faults known to him which materially interfere with the use of goods or expose the bailee to extraordinary risks; liable for damage if he fails. In case of bailment for hire, the bailor is responsible even if he did not know of the defects (hire - bailor is liable for faults whether or not known).
  3. Sec 151: duty of care of the bailee - the care which a man of ordinary prudence would take of his own goods of the same bulk, quality and value. Sec 152: bailee not liable for loss/destruction/deterioration if he has taken the Sec 151 care. Sec 153: bailment terminable at bailor's option if bailee does an act inconsistent with the conditions. Sec 154: a bailee making unauthorised use is liable for any damage even if due to unforeseen event. Sec 155-157: mixing of goods - with consent: parties share proportionately; without consent but separable: bailee bears the cost of separation; inseparable: bailee must compensate the bailor for the loss of the goods. Sec 158: bailor's duty to repay necessary expenses. Sec 160: duty to return goods without demand when time expires or purpose accomplished. Sec 161: if bailee fails to return in time, he's responsible for any loss from that time even if not negligent. Sec 163: bailee must deliver to the bailor accretions (increase) in the absence of contract. Sec 164: bailor liable for loss caused to bailee due to defective title.
  4. Sec 168: finder's right to sue for specific reward and to retain the goods until reward. Sec 169: where the owner cannot with reasonable diligence be found, or refuses on demand to pay lawful charges, the finder may sell if (a) the thing is in danger of perishing or losing the greater part of its value, or (b) the lawful charges of the finder in respect of the thing found amount to two-thirds of its value.
  5. Sec 170: BAILEE'S PARTICULAR LIEN - where the bailee has, in accordance with the purpose of the bailment, rendered any service involving the exercise of labour or skill in respect of the goods bailed, he has, in the absence of a contract to the contrary, a right to retain such goods until he receives due remuneration. Sec 171: GENERAL LIEN - bankers, factors, wharfingers, attorneys of a High Court and policy-brokers may, in the absence of a contract to the contrary, retain, as security for a general balance of account, any goods bailed to them. Note: only these five categories have general lien.
  6. Sec 172: PLEDGE (pawn) - the bailment of goods as security for payment of a debt or performance of a promise. Bailor = pawnor; bailee = pawnee. Sec 173: pawnee's right of retainer; Sec 174: no retention for subsequent advances unless contract; Sec 175: right to extraordinary expenses; Sec 176: where the pawnor makes default, the pawnee may (i) bring a suit against the pawnor upon the debt, retaining the goods as collateral, or (ii) sell the thing pledged after giving the pawnor reasonable notice of the sale; in case of sale, surplus must be paid to the pawnor; deficit remains payable. Sec 177: pawnor's right to redeem at any time before actual sale, on payment of principal, interest and expenses.
  7. Pledge by non-owners: Sec 178 - pledge by a mercantile agent in possession with the owner's consent is valid if made in the ordinary course of business and the pawnee acts in good faith without notice. Sec 178A: pledge by a person in possession under a voidable contract is valid if the contract has not been rescinded. Sec 179: pledge by a person having a limited interest - valid to the extent of such interest. Sec 180-181: pledge by pawnor/ suit for redemption.

!Exam traps

  • TrapParticular lien (Sec 170) is a right of every skilled bailee; general lien (Sec 171) belongs ONLY to bankers, factors, wharfingers, attorneys, policy-brokers.
  • TrapPledge is a species of bailment - requires delivery; pledge of movable only. Mortgage is for immovables.
  • TrapFinder's right of sale needs perishing or lawful charges >= two-thirds of value.
  • TrapBailee's care is the same for gratuitous and non-gratuitous bailments (Sec 151).

?Likely questions

  1. General lien is available to: bankers, factors, wharfingers, attorneys, policy-brokers.
  2. Bailment of goods as security for a debt is: pledge.
  3. Care required of a bailee is: that of a man of ordinary prudence.
