Indemnity and Guarantee (Secs 124-147)

High yield1 Qs expected4 min readSec 124-125; Sec 126-129; Sec 133-135; Sec 140-147

iOverview

A near-certain 1-question area: definitions, distinction, surety's rights, discharge, continuing guarantee.

★Must know

  1. Sec 124: contract of indemnity - a contract by which one party promises to save the other from loss caused to him by the conduct of the promisor himself, or by the conduct of any other person. (The Act's definition is narrower than English law - covers only loss caused by human conduct, not accidents like fire; but Courts apply equitable principles to other losses.) Sec 125: rights of indemnity-holder: damages he is compelled to pay in a suit; costs; sums paid under compromise. Gajanan Moreshwar Parelkar v Moreshwar Madan Mantri (1942): indemnity-holder may call upon the indemnifier to be relieved of liability before actually paying.
  2. Sec 126: contract of guarantee - a contract to perform the promise, or discharge the liability, of a third person in case of his default. The person who gives the guarantee is the SURETY; the person in respect of whose default it is given is the PRINCIPAL DEBTOR; the person to whom it is given is the CREDITOR. The guarantee may be oral or written.
  3. Three parties; three contracts (principal contract, guarantee, implied contract between surety and principal debtor). In guarantee, there is a primary liability of the principal debtor and secondary liability of the surety. In indemnity, there are two parties and a primary liability of the indemnifier.
  4. Sec 127: anything done, or any promise made, for the benefit of the principal debtor may be a sufficient consideration to the surety. No separate consideration for the surety is needed. Sec 128: the liability of the surety is co-extensive with that of the principal debtor, unless otherwise provided (Bank of Bihar v Damodar Prasad, 1969: creditor may proceed against surety without first suing the principal debtor). Sec 129: continuing guarantee = extends to a series of transactions. Sec 130: continuing guarantee may be revoked as to future transactions by notice to the creditor. Sec 131: death of surety operates as revocation as to future transactions, in the absence of contract to contrary.
  5. Sec 133: any variance made without the surety's consent in the terms of the contract between the principal debtor and creditor discharges the surety as to transactions subsequent to the variance. Sec 134: the surety is discharged by any contract between the creditor and principal debtor by which the principal debtor is released, or by any act or omission of the creditor, the legal consequence of which is the discharge of the principal debtor. Sec 135: a contract between creditor and principal debtor by which the creditor makes a composition with, or promises to give time to, or not to sue, the principal debtor, discharges the surety unless the surety assents. Sec 139: creditor's act or omission impairing the surety's eventual remedy discharges the surety. Sec 136-138: creditor's promise to third person to give time to principal debtor, release of one co-surety does not discharge the other co-sureties.
  6. Sec 140: surety's rights on payment - subrogation to the creditor's rights. Sec 141: surety's right to the benefit of the creditor's securities (even if unaware). Sec 142: guarantee obtained by misrepresentation (by creditor or with his knowledge) is invalid. Sec 143: guarantee obtained by concealment of a material circumstance is invalid. Sec 144: a guarantee given on the condition that the creditor shall not act upon it until another person has joined as co-surety - invalid if that person doesn't join. Sec 145: implied promise by principal debtor to indemnify the surety. Sec 146-147: co-sureties are liable equally (unless contract otherwise) to contribute; where bound in different sums, they contribute equally subject to the maximum limit of each.
  7. Contract of guarantee by a company/bank: bank guarantees are independent of the underlying contract; payment on demand is obligatory except in fraud or irretrievable injustice (U.P. Co-operative Federation v Singh Consultants, 1988). Letters of credit similar.
  8. Banking guarantee note: 'performance guarantee' encashment cannot be injuncted save for fraud/ special equities.

⚖Landmark cases

Bank of Bihar v Damodar Prasad

1969

Surety's liability is immediate and co-extensive; creditor needn't exhaust remedies against the principal debtor.

Gajanan Moreshwar Parelkar v Moreshwar Madan Mantri

1942

Rights of indemnity-holder under Sec 125 are not exhaustive; equitable relief available.

State of Maharashtra v M.N. Kaul

1967

Surety discharged where creditor's act (variance) affects principal debtor without his consent.

!Exam traps

  • TrapSec 128 - co-extensive does not mean identical: the surety's liability may be limited by contract.
  • TrapSec 133: variance discharges the surety only for subsequent transactions.
  • TrapA guarantee for a continuing account - death revokes future liability (Sec 131).
  • TrapIn indemnity, the indemnifier's promise is primary; in guarantee the surety's liability is secondary/collateral.

?Likely questions

  1. The liability of a surety is: co-extensive with the principal debtor.
  2. Which section defines contract of guarantee? (126)
  3. Release of principal debtor by creditor: surety discharged (Sec 134).
