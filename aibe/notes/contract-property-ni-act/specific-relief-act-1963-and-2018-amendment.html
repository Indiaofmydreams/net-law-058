Specific Relief Act: Overview and the 2018 Amendment

iOverview

The Specific Relief Act 1963 was substantially amended by the Specific Relief (Amendment) Act 2018 (in force 1 October 2018). Examiners increasingly test post-2018 law: specific performance is now a general rule, not a discretion; substituted performance; infrastructure contracts.

★Must know

  1. Sec 4: specific relief can be granted only for the purpose of enforcing individual civil rights, not for enforcing penal laws.
  2. Specific relief in the Act: recovery of possession of property (Secs 5-8), specific performance of contracts (Secs 9-25), rectification (Sec 26), rescission (Secs 27-30), cancellation (Secs 31-33), declaratory decrees (Secs 34-35), injunctions (Secs 36-42).
  3. 2018 Amendment highlights: (1) Sec 10 - specific performance 'shall' be enforced (earlier 'may be enforced' in the court's discretion; the Sec 20 discretionary clause was repealed and replaced). (2) Sec 14 - rewritten, deleting 'compensation adequate relief' as a ground for refusal. (3) Sec 20 - substituted performance by a third party/ own agency after 30 days' notice. (4) Sec 20A-20C - special provisions for infrastructure project contracts (no injunction; special courts; disposal within 12 months). (5) Sec 21 - compensation in addition to or in substitution, with amendment of plaint allowed. (6) Sec 41(ha) - no injunction impeding infrastructure projects. (7) Sec 16 (personal bars) revised. (8) Secs 6-8 (possession suits) unchanged in substance.
  4. Prospective operation: the Supreme Court has held that the 2018 amendments are substantive and operate prospectively - agreements made before 1 October 2018 are governed by the unamended Act (Katta Sujatha Reddy v Siddamsetty Infra Projects, 2022; recent judgments therefore say 'prior to 2018 amendment'). Be ready for a question distinguishing pre-2018 and post-2018 law.
  5. Sec 9: defences in suits for specific performance are available as in any contract suit - the defendant may plead any ground available under the law relating to contracts.

⚖Landmark cases

Katta Sujatha Reddy v Siddamsetty Infra Projects

2022

2018 amendments to SRA are prospective.

!Exam traps

  • TrapPost-2018: Specific performance is no longer a discretionary remedy in the old sense; but equitable defences (Sec 16, Sec 14) still limit it.
  • TrapPre-2018 contracts: unamended Sec 20 discretion applies.

?Likely questions

  1. Specific Relief (Amendment) Act 2018 came into force on: 1 October 2018.
  2. Which new section introduced 'substituted performance'? Sec 20.
  3. Under the amended Act specific performance is: general rule/ enforceable subject to Secs 11(2), 14, 16.
