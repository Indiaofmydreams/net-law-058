Doctrines under the TPA (Election, Ostensible Owner, Estoppel, Lis Pendens, Part Performance)

iOverview

Sections 35, 41, 43, 52, 53, 53A are the most frequently tested doctrinal provisions.

★Must know

  1. Sec 35 ELECTION: where a person professes to transfer property which he has no right to transfer, and as part of the same transaction confers any benefit on the owner of the property, such owner must elect either to confirm such transfer or to dissent from it; and, in the latter case, he shall relinquish the benefit so conferred, and the benefit so relinquished reverts to the transferor or his representative as if it had not been disposed of, subject to the charge of making good to the disappointed transferee the amount or value of the property attempted to be transferred to him. Based on the maxim: 'he who accepts a benefit under an instrument must adopt the whole.' Election arises only from a single transaction; the elector must have knowledge of the facts and the right to elect.
  2. Sec 41 OSTENSIBLE OWNER: where, with the consent, express or implied, of the persons interested in immovable property, a person is the ostensible owner of such property and transfers the same for consideration, the transfer shall not be voidable on the ground that the transferor was not authorised to make it, provided that the transferee, after taking reasonable care to ascertain that the transferor had power to make the transfer, has acted in good faith. Conditions: (1) ostensible ownership; (2) consent of real owner (express or implied); (3) transfer for consideration; (4) transferee's good faith and reasonable care. Applies only to immovable property; real owner's omission or willful absence from interference is implied consent (Ram Coomar Coondoo; Ramcoomar v Macqueen; and the leading modern case Jayadayal Poddar v Bibi Hazra, 1974 - burden on transferee).
  3. Sec 43 FEEDING THE GRANT BY ESTOPPEL: where a person fraudulently or erroneously represents that he is authorised to transfer certain immovable property and professes to transfer such property for consideration, such transfer shall, at the option of the transferee, operate on any interest which the transferor may acquire in such property at any time during which the contract of transfer subsists. Proviso: nothing in this section shall impair the right of transferees in good faith for consideration without notice of the existence of the said option. The transfer operates on the after-acquired interest (the grantor's subsequently acquired title 'feeds' the earlier grant). The transferee must not have had knowledge of the transferor's lack of title (a transferee with knowledge cannot invoke Sec 43). Distinguish from Sec 41: Sec 41 protects against the OWNER; Sec 43 enforces against the TRANSFEROR's subsequently acquired interest.
  4. Sec 51: improvements by bona fide holder under defective title - the transferee who makes improvements believing in good faith that he is absolutely entitled is entitled to compensation, or to have the improvements removed, on eviction by the person having a better title. Sec 52 LIS PENDENS: during the pendency in any court having authority within the limits of India of a suit or proceeding which is not collusive and in which any right to immovable property is directly and specifically in question, the property cannot be transferred or otherwise dealt with by any party to the suit or proceeding so as to affect the rights of any other party thereto under any decree or order which may be made therein, except under the authority of the court and on such terms as it may impose. Based on the maxim pendente lite nihil innovetur. Lis pendens operates as a rule of public policy irrespective of notice; the transfer is not void but subordinate to the decree (it is 'subject to the outcome'). The transferee pendente lite may be impleaded and is bound by the decree. The lis commences with the presentation of the plaint and continues until final disposal and satisfaction of the decree. 'Right to immovable property directly and specifically in question': suits for partition, redemption, specific performance of sale, title.
  5. Sec 53 FRAUDULENT TRANSFER: (1) every transfer of immovable property made with intent to defeat or delay the creditors of the transferor shall be voidable at the option of any creditor so defeated or delayed. Nothing shall impair the rights of a transferee in good faith and for consideration. (2) Every transfer of immovable property made without consideration with intent to defraud a subsequent transferee shall be voidable at the option of such transferee. The transferee in good faith and for consideration is protected. The suit by a creditor shall be instituted on behalf of or for the benefit of all the creditors (Sec 53(1) explanation). Intention of the transferor to defraud is the key (Twyne's case, 1601). Benami transactions are separately regulated.
  6. Sec 53A PART PERFORMANCE: where any person contracts to transfer for consideration any immovable property by writing signed by him or on his behalf from which the terms necessary to constitute the transfer can be ascertained with reasonable certainty, and the transferee has, in part performance of the contract, taken possession of the property or any part thereof, or the transferee, being already in possession, continues in possession in part performance of the contract and has done some act in furtherance of the contract, and has performed or is willing to perform his part of the contract, then, notwithstanding that the contract, though required to be registered, has not been registered, or, where there is an instrument of transfer, that the transfer has not been completed in the manner prescribed therefor by the law for the time being in force, the transferor or any person claiming under him shall be debarred from enforcing against the transferee and persons claiming under him any right in respect of the property of which the transferee has taken or continued in possession, other than a right expressly provided by the terms of the contract. Essential conditions: (i) written contract signed by the transferor, with ascertainable terms; (ii) transferee took/continued possession in part performance; (iii) transferee did some act in furtherance; (iv) transferee has performed or is willing to perform his part. It is a SHIELD (a defence), not a SWORD - the transferee cannot sue for title on the basis of Sec 53A alone (Ghantesher Ghosh v Madan Mohan Ghosh, 1996 - principle; Delhi Motor Co v U.A. Basrurkar, 1968; Shrimant Shamrao Suryavanshi v Pralhad Bhairoba Suryavanshi, 2002). After the 2001 amendment (Act 48 of 2001), the contract must be REGISTERED under Registration Act Sec 17(1A) to claim Sec 53A protection for contracts executed on or after 24 January 2001; unregistered agreements to sell cannot be relied upon for Sec 53A.
  7. Sec 48: where a person purports to create by transfer at different times rights in or over the same immovable property, and such rights cannot all exist or be exercised to their full extent together, each later right shall, in the absence of a special contract or reservation binding the earlier transferees, be subject to the rights previously created (priority - 'qui prior est tempore potior est jure'). Sec 50: rent; Sec 44: transfer by one co-owner - the transferee acquires the transferor's share and has the right to joint possession/ partition.
  8. GPA/ SA/ WILL transactions: Suraj Lamp & Industries v State of Haryana (2012) - immovable property can be legally and lawfully transferred or conveyed only by a registered deed of conveyance; transactions of sale through agreement to sell, general power of attorney, and will (SA/GPA/WILL) do not convey title or create any interest in an immovable property; they may only be treated as agreement to sell and Sec 53A protection (if registered). This ruling is prospective for completed transactions but, going forward, binding. A power of attorney is not an instrument of transfer.

⚖Landmark cases

Suraj Lamp & Industries v State of Haryana

2012

SA/GPA/WILL transactions are not valid transfers of immovable property; only registered conveyance transfers title.

Jayadayal Poddar v Bibi Hazra

1974

Burden of proving Sec 41 conditions lies on the transferee.

Shrimant Shamrao Suryavanshi v Pralhad Bhairoba Suryavanshi

2002

Sec 53A is a shield, not a sword - defendant in possession can protect, but not sue on this.

Jumma Masjid v Kodimaniandra Deviah

1962

Sec 52 - transfer pendente lite is subject to the decree; applies irrespective of notice.

Twyne's Case

1601

Fraudulent transfers to defeat creditors.

!Exam traps

  • TrapSec 41 needs CONSIDERATION + GOOD FAITH + REASONABLE CARE; Sec 43 needs transfer by one who erroneously/ fraudulently represents authority.
  • TrapLis pendens: transfer is not void; it is subject to the final decree. Applies irrespective of notice.
  • TrapSec 53A cannot be used by plaintiff as basis for a title suit; and post-2001 the agreement must be registered.
  • TrapSec 53(1) is voidable at the option of a defrauded creditor; Sec 53(2): subsequent transferee.

?Likely questions

  1. Doctrine of feeding the grant by estoppel: Sec 43.
  2. Doctrine of lis pendens: Sec 52.
  3. Doctrine of part performance: Sec 53A - shield not sword.
  4. Which doctrine is based on 'he who takes benefit must bear the burden'? Election.
