Transfer of Property Act: Lease (Secs 105-117)

iOverview

Definition, formalities, duration (Sec 106), lessor/lessee rights, determination (Sec 111), forfeiture and holding over are recurring topics; lease vs licence is a perennial favourite.

★Must know

  1. Sec 105: a lease of immovable property is a transfer of a right to enjoy such property, made for a certain time, express or implied, or in perpetuity, in consideration of a price paid or promised, or of money, a share of crops, service or any other thing of value, to be rendered periodically or on specified occasions to the transferor by the transferee, who accepts the transfer on such terms. Transferor = lessor; transferee = lessee; the price = premium; the money, share, service or other thing = rent.
  2. Essentials of a lease: (1) parties competent; (2) subject-matter - immovable property; (3) transfer of a right to enjoy (not ownership), i.e., exclusive possession; (4) for a certain time or in perpetuity; (5) consideration - premium or rent; (6) acceptance by lessee. A lease is a transfer of an INTEREST in property (it creates an estate); a LICENCE (Easements Act Sec 52) merely makes lawful what would otherwise be unlawful and creates no interest. The test is whether the document gives exclusive possession and an interest (intention of the parties) - Associated Hotels of India v R.N. Kapoor (1959); Sohan Lal v Union of India (1957).
  3. Sec 106: DURATION - in the absence of a contract or local law or usage to the contrary, a lease of immovable property for agricultural or manufacturing purposes shall be deemed to be a lease from year to year, terminable, on the part of either lessor or lessee, by six months' notice; and a lease of immovable property for any other purpose shall be deemed to be a lease from month to month, terminable, on the part of either lessor or lessee, by fifteen days' notice (amended in 2002). Such notice must be in writing, signed by or on behalf of the person giving it, and either sent by post to the party who is intended to be bound by it or tendered or delivered personally to such party, or to one of his family or servants at his residence, or (if such tender or delivery is not practicable) affixed to a conspicuous part of the property. After the 2002 amendment the notice period for a monthly tenancy is fifteen days.
  4. Sec 107: A lease of immovable property from year to year, or for any term exceeding one year, or reserving a yearly rent, can be made only by a registered instrument. All other leases of immovable property may be made either by a registered instrument or by oral agreement accompanied by delivery of possession. Where a lease of immovable property is made by a registered instrument, such instrument or, where there are more instruments than one, each such instrument shall be executed by both the lessor and the lessee (Sec 107 proviso - the State Government may exempt leases not exceeding 3 years). An unregistered lease for more than one year is invalid as a lease but may be evidence of the nature of possession (a tenancy from year to year or month to month may be implied by acceptance of rent and possession - Sec 116 and Anthony v K.C. Ittoop & Sons, 2000: it is a tenancy from year to year/ month to month; the unregistered deed is admissible for collateral purposes).
  5. Sec 108 RIGHTS AND LIABILITIES: Lessor: (a) to disclose to the lessee any material defect in the property, with reference to its intended use, of which the lessor is and the lessee is not aware, and which the lessee could not with ordinary care discover; (b) to put the lessee in possession of the property on his request; (c) QUIET ENJOYMENT - the lessor is deemed to contract with the lessee that, if the lessee pays the rent reserved and performs the contracts binding on the lessee, he may hold the property during the time limited by the lease without interruption. Lessee: (d) to disclose facts as to the nature or extent of the interest which the lessee is about to take of which the lessee is, and the lessor is not, aware, and which materially increase the value of such interest; (e) rent - to pay or tender, at the proper time and place, the premium or rent; (f) to keep the property in good condition; (g) the lessee may remove, at any time during the continuance of the lease, all things which he has attached to the earth, provided he leaves the property in the state in which he received it; (h) the lessee may transfer absolutely or by way of mortgage or sub-lease the whole or any part of his interest in the property, and any transferee of such interest or part may again transfer it (the lessee remains liable to the lessor under the lease); the lessee must not commit waste, must protect the property from injury, and on determination of the lease must put the lessor into possession. The lessee is entitled to the benefit of any accession to the property during the term.
  6. Sec 109: if the lessor transfers the property leased, or any part thereof, or any part of his interest therein, the transferee, in the absence of a contract to the contrary, shall possess all the rights, and, if the lessee so elects, be subject to all the liabilities of the lessor as to the property or part transferred so long as he is the owner of it (the transferee can sue the lessee for rent; the lessee can attorn). Sec 110: exclusion of day on which term commences - where the time limited by a lease of immovable property is expressed as commencing from a particular day, the day is to be excluded; and where it is expressed as ending on a particular day, that day is included in the term.
  7. Sec 111 DETERMINATION OF LEASE: (a) by efflux of the time limited thereby; (b) where such time is limited conditionally on the happening of some event - by the happening of such event; (c) where the interest of the lessor in the property terminates on, or his power to dispose of the same extends only to, the happening of any event - by the happening of such event; (d) where the interest of the lessee and the interest of the lessor in the whole of the property become vested at the same time in one person in the same right (merger); (e) by express surrender, i.e., in case the lessee yields up his interest under the lease to the lessor, by mutual agreement between them; (f) by implied surrender; (g) by forfeiture - (1) in case the lessee breaks an express condition which provides that, on breach thereof, the lessor may re-enter; or (2) in case the lessee renounces his character as such by setting up a title in a third person or by claiming title in himself; or (3) the lessee is adjudicated an insolvent and the lease provides that the lessor may re-enter on the happening of such event; and in any of these cases the lessor or his transferee gives notice in writing to the lessee of his intention to determine the lease; (h) on the expiration of a notice to determine the lease, or to quit, or of intention to quit, the property leased, duly given by one party to the other (notice to quit).
  8. Sec 112: waiver of forfeiture - a forfeiture under Sec 111(g) is waived by acceptance of rent which has become due since the forfeiture, or by distress for such rent, or by any other act on the part of the lessor showing an intention to treat the lease as subsisting. Sec 113: waiver of notice to quit - a notice given under Sec 111(h) is waived, with the express or implied consent of the person to whom it is given, by any act on the part of the person giving it showing an intention to treat the lease as subsisting. Sec 114: relief against forfeiture for non-payment of rent - where a lease of immovable property has been determined by forfeiture for non-payment of rent, and the lessor sues to eject the lessee, if, at the hearing of the suit, the lessee pays or tenders to the lessor the rent in arrear, together with interest thereon and his full costs of the suit, or gives security to the satisfaction of the Court for payment of such rent, interest and costs, the Court may, in lieu of making a decree for ejectment, pass an order relieving the lessee against the forfeiture. Sec 114A: relief against forfeiture in certain other cases - notice of the breach is required to be given to the lessee (not applicable to breach of express condition against assigning, under-letting, etc.). Sec 115: effect of notice to determine lease. Sec 116: EFFECT OF HOLDING OVER - if a lessee or under-lessee of property remains in possession thereof after the determination of the lease granted to the lessee, and the lessor or his legal representative accepts rent from the lessee or under-lessee, or otherwise assents to his continuing in possession, the lease is, in the absence of an agreement to the contrary, renewed from year to year, or from month to month, according to the purpose for which the property is leased, as specified in Sec 106 (a tenancy by holding over is a new tenancy, not an extension). Sec 117: exemption of leases for agricultural purposes - none of the provisions of Secs 105 to 116 apply to leases for agricultural purposes, except in so far as the State Government may by notification in the Official Gazette so direct.
  9. A lessee has an interest - heritable and transferable; the lease isn't terminated by the death of lessor or lessee. Eviction under rent control legislation: tenant protections override Sec 106 in many States (so a lease determined by notice to quit may still require a statutory ground for eviction; the 'statutory tenant' is entitled to remain). Rent Acts: the Model Tenancy Act 2021 (Central) - encouraged States to adopt; check your State.
  10. Lease vs licence (key distinctions): (1) Interest: lease creates an interest in the property; licence is a personal privilege; (2) Transferability: lease is transferable; licence is not; (3) Revocability: lease is not revocable (except by determination in accordance with the Act); licence is revocable (subject to Easements Act Sec 60); (4) Heritability: lease passes to the legal representatives; licence terminates on the death of the licensor/ licensee; (5) Possession: lessee has exclusive possession; licensee has only the right of occupation; (6) Suing: lessee can sue trespassers; licensee cannot. Court looks at substance and not the label (Associated Hotels of India v R.N. Kapoor; B.M. Lall v Dunlop Rubber, 1968).

⚖Landmark cases

Associated Hotels of India v R.N. Kapoor

1959

Lease vs licence: the test is exclusive possession with intention to create an interest.

Anthony v K.C. Ittoop & Sons

2000

Unregistered lease for more than a year; holding over leads to year-to-year/ month-to-month tenancy.

Bhagabandas Agarwalla v Bhagwandas Kanu

1977

Waiver/ forfeiture and acceptance of rent.

!Exam traps

  • TrapSec 106: AGRICULTURAL/ MANUFACTURING = year to year + 6 months' notice; OTHERS = month to month + 15 days' notice.
  • TrapSec 107: lease > 1 year, year to year or yearly rent = REGISTERED instrument. Lease up to one year = registered or oral with possession.
  • TrapSec 117: Chapter on lease does not apply to agricultural leases unless notified.
  • TrapSec 111(g) forfeiture needs a written notice of intention to determine; waiver under Sec 112 by acceptance of rent.
  • TrapSec 116 - holding over requires lessor's acceptance of rent or assent; otherwise tenant is a tenant at sufferance.

?Likely questions

  1. Notice period for terminating a monthly (non-agricultural) tenancy: 15 days.
  2. Lease for 1 year and above must be made by: registered instrument.
  3. Effect of holding over: Sec 116.
  4. Which of the following is a licence? ... Easements Act Sec 52.
