Transfer of Property Act: General Principles (Secs 1-9)

iOverview

Definitions and the basic rules of what can be transferred, by whom, and how. Appears in almost every paper.

★Must know

  1. Sec 3: immovable property does not include standing timber, growing crops or grass. (General Clauses Act Sec 3(26): land, benefits arising out of land, and things attached to the earth or permanently fastened to anything attached to the earth.) 'Attached to the earth' means rooted in the earth (trees, shrubs), imbedded in the earth (walls, buildings), or attached to what is so imbedded for permanent beneficial enjoyment.
  2. Sec 3: a person is said to have NOTICE of a fact when he actually knows it, or when but for wilful abstention from an enquiry or search which he ought to have made, or gross negligence, he would have known it. Constructive notice: registration of a document is notice; possession is notice of the possessor's title.
  3. Sec 5: 'transfer of property' is an act by which a living person conveys property, in present or in future, to one or more other living persons, or to himself and one or more other living persons; 'living person' includes a company or association or body of individuals, whether incorporated or not. Hence a transfer to an unborn person is possible only through Sec 13 (trust), and a transfer by will is not a transfer under the Act.
  4. Sec 6: property of any kind may be transferred except: (a) the chance of an heir-apparent succeeding to an estate (spes successionis), the chance of a relation obtaining a legacy on the death of a kinsman, or any other mere possibility of a like nature; (b) a mere right of re-entry for breach of a condition subsequent; (c) an easement apart from the dominant heritage; (d) an interest in property restricted in its enjoyment to the owner personally; (dd) a right to future maintenance, in whatever manner arising, secured, or determined; (e) a mere right to sue; (f) a public office, and the salary of a public officer, whether before or after it has become payable; (g) stipends allowed to military, naval, air-force and civil pensioners of the Government and political pensions; (h) no transfer can be made (1) insofar as it is opposed to the nature of the interest affected thereby, or (2) for an unlawful object or consideration within the meaning of Sec 23 of the Contract Act, or (3) to a person legally disqualified to be transferee; (i) an untransferable interest of an occupancy tenant (subject to law).
  5. Sec 7: every person competent to contract and entitled to transferable property, or authorised to dispose of transferable property not his own, is competent to transfer such property. Sec 8: unless a different intention is expressed or necessarily implied, a transfer of property passes forthwith to the transferee all the interest which the transferor is then capable of passing, and the legal incidents thereof (including easements, rents, profits, and arrears). Sec 9: a transfer of property may be made without writing in every case in which a writing is not expressly required by law (oral transfers - e.g., sale of tangible immovable property below Rs 100 with delivery of possession, lease for not more than a year, mortgage by deposit of title deeds).
  6. Sec 10: where property is transferred subject to a condition or limitation absolutely restraining the transferee from parting with or disposing of his interest in the property, the condition or limitation is void; exception: lease, where the condition is for the benefit of the lessor or those claiming under him (e.g., no sub-letting). Sec 11: where, on a transfer of property, an interest therein is created absolutely in favour of any person, but the transferee is directed to deal with the interest in a particular manner for the benefit of another, the transferee takes the interest free of the direction, unless there is a legal obligation (not applied where there is an obligation in the nature of a trust). Sec 12: condition that the transfer shall cease to have effect if the transferee becomes insolvent or attempts to alienate is void (not applicable to lessee for the benefit of the lessor).
  7. Sec 13: where on a transfer of property an interest therein is created for the benefit of a person not in existence at the date of the transfer, subject to a prior interest created by the same transfer, the interest created for the benefit of such person shall not take effect, unless it extends to the whole of the remaining interest of the transferor in the property. Sec 14: RULE AGAINST PERPETUITIES - no transfer of property can operate to create an interest which is to take effect after the life-time of one or more persons living at the date of such transfer, and the minority of some person who shall be in existence at the expiration of that period, and to whom, if he attains full age, the interest created is to belong. In effect: the vesting must be within a life in being + minority (18 years). Sec 15: transfer to a class some of whom cannot take; Sec 16: transfer to take effect on failure of prior transfer; Sec 17: direction for accumulation - void beyond life of the transferor, or 18 years from the date of transfer, whichever is shorter; Sec 18: perpetuity exception in favour of the public (charities).
  8. Vested and contingent interests: Sec 19 - a vested interest is created in favour of a person without reference to the time of enjoyment; Sec 20 - unborn person acquires vested interest on birth; Sec 21 - contingent interest depends on a specified uncertain event; Sec 22 - transfer to members of a class; Secs 23-34 - conditional transfers and conditions precedent/ subsequent: Sec 25 - an interest created on a condition that is impossible, unlawful or immoral is void; Sec 31 - an interest created on transfer for the benefit of any person, but which is to cease on a specified uncertain event, is valid if the condition is not illegal.

!Exam traps

  • TrapSpes successionis (chance of succession) is NOT transferable. Mere right to sue is NOT transferable (but a right to sue for specific property or for money due is transferable as an actionable claim).
  • TrapRight to future maintenance is not transferable; arrears of maintenance are.
  • TrapSec 10 restraint on alienation is void - but partial restraints (time-bound or limited to class) may be valid. Lease restraints in favour of lessor are valid.
  • TrapPerpetuity: life in being + minority period (18 years).

?Likely questions

  1. Which of the following cannot be transferred under Sec 6? spes successionis / mere right to sue / public office / personal easement.
  2. Rule against perpetuities is in Sec 14.
  3. Sec 5 defines transfer of property: between living persons.
