Capacity to Contract and Free Consent (Sections 11-22)

iOverview

Almost every AIBE paper has at least one question here: minor's agreements, coercion vs undue influence, fraud vs misrepresentation, mistake.

★Must know

  1. Sec 11: competent = (a) major (18 years under Indian Majority Act 1875; 21 years if a guardian of person/property has been appointed by court or the minor is under Court of Wards), (b) of sound mind, (c) not disqualified by any law (alien enemy, insolvent, convicts, foreign sovereigns/ ambassadors - subject to law).
  2. Minor's agreement is VOID AB INITIO (Mohori Bibee v Dharmodas Ghose, Privy Council 1903). Consequences: no specific enforcement against the minor; no ratification on attaining majority (as a void agreement cannot be ratified by fresh consideration-less promise); minor cannot be estopped from pleading minority; no restitution under Sec 64/65 of money advanced (Sec 65 not applicable to minors per Mohori Bibee; but restitution possible if minor obtained property by fraud and property is identifiable - Equity doctrine, Khan Gul v Lakha Singh).
  3. But: a minor can be a promisee/beneficiary, can be an agent (principal bound, agent not personally liable), can be admitted to benefits of a partnership (Sec 30 Partnership Act), and a minor's estate is liable for 'necessaries' supplied (Sec 68). Contracts by a guardian on behalf of minor, for minor's benefit and within guardian's authority, are enforceable (Mir Sarwarjan v Fakhruddin).
  4. Sec 12: sound mind = capable of understanding the contract and forming a rational judgment as to its effect on his interests at the time of contracting. A lunatic may contract during lucid intervals; a sane man cannot contract when delirious or drunk.
  5. Sec 13: consent = two or more persons agree upon the same thing in the same sense (consensus ad idem). Sec 14: consent is free when not caused by coercion (Sec 15), undue influence (Sec 16), fraud (Sec 17), misrepresentation (Sec 18) or mistake (Secs 20-22).
  6. Sec 15 COERCION: committing, or threatening to commit, any act forbidden by the Indian Penal Code (now read as the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita 2023), or the unlawful detaining, or threatening to detain, any property, to the prejudice of any person, with the intention of causing any person to enter into an agreement. It is immaterial whether the IPC/BNS is in force at the place where coercion is employed. Effect: contract voidable at the option of the party whose consent was so caused (Sec 19). Coercion may be by a stranger and may be directed at anyone. A threat to commit suicide is coercion (Ammiraju v Seshamma).
  7. Sec 16 UNDUE INFLUENCE: contract induced by a relationship where one party is in a position to dominate the will of the other and uses it to obtain unfair advantage. Presumed where: real/apparent authority (e.g., parent-child, guru-disciple, doctor-patient, solicitor-client, trustee-beneficiary), fiduciary relation, or the party is mentally distressed (illness, age). Sec 16(3): where the transaction appears unconscionable and the dominant party is in a position to dominate, the burden of proving no undue influence lies on the dominant party. Pardanashin women: special protection - the party must show the contract was explained and she understood.
  8. Sec 17 FRAUD: act committed by a party (or with his connivance or by his agent) with intent to deceive another or to induce him to enter the contract: (1) suggestion as a fact of that which is not true by one who does not believe it to be true; (2) active concealment of a fact by one having knowledge or belief of the fact; (3) a promise made without any intention of performing it; (4) any other act fitted to deceive; (5) any act or omission the law specially declares fraudulent. Mere silence is not fraud unless there is a duty to speak (e.g., contracts uberrimae fidei such as insurance, or fiduciary relations) or the silence is, in itself, equivalent to speech. Derry v Peek: fraud means a false statement made knowingly, without belief in its truth, or recklessly.
  9. Sec 18 MISREPRESENTATION: (1) positive assertion not warranted by the information of the person making it, though he believes it to be true; (2) breach of duty which, without intent to deceive, gains an advantage by misleading another; (3) causing, however innocently, a party to make a mistake as to the substance of the thing.
  10. Sec 19: contract caused by coercion, fraud or misrepresentation is voidable at the option of the party whose consent was so caused. Exception: if the consent was caused by misrepresentation or by silence fraudulent under Sec 17, the contract is not voidable if the party had the means of discovering the truth with ordinary diligence. This exception does NOT apply to fraud by active statement (Sec 19 Explanation). Sec 19A: undue influence - the contract may be set aside absolutely or, if the party has received any benefit, upon such terms as the court deems just.
  11. Sec 20: where both parties are under a mistake as to a matter of fact ESSENTIAL to the agreement, the agreement is void (bilateral mistake of fact). Illustration: Raffles v Wichelhaus (two ships named Peerless). Sec 21: mistake as to a law NOT in force in India has the same effect as a mistake of fact; a contract is not voidable merely because it was caused by a mistake as to any law in force in India. Sec 22: a contract is not voidable merely because it was caused by one of the parties being under a mistake as to a matter of fact (unilateral mistake). Exceptions in case law: mistake as to the identity of the party/ nature of the document (non est factum).
  12. Effect summary: coercion/undue influence/fraud/misrepresentation = VOIDABLE; bilateral mistake of essential fact = VOID; unilateral mistake = valid.

⚖Landmark cases

Mohori Bibee v Dharmodas Ghose

1903 PC

Minor's agreement void ab initio; no restitution under Sec 65; no estoppel against minor.

Chikkam Ammiraju v Chikkam Seshamma

1917

Threat of suicide amounts to coercion.

Ranganayakamma v Alwar Setti

1889

Coercion by preventing cremation of husband's body until the widow adopted a boy: voidable.

Raghunath Prasad v Sarju Prasad

1924 PC

Undue influence and unconscionable interest rate: burden on the dominant party.

Subhas Chandra Das Mushib v Ganga Prasad Das Mushib

1967

Three-stage test for undue influence: relationship; dominance; unfair advantage.

Derry v Peek

1889

Definition of fraud.

Raffles v Wichelhaus

1864

Mutual mistake: no consensus ad idem; contract void.

✎Memory aids

  • Voidable group: C-U-F-M(isrep) = 'Can U Find Me?' Coercion, Undue influence, Fraud, Misrepresentation. Mistake (bilateral) = void.

!Exam traps

  • TrapMinor's agreement is VOID (not voidable) in India. English law makes some voidable.
  • TrapCoercion needs an act 'forbidden by IPC/BNS' - it need not be punishable in the place; mere threat of civil suit is NOT coercion.
  • TrapFraud by silence vs fraud by statement: Sec 19 exception (ordinary diligence) protects the innocent only in misrepresentation/silence cases, not in positive fraud.
  • TrapMisrepresentation does not require intention to deceive; fraud does.
  • TrapUnilateral mistake does not affect the contract (Sec 22).

?Likely questions

  1. Mohori Bibee v Dharmodas Ghose is about? (minor's agreement)
  2. A threatens to commit suicide unless B agrees: this is? (coercion)
  3. Contract entered into by a person under the influence of a person in a dominant position is: voidable.
  4. A contract caused by bilateral mistake of fact is: void.
