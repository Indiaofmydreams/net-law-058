Transfer of Property Act: Mortgage (Secs 58-104)

Very high yield1 Qs expected12 min readSec 58(a)-(g); Sec 59; Sec 60-62; Sec 65A; Sec 67-69; Sec 76; Sec 78; Sec 81-83; Sec 91-92

iOverview

The single most important chapter of the TPA. Expect questions on definition, the six types, mode of creation (Sec 59), redemption (Sec 60), foreclosure and sale (Sec 67), mortgagee-in-possession and priority.

★Must know

  1. Sec 58(a): a mortgage is the transfer of an interest in specific immovable property for the purpose of securing the payment of money advanced or to be advanced by way of loan, an existing or future debt, or the performance of an engagement which may give rise to a pecuniary liability. The transferor is called the mortgagor, the transferee a mortgagee; the principal money and interest of which payment is secured for the time being are called the mortgage-money, and the instrument (if any) by which the transfer is effected is called a mortgage-deed. A mortgage is a transfer of an INTEREST (not ownership); the mortgagor retains the equity of redemption.
  2. Sec 58(b) SIMPLE MORTGAGE: without delivering possession of the mortgaged property, the mortgagor binds himself personally to pay the mortgage-money, and agrees, expressly or impliedly, that, in the event of his failing to pay according to his contract, the mortgagee shall have a right to cause the mortgaged property to be sold and the proceeds of sale to be applied, so far as may be necessary, in payment of the mortgage-money. REMEDY: suit for sale (Sec 67); personal remedy. Possession remains with mortgagor.
  3. Sec 58(c) MORTGAGE BY CONDITIONAL SALE: the mortgagor ostensibly sells the mortgaged property (i) on condition that on default of payment of the mortgage-money on a certain date the sale shall become absolute, or (ii) on condition that on such payment being made the sale shall become void, or (iii) on condition that on such payment being made the buyer shall transfer the property to the seller. Proviso: no such transaction shall be deemed to be a mortgage, unless the condition is embodied in the document which effects or purports to effect the sale. REMEDY: foreclosure (Sec 67). Distinguish from a sale with a condition of repurchase (a separate document or a contemporaneous contract would be a sale with option to repurchase, not a mortgage).
  4. Sec 58(d) USUFRUCTUARY MORTGAGE: the mortgagor delivers possession or expressly or by implication binds himself to deliver possession of the mortgaged property to the mortgagee, and authorises him to retain such possession until payment of the mortgage-money, and to receive the rents and profits accruing from the property or any part of such rents and profits and to appropriate the same in lieu of interest, or in payment of the mortgage-money, or partly in lieu of interest or partly in payment of the mortgage-money. REMEDY: retention of possession until the debt is satisfied; no right to foreclose or sell (unless the deed provides in addition); no personal liability unless the mortgagor has so covenanted. Mortgagor's right: recover possession on payment or on the rents and profits discharging the debt (Sec 62).
  5. Sec 58(e) ENGLISH MORTGAGE: the mortgagor binds himself to repay the mortgage-money on a certain date, and transfers the mortgaged property absolutely to the mortgagee, but subject to a proviso that he will re-transfer it to the mortgagor upon payment of the mortgage-money as agreed. Features: absolute transfer + personal covenant + re-transfer proviso. Sec 69: power of sale without court intervention is available to an English mortgagee in specified places (where the mortgagor and mortgagee are Hindu, Muslim, Buddhist - excluded in some towns - and where the mortgagee is the Government), subject to notice and conditions. Sec 58(f) EQUITABLE MORTGAGE (MORTGAGE BY DEPOSIT OF TITLE-DEEDS): where a person in any of the towns of Calcutta, Madras and Bombay, and in any other town which the State Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, specify, delivers to a creditor or his agent documents of title to immovable property, with intent to create a security thereon, the transaction is called a mortgage by deposit of title-deeds. Requires: (i) a debt; (ii) deposit of title-deeds; (iii) intention to create security. No writing or registration is required (Sec 59 exempts). If a memorandum of deposit is executed that itself creates the mortgage, it needs registration. REMEDY: sale (and foreclosure not available); personal liability if there is a covenant. Sec 58(g) ANOMALOUS MORTGAGE: a mortgage which is not a simple mortgage, a mortgage by conditional sale, an usufructuary mortgage, an English mortgage or a mortgage by deposit of title-deeds within the meaning of this section.
  6. Sec 59: where the principal money secured is one hundred rupees or upwards, a mortgage can be effected only by a registered instrument signed by the mortgagor and attested by at least two witnesses. Where the principal money secured is less than one hundred rupees, a mortgage may be effected either by a registered instrument signed and attested as aforesaid, or (except in the case of a simple mortgage) by delivery of the property. Mortgage by deposit of title-deeds is excepted. A mortgage not attested by two witnesses is invalid as a mortgage (not saved by Sec 59 later amendment). Attestation requires the witness to see the executant sign (Sec 3 TPA 'attested').
  7. Sec 60 RIGHT TO REDEEM: at any time after the principal money has become payable, the mortgagor has a right, on payment or tender, at a proper time and place, of the mortgage-money, to require the mortgagee (a) to deliver to the mortgagor the mortgage-deed and all documents relating to the mortgaged property which are in the possession or power of the mortgagee; (b) where the mortgagee is in possession of the mortgaged property, to deliver possession thereof to the mortgagor; (c) at the cost of the mortgagor either to re-transfer the mortgaged property to him or to such third person as he may direct, or to execute and (where the mortgage has been effected by a registered instrument) to have registered an acknowledgment in writing that any right in derogation of his interest transferred to the mortgagee has been extinguished. The right conferred is called a right to redeem, and a suit to enforce it is a suit for redemption. The right of redemption is not extinguished except by the act of the parties or by decree of a court. The CLOG ON EQUITY OF REDEMPTION rule - 'once a mortgage always a mortgage': any stipulation that restricts or fetters the right of redemption (e.g., condition postponing redemption for an unreasonable time or giving the mortgagee a collateral advantage) is void (Seth Ganga Dhar v Shankar Lal, 1958; Noakes & Co v Rice). Sec 60A: mortgagor's right to require assignment instead of re-transfer. Sec 61: right to redeem separately or simultaneously where mortgagor has executed two or more mortgages in favour of the same mortgagee (consolidation of mortgages is permitted only where the mortgages contain such a condition).
  8. Sec 62: usufructuary mortgagor's right to recover possession - on the mortgagee's receipt of rents and profits satisfying the principal and interest, or on payment of the remaining sum or deposit; Sec 63: accession to mortgaged property - in the absence of contract, the mortgagee in possession is entitled to the accession; Sec 63A: improvements to mortgaged property; Sec 65: mortgagor's rights while in possession; Sec 65A: MORTGAGOR'S POWER TO LEASE - a mortgagor in possession may lease the property: agricultural lease for a term not exceeding 5 years; non-agricultural lease (house, etc.) not exceeding 3 years; the lease must take effect from a date not later than six months from the date on which it is made, reserve the best rent obtainable, without premium and without payment of rent in advance, and not contain a covenant for renewal. Sec 66: mortgagor's waste (a mortgagor in possession must not commit acts destructive or permanently injurious to the property).
  9. Sec 67: RIGHT TO FORECLOSE OR SELL - at any time after the mortgage-money has become due to him, and before a decree has been made for the redemption of the mortgaged property, or the mortgage-money has been paid or deposited as hereinafter provided, the mortgagee has, subject to the provisions of Sec 67A, a right to obtain from the Court an order that the mortgagor shall be absolutely debarred of his right to redeem the property (a decree for foreclosure), or an order that the property be sold (a decree for sale). Foreclosure is available only in mortgage by conditional sale and in anomalous mortgage if the deed so provides; sale is available in simple, English (but English mortgagee can sell only by court unless Sec 69), equitable (deposit of title deeds) mortgages. Usufructuary mortgagee: neither foreclosure nor sale (unless deed provides otherwise). Sec 68: right to sue for the mortgage-money - only in cases where the mortgagor binds himself to repay, or where the mortgagee is deprived of possession/ security is destroyed, or where the mortgagee is entitled to possession but the mortgagor fails to deliver. Sec 69: power of sale without intervention of the court (only for English mortgages and for mortgages in which the deed gives a power of sale - to be exercised after notice and 3 months' default, and subject to the limitations in Sec 69); Sec 69A: power to appoint receiver.
  10. Sec 70: accession to mortgaged property; Sec 72: rights of mortgagee in possession - to spend money necessary for the preservation of the property, defend title, protect from forfeiture; Sec 73: charge on proceeds of insurance and on the compensation (acquired under the Land Acquisition law); Sec 76: LIABILITIES OF MORTGAGEE IN POSSESSION - (a) to manage the property as a person of ordinary prudence would manage; (b) to use his best endeavours to collect the rents and profits; (c) to apply the receipts in due order; (d) to keep accounts; (e) not to commit waste; and he is liable for loss due to his wilful default or gross negligence. Sec 77: receipts in lieu of interest. Sec 78: POSTPONEMENT OF PRIOR MORTGAGEE - where, through the fraud, misrepresentation or gross neglect of a prior mortgagee, another person has been induced to advance money on the security of the mortgaged property, the prior mortgagee shall be postponed to the subsequent mortgagee (e.g., leaving title deeds with the mortgagor). Sec 79: mesne mortgagee has the same rights against prior and subsequent mortgagees as a mortgagee. Sec 81: marshalling securities - if the owner of two or more properties mortgages them to one person and then mortgages one or more of the properties to another person, the subsequent mortgagee is, in the absence of a contract to the contrary, entitled to have the prior mortgage-debt satisfied out of the property or properties not mortgaged to him. Sec 82: contribution to mortgage-debt where properties of different persons are mortgaged. Sec 83: deposit in court by mortgagor - mortgagor may deposit the amount due in court and the interest ceases from the date of deposit.
  11. Sec 85: all persons having an interest in the property or the right of redemption (mortgagor/ subsequent mortgagees/ transferees) must be joined as parties in suits for foreclosure, sale and redemption (Order XXXIV CPC); Sec 88-90: preliminary and final decrees; Sec 90 (deficiency decree): if the sale proceeds are insufficient the mortgagee may obtain personal decree for the balance (Order XXXIV Rule 6 CPC). Sec 91: persons who may sue for redemption: any person (other than the mortgagee of the interest sought to be redeemed) who has any interest in, or charge upon, the property mortgaged or in or upon the right to redeem the same, or any surety for the payment of the mortgage-debt or any part thereof. Sec 92: SUBROGATION - any of the persons referred to in Sec 91 (other than the mortgagor), and any co-mortgagor, shall, on redeeming property subject to the mortgage, have, so far as regards the redemption, foreclosure or sale of such property, the same rights as the mortgagee whose mortgage he redeems. Sec 93-96: Sec 94 priority of a person redeeming (not necessary here).
  12. Limitation (Limitation Act 1963): suit by mortgagor to redeem - 30 years from when the right to redeem or to recover possession accrues (Article 61(a)); suit by mortgagee for foreclosure - 30 years from the date the money sued for becomes due (Article 63(a)); suit by mortgagee for possession of the property mortgaged - 12 years from the date of the mortgage-money becoming due (Article 63(b)); suit to enforce payment of money secured by a mortgage or charged upon immovable property (sale) - 12 years from when the money sued for becomes due (Article 62).
  13. Recent trend: SARFAESI Act and IBC lawyers - mortgages enforced extra-judicially by banks under SARFAESI Sec 13; Sec 69 TPA power of sale. Also: registration of mortgage by deposit of title deeds - banks register memorandum/ CERSAI registration (Central Registry of Securitisation Asset Reconstruction and Security Interest) for creation of security interest.

⚖Landmark cases

Seth Ganga Dhar v Shankar Lal

1958

Clog on equity of redemption - any fetter on the right to redeem is void.

!Exam traps

  • TrapForeclosure is available ONLY for mortgage by conditional sale (and anomalous if so provided).
  • TrapEquitable mortgage requires: deposit of title deeds in a notified town with the intention to create security; no writing or registration necessary.
  • TrapMortgage of Rs 100 and above requires a registered instrument signed by the mortgagor and attested by 2 witnesses.
  • TrapMortgagor's lease power under Sec 65A: agricultural up to 5 years; non-agricultural up to 3 years.
  • TrapRight of redemption cannot be fettered - clog on equity of redemption.
  • TrapUsufructuary mortgagee cannot foreclose or sue for sale.

?Likely questions

  1. Mortgage by deposit of title-deeds: Sec 58(f).
  2. Which mortgage gives right of foreclosure? Mortgage by conditional sale.
  3. Mortgagee's right to foreclose or sell: Sec 67.
  4. Right to redeem: Sec 60; subrogation: Sec 92; marshalling: Sec 81.
  5. Mortgage of Rs 100 and above: registered instrument, 2 attesting witnesses.
