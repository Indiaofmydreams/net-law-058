iOverview

Not always asked but sometimes appear in this group. Revise only after finishing core areas.

★Must know

  1. SALE OF GOODS ACT: Sec 4 - sale = a contract whereby the seller transfers or agrees to transfer the property in goods to the buyer for a price. Sale (executed; property passes) vs agreement to sell (executory; future/ conditional). Goods: all movable property other than actionable claims and money; includes stock, shares, growing crops, grass, things attached to or forming part of land agreed to be severed.
  2. Sec 12: stipulation may be condition (essential to main purpose; breach gives right to repudiate) or warranty (collateral; breach gives only damages). A breach of condition may be treated as breach of warranty (Sec 13). Implied conditions: title (Sec 14(a)), sale by description (Sec 15), quality/fitness (Sec 16 - caveat emptor is the rule with exceptions), merchantability, sale by sample (Sec 17). Implied warranties: quiet possession, freedom from encumbrance (Sec 14(b),(c)).
  3. Passing of property: Sec 18 - specific/ascertained goods needed for transfer; Sec 19 - property passes when parties intend; Sec 20 - specific goods in deliverable state: passes at contract; Sec 23 - unascertained goods: by unconditional appropriation; Sec 24 - sale on approval. Risk follows property (Sec 26) unless otherwise agreed.
  4. Nemo dat quod non habet: Sec 27 - seller cannot give better title than he has. Exceptions: sale by mercantile agent (Sec 27 proviso/ Factors Act), by co-owner in possession (Sec 28), under voidable title (Sec 29), seller or buyer in possession after sale (Sec 30), estoppel, sale by order of court, sale by finder/ pawnee, etc.
  5. Unpaid seller (Sec 45): rights against goods - lien (Sec 47), stoppage in transit (Sec 50), resale (Sec 54); suit for price (Sec 55) and damages for non-acceptance (Sec 56). Buyer's remedies: damages (Sec 57), repudiation, suit for breach of warranty (Sec 59).
  6. PARTNERSHIP ACT: Sec 4 - relation between persons who have agreed to share the profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all. Not status but contract (Sec 5). Mode of determining existence: Sec 6 (real relation; mere sharing of profits is not conclusive). Sec 9: general duties (utmost good faith). Sec 13: rights/duties between partners (interest on capital, remuneration, etc.). Sec 18-19: partner is agent of the firm; implied authority; Sec 19(2) lists acts a partner cannot do under implied authority (e.g., submit disputes to arbitration, open a bank account in own name, compromise claim, withdraw a suit, admit liability, acquire immovable property on behalf of firm - subject to contrary custom). Sec 25: liability of partners joint and several (for all acts of the firm while a partner). Sec 28: holding out (partner by estoppel). Sec 30: minor admitted to the benefits of partnership (no personal liability; may elect to become partner within six months of attaining majority - Sec 30(5)). Sec 32: retirement; Sec 40-44: dissolution; Sec 69: effects of non-registration - an unregistered firm cannot sue (Sec 69(1) - no suit by partner against firm/ other partners; Sec 69(2) - firm against third parties) to enforce a contractual right; but can sue for statutory rights, and can be sued; set-off up to Rs 100 allowed; unregistered status does not affect dissolution suits, receiver actions, etc.

!Exam traps

  • TrapSale of Goods: condition vs warranty - remedies differ.
  • TrapPartnership: Sec 69 bars suit by unregistered firm for CONTRACTUAL rights; it doesn't invalidate the partnership itself.
