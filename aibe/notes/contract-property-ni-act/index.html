%Where the marks come from

Contract, Specific Relief, Property and Negotiable Instruments carries about 8 of 100 questions. 100 questions, Objective multiple-choice (MCQ), offline OMR; 3 hours 30 minutes; negative marking: none; open book: Yes - candidates may refer to bare Acts and permitted study material as per BCI instructions on the admit card.

BCI does not publish topic-level distribution. The split above reflects the subject grouping in the AIBE weightage chart (about 8 questions from Contract, Specific Relief, Property and NI Act together, and about 2 from Land Acquisition). (Scale: 0 to 10 questions.)

What the examiner tests

  • AIBE tests recognition, not long analysis. Typical question styles: (1) 'Which section deals with...?'; (2) 'Which case laid down...?'; (3) numbers and periods (days, months, years, percentages); (4) 'Which of the following is correct/incorrect?'; (5) pair-comparison questions (void vs voidable, mortgage vs charge, lease vs licence, indemnity vs guarantee).
  • Because the paper is open-book, questions reward knowing WHERE to look quickly. Tab and underline: Contract Act Secs 2, 10, 15-19, 23-30, 56, 73-74; SRA Secs 6, 10, 14, 16, 20, 34, 38-42; TPA Secs 41, 43, 52, 53A, 54, 58, 59, 105-107, 111, 122-123; NI Act Secs 138-148; RFCTLARR Secs 24-26, 30, 101.
  • Examiners pick provisions that were amended recently (SRA 2018; NI Act 2015 and 2018), that are overlaid by landmark cases, and that carry crisp numbers.
  • The transition from IPC/CrPC/Evidence Act to BNS/BNSS/BSA (in force from 1 July 2024) is a standing source of 'updating' questions across this subject: Sec 15 Contract Act (coercion) refers to IPC, now read as BNS; NI Act trials use summary-trial procedure (now BNSS).
