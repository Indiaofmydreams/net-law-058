Contract, Specific Relief, Property and Negotiable Instruments

Latest developments

Recent judgments, amendments and institutional changes that are favourite current-affairs questions. Notes cover developments up to 17 Nov 2026; anything tagged Verify should be checked against the official source.

Specific performance: continuous readiness and willingness

June-July 2026

Supreme Court judgments of 2026 (e.g., Mohammed Khaleel v Jayamma, 23 June 2026; V.N.A.S. Chandran v S. Venila, 31 July 2026) restate that Sec 16(c) SRA requires continuous readiness (financial capacity) and willingness (conduct), pleaded and proved from the date of agreement to decree; post-suit financial documents are insufficient and long unexplained delay can justify refusal of a decree. Other 2026 rulings hold that a buyer's delay in sending a legal notice is not fatal where nearly the whole price was paid (2026 INSC 573).

Why it matters: Principle-based MCQ: 'Readiness refers to financial capacity; willingness refers to conduct.'

Specific Relief (Amendment) Act 2018 and its prospectivity

Ongoing

Specific performance is now enforceable as a general rule (Sec 10), substituted performance (Sec 20), infrastructure contracts (Secs 20A-20C, 41(ha)). The Supreme Court treats the 2018 amendments as prospective, so older agreements are tested under the unamended Act.

Why it matters: Distinguish pre- and post-2018 law.

Section 138 NI Act: recent decisions

Jan-Nov 2026

(1) Multiple cheques from one transaction can ground separate complaints (Jan 2026). (2) Complaint cannot be quashed at the pre-trial stage once Sec 138 ingredients are prima facie met (Renuka v State of Maharashtra, 7 Apr 2026). (3) Bank's failure to present a cheque within validity can be deficiency in service (Canara Bank v Kavita Chowdhary, Apr 2026). (4) Conviction set aside where the cheque showed patent tampering (17 July 2026). (5) Mere denial of debt does not rebut the Sec 139 presumption (4 Aug 2026). (6) An Asset Reconstruction Company can pursue Sec 138 as assignee of the loan (4 Nov 2026). (7) The Sanjabij Tari line of cases consolidates procedural directions and revisits compounding (verify exact directions in the judgment).

Why it matters: Expect statement-type MCQs on presumption (Sec 139), jurisdiction (Sec 142(2)), interim compensation (Sec 143A), appeal deposit (Sec 148), and compounding (Sec 147).

Art 300A and procedural rights

Jan 2024 (settled law, still hot)

Kolkata Municipal Corporation v Bimal Kumar Shaw: seven sub-rights (notice, hearing, reasoned decision, public purpose, compensation, efficient process, conclusion).

Why it matters: Direct recall question likely.

BNS/BNSS/BSA effect

From 1 July 2024

References in the Contract Act (Sec 15 - IPC) and NI Act procedure (CrPC) are read with the new codes through the General Clauses Act; summary trial provisions now in BNSS (Secs 283-288).

Why it matters: Updating questions.

Property transactions: registered conveyance

Settled law, repeatedly cited

Suraj Lamp (2012) continues to be cited: GPA/SA/WILL arrangements do not transfer title to immovable property; the registration of agreements to sell for Sec 53A protection has been mandatory since 2001.

Why it matters: Property title and registration questions.
