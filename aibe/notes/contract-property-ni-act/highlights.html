1

Nature of Contract, Offer and Acceptance (Sections 1-10)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co 1893 Advertisement with a deposit to show sincerity = general offer; performance of conditions = acceptance; no need to communicate acceptance.
  • Lalman Shukla v Gauri Dutt 1913 Knowledge of the offer is essential to acceptance.
  • Harvey v Facey 1893 A statement of the lowest price is an invitation to treat, not an offer.
  • Felthouse v Bindley 1862 Silence cannot be imposed as acceptance.
  • Balfour v Balfour 1919 Domestic agreements lack intention to create legal relations.
  • Pharmaceutical Society v Boots 1953 Goods on a shelf are an invitation to treat; the customer makes the offer at the till.

Memory aids

  • Sec 4: 'Offer = knowledge; Acceptance = despatch (against proposer) / knowledge (against acceptor)'.
  • Sec 6 revocation modes = N-L-C-D: Notice, Lapse, Condition failure, Death/insanity.

Exam traps

  • TrapOffer is complete on KNOWLEDGE; acceptance is complete against the proposer on DESPATCH. Questions swap these.
  • Trap'Invitation to treat' vs 'offer': advertisements, price-lists, tender notices are NOT offers (unless Carlill-type definite general offer).
  • TrapA counter-offer destroys the original offer (Hyde v Wrench).
  • TrapVoid agreement vs void contract vs voidable contract: read Sec 2(g), (i), (j) carefully.
2

Consideration and Privity (Sections 2(d), 25)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Chinnaya v Ramayya 1882 Consideration can move from a third party; promisee need not be the one giving it.
  • Durga Prasad v Baldeo 1880 Consideration must be at the desire of the promisor; a person who built shops on his own initiative cannot claim.
  • Kedar Nath v Gorie Mohamed 1886 Subscribers' promise to build a Town Hall: expenses incurred on the faith of the promise constituted consideration.
  • M.C. Chacko v State Bank of Travancore 1969 A beneficiary can enforce a contract made for his benefit.
  • Motilal Padampat Sugar Mills v State of UP 1979 Promissory estoppel applicable against Government.
  • Tweddle v Atkinson (1861); Dunlop v Selfridge 1915 Privity of contract.

Exam traps

  • TrapSec 25(1) needs BOTH writing and REGISTRATION and 'natural love and affection' and 'near relation'.
  • TrapSec 25(3) (time-barred debt) needs a WRITTEN promise SIGNED by the debtor; oral promises don't qualify. It is a new promise, not an extension of limitation.
  • Trap'Consideration need not be adequate but must be real'. A question may ask which of these is 'not' valid consideration: past consideration is valid in India, but past voluntary act not at promisor's desire is not.
3

Capacity to Contract and Free Consent (Sections 11-22)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Mohori Bibee v Dharmodas Ghose 1903 PC Minor's agreement void ab initio; no restitution under Sec 65; no estoppel against minor.
  • Chikkam Ammiraju v Chikkam Seshamma 1917 Threat of suicide amounts to coercion.
  • Ranganayakamma v Alwar Setti 1889 Coercion by preventing cremation of husband's body until the widow adopted a boy: voidable.
  • Raghunath Prasad v Sarju Prasad 1924 PC Undue influence and unconscionable interest rate: burden on the dominant party.
  • Subhas Chandra Das Mushib v Ganga Prasad Das Mushib 1967 Three-stage test for undue influence: relationship; dominance; unfair advantage.
  • Derry v Peek 1889 Definition of fraud.
  • Raffles v Wichelhaus 1864 Mutual mistake: no consensus ad idem; contract void.

Memory aids

  • Voidable group: C-U-F-M(isrep) = 'Can U Find Me?' Coercion, Undue influence, Fraud, Misrepresentation. Mistake (bilateral) = void.

Exam traps

  • TrapMinor's agreement is VOID (not voidable) in India. English law makes some voidable.
  • TrapCoercion needs an act 'forbidden by IPC/BNS' - it need not be punishable in the place; mere threat of civil suit is NOT coercion.
  • TrapFraud by silence vs fraud by statement: Sec 19 exception (ordinary diligence) protects the innocent only in misrepresentation/silence cases, not in positive fraud.
  • TrapMisrepresentation does not require intention to deceive; fraud does.
  • TrapUnilateral mistake does not affect the contract (Sec 22).
4

Lawful Object, Void Agreements and Restraints (Sections 23-30)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Niranjan Shankar Golikari v Century Spinning 1967 Restraint during employment term is valid; not hit by Sec 27.
  • Superintendence Co of India v Krishan Murgai 1981 Post-termination restraint is void under Sec 27.
  • Gherulal Parakh v Mahadeodas Maiya 1959 Wager vs speculation; immoral objects.
  • Central Inland Water Transport v Brojo Nath Ganguly 1986 Unconscionable terms in standard-form (service) contracts are void under Sec 23 and Art 14.
  • Hakam Singh v Gammon (India) Ltd 1971 Parties may choose between courts having jurisdiction; ouster of all courts is void.
  • Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball - Not a wager - Sec 30 distinction.

Exam traps

  • TrapSec 27 has only ONE statutory exception in the Contract Act: sale of goodwill. Others come from Partnership Act or case law.
  • TrapArbitration clauses are an exception to Sec 28.
  • TrapA wager is void, NOT illegal/forbidden (except in certain States); collateral transactions are enforceable.
  • TrapRestraint on marriage: only of a MINOR is excepted.
5

Contingent Contracts, Performance, Time and Impossibility (Secs 31-58)

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Satyabrata Ghose v Mugneeram Bangur 1954 Sec 56 is exhaustive of frustration in India; 'impossible' includes practical impossibility; no implied-term theory.
  • Taylor v Caldwell 1863 Destruction of subject-matter frustrates the contract.
  • Krell v Henry 1903 Frustration of purpose.
  • Energy Watchdog v CERC 2017 Rise in coal prices does not frustrate a contract; commercial hardship is no excuse.
  • Hochster v De la Tour 1853 Anticipatory breach.
  • Chand Rani v Kamal Rani 1993 Time not of essence for immovable property sale unless expressly stated or implied.

Exam traps

  • TrapSec 56 paragraph 2: contract becomes void on impossibility; the loss lies where it falls, but restitution is allowed under Sec 65.
  • TrapMere increase in cost or difficulty is NOT impossibility.
  • TrapTime of essence for immovable property: not presumed; in commercial/mercantile contracts: presumed.
  • TrapSec 38 tender: tender of goods vs tender of money (money tender does not discharge debt).
6

Discharge, Breach, Damages and Quasi-Contracts (Secs 62-75)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Hadley v Baxendale 1854 Remoteness of damage: natural consequence or special knowledge.
  • Fateh Chand v Balkishan Das 1963 Sec 74: reasonable compensation; the party cannot forfeit more than reasonable compensation; the Court can grant relief even if no loss proved up to the named amount.
  • ONGC v Saw Pipes 2003 Liquidated damages enforceable where loss is difficult to assess; Sec 74 and arbitral awards.
  • Kailash Nath Associates v DDA 2015 Forfeiture of earnest money must be proved reasonable; party must show actual loss when quantifiable.
  • State of West Bengal v B.K. Mondal 1962 Sec 70 - benefits enjoyed, no gratuitous intent.
  • Sales Tax Officer v Kanhaiya Lal Mukund Lal 1959 Sec 72 - tax paid under mistake/ coercion refundable.

Exam traps

  • TrapSec 74 is about reasonable compensation 'not exceeding' the named sum - the sum is a ceiling.
  • TrapSec 70 requires the benefit to be ENJOYED - not merely conferred.
  • TrapQuasi-contract is NOT based on consent; it is based on equity and justice.
  • TrapSec 65 applies to void agreements discovered and contracts that become void; Sec 64 to voidable contracts rescinded.
  • TrapDamages for mental agony: not usually granted in breach of contract (exceptions: marriage promise, hotel booking cases in consumer law).
7

Indemnity and Guarantee (Secs 124-147)

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Bank of Bihar v Damodar Prasad 1969 Surety's liability is immediate and co-extensive; creditor needn't exhaust remedies against the principal debtor.
  • Gajanan Moreshwar Parelkar v Moreshwar Madan Mantri 1942 Rights of indemnity-holder under Sec 125 are not exhaustive; equitable relief available.
  • State of Maharashtra v M.N. Kaul 1967 Surety discharged where creditor's act (variance) affects principal debtor without his consent.

Exam traps

  • TrapSec 128 - co-extensive does not mean identical: the surety's liability may be limited by contract.
  • TrapSec 133: variance discharges the surety only for subsequent transactions.
  • TrapA guarantee for a continuing account - death revokes future liability (Sec 131).
  • TrapIn indemnity, the indemnifier's promise is primary; in guarantee the surety's liability is secondary/collateral.

Bailment and Pledge (Secs 148-181)

Medium-high yieldRead the full chapter →

Exam traps

  • TrapParticular lien (Sec 170) is a right of every skilled bailee; general lien (Sec 171) belongs ONLY to bankers, factors, wharfingers, attorneys, policy-brokers.
  • TrapPledge is a species of bailment - requires delivery; pledge of movable only. Mortgage is for immovables.
  • TrapFinder's right of sale needs perishing or lawful charges >= two-thirds of value.
  • TrapBailee's care is the same for gratuitous and non-gratuitous bailments (Sec 151).

Agency (Secs 182-238)

Medium yieldRead the full chapter →

Exam traps

  • TrapMinor can be an agent but is not responsible to his principal (Sec 184); principal must be major and sound minded (Sec 183).
  • TrapRatification must be of the whole transaction; a void act cannot be ratified.
  • TrapAgency coupled with interest is irrevocable and doesn't terminate on the principal's death/insanity (Sec 202, 201).
  • TrapSec 185: no consideration needed for agency.

Specific Relief Act: Overview and the 2018 Amendment

Cases in one line

  • Katta Sujatha Reddy v Siddamsetty Infra Projects 2022 2018 amendments to SRA are prospective.

Exam traps

  • TrapPre-2018 contracts: unamended Sec 20 discretion applies.

Specific Relief Act: Recovery of Possession (Secs 5-8)

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Nair Service Society v K.C. Alexander 1968 Possession is protected against dispossession other than by due process; Sec 6 is a summary remedy.
  • Sanjay Kumar Pandey v Gulbahar Sheikh 2004 Sec 6 suit is to protect settled possession; title not examined; and its scope.

Exam traps

  • TrapSix months limit (not 12 years); no appeal and no review; not against the Government.
  • TrapA suit under Sec 6 does not bar a later title suit.
13

Specific Relief Act: Specific Performance of Contracts (Secs 9-25)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Mohammed Khaleel v Jayamma (SC, 23 Jun 2026) - Continuous readiness and willingness from the date of agreement to decree; readiness is financial capacity, willingness is conduct; post-suit financial documents insufficient.
  • V.N.A.S. Chandran v S. Venila (SC, 31 Jul 2026) - Specific performance refused after prolonged delay and inconsistent conduct; decree not an automatic consequence of a valid agreement.
  • Lourdu Mari David v Louis Chinnaya Arogiaswamy 1996 Plaintiff must plead and prove readiness and willingness.
  • K.S. Vidyanadam v Vairavan 1997 Time not of essence for immovable property, but unreasonable delay in rising markets matters.
  • Saradamani Kandappan v S. Rajalakshmi 2011 Rising property prices: courts require prompt action.
  • Chand Rani v Kamal Rani 1993 Time is not of the essence in agreements for sale of immovable property.
  • Satya Jain v Anis Ahmed Rushdie 2013 Equitable considerations in specific performance, burden on plaintiff.

Exam traps

  • TrapPost-2018 Sec 10: 'shall be enforced'. Pre-2018: 'may be enforced' with discretion (old Sec 20).
  • TrapSec 14 grounds now are only four (a-d). 'Compensation adequate relief' ground was DELETED in 2018.
  • TrapSec 20: substituted performance needs at least 30 days' written notice.
  • TrapSec 20C: 12 months (+6 months) for infrastructure suits.
  • TrapReadiness and willingness is a question of fact, and must be PLEADED and PROVED.

High yieldRead the full chapter →

  • Lumley v Wagner 1852 Negative covenants enforceable by injunction.

Transfer of Property Act: General Principles (Secs 1-9)

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Exam traps

  • TrapSpes successionis (chance of succession) is NOT transferable. Mere right to sue is NOT transferable (but a right to sue for specific property or for money due is transferable as an actionable claim).
  • TrapRight to future maintenance is not transferable; arrears of maintenance are.
  • TrapSec 10 restraint on alienation is void - but partial restraints (time-bound or limited to class) may be valid. Lease restraints in favour of lessor are valid.
  • TrapPerpetuity: life in being + minority period (18 years).
18

Doctrines under the TPA (Election, Ostensible Owner, Estoppel, Lis Pendens, Part Performance)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Suraj Lamp & Industries v State of Haryana 2012 SA/GPA/WILL transactions are not valid transfers of immovable property; only registered conveyance transfers title.
  • Jayadayal Poddar v Bibi Hazra 1974 Burden of proving Sec 41 conditions lies on the transferee.
  • Shrimant Shamrao Suryavanshi v Pralhad Bhairoba Suryavanshi 2002 Sec 53A is a shield, not a sword - defendant in possession can protect, but not sue on this.
  • Jumma Masjid v Kodimaniandra Deviah 1962 Sec 52 - transfer pendente lite is subject to the decree; applies irrespective of notice.
  • Twyne's Case 1601 Fraudulent transfers to defeat creditors.

Exam traps

  • TrapSec 41 needs CONSIDERATION + GOOD FAITH + REASONABLE CARE; Sec 43 needs transfer by one who erroneously/ fraudulently represents authority.
  • TrapLis pendens: transfer is not void; it is subject to the final decree. Applies irrespective of notice.
  • TrapSec 53A cannot be used by plaintiff as basis for a title suit; and post-2001 the agreement must be registered.
  • TrapSec 53(1) is voidable at the option of a defrauded creditor; Sec 53(2): subsequent transferee.

Transfer of Property Act: Sale of Immovable Property (Secs 54-57)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Exam traps

  • TrapSale of tangible immovable property worth Rs 100 and above: ONLY by registered instrument; below Rs 100 - registered instrument OR delivery of possession.
  • TrapAgreement to sell does NOT create any interest in or charge on the property.
  • TrapSeller's charge (Sec 55(4)) arises for unpaid price with interest from date of delivery of possession.
  • TrapBuyer bears the loss after ownership has passed (Sec 55(5)(d)).

Transfer of Property Act: Mortgage (Secs 58-104)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Seth Ganga Dhar v Shankar Lal 1958 Clog on equity of redemption - any fetter on the right to redeem is void.

Exam traps

  • TrapForeclosure is available ONLY for mortgage by conditional sale (and anomalous if so provided).
  • TrapEquitable mortgage requires: deposit of title deeds in a notified town with the intention to create security; no writing or registration necessary.
  • TrapMortgage of Rs 100 and above requires a registered instrument signed by the mortgagor and attested by 2 witnesses.
  • TrapMortgagor's lease power under Sec 65A: agricultural up to 5 years; non-agricultural up to 3 years.
  • TrapRight of redemption cannot be fettered - clog on equity of redemption.
  • TrapUsufructuary mortgagee cannot foreclose or sue for sale.

22

Transfer of Property Act: Lease (Secs 105-117)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Associated Hotels of India v R.N. Kapoor 1959 Lease vs licence: the test is exclusive possession with intention to create an interest.
  • Anthony v K.C. Ittoop & Sons 2000 Unregistered lease for more than a year; holding over leads to year-to-year/ month-to-month tenancy.
  • Bhagabandas Agarwalla v Bhagwandas Kanu 1977 Waiver/ forfeiture and acceptance of rent.

Exam traps

  • TrapSec 106: AGRICULTURAL/ MANUFACTURING = year to year + 6 months' notice; OTHERS = month to month + 15 days' notice.
  • TrapSec 107: lease > 1 year, year to year or yearly rent = REGISTERED instrument. Lease up to one year = registered or oral with possession.
  • TrapSec 117: Chapter on lease does not apply to agricultural leases unless notified.
  • TrapSec 111(g) forfeiture needs a written notice of intention to determine; waiver under Sec 112 by acceptance of rent.
  • TrapSec 116 - holding over requires lessor's acceptance of rent or assent; otherwise tenant is a tenant at sufferance.
23

Exchange, Gift and Actionable Claims (Secs 118-137)

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Renikuntla Rajamma v K. Sarwanamma 2014 A registered gift deed is valid even without delivery of possession; acceptance may be inferred.
  • Asokan v Lakshmikutty 2007 Acceptance of gift, possession and its relevance (gifts under TPA).

Exam traps

  • TrapGift of IMMOVABLE property: registered instrument, signed by donor, attested by at least 2 witnesses - ALWAYS (no value threshold).
  • TrapGift of movable property: registered instrument OR delivery.
  • TrapGift of future property is void to that extent (Sec 124). A gift revocable at the donor's mere will is void.
  • TrapActionable claim: transfer is complete on execution of an instrument in writing; notice to the debtor is NOT a condition of validity (only protects the debtor).
  • TrapMuslim gifts are outside Chapter VII (Sec 129).

Easements and Licences (Indian Easements Act 1882)

Medium yieldRead the full chapter →

Exam traps

  • TrapEasement vs licence: easement is an interest; licence is a personal permission, revocable unless Sec 60 exceptions.
  • TrapPrescription: 20 years (private land); 30 years (Government land).
  • TrapEasement cannot be separated from the dominant heritage; it is not transferable apart from it.
25

Registration Act 1908 (essentials for property questions)

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • K.B. Saha & Sons v Development Consultant Ltd 2008 Unregistered documents can be used for collateral purposes (e.g., to prove the character of possession), not to prove the transaction itself.
  • Suraj Lamp & Industries v State of Haryana 2012 Only registered conveyance can transfer title to immovable property.
  • Park View Enterprises v State of Tamil Nadu 1990 Registering authority cannot decide the validity of title.

Exam traps

  • TrapGift of immovable property: registration is compulsory REGARDLESS of value; sale/ mortgage/ lease - value threshold Rs 100 (lease: 1 year).
  • TrapTime for presentation: 4 months from execution; further 4 months with fine up to ten times the registration fee (so the maximum is 8 months).
  • TrapA registered document operates from the date of EXECUTION, not registration (Sec 47).
  • TrapWills: registration optional.

Negotiable Instruments: Definitions, Types and Parties (Secs 1-17)

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Exam traps

  • TrapCheque is payable ON DEMAND only; days of grace do not apply to cheques/ demand instruments.
  • TrapHolder in due course must have taken BEFORE maturity and for CONSIDERATION and in good faith.
  • TrapSec 25: if the maturity day is a public holiday, due on the preceding business day.
  • TrapCrossing 'not negotiable' does not make the cheque non-transferable; it only removes the ability to confer better title (Sec 130).
  • TrapUnder Sec 6, electronic image of truncated cheque (CTS) and cheque in electronic form are cheques.
  • TrapSec 118 presumptions are rebuttable.
27

Dishonour of Cheque: Section 138 (Chapter XVII, Secs 138-148)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Dashrath Rupsingh Rathod v State of Maharashtra 2014 Held place of drawee bank is the venue; superseded by the 2015 Amendment (Sec 142(2)).
  • Bridgestone India v Inderpal Singh 2016 Effect of 2015 amendment and pending cases; cause of action/ jurisdiction.
  • Rangappa v Sri Mohan 2010 Sec 139 presumption includes existence of legally enforceable debt; rebuttable.
  • Bir Singh v Mukesh Kumar 2019 Signed blank cheque voluntarily given attracts the presumption; filling of cheque by another person is not a defence.
  • S.M.S. Pharmaceuticals v Neeta Bhalla 2005 Vicarious liability of directors must be specifically pleaded; signatory to the cheque is liable.
  • Aneeta Hada v Godfather Travels 2012 Company must be arraigned for directors to be prosecuted under Sec 141.
  • Meters and Instruments v Kanchan Mehta 2018 Summary trial; compounding; offence is essentially civil in nature.
  • Damodar S. Prabhu v Sayed Babalal H 2010 Compounding guidelines with graded costs.
  • Surinder Singh Deswal v Virender Gandhi 2019 Sec 148 applies to appeals even for earlier cheques.
  • G.J. Raja v Tejraj Surana 2019 Sec 143A prospective.
  • Jamboo Bhandari v M.P. State Industrial Development Corp 2023 20% deposit under Sec 148 to be the norm.
  • MSR Leathers v S. Palaniappan 2013 Successive presentation and successive causes of action.
  • C.C. Alavi Haji v Palapetty Muhammed 2007 Service of notice and deemed service; drawer's failure to pay within 15 days.
  • Renuka v State of Maharashtra (SC, 7 Apr 2026) - Complaint cannot be quashed at pre-trial stage once Sec 138 ingredients are prima facie satisfied.
  • Sanjabij Tari v Kishore S. Borcar (SC, recent) - Consolidated directions on Sec 138 procedure; revisited compounding framework; Sec 269SS issue.

Exam traps

  • TrapCheque validity is 3 months (practically), though the statute says 'six months or validity, whichever is earlier'.
  • TrapNotice: 30 days from RECEIPT OF INFORMATION from the bank (not from the date of the cheque). Payment: 15 days from receipt of NOTICE. Complaint: 1 month from the expiry of the 15 days.
  • TrapMaximum punishment: 2 years (since 2018) - not 1 year. Fine: up to twice the cheque amount.
  • Trap143A is for interim compensation by trial court (20% cap); 148 is a deposit in appeal (minimum 20%). Don't confuse 'cap' with 'minimum'.
  • TrapOffence is compoundable (Sec 147) - at any stage with costs as per Damodar Prabhu.
  • TrapJurisdiction post-2015: payee's bank branch (collection through account), not the drawee bank.
  • TrapSec 141: the company must be made an accused; directors need specific averments; signatory automatically liable.
