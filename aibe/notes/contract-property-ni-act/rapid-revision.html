≠Pairs the examiner likes

Void agreement vs voidable contract vs void contract
Void agreement: not enforceable from the start (Sec 2(g)); voidable: enforceable at option of aggrieved party (Sec 2(i)); void contract: valid when made, later ceases to be enforceable (Sec 2(j)).
Coercion vs undue influence
Coercion: act forbidden by IPC/BNS or unlawful detention; undue influence: dominance of will in a relationship. Both make the contract voidable.
Fraud vs misrepresentation
Fraud: intent to deceive; misrepresentation: innocent. Sec 19 diligence exception does not apply to fraud by positive statement.
Liquidated damages vs penalty
Indian law (Sec 74): reasonable compensation not exceeding the sum named, whichever label is used.
Indemnity vs guarantee
Two vs three parties; primary vs secondary liability.
Mortgage vs charge
Mortgage transfers an interest, only by act of parties; charge creates a security without transfer, by act of parties or operation of law.
Lease vs licence
Lease transfers an interest with exclusive possession; licence is a revocable personal permission (Easements Act Sec 52).
Sec 41 vs Sec 43 TPA
Sec 41 protects a good-faith transferee from an ostensible owner against the real owner; Sec 43 enforces the transfer against the transferor's after-acquired interest.
Sec 53A vs specific performance
Sec 53A is a defence (shield) available to a transferee in possession; specific performance is the plaintiff's remedy (sword).
Temporary vs perpetual injunction
Temporary: until further order (CPC Order 39); perpetual: by decree on merits (Sec 38).
Sec 143A vs Sec 148 NI Act
143A: trial court, interim compensation up to 20%, by drawer, refundable on acquittal; 148: appellate court, minimum 20% deposit of fine/compensation.
Sec 138 vs Sec 139
138 defines the offence; 139 raises a rebuttable presumption that the cheque was for a debt or liability.
