Specific Relief Act: Rectification, Rescission and Cancellation (Secs 26-33)

Medium yield1 Qs expected3 min readSec 26; Sec 27-30; Sec 31-33

iOverview

Short, bare-act style questions - who can sue, when relief is refused.

★Must know

  1. Sec 26 RECTIFICATION: where through fraud or a mutual mistake of the parties, a contract or other instrument in writing (not being the articles of association of a company) does not express their real intention, either party (or his representative) may institute a suit to have the instrument rectified; the plaintiff may claim rectification in the suit itself. Rectification is available only for a mistake common to both parties (not unilateral); court may rectify and then enforce the contract. Not for the articles of association of a company.
  2. Sec 27 RESCISSION: any person interested in a contract may sue to have it rescinded in cases where (a) the contract is voidable or terminable by the plaintiff; (b) the contract is unlawful for causes not apparent on its face and the defendant is more to blame than the plaintiff. Rescission is refused where (a) the plaintiff has expressly or impliedly ratified the contract; (b) owing to change of circumstances (not due to defendant's act) the parties cannot be substantially restored; (c) third parties have, during the subsistence of the contract, acquired rights in good faith without notice; (d) the contract is severable and only a part is sought to be rescinded and the part is not severable.
  3. Sec 28: where a decree for specific performance of a contract for sale/lease of immovable property has been passed and the purchaser or lessee does not pay the purchase money or other sum within the period fixed (or extended), the vendor or lessor may apply in the same suit to have the contract rescinded; the court may rescind it for the party in default or altogether. The court has power to extend the time for payment even after the decree (the decree is a preliminary decree in effect).
  4. Sec 29: alternative prayer: in a suit for specific performance, the plaintiff may claim rescission in the alternative. Sec 30: the court may require the party to whom relief is granted to restore benefit received (do equity). Sec 31: CANCELLATION: any person against whom a written instrument is void or voidable, and who has a reasonable apprehension that such instrument, if left outstanding, may cause him serious injury, may sue to have it adjudged void or voidable; the court may order it to be delivered up and cancelled; if the instrument has been registered, the court shall send a copy of the decree to the officer in whose office it has been registered, who shall note on the copy of the instrument the fact of its cancellation. Sec 32: partial cancellation of an instrument. Sec 33: power to require restoration of benefit and compensation.
  5. Limitation: suit to cancel an instrument - three years from when facts entitling cancellation become known (Art 59, Limitation Act).

!Exam traps

  • TrapRectification needs mutual mistake or fraud; not available for articles of association.
  • TrapCancellation is for WRITTEN instruments only and needs reasonable apprehension of serious injury.

?Likely questions

  1. Rectification of instruments is provided in Sec 26.
  2. Suit for cancellation lies where written instrument is void or voidable (Sec 31).
