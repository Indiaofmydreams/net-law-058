iOverview

Doctrines that appear in MCQs as 'which doctrine is described?' or 'which case propounded?'.

★Must know

  1. Pith and substance - true nature of legislation (Balsara 1951). Colourable legislation - indirect exercise of power one does not have. Doctrine of harmonious construction. Doctrine of eclipse - pre-Constitution law dormant. Doctrine of severability. Doctrine of waiver - FR cannot be waived. Doctrine of territorial nexus - extra-territorial operation needs nexus with India (State of Bombay v. R.M.D.C.). Doctrine of repugnancy (Art 254). Doctrine of occupied field. Doctrine of basic structure (Kesavananda). Doctrine of prospective overruling (Golak Nath). Doctrine of legitimate expectation (Navjyoti Co-op Group Housing Society v. Union of India, 1992). Doctrine of proportionality (Modern Dental 2016; Puttaswamy 2017). Doctrine of pleasure (Art 310, Art 156, Art 76). Doctrine of 'ejusdem generis' and 'noscitur a sociis' used in interpretation. Doctrine of separation of powers - Ram Jawaya Kapur (1955), Indira Gandhi v. Raj Narain, Kesavananda. Doctrine of public trust (M.C. Mehta v. Kamal Nath, 1997). Doctrine of 'law of the land' vs 'due process'.
  2. Presumption of constitutionality of statutes; burden on the challenger (Ram Krishna Dalmia v. S.R. Tendolkar, 1958). Courts do not strike down a statute merely because it is harsh; no judicial review of wisdom of legislation. Laws are tested on: legislative competence (Arts 245-254), violation of FR (Part III), violation of other provisions, basic structure for amendments.
  3. Rule of law (A.V. Dicey) - supremacy of law, equality before law, predominance of legal spirit; basic structure feature in Indian context. Separation of powers is not rigid in India but its essence is basic structure (In re Delhi Laws Act, 1951; Ram Jawaya Kapur). Delegation of legislative power - excessive delegation impermissible; essential legislative functions cannot be delegated (In re Delhi Laws Act 1951; Hamdard Dawakhana v. Union of India, 1960; Gwalior Rayon). Delegated legislation becomes enforceable only upon publication in the manner required by statute - Viraj Impex v. Union of India (2026) Verify · citation. [Cross-ref: Administrative Law.]
  4. Federalism: Indian Constitution is 'federal in structure but unitary in spirit' - quasi-federal (K.C. Wheare). S.R. Bommai (1994): federalism is basic feature. State of W.B. v. Union of India (1962): States are not sovereign. 'Indestructible Union of destructible States' (Ambedkar). 'Cooperative federalism' and 'dialogic federalism' - evolved in recent Governor-assent opinions.
  5. Secularism - 'positive secularism': the State maintains equal distance from all religions (S.R. Bommai, 1994). Democracy - free and fair elections is a basic feature. Judicial review is the heart of the Constitution.

!Exam traps

  • TrapPublic trust doctrine is a doctrine of environmental/administrative law - appears as 'doctrine arising from Roman law'.
  • TrapDo not confuse legitimate expectation (procedural/substantive benefit) with promissory estoppel (contract-like representation).
