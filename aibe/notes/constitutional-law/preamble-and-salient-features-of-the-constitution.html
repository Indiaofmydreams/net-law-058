iOverview

Foundation topic. Questions are factual: dates, persons, sources of borrowed features, Preamble keywords and the Preamble cases.

★Must know

  1. Constituent Assembly was formed under the Cabinet Mission Plan (1946). First meeting: 9 December 1946. Dr Sachchidananda Sinha was provisional president; Dr Rajendra Prasad became permanent president (11 Dec 1946).
  2. Objectives (Objective) Resolution moved by Jawaharlal Nehru on 13 December 1946; adopted 22 January 1947.
  3. Drafting Committee set up 29 August 1947 under Dr B.R. Ambedkar (7 members). B.N. Rau was Constitutional Adviser.
  4. Constitution adopted 26 November 1949 (Constitution Day / Samvidhan Divas); fully in force 26 January 1950 (Republic Day). Certain provisions (citizenship, elections, interim Parliament, etc.: Arts 5-9, 60, 324, 366, 367, 379, 380, 388, 391, 392, 393) came into force on 26 Nov 1949.
  5. Original Constitution: 395 Articles, 22 Parts, 8 Schedules. Present count: about 448 Articles, 25 Parts, 12 Schedules.
  6. Preamble: 'We, the people of India' - Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic; secures Justice (social, economic, political), Liberty (thought, expression, belief, faith, worship), Equality (status, opportunity), Fraternity (dignity of the individual and unity and integrity of the Nation).
  7. 'Socialist', 'Secular' and 'Integrity' were added by the 42nd Amendment (1976). This is the only amendment to the Preamble.
  8. Preamble is neither a source of power nor a limitation on power, and is non-justiciable, but it is an aid to interpretation.
  9. Nature of polity: Union of States; federal in form with a strong unitary bias (quasi-federal); parliamentary government; independent integrated judiciary; single citizenship; universal adult suffrage (age 18 after the 61st Amendment, 1988).

⚖Landmark cases

In re Berubari Union (Reference under Art 143)

1960

Preamble is not a part of the Constitution; it is a key to the minds of the framers. Cession of Indian territory requires a Constitution amendment under Art 368.

Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala

1973

Preamble IS a part of the Constitution and can be amended, subject to the basic structure.

LIC of India v. Consumer Education & Research Centre

1995

Reaffirmed that Preamble is an integral part of the Constitution.

S.R. Bommai v. Union of India

1994

Secularism is a basic feature of the Constitution; State must maintain equal distance from all religions.

Kesavananda Bharati (supremacy principle)

1973

Sovereignty vests in the people; the Constitution, not Parliament, is supreme.

✎Memory aids

  • Preamble keywords: 'S-S-S-D-R' = Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic.
  • Justice-Liberty-Equality-Fraternity: 'JLEF'.

!Exam traps

  • TrapIndia is described as 'Union of States' (Art 1) - not a 'federation'; the word 'federal' is not used in the Constitution.
  • TrapDate the Constitution was adopted (26 Nov 1949) differs from the date it commenced (26 Jan 1950).
