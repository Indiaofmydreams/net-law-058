Right to Freedom: Art 19 (Six Freedoms) and Reasonable Restrictions

iOverview

Know each freedom, the corresponding restriction clause and the grounds. Free speech cases (Romesh Thappar, Shreya Singhal, Anuradha Bhasin, Kaushal Kishor, Electoral Bonds) are hot.

★Must know

  1. Art 19(1) (citizens only): (a) speech and expression; (b) assemble peaceably and without arms; (c) form associations or unions or co-operative societies (97th Amendment); (d) move freely throughout India; (e) reside and settle anywhere; (g) practise any profession, or carry on any occupation, trade or business. (f) (property) was deleted by the 44th Amendment (1978).
  2. Art 19(2) grounds for 19(1)(a): sovereignty and integrity of India, security of the State, friendly relations with foreign States, public order, decency or morality, contempt of court, defamation, incitement to an offence. ('Public order' added by 1st Amendment 1951; 'sovereignty and integrity' by 16th Amendment 1963.)
  3. Art 19(3): restrictions on 19(1)(b) - sovereignty, integrity and public order. Art 19(4): on 19(1)(c) - same grounds + morality. Art 19(5): on 19(1)(d),(e) - general public interest + protection of Scheduled Tribes. Art 19(6): on 19(1)(g) - public interest; professional or technical qualifications; State monopolies.
  4. Restrictions must be 'reasonable' - both substantive and procedural reasonableness (State of Madras v. V.G. Row, 1952). Test of proportionality applied (Modern Dental College, 2016; Puttaswamy, 2017; Anuradha Bhasin, 2020).
  5. Freedom of speech includes: freedom of press (Romesh Thappar, 1950; Sakal Papers, 1962); right to circulate (Bennett Coleman, 1973); right to know / information (Raj Narain, 1975; S.P. Gupta, 1981); right to know candidates' antecedents (ADR, 2002; PUCL, 2003); right to broadcast (Cricket Association of Bengal, 1995); freedom of silence (Bijoe Emmanuel, 1986); right to fly the national flag (Naveen Jindal, 2004); commercial speech (Tata Press, 1995); internet access in a restricted manner (Anuradha Bhasin, 2020); right to criticise.
  6. No fundamental right to strike (T.K. Rangarajan v. Govt of T.N., 2003), no right to bandh (Communist Party of India (M) v. Bharat Kumar, 1998). No right to hold demonstrations on public roads indefinitely (Amit Sahni, 2020).
  7. Obscenity: Ranjit Udeshi (1965) Hicklin test; Aveek Sarkar v. State of W.B. (2014) shifted to 'community standards' test.
  8. Hate speech / sedition: Kedar Nath Singh v. State of Bihar (1962) upheld sedition (s.124A IPC) with incitement to violence requirement; Supreme Court kept s.124A in abeyance in S.G. Vombatkere v. Union of India (May 2022). BNS 2023 does not contain sedition but s.152 punishes acts endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity of India.
  9. Defamation: Subramanian Swamy v. Union of India (2016) upheld criminal defamation (ss.499/500 IPC).
  10. Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015): s.66A IT Act struck down as vague and overbroad; s.69A upheld; s.79 read down; distinction between discussion, advocacy and incitement.
  11. Anuradha Bhasin v. Union of India (2020): freedom of speech on internet and the right to carry on trade via internet enjoy Art 19 protection; indefinite internet shutdown impermissible; orders must be published and be proportionate.
  12. Kaushal Kishor v. State of U.P. (2023): grounds in Art 19(2) are exhaustive - no additional restrictions on free speech can be imposed (e.g., by invoking Art 21); Art 19/21 rights can have horizontal effect.
  13. Association for Democratic Reforms v. Union of India (15 Feb 2024): Electoral Bond Scheme struck down as violating voters' right to information under Art 19(1)(a).
  14. Right to practise profession includes the right not to practise (Art 19(1)(g)); no right to carry on trade in 'res extra commercium' like liquor (State of Punjab v. Devans Modern Breweries, 2003); State monopolies protected under Art 19(6)(ii).
  15. Right to move and reside: right to travel abroad is not in Art 19 - it is part of personal liberty under Art 21 (Satwant Singh Sawhney, 1967; Maneka Gandhi, 1978).

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
Art 19(1)(a)Freedom of speech and expression (includes press, information, silence).
Art 19(2)Eight grounds of restriction - memorise: sovereignty & integrity, security of State, friendly relations, public order, decency/morality, contempt, defamation, incitement.
Art 19(6)State can impose restrictions, set professional qualifications, and carry on trade itself/exclude citizens (monopoly).

⚖Landmark cases

Romesh Thappar v. State of Madras

1950

Freedom of press is part of Art 19(1)(a); ban on journal circulation violated Art 19 as 'public order' was not then a ground.

Sakal Papers v. Union of India

1962

Price-page schedule on newspapers violated Art 19(1)(a).

Bennett Coleman & Co. v. Union of India

1973

Newsprint policy violates press freedom; freedom to circulate and choose size.

S. Rangarajan v. P. Jagjivan Ram

1989

Restriction must be proximate - 'spark in a powder keg' test.

Bijoe Emmanuel v. State of Kerala

1986

Students refusing to sing national anthem on religious conscience cannot be expelled.

Shreya Singhal v. Union of India

2015

Sec 66A IT Act void; chilling effect and vagueness.

Anuradha Bhasin v. Union of India

2020

Internet shutdowns subject to proportionality; Art 19(1)(a) and (g) cover internet.

Kaushal Kishor v. State of U.P.

2023

Art 19(2) grounds are exhaustive.

T.K. Rangarajan v. Government of T.N.

2003

No fundamental right to strike.

Himat Lal K. Shah v. Commissioner of Police

1973

Right to assemble includes right to hold meetings on public streets subject to reasonable regulation.

Modern Dental College v. State of M.P.

2016

Applies proportionality to Art 19(1)(g) restrictions.

Association for Democratic Reforms v. Union of India

2024

Electoral bonds struck down; undisclosed political funding violates Art 19(1)(a).

✎Memory aids

  • Remaining Art 19 freedoms 'SAAMRP': Speech, Assembly, Association, Movement, Residence, Profession (property deleted).
  • Art 19(2) grounds: 'SSF-PDC-DI' = Sovereignty & integrity, Security of State, Friendly relations, Public order, Decency/morality, Contempt, Defamation, Incitement.

!Exam traps

  • Trap'Public order' (Art 19(2)) is different from 'law and order' and 'security of the State'.
  • TrapArt 19 is suspended automatically (Art 358) only when Emergency is declared on war or external aggression - not for armed rebellion.
  • Trap'Defamation' and 'contempt of court' are both Art 19(2) grounds.
  • TrapPress freedom is not a separate right - it is implied in Art 19(1)(a).
