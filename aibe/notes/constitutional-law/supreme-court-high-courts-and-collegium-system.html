The Judiciary: Supreme Court, High Courts, Subordinate Courts, Tribunals, Collegium, Contempt

iOverview

Jurisdictions of SC (original, appellate, advisory, special leave, review), judges' appointment and removal, Collegium cases, Art 142 and tribunals.

★Must know

  1. Art 124: Supreme Court of India; judges appointed by President after consultation with such SC and HC judges as the President deems necessary; CJI always consulted for others (Art 124(2)). Sanctioned strength 34 including CJI (2019 amendment to the 1956 Act); reports in 2026 indicate an increase to 38 via ordinance Verify. Qualification (Art 124(3)): citizen; at least 5 years as HC judge, or 10 years as HC advocate, or distinguished jurist (in President's opinion). Retirement at 65; HC at 62. Salaries are charged on Consolidated Fund and cannot be varied to disadvantage (Art 125).
  2. Removal (Art 124(4)-(5)): by President on an address by each House supported by a majority of total membership and two-thirds of members present and voting, on grounds of proved misbehaviour or incapacity; Judges (Inquiry) Act 1968. No judge has been successfully removed (Justice V. Ramaswami, 1993, motion failed). Art 121: no discussion in Parliament on conduct of a judge except on removal motion.
  3. Appointments - Judges cases: First Judges case (S.P. Gupta, 1981): 'consultation' does not mean concurrence; executive primacy. Second Judges case (Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association v. Union of India, 1993, 9 judges): CJI's opinion has primacy; 'consultation' = 'concurrence'; collegium of CJI + 2 senior-most judges. Third Judges case (Special Reference No. 1 of 1998, Art 143): collegium expanded to CJI + 4 senior-most judges (SC) and CJI + 2 senior-most judges for HC; Fourth Judges case (SCAORA, 2015): 99th Amendment (Art 124A, NJAC) held unconstitutional (4:1) - independence of judiciary is basic structure; Justice Chelameswar dissented.
  4. SC jurisdiction: Art 32 (writs), Art 131 (original - disputes between Centre and State(s) or between States on a question of law or fact involving legal right; excludes private parties; Art 131 does not cover pre-Constitution treaties); Art 132-134 (appeals in constitutional, civil, criminal matters; Art 134A certificate); Art 136 (Special Leave Petition - discretionary, not confined to HC judgments, can be against any court/tribunal except military tribunals - Art 136(2)); Art 137 (review), Art 139 (writs for purposes other than FR); Art 139A (transfer of cases); Art 141 (law declared by SC is binding on all courts in India); Art 142 (complete justice - Union Carbide Corp v. Union of India 1991; Supreme Court Bar Association v. Union of India 1998 - cannot override express statutory prohibition or substantive law; used for dissolution of marriage on irretrievable breakdown in Shilpa Sailesh v. Varun Sreenivasan, 2023); Art 143 (advisory); Art 144 (civil and judicial authorities to aid SC); Art 129 (SC is a court of record with power to punish for contempt); Art 145 (rules); Art 147 (interpretation - substantial question of law as to Constitution - at least 5 judges, Art 145(3)).
  5. High Courts: Art 214 - HC for each State; Art 215 - court of record; Art 216 - CJ and other judges; Art 217 - appointment by President after consultation with CJI, Governor and Chief Justice of HC; qualification (Art 217(2)): citizen, 10 years judicial office or 10 years HC advocate; retirement at 62; Art 222 transfer by President after consulting CJI; Art 224 additional/acting judges; Art 224A retired judges (ad hoc); Art 226 writs (see 1.9); Art 227 superintendence; Art 228 transfer of cases involving substantial questions of law; Art 231 common HC for two or more States. Presently 25 High Courts.
  6. Subordinate courts: Art 233 - District Judge appointed by the Governor in consultation with the HC; qualification - not in service, advocate for 7 years and recommended by HC (Chandra Mohan v. State of U.P., 1966). Art 234 - recruitment of persons other than DJ; Art 235 - control over subordinate judiciary vests in the HC (disciplinary control); Art 236-237; Art 247 - Parliament may create additional courts; Art 312 - All India Judicial Service.
  7. Tribunals: Art 323A (administrative tribunals), 323B (other tribunals) inserted by the 42nd Amendment; Administrative Tribunals Act 1985; L. Chandra Kumar (1997): exclusion of HC jurisdiction struck down; tribunals are subject to judicial review by HC (Division Bench). Madras Bar Association cases: Tribunals Reforms Act 2021 provisions were struck down in 2021 and again in 2025 Verify.
  8. Contempt: Contempt of Courts Act 1971 - civil and criminal contempt; punishment: simple imprisonment up to 6 months, fine up to Rs 2,000 or both; s.13 (truth as defence if bona fide and public interest - 2006 amendment); limitation 1 year (s.20). SC and HC are courts of record (Arts 129, 215). Re: Prashant Bhushan (2020): fine of Re 1. In re Arundhati Roy (2002).
  9. Judicial review types and basic features: judicial review is basic structure (Kesavananda, L. Chandra Kumar, Minerva Mills). Separation of powers is basic structure. Court cannot legislate but may fill vacuum through guidelines (Vishaka), and Art 142 powers. Doctrine of Judicial restraint and non-justiciability of political questions (e.g., Art 74(2)).
  10. CJI: Justice Surya Kant became the 53rd Chief Justice of India on 24 Nov 2025; retirement due 9 Feb 2027 Verify. Appointment of CJI by seniority (Memorandum of Procedure); President is bound to appoint the senior-most.

⚖Landmark cases

S.P. Gupta v. Union of India (First Judges case)

1981

Executive primacy in judicial appointments.

Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association v. Union of India (Second Judges case)

1993

CJI primacy; collegium.

Special Reference No. 1 of 1998 (Third Judges case)

1998

Five-member collegium for SC.

Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association v. Union of India (NJAC case)

2015

99th Amendment struck down.

Union Carbide Corporation v. Union of India

1991

Art 142 powers wide but cannot be used to bypass substantive law.

Supreme Court Bar Association v. Union of India

1998

Art 142 cannot be used to bar a statutory remedy.

Shilpa Sailesh v. Varun Sreenivasan

2023

Art 142 used to dissolve marriage on irretrievable breakdown (5 judges).

L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India

1997

Tribunals subject to HC; Arts 226/227/32 basic structure.

State of Bihar v. Kameshwar Singh

1952

Early case on judicial power and Zamindari Abolition.

Re: Prashant Bhushan

2020

Contempt - Re 1 fine.

✎Memory aids

  • Judges cases: 1981 (S.P. Gupta) - Executive; 1993 - CJI primacy; 1998 - Five judges; 2015 - NJAC struck. 'E-C-F-N'.
  • SC strength 34 (1+33); retirement SC 65 / HC 62.

!Exam traps

  • TrapArt 131 does not cover disputes involving private parties.
  • TrapArt 136 SLP - not a right of appeal; discretionary.
  • TrapCollegium for SC: CJI + 4 senior-most judges; HC: CJI + 2 senior-most.
  • TrapContempt of Court - truth is a defence since the 2006 amendment.
