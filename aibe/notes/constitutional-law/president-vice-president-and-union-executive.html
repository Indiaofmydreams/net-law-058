Union Executive: President, Vice-President, Council of Ministers, Attorney General

iOverview

Election, qualification, impeachment, ordinance, pardon and aid-and-advice questions recur. Numbers (35 years, 14 days, 1/4, 2/3, 6 months, 15%) are the score-makers.

★Must know

  1. Art 52-53: President is head of the Union; executive power vested in President, exercised directly or through officers. Art 54: elected by an electoral college of ELECTED members of both Houses of Parliament and ELECTED members of Legislative Assemblies of States (including Delhi and Puducherry). Nominated members and Legislative Council members do not vote. Art 55: system of proportional representation by single transferable vote, secret ballot. Disputes about the election: Supreme Court alone (Art 71).
  2. Art 56: term 5 years; continues until successor enters office. Re-election allowed. Resignation to Vice-President. Art 57: eligible for re-election. Art 58 qualifications: citizen of India, completed 35 years, qualified for election as Lok Sabha member, holds no office of profit. Art 59: oath before the Chief Justice of India (Art 60).
  3. Art 61 impeachment: only for 'violation of the Constitution'; charge by either House on a resolution moved after 14 days' written notice signed by at least one-fourth of total membership; passed by two-thirds of TOTAL membership; other House investigates; President has the right to appear/be represented; if the other House also passes by two-thirds of total membership, President is removed from the date of passing. Nominated members can participate.
  4. Art 62: election to fill a vacancy within 6 months; Art 65: VP acts as President (up to the end of vacancy); Art 70: Parliament may make other provisions. Art 71: SC decides doubts/disputes; election cannot be challenged on the ground of vacancy in the electoral college.
  5. Art 72 - pardoning power of President: pardon, reprieve, respite, remission, suspension, commutation - for offences by court martial; offences against Union law; death sentence. Judicially reviewable on limited grounds (Maru Ram, 1980; Kehar Singh v. Union of India, 1989 - President may scrutinise evidence afresh; Epuru Sudhakar v. Govt of A.P., 2006). Exercised on aid and advice of the Council of Ministers. Courts cannot examine merits; reasons need not be given. Compare Art 161 (Governor cannot pardon death sentence or court martial sentence).
  6. Art 74: Council of Ministers with PM at head to aid and advise the President (42nd Amendment made advice binding; 44th Amendment allows President to ask for reconsideration once; second advice binding). Art 74(2): courts cannot inquire into the advice tendered. Shamsher Singh v. State of Punjab (1974): President and Governor are constitutional heads and act on ministerial advice except where Constitution expressly gives discretion.
  7. Art 75: PM appointed by President; other ministers on PM's advice; ministers hold office during President's pleasure; collective responsibility to Lok Sabha (75(3)); total ministers not to exceed 15% of Lok Sabha strength (91st Amendment 2003; Art 75(1A)); a member disqualified under Tenth Schedule is also disqualified from being a minister (75(1B)); a non-member can be minister for 6 months (Art 75(5)). S.R. Chaudhuri v. State of Punjab (2001): non-member cannot be re-appointed after 6 months without being elected.
  8. Art 76: Attorney General - qualified to be SC judge; appointed by President; holds office during pleasure; right to speak in Parliament (Art 88) but no vote. Solicitor General is not a constitutional office (statutory/administrative). Art 148: CAG (see 1.17).
  9. Art 63-71 Vice-President: elected by electoral college of all members (elected AND nominated) of both Houses of Parliament; proportional representation by single transferable vote; qualifications - citizen, 35 years, qualified for Rajya Sabha, no office of profit; term 5 years; ex officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha (Art 64) with casting vote only (Art 100); removal (Art 67(b)) by resolution of Rajya Sabha passed by an effective majority (majority of all then members) and agreed to by Lok Sabha by simple majority, after 14 days' notice. Dhankhar resigned in July 2025; C.P. Radhakrishnan was elected the 15th Vice-President in Sept 2025 Verify.
  10. Legislative powers of President: summon, prorogue (Art 85), dissolve Lok Sabha; address (Art 86-87); nominate 12 to Rajya Sabha (Art 80(3)) - literature, science, art, social service; nominate 2 Anglo-Indians (discontinued by 104th Amendment); assent to Bills (Art 111) - absolute veto, suspensive veto (return once for reconsideration - not for Money Bill; must assent if re-passed); pocket veto (inaction - Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill 1986; not expressly provided).
  11. Art 123 Ordinance: when Parliament is not in session and the President is satisfied that immediate action is necessary; same force as Act; must be laid before both Houses and ceases to operate at the expiration of six weeks from reassembly (or earlier on disapproving resolution); can be withdrawn at any time; cannot alter the Constitution; limitations same as Parliament. D.C. Wadhwa v. State of Bihar (1987): repeated re-promulgation is a fraud on the Constitution; Krishna Kumar Singh v. State of Bihar (2017): 7-judge bench - re-promulgation is constitutional fraud; legislative satisfaction is judicially reviewable to limited extent; ordinance-making is not an unfettered power. R.C. Cooper v. Union of India (1970): satisfaction of President under Art 123 is justiciable.
  12. Art 143: advisory jurisdiction - on question of law or fact of public importance; the Court may decline to answer (Ayodhya reference, Ismail Faruqui 1994); opinion is not binding on the President but is of high persuasive value and (per Special Courts Bill, 1978 and Natural Resources Allocation, 2012) is binding on courts. 16th Presidential Reference (Art 143) on Arts 200/201 decided Nov 2025 - see Latest Developments.

⚖Landmark cases

Ram Jawaya Kapur v. State of Punjab

1955

Real executive power lies with Council of Ministers; Indian system is parliamentary.

U.N. Rao v. Indira Gandhi

1971

Council of Ministers continues even after dissolution of Lok Sabha until the new one meets.

Shamsher Singh v. State of Punjab

1974

President/Governor are constitutional heads; act on ministerial advice.

Maru Ram v. Union of India

1980

President/Governor's pardon power exercised on advice; not arbitrary.

Kehar Singh v. Union of India

1989

President may examine evidence afresh while deciding mercy petition; no oral hearing needed.

D.C. Wadhwa v. State of Bihar

1987

Ordinance re-promulgation 'colourable' and unconstitutional.

Krishna Kumar Singh v. State of Bihar

2017

Ordinance lapses; its effect doesn't survive; fraud on the Constitution if re-promulgated without legislative nod.

Rameshwar Prasad v. Union of India

2006

Dissolution of Bihar Assembly under Art 356 declared unconstitutional.

S.R. Chaudhuri v. State of Punjab

2001

Non-legislator cannot be repeatedly appointed minister for 6-month terms.

Special Reference No. 1 of 2025 (Presidential Reference on Arts 200-201)

2025

No judicially imposed timelines; no 'deemed assent' - see 1.19.

✎Memory aids

  • President: 35 years; VP: 35 years; Rajya Sabha MP: 30; Lok Sabha MP: 25; Governor: 35.

!Exam traps

  • TrapElected members of Legislative Councils do not vote in Presidential election; nominated MPs do vote in VP election.
  • Trap'Violation of the Constitution' is the sole ground for President's impeachment; for judges it is 'proved misbehaviour or incapacity'.
  • TrapVice-President's removal needs no impeachment - only Rajya Sabha resolution + LS consent.
  • TrapAttorney General has no fixed tenure and no removal procedure specified.
