Centre-State Relations: Legislative, Administrative, Financial; Trade and Commerce; Inter-State Disputes

iOverview

Seventh Schedule lists, doctrines (pith and substance, repugnancy), Art 249/250/252/253, Art 254, GST Council and Finance Commission are standard MCQ topics.

★Must know

  1. Art 245: Parliament for whole/part of India; State legislature for its State; Parliament's laws can have extra-territorial operation (Art 245(2)). Art 246: Union List (List I - about 100 entries), State List (List II - about 61 entries), Concurrent List (List III - about 52 entries); Parliament's exclusive power over List I; State exclusive over List II; both over List III; Parliament has overriding power. Art 248 and List I Entry 97: residuary power vests in Parliament (Canadian model).
  2. 42nd Amendment (1976) moved five subjects from State List to Concurrent List: education, forests, weights and measures, protection of wild animals and birds, administration of justice. Art 246A (101st Amendment): both Parliament and State legislatures can make laws on GST; Parliament has exclusive power on inter-State supply of goods/services.
  3. Doctrine of pith and substance (State of Bombay v. F.N. Balsara, 1951; Prafulla Kumar Mukherjee, 1947): incidental encroachment is permissible if the true nature of the law falls within the legislature's list. Doctrine of colourable legislation (K.C. Gajapati Narayan Deo v. State of Orissa, 1953): what cannot be done directly cannot be done indirectly. Doctrine of harmonious construction, occupied field and territorial nexus (State of Bombay v. R.M.D. Chamarbaugwala, 1957).
  4. Art 254 - repugnancy between Union and State law on Concurrent List: Union law prevails and State law is void to the extent of repugnancy; exception under Art 254(2) - if State law was reserved for the President and received assent, State law prevails in that State, but Parliament can subsequently override it by law. Zaverbhai Amaidas v. State of Bombay (1954); M. Karunanidhi v. Union of India (1979) - tests of repugnancy: direct inconsistency, occupied field.
  5. Exceptional Parliamentary powers over State List: Art 249 (national interest - Rajya Sabha resolution passed by two-thirds of members present and voting; law operates for up to 1 year, extendable year by year); Art 250 (during Emergency); Art 252 (consent of two or more States by resolution; others may adopt - e.g., Prize Competitions Act); Art 253 (to implement treaties, international agreements or decisions - Union can legislate on any subject); Art 258-A; Art 356(1)(b).
  6. Administrative relations: Art 256 - States to comply with Union laws; Art 257 - Union can give directions; Art 258 - delegation of Union functions to States; Art 261 - full faith and credit to public acts, records, and judicial proceedings; Art 262 - adjudication of inter-State water disputes (Inter-State River Water Disputes Act 1956); Art 263 - Inter-State Council (established 1990 on Sarkaria Commission's recommendation). All-India Services (IAS, IPS, IFS) - Art 312. Zonal Councils are statutory (States Reorganisation Act 1956), not constitutional. Sarkaria Commission (1983-88) and Punchhi Commission (2007-10) reports on Centre-State relations.
  7. Financial relations: Art 264-267, 268 (duties levied by Union, collected and appropriated by States - stamp duties), 269-270 (Union-levied taxes shared with States - income tax other than agricultural income, GST/IGST), 271 (surcharge - Union only), 272 (omitted), 273-275 (grants-in-aid - Art 275 statutory grants; Art 282 discretionary grants), 280 (Finance Commission - constituted every 5 years by President; chairman + 4 members; recommends distribution of net proceeds of taxes), 281, 292-293 (borrowings), 285-289 (immunities: Union property exempt from State tax (Art 285), State property from Union tax (Art 289)).
  8. Finance Commissions: 15th FC (chair N.K. Singh) - States' share in divisible pool 41% for 2021-26. 16th FC (chair Arvind Panagariya; constituted Dec 2023) - award period 2026-31; recommendations tabled with the Union Budget 2026-27 Verify · particulars before exam.
  9. GST: Art 279A (101st Amendment 2016) - GST Council chaired by Union Finance Minister with State finance ministers; decisions by 3/4 weighted majority (Centre one-third weight; States two-thirds); Mohit Minerals Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India (2022): GST Council recommendations are not binding but persuasive; Parliament and States have simultaneous power under Art 246A.
  10. Trade and commerce (Part XIII): Art 301 - free trade, commerce and intercourse throughout India; Art 302 - Parliament may impose restrictions in public interest; Art 303 - no discrimination between States; Art 304 - State may impose non-discriminatory taxes / reasonable restrictions (with President's previous sanction for the Bill under 304(b)); Art 305; Art 307 - authority to carry out purposes of Arts 301-304. Cases: Atiabari Tea Co. v. State of Assam (1961); Automobile Transport (Rajasthan) v. State of Rajasthan (1962) - regulatory and compensatory measures do not violate Art 301.
  11. Art 298-300: executive power to carry on trade; Art 299 - contracts by Union/State must be expressed to be made by the President/Governor and executed by authorised persons (Bhikraj Jaipuria v. Union of India, 1962; Mulamchand v. State of M.P., 1968 - s.70 Contract Act remedy). Art 300: suits and proceedings by or against the Government; Art 300A - property. Art 361: President/Governor immunity; civil proceedings need 2 months' notice (Art 361(4)).

✎Memory aids

  • Five subjects moved to Concurrent List: 'E-F-W-W-A' - Education, Forests, Weights & measures, Wild animals, Administration of justice.
  • Art 249 = RS resolution; Art 252 = States' consent; Art 253 = treaties.

!Exam traps

  • TrapResiduary powers lie with Parliament (not States) - Art 248.
  • TrapZonal Councils are statutory; Inter-State Council is constitutional (Art 263).
  • TrapArt 249 resolution must be by Rajya Sabha - not Lok Sabha.
  • TrapGST Council recommendations are persuasive, not binding.

?Likely questions

  1. Which Article provides for residuary legislative powers?
  2. Which subject is on the Concurrent List - Education / Police / Public Order?
  3. Under which Article does Parliament legislate on State List by a Rajya Sabha resolution?
  4. Which Article constitutes the Finance Commission?
