Right to Equality (Arts 14-18) and Reservation Jurisprudence

iOverview

Art 14 tests (classification and arbitrariness), Art 15/16 clauses with the amendments that added them, Indra Sawhney (and its progeny) and Art 17/18 basics.

★Must know

  1. Art 14: 'equality before law' (British/Dicey - negative, no special privilege) + 'equal protection of laws' (American - positive, equals treated alike). Available to all persons including corporations.
  2. Reasonable classification test (State of W.B. v. Anwar Ali Sarkar, 1952; Ram Krishna Dalmia, 1958): (i) intelligible differentia; (ii) rational nexus with the object. Presumption of constitutionality lies on the law.
  3. New concept of equality - arbitrariness is antithesis of equality (E.P. Royappa v. State of T.N., 1974; Maneka Gandhi, 1978). Ajay Hasia (1981) extended to administrative action.
  4. Manifest arbitrariness as ground to strike down legislation: Shayara Bano v. Union of India (2017) (instant triple talaq void); Navtej Singh Johar (2018); Joseph Shine v. Union of India (2018) (adultery, s.497 IPC, struck down).
  5. Exceptions to Art 14: President and Governors (Art 361); privileges of legislators (Arts 105, 194); foreign sovereigns and diplomats; Art 31C.
  6. Equal pay for equal work: Randhir Singh v. Union of India (1982) (derived from Arts 14, 16, 39(d)).
  7. Art 15(1): State shall not discriminate on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth. Art 15(2): access to shops, public restaurants, wells, tanks, roads - binds even private persons. Art 15(3): special provisions for women and children. Art 15(4): SEBC/SC/ST (1st Amendment, 1951 after State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan). Art 15(5): admissions to educational institutions including private unaided (93rd Amendment, 2005) - except minority institutions (Pramati Educational Trust, 2014). Art 15(6): EWS (103rd Amendment, 2019).
  8. Art 16(1): equality of opportunity in public employment; 16(2): no discrimination only on religion, race, caste, sex, descent, place of birth, residence; 16(3): Parliament may prescribe residence requirement (only Parliament, not State); 16(4): reservation for backward classes not adequately represented; 16(4A): promotions for SC/ST (77th Amendment, 1995); 16(4B): carry-forward of unfilled vacancies (81st Amendment, 2000); 16(5): office-bearers of religious institutions; 16(6): EWS (103rd Amendment).
  9. Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992), 9 judges, 6:3: upheld 27% OBC reservation (Mandal); excluded 'creamy layer'; 50% ceiling on total reservations (barring extraordinary situations); no reservation in promotions (prospectively, 5 years - later overridden by 77th Amendment for SC/ST); Art 16(4) is not an exception to 16(1); economic criterion alone cannot define backwardness (EWS later enabled through 103rd Amendment).
  10. M. Nagaraj v. Union of India (2006): upheld 77th, 81st, 82nd and 85th Amendments subject to conditions (quantifiable data on backwardness, inadequacy of representation, efficiency). Jarnail Singh v. Lachhmi Narain Gupta (2018): no need for quantifiable data of backwardness for SC/ST; need for inadequacy data remains.
  11. State of Punjab v. Davinder Singh (1 Aug 2024), 7 judges, 6:1: sub-classification of Scheduled Castes/Scheduled Tribes for granting preferential reservation is permissible; overruled E.V. Chinnaiah (2004); some judges opined that creamy layer should apply to SC/ST.
  12. Dr Jaishri Laxmanrao Patil v. Chief Minister (2021): Maratha reservation struck down; 50% ceiling reaffirmed; 102nd Amendment (Art 342A) removed States' power to identify SEBCs - later restored by 105th Amendment (2021).
  13. Art 17: abolition of untouchability; Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955 (earlier Untouchability (Offences) Act 1955), plus SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act 1989. Art 17 is enforceable against private persons.
  14. Art 18: abolishes titles; no citizen can accept title from foreign State; exception: military and academic distinctions. Bharat Ratna/Padma awards are not titles (Balaji Raghavan v. Union of India, 1996).

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
Art 14State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or the equal protection of the laws within the territory of India.
Art 15(6)EWS reservation up to 10% in addition to existing reservations; not available to SEBC/SC/ST already covered.
Art 16(4A)Reservation in matters of promotion with consequential seniority for SC/ST.
Art 335Claims of SC/ST to services to be considered consistent with maintenance of efficiency of administration.
Art 338 / 338A / 338BNational Commission for SC / ST / Backward Classes (102nd Amendment gave NCBC constitutional status).
Art 340-342340: Commission to investigate conditions of backward classes; 341: SC list by President; 342: ST list; 342A: SEBC list by President (102nd), State power restored by 105th Amendment.

⚖Landmark cases

State of West Bengal v. Anwar Ali Sarkar

1952

Special courts act struck for unguided classification.

State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan

1951

Communal G.O. struck down; led to 1st Amendment (Art 15(4)).

E.P. Royappa v. State of Tamil Nadu

1974

Equality is a dynamic concept; arbitrariness violates Art 14.

Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (Mandal case)

1992

50% cap, creamy layer exclusion, no reservation in promotions (prospective).

Ashoka Kumar Thakur v. Union of India

2008

93rd Amendment and Central Educational Institutions (Reservation in Admission) Act upheld; creamy layer must be excluded for OBC.

Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India

2022

EWS quota valid (3:2).

Shayara Bano v. Union of India

2017

Instant triple talaq void for manifest arbitrariness (3:2 on Art 14; 2 judges on reasoning under Art 25).

Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India

2018

Sec 377 IPC struck down to the extent it criminalises consensual same-sex acts between adults.

Joseph Shine v. Union of India

2018

Adultery (s.497 IPC) unconstitutional; Sec 198(2) CrPC struck.

Supriyo @ Supriya Chakraborty v. Union of India

2023

Constitution Bench: no fundamental right to marry or civil union; Special Marriage Act not read down; Parliament/Executive to examine rights of queer couples (Oct 2023).

National Legal Services Authority v. Union of India (NALSA)

2014

Recognised transgender persons as 'third gender'; Art 14, 15, 16, 19, 21 apply.

State of Punjab v. Davinder Singh

2024

Sub-classification within SC/ST allowed (6:1).

✎Memory aids

  • Art 15 sub-clause chronology: (3) women/children - original; (4) 1st Am. 1951; (5) 93rd Am. 2005; (6) 103rd Am. 2019.
  • Art 16 sub-clauses: (4) backwards; (4A) promotions SC/ST; (4B) carry forward; (5) religious offices; (6) EWS.

!Exam traps

  • TrapArt 16(3) residence requirement can be imposed only by Parliament.
  • TrapArt 14 applies to non-citizens; Art 15, 16 only to citizens.
  • TrapThe 50% ceiling is a judge-made rule, not in the text of the Constitution.
  • TrapEWS quota is over and above 50% but limited to 10%.
