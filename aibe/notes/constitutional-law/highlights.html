1

Making of the Constitution, Salient Features and the Preamble

Medium yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

PreambleKey words: Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic. Date of adoption mentioned: 26 November 1949.
Borrowed features (source)UK: parliamentary system, rule of law, single citizenship, cabinet system, writs, bicameralism, Speaker. USA: Fundamental Rights, judicial review, independence of judiciary, impeachment, Vice-President, Preamble. Ireland: DPSP, nomination to Rajya Sabha, method of Presidential election. Canada: federation with strong Centre, residuary powers with Centre, appointment of Governors, advisory jurisdiction. Australia: Concurrent List, freedom of trade (Part XIII), joint sitting. Germany (Weimar): suspension of FR during Emergency. USSR: Fundamental Duties. France: Republic; ideals of Liberty, Equality, Fraternity. South Africa: procedure for amendment, election of Rajya Sabha members. Japan: procedure established by law. Government of India Act 1935: federal scheme, Governor's office, Public Service Commissions, emergency provisions, administrative details.

Cases in one line

  • In re Berubari Union (Reference under Art 143) 1960 Preamble is not a part of the Constitution; it is a key to the minds of the framers. Cession of Indian territory requires a Constitution amendment under Art 368.
  • Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala 1973 Preamble IS a part of the Constitution and can be amended, subject to the basic structure.
  • LIC of India v. Consumer Education & Research Centre 1995 Reaffirmed that Preamble is an integral part of the Constitution.
  • S.R. Bommai v. Union of India 1994 Secularism is a basic feature of the Constitution; State must maintain equal distance from all religions.
  • Kesavananda Bharati (supremacy principle) 1973 Sovereignty vests in the people; the Constitution, not Parliament, is supreme.

Memory aids

  • Preamble keywords: 'S-S-S-D-R' = Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic.
  • Justice-Liberty-Equality-Fraternity: 'JLEF'.

Exam traps

  • TrapIndia is described as 'Union of States' (Art 1) - not a 'federation'; the word 'federal' is not used in the Constitution.
  • TrapDate the Constitution was adopted (26 Nov 1949) differs from the date it commenced (26 Jan 1950).

Union and its Territory; Citizenship (Arts 1-11)

Low-medium yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Art 3 (proviso)Bill needs President's recommendation + reference to State Legislature (for effect on a State's area/boundaries/name).
Art 11Parliament has plenary power to make law on acquisition and termination of citizenship.

Cases in one line

  • Berubari Union (1960) 1960 Art 3 does not cover cession of territory to a foreign State; Art 368 amendment required.
  • Babulal Parante v. State of Bombay 1960 State Legislature's views under Art 3 are not binding on Parliament; fresh reference not needed after amendments to the Bill.
  • State of Arunachal Pradesh v. Khudiram Chakma 1994 Chakma refugees are entitled to life and personal liberty; State must protect them.
  • Sarbananda Sonowal v. Union of India 2005 IMDT Act struck down; Assam faced 'external aggression' through illegal migration.
  • In re Section 6A, Citizenship Act 2024 Constitution Bench (4:1) upheld Section 6A as a valid legislative solution for Assam.

Exam traps

  • TrapArt 3 changes do not need a special majority or State ratification.
  • TrapCitizenship under Part II is a Union subject (List I, Entry 17); States have no say.

Amendment of the Constitution (Art 368) and the Basic Structure Doctrine

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Art 368(1)Constituent power of Parliament to amend by way of addition, variation or repeal (inserted by 24th Amendment).
Art 368(2)Special majority + ratification where required; President shall give assent.
Art 368(3)Nothing in Art 13 shall apply to any amendment under this Article (24th Amendment).
Art 368(4) and (5)Inserted by the 42nd Amendment (no amendment can be questioned in court; unlimited amending power); struck down in Minerva Mills (1980).
Art 13(2) and 13(4)13(2): no law shall take away or abridge FR; 13(4) added by the 24th Amendment: Art 13 does not apply to Art 368 amendments.
Important amendments (quick list)1st (1951) Ninth Schedule, Art 15(4), 19(2) additions; 7th (1956) reorganisation of States; 24th (1971) amending power; 25th (1971) Art 31C; 42nd (1976) 'mini-Constitution': Preamble, FDs (Art 51A), DPSP changes, Art 368(4)-(5); 44th (1978) property removed from Part III, Art 300A, Emergency safeguards; 52nd (1985) Tenth Schedule; 61st (1988) voting age 18; 69th (1991) Delhi NCT; 73rd and 74th (1992) local governments; 86th (2002) Art 21A; 91st (2003) 15% ministers cap, anti-defection tightening; 97th (2011) cooperatives; 99th (2014) NJAC (struck down 2015); 101st (2016) GST; 102nd (2018) NCBC constitutional status; 103rd (2019) EWS reservation; 104th (2020) SC/ST reservation in legislatures extended, Anglo-Indian seats ended; 105th (2021) States' power to identify SEBCs restored; 106th (2023) women's reservation (Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam).

Cases in one line

  • Shankari Prasad Singh Deo v. Union of India 1951 Parliament can amend Fundamental Rights; 'law' in Art 13 does not include constitutional amendments.
  • Sajjan Singh v. State of Rajasthan 1965 Reaffirmed Shankari Prasad (3:2); Hidayatullah and Mudholkar JJ. doubted if basic features could be amended.
  • I.C. Golak Nath v. State of Punjab 1967 11 judges, 6:5. Parliament cannot amend Part III; constitutional amendment is 'law' under Art 13(2). Applied prospective overruling.
  • Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala 1973 13 judges, 7:6. Overruled Golak Nath. Parliament can amend any provision but not the 'basic structure'. Longest hearing (68 days).
  • Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain 1975 Art 329A(4) (inserted by the 39th Amendment) struck down - free and fair elections, rule of law and judicial review are basic features.
  • Minerva Mills Ltd. v. Union of India 1980 Struck down Sec 4 and 55 of the 42nd Amendment (Art 31C extension and Art 368(4)-(5)). Harmony between FR and DPSP is basic structure; 'limited amending power is itself a basic feature'.
  • Waman Rao v. Union of India 1981 Cut-off 24 April 1973: amendments to Ninth Schedule before that date are valid; after that open to review.
  • S.P. Sampath Kumar v. Union of India 1987 Tribunal as substitute for HC upheld (later modified by L. Chandra Kumar).
  • L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India 1997 Power of judicial review of HC and SC (Arts 226/227/32) is basic structure; tribunals' decisions subject to HC scrutiny.
  • I.R. Coelho v. State of Tamil Nadu 2007 9 judges. Ninth Schedule laws are not beyond judicial review if they damage basic structure or Arts 14, 19, 21 core.
  • Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association v. Union of India (Fourth Judges case) 2015 99th Amendment and NJAC Act struck down (4:1); collegium system restored; independence of judiciary is basic structure.
  • Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India 2022 103rd Amendment (10% EWS reservation, Arts 15(6), 16(6)) upheld 3:2. Economic criterion alone can be a ground; 50% ceiling applies to existing reservations only.
  • Property Owners Association v. State of Maharashtra 2024 9 judges: 'material resources of the community' in Art 39(b) does not automatically include all privately owned resources; Art 31C as upheld in Kesavananda remains good law.

Memory aids

  • Chronology: 'S-S-G-K-M' = Shankari (1951), Sajjan (1965), Golak Nath (1967), Kesavananda (1973), Minerva (1980).
  • Special majority = 'Majority of the total + 2/3 of present and voting'. Remember the word TOTAL.

Exam traps

  • TrapNumber of judges in Kesavananda = 13; majority 7:6 (a razor-thin margin).
  • TrapGolak Nath = 11 judges (6:5).
  • TrapBasic structure is not found in the text - judge-made. Art 368 has no express mention.
  • TrapThe Preamble can be amended (done once, by the 42nd Amendment) but not so as to alter the basic structure.

Fundamental Rights: Arts 12 and 13 (State, Law, Doctrines)

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Art 12Definition of 'State' for Part III.
Art 13(1)Pre-constitutional laws, to the extent of inconsistency, are void.
Art 13(2)Post-constitutional laws, to the extent of contravention, are void.
Art 13(3)'Law' defined; 'laws in force' defined.
Art 13(4)Amendment under Art 368 is not 'law' under Art 13.

Cases in one line

  • Rajasthan SEB v. Mohan Lal 1967 Wide interpretation of 'other authorities'.
  • Sukhdev Singh v. Bhagat Ram 1975 Statutory corporations are 'State'.
  • Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Mujib Sehravardi 1981 Five-factor test for 'instrumentality or agency of State'.
  • Pradeep Kumar Biswas v. IICB 2002 Overruled the narrow Sabhajit Tewary; 'dominant control' test.
  • Zee Telefilms v. Union of India 2005 BCCI not 'State', but writ may lie for public duties.
  • Bhikaji Narain Dhakras v. State of M.P. 1955 Doctrine of eclipse - pre-constitution law not dead but dormant.
  • Behram Khurshed Pesikaka v. State of Bombay 1955 No waiver of FR.
  • Deep Chand v. State of U.P. 1959 Post-constitution law violating Art 13(2) is stillborn/void ab initio.
  • Kaushal Kishor v. State of U.P. 2023 FR under Arts 19/21 are enforceable against private persons; no additional restrictions on Art 19(1)(a) beyond Art 19(2).

Exam traps

  • TrapEclipse vs severability: eclipse deals with timing (revival when obstruction removed); severability deals with extent (part void).
  • TrapDoctrine of eclipse for post-constitution laws applies only in respect of non-citizens.

Right to Equality (Arts 14-18) and Reservation Jurisprudence

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Art 14State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or the equal protection of the laws within the territory of India.
Art 15(6)EWS reservation up to 10% in addition to existing reservations; not available to SEBC/SC/ST already covered.
Art 16(4A)Reservation in matters of promotion with consequential seniority for SC/ST.
Art 335Claims of SC/ST to services to be considered consistent with maintenance of efficiency of administration.
Art 338 / 338A / 338BNational Commission for SC / ST / Backward Classes (102nd Amendment gave NCBC constitutional status).
Art 340-342340: Commission to investigate conditions of backward classes; 341: SC list by President; 342: ST list; 342A: SEBC list by President (102nd), State power restored by 105th Amendment.

Cases in one line

  • State of West Bengal v. Anwar Ali Sarkar 1952 Special courts act struck for unguided classification.
  • State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan 1951 Communal G.O. struck down; led to 1st Amendment (Art 15(4)).
  • E.P. Royappa v. State of Tamil Nadu 1974 Equality is a dynamic concept; arbitrariness violates Art 14.
  • Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (Mandal case) 1992 50% cap, creamy layer exclusion, no reservation in promotions (prospective).
  • Ashoka Kumar Thakur v. Union of India 2008 93rd Amendment and Central Educational Institutions (Reservation in Admission) Act upheld; creamy layer must be excluded for OBC.
  • Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India 2022 EWS quota valid (3:2).
  • Shayara Bano v. Union of India 2017 Instant triple talaq void for manifest arbitrariness (3:2 on Art 14; 2 judges on reasoning under Art 25).
  • Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India 2018 Sec 377 IPC struck down to the extent it criminalises consensual same-sex acts between adults.
  • Joseph Shine v. Union of India 2018 Adultery (s.497 IPC) unconstitutional; Sec 198(2) CrPC struck.
  • Supriyo @ Supriya Chakraborty v. Union of India 2023 Constitution Bench: no fundamental right to marry or civil union; Special Marriage Act not read down; Parliament/Executive to examine rights of queer couples (Oct 2023).
  • National Legal Services Authority v. Union of India (NALSA) 2014 Recognised transgender persons as 'third gender'; Art 14, 15, 16, 19, 21 apply.
  • State of Punjab v. Davinder Singh 2024 Sub-classification within SC/ST allowed (6:1).

Memory aids

  • Art 15 sub-clause chronology: (3) women/children - original; (4) 1st Am. 1951; (5) 93rd Am. 2005; (6) 103rd Am. 2019.
  • Art 16 sub-clauses: (4) backwards; (4A) promotions SC/ST; (4B) carry forward; (5) religious offices; (6) EWS.

Exam traps

  • TrapArt 16(3) residence requirement can be imposed only by Parliament.
  • TrapArt 14 applies to non-citizens; Art 15, 16 only to citizens.
  • TrapThe 50% ceiling is a judge-made rule, not in the text of the Constitution.
  • TrapEWS quota is over and above 50% but limited to 10%.

Right to Freedom: Art 19 (Six Freedoms) and Reasonable Restrictions

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Art 19(1)(a)Freedom of speech and expression (includes press, information, silence).
Art 19(2)Eight grounds of restriction - memorise: sovereignty & integrity, security of State, friendly relations, public order, decency/morality, contempt, defamation, incitement.
Art 19(6)State can impose restrictions, set professional qualifications, and carry on trade itself/exclude citizens (monopoly).

Cases in one line

  • Romesh Thappar v. State of Madras 1950 Freedom of press is part of Art 19(1)(a); ban on journal circulation violated Art 19 as 'public order' was not then a ground.
  • Sakal Papers v. Union of India 1962 Price-page schedule on newspapers violated Art 19(1)(a).
  • Bennett Coleman & Co. v. Union of India 1973 Newsprint policy violates press freedom; freedom to circulate and choose size.
  • S. Rangarajan v. P. Jagjivan Ram 1989 Restriction must be proximate - 'spark in a powder keg' test.
  • Bijoe Emmanuel v. State of Kerala 1986 Students refusing to sing national anthem on religious conscience cannot be expelled.
  • Shreya Singhal v. Union of India 2015 Sec 66A IT Act void; chilling effect and vagueness.
  • Anuradha Bhasin v. Union of India 2020 Internet shutdowns subject to proportionality; Art 19(1)(a) and (g) cover internet.
  • Kaushal Kishor v. State of U.P. 2023 Art 19(2) grounds are exhaustive.
  • T.K. Rangarajan v. Government of T.N. 2003 No fundamental right to strike.
  • Himat Lal K. Shah v. Commissioner of Police 1973 Right to assemble includes right to hold meetings on public streets subject to reasonable regulation.
  • Modern Dental College v. State of M.P. 2016 Applies proportionality to Art 19(1)(g) restrictions.
  • Association for Democratic Reforms v. Union of India 2024 Electoral bonds struck down; undisclosed political funding violates Art 19(1)(a).

Memory aids

  • Remaining Art 19 freedoms 'SAAMRP': Speech, Assembly, Association, Movement, Residence, Profession (property deleted).
  • Art 19(2) grounds: 'SSF-PDC-DI' = Sovereignty & integrity, Security of State, Friendly relations, Public order, Decency/morality, Contempt, Defamation, Incitement.

Exam traps

  • Trap'Public order' (Art 19(2)) is different from 'law and order' and 'security of the State'.
  • TrapArt 19 is suspended automatically (Art 358) only when Emergency is declared on war or external aggression - not for armed rebellion.
  • Trap'Defamation' and 'contempt of court' are both Art 19(2) grounds.
  • TrapPress freedom is not a separate right - it is implied in Art 19(1)(a).

Protection in Criminal Proceedings, Right to Life and Personal Liberty, Arrest and Detention (Arts 20, 21, 21A, 22)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Art 20(1)No conviction except for violation of law in force at the time; no penalty greater than that prescribed at the time.
Art 20(2)No person shall be prosecuted and punished for the same offence more than once.
Art 20(3)No accused shall be compelled to be witness against himself.
Art 21Protection of life and personal liberty.
Art 21ARight to education (6-14).
Art 22(2)Production before Magistrate within 24 hours.
Art 22(4)Preventive detention beyond three months only with Advisory Board report.
Art 359(1)Presidential order can suspend enforcement of FR during Emergency except Arts 20 and 21 (44th Amendment).

Cases in one line

  • A.K. Gopalan v. State of Madras 1950 Narrow view: 'procedure established by law' = any procedure enacted by law.
  • Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India 1978 Procedure must be fair, just and reasonable; overruled Gopalan; passport impounding.
  • ADM Jabalpur v. Shivkant Shukla 1976 During Emergency, no habeas corpus - overruled by Puttaswamy (2017).
  • Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar 1979 Right to speedy trial and free legal aid; under-trial prisoners.
  • Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corporation 1985 Right to livelihood within right to life; pavement dwellers; no estoppel against FR.
  • Francis Coralie Mullin v. Administrator, UT of Delhi 1981 Right to life means to live with dignity.
  • Unni Krishnan v. State of A.P. 1993 Right to education up to 14 years flows from Art 21.
  • Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan 1997 Guidelines against sexual harassment (now POSH Act 2013); international conventions read into FR.
  • D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal 1997 Guidelines to be followed in arrest/detention.
  • Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India 2017 Right to privacy is fundamental.
  • Common Cause v. Union of India 2018 Right to die with dignity - passive euthanasia and advance directive recognised.
  • Selvi v. State of Karnataka 2010 Involuntary narco/polygraph/brain mapping violate Art 20(3), 21.
  • Kathi Kalu Oghad 1961 Giving specimen handwriting/fingerprints is not testimony.
  • Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab 1980 Death penalty valid; rarest of rare.
  • Mohd. Ahmed Khan v. Shah Bano Begum 1985 Muslim wife entitled to maintenance under s.125 CrPC; DPSP Art 44 referred.
  • Mohd. Abdul Samad v. State of Telangana 2024 Divorced Muslim woman can claim maintenance under s.125 CrPC (now s.144 BNSS).
  • M.K. Ranjitsinh v. Union of India 2024 Right to be free from adverse effects of climate change is part of Art 14 and 21.
  • Union of India v. K.A. Najeeb 2021 Prolonged incarceration without trial can justify bail even under statutory restrictions (UAPA).

Memory aids

  • Art 20 = 'EDS' - Ex post facto, Double jeopardy, Self-incrimination.
  • Art 22: '24-3-' = 24 hours production; 3 months detention before Advisory Board.

Exam traps

  • TrapArt 20 and 21 cannot be suspended even in Emergency (44th Amendment).
  • TrapArt 21 applies to all persons, citizens or not.
  • TrapArt 22(1)-(2) rights are not available under preventive detention; Art 22(4)-(7) is only for preventive detention.
  • Trap'Procedure established by law' is different from 'due process' - but Maneka Gandhi brought it near to due process.
  • TrapArt 21 vs Art 21A: age bracket 6-14 only for 21A.

Rights against Exploitation, Freedom of Religion, Cultural and Educational Rights (Arts 23-30)

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Art 25(2)Regulation of secular activities, social reform and opening of Hindu institutions to all Hindus.
Art 26Rights of denominations - 4 clauses (a)-(d).
Art 28Freedom from attending religious instruction.
Art 30Minorities' right to establish and administer educational institutions.

Cases in one line

  • Commissioner, HRE v. Sri Lakshmindra Thirtha Swamiar of Sri Shirur Mutt 1954 'Religion' includes practices; essential parts of religion decided by the religion's own tenets.
  • Durgah Committee, Ajmer v. Syed Hussain Ali 1961 Superstitious/non-essential practices not protected.
  • Rev. Stanislaus v. State of M.P. 1977 Right to propagate does not include right to convert.
  • Bijoe Emmanuel v. State of Kerala 1986 Freedom of conscience upheld (Jehovah's Witnesses).
  • Ismail Faruqui v. Union of India 1994 Acquisition of mosque site valid; namaz at mosque not essential religious practice.
  • T.M.A. Pai Foundation v. State of Karnataka 2002 Minority determined at State level; scope of Art 30 and private education.
  • Indian Young Lawyers Association v. State of Kerala (Sabarimala) 2018 Women of all ages can enter Sabarimala; practice not essential; 4:1.
  • Shayara Bano v. Union of India 2017 Triple talaq not essential to Islam; struck.
  • Aishat Shifa v. State of Karnataka (SC split) 2022 Hijab ban case ended with a split verdict in Oct 2022; referred to a larger bench Verify · status.
  • PUDR v. Union of India (Asiad Workers) 1982 Less than minimum wage = forced labour under Art 23; PIL.
  • Pramati Educational and Cultural Trust v. Union of India 2014 RTE Act not applicable to minority institutions; Art 21A and Art 15(5) upheld.

Exam traps

  • TrapArt 25 (individuals) vs Art 26 (denominations).
  • TrapArt 29 is not limited to minorities; Art 30 is limited to minorities.
  • TrapArt 23 'begar' = unpaid forced labour; Art 23 protection is against State AND private.
  • TrapArt 25 does not give right to convert others.

Right to Property (Art 300A), Savings of Certain Laws (Arts 31A-31C), Constitutional Remedies and Writs (Arts 32-35, 226)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Art 32(2)Writs: habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, quo warranto, certiorari.
Art 226(1)HC writ power 'for the enforcement of any of the rights conferred by Part III and for any other purpose'.
Art 300ARight to property - no deprivation save by authority of law.
Art 31BLaws in Ninth Schedule - saved from invalidity on grounds of violation of FR (subject to I.R. Coelho).

Cases in one line

  • Kameshwar Singh v. State of Bihar 1952 Zamindari abolition; led to 1st Amendment.
  • State of West Bengal v. Subodh Gopal Bose 1954 Property provisions; 'deprivation' vs 'acquisition'.
  • Daryao v. State of U.P. 1961 Res judicata applies to Art 32 after Art 226 decision on merits.
  • University of Mysore v. C.D. Govinda Rao 1964 Quo warranto - qualifications for public office.
  • S.P. Gupta v. Union of India 1981 Liberal locus standi; PIL; judges transfers; first judges case.
  • Bandhua Mukti Morcha v. Union of India 1984 Letter petitions; bonded labour; right to live with dignity.
  • L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India 1997 Judicial review part of basic structure; HC supervisory power over tribunals.
  • Whirlpool Corporation v. Registrar of Trade Marks 1998 Exceptions to alternative remedy rule.
  • State of Uttaranchal v. Balwant Singh Chaufal 2010 Guidelines to curb PIL abuse.
  • Kolkata Municipal Corporation v. Bimal Kumar Shah 2024 Seven procedural rights under Art 300A; property is human right.
  • Mineral Area Development Authority v. SAIL 2024 State power to tax mineral rights.

Memory aids

  • Writs: 'HM-PCQ' - Habeas, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo warranto.
  • Prohibition = prevent (before order); Certiorari = cancel (after order).

Exam traps

  • TrapArt 32 is for FR only; Art 226 for FR + any other legal right.
  • TrapArt 32 is itself a FR; Art 226 is not a FR.
  • TrapRight to property today is NOT a fundamental right but a constitutional (and human) right.
  • TrapHabeas corpus can be filed by a third person; quo warranto can be filed by any person (not only aggrieved).

Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV) and Fundamental Duties (Part IV-A)

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Art 37DPSP not justiciable; fundamental to governance.
Art 39(b)-(c)Distribution of material resources of community and prevention of concentration of wealth - Art 31C link.
Art 44Uniform civil code.
Art 51A11 Fundamental Duties of citizens.

Cases in one line

  • State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan 1951 DPSP subordinate to FR.
  • Minerva Mills v. Union of India 1980 Harmony between Part III and IV is basic structure.
  • Unni Krishnan v. State of A.P. 1993 FR to education read with Art 41/45.
  • Mohini Jain v. State of Karnataka 1992 Right to education from Art 21 and Art 41; capitation fee invalid.
  • State of Gujarat v. Mirzapur Moti Kureshi Kassab Jamat 2005 Total ban on cow slaughter upheld citing Art 48.
  • Sarla Mudgal v. Union of India 1995 Conversion to Islam for bigamy is a fraud; UCC need highlighted.
  • Randhir Singh v. Union of India 1982 Equal pay for equal work from Arts 14, 16, 39(d).

Memory aids

  • DPSP Articles 36-51 (16 Articles). 'Fundamental Duties: 51A(a) to (k)'.
  • '44 = UCC, 45 = Under-6 education, 48A = Environment, 50 = Judiciary separation'.

Exam traps

  • TrapArt 45 now concerns children below 6 (post-86th Amendment).
  • TrapThe right to education is Art 21A (FR), not DPSP.
  • TrapArt 39A was inserted by the 42nd Amendment (not the 44th).
  • TrapFundamental Duties were not in the original Constitution and are not enforceable by writ.
11

Union Executive: President, Vice-President, Council of Ministers, Attorney General

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Art 54Electoral college for Presidential election: elected members only.
Art 61Impeachment: violation of Constitution; 1/4 notice; 2/3 of total membership in each House.
Art 72Pardoning power of the President.
Art 74(2)Advice by ministers cannot be inquired into by any court.
Art 75(1A)Council of Ministers not to exceed 15% of Lok Sabha strength.
Art 123Ordinance-making power of the President.

Cases in one line

  • Ram Jawaya Kapur v. State of Punjab 1955 Real executive power lies with Council of Ministers; Indian system is parliamentary.
  • U.N. Rao v. Indira Gandhi 1971 Council of Ministers continues even after dissolution of Lok Sabha until the new one meets.
  • Shamsher Singh v. State of Punjab 1974 President/Governor are constitutional heads; act on ministerial advice.
  • Maru Ram v. Union of India 1980 President/Governor's pardon power exercised on advice; not arbitrary.
  • Kehar Singh v. Union of India 1989 President may examine evidence afresh while deciding mercy petition; no oral hearing needed.
  • D.C. Wadhwa v. State of Bihar 1987 Ordinance re-promulgation 'colourable' and unconstitutional.
  • Krishna Kumar Singh v. State of Bihar 2017 Ordinance lapses; its effect doesn't survive; fraud on the Constitution if re-promulgated without legislative nod.
  • Rameshwar Prasad v. Union of India 2006 Dissolution of Bihar Assembly under Art 356 declared unconstitutional.
  • S.R. Chaudhuri v. State of Punjab 2001 Non-legislator cannot be repeatedly appointed minister for 6-month terms.
  • Special Reference No. 1 of 2025 (Presidential Reference on Arts 200-201) 2025 No judicially imposed timelines; no 'deemed assent' - see 1.19.

Memory aids

  • President: 35 years; VP: 35 years; Rajya Sabha MP: 30; Lok Sabha MP: 25; Governor: 35.

Exam traps

  • TrapElected members of Legislative Councils do not vote in Presidential election; nominated MPs do vote in VP election.
  • Trap'Violation of the Constitution' is the sole ground for President's impeachment; for judges it is 'proved misbehaviour or incapacity'.
  • TrapVice-President's removal needs no impeachment - only Rajya Sabha resolution + LS consent.
  • TrapAttorney General has no fixed tenure and no removal procedure specified.

Parliament: Composition, Procedure, Privileges, Money Bills and Anti-Defection (Tenth Schedule)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Art 105(2)No member liable in any court for anything said or vote given in Parliament - bribery is not protected (Sita Soren, 2024).
Art 108Joint sitting - not for Money Bills or Constitution Amendment Bills.
Art 110Money Bill definition and certification by the Speaker.
Tenth Schedule para 4Merger exception - 2/3 of legislature party.
Tenth Schedule para 6Decision by Chairman or Speaker.

Cases in one line

  • Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu 1992 Tenth Schedule valid; para 7 struck down; Speaker's decision reviewable.
  • Raja Ram Pal v. Speaker, Lok Sabha 2007 Expulsion of MPs valid; privileges reviewable to limited extent.
  • Lily Thomas v. Union of India 2013 s.8(4) RP Act struck down; immediate disqualification on conviction.
  • Jaya Bachchan v. Union of India 2006 'Office of profit' - actual receipt of pecuniary gain is irrelevant; potential is enough.
  • P.V. Narasimha Rao v. State 1998 Bribery immunity (overruled in 2024).
  • Sita Soren v. Union of India 2024 7 judges: no immunity for bribery by legislators.
  • Keisham Meghachandra Singh v. Speaker 2020 Speaker to decide disqualification within 3 months.
  • Subhash Desai v. Principal Secretary, Govt of Maharashtra 2023 Governor's floor test power; Speaker's powers; whip.
  • Rojer Mathew v. South Indian Bank 2019 Money Bill reference to larger bench (also tribunals).

Memory aids

  • Art 108 joint sitting - 'No for M-C': Money Bill and Constitution amendment Bill.
  • LS 543 = 530 + 13 UTs; RS 245 = 233 + 12.

Exam traps

  • TrapMoney Bill cannot be introduced in Rajya Sabha; only LS decides what is a Money Bill (Speaker).
  • TrapSpeaker is NOT removed by impeachment but by a resolution with 14 days' notice.
  • TrapThe nominee limit of 12 in Rajya Sabha is under Art 80(3), not Art 80(2).
  • TrapAnti-defection cannot be invoked for 'merger' of 2/3 of the legislature party.

Art 163Aid and advice of Council of Ministers; discretionary functions.
Art 200Governor's options on Bills passed by State Legislature.

Cases in one line

  • B.P. Singhal v. Union of India 2010 Governors cannot be removed arbitrarily; pleasure not unfettered.
  • Nabam Rebia v. Deputy Speaker, Arunachal 2016 Governor's discretion in summoning/advancing session limited.
  • S.R. Bommai v. Union of India 1994 Art 356 judicially reviewable; floor test; secularism basic.
  • Rameshwar Prasad v. Union of India 2006 Dissolution of Bihar Assembly mala fide.
  • Hargovind Pant v. Raghukul Tilak 1979 Governor as constitutional functionary; Central appointment not a 'service'.
  • State of Tamil Nadu v. Governor of Tamil Nadu 2025 8 April 2025 - deemed assent for 10 Bills, timelines for Governor/President; effectively disapproved in the Nov 2025 Presidential Reference opinion.
  • State (NCT of Delhi) v. Union of India 2023 Services under Delhi govt control (except police, public order, land).

Exam traps

  • TrapGovernor can reserve a Bill for President under Art 200; Governor's assent to Money Bill cannot be withheld in effect (can be but not return).
  • TrapCouncil creation uses Art 169 - not Art 368.
  • TrapA Governor's pardon power does not extend to death sentences - only suspend/remit/commute.

The Judiciary: Supreme Court, High Courts, Subordinate Courts, Tribunals, Collegium, Contempt

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Art 124(4)Removal of SC judges: special majority of both Houses; proved misbehaviour/incapacity.
Art 131Original jurisdiction - Union v. State; State v. State; excludes private parties.
Art 136Special leave to appeal - discretionary jurisdiction.
Art 141Law declared by SC binding on all courts.
Art 142Power to do complete justice.
Art 143Advisory opinion.
Art 233Appointment of district judges.

Cases in one line

  • S.P. Gupta v. Union of India (First Judges case) 1981 Executive primacy in judicial appointments.
  • Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association v. Union of India (Second Judges case) 1993 CJI primacy; collegium.
  • Special Reference No. 1 of 1998 (Third Judges case) 1998 Five-member collegium for SC.
  • Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association v. Union of India (NJAC case) 2015 99th Amendment struck down.
  • Union Carbide Corporation v. Union of India 1991 Art 142 powers wide but cannot be used to bypass substantive law.
  • Supreme Court Bar Association v. Union of India 1998 Art 142 cannot be used to bar a statutory remedy.
  • Shilpa Sailesh v. Varun Sreenivasan 2023 Art 142 used to dissolve marriage on irretrievable breakdown (5 judges).
  • L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India 1997 Tribunals subject to HC; Arts 226/227/32 basic structure.
  • State of Bihar v. Kameshwar Singh 1952 Early case on judicial power and Zamindari Abolition.
  • Re: Prashant Bhushan 2020 Contempt - Re 1 fine.

Memory aids

  • Judges cases: 1981 (S.P. Gupta) - Executive; 1993 - CJI primacy; 1998 - Five judges; 2015 - NJAC struck. 'E-C-F-N'.
  • SC strength 34 (1+33); retirement SC 65 / HC 62.

Exam traps

  • TrapArt 131 does not cover disputes involving private parties.
  • TrapArt 136 SLP - not a right of appeal; discretionary.
  • TrapCollegium for SC: CJI + 4 senior-most judges; HC: CJI + 2 senior-most.
  • TrapContempt of Court - truth is a defence since the 2006 amendment.

Cases in one line

  • State of Bombay v. F.N. Balsara 1951 Pith and substance doctrine; incidental encroachment.
  • K.C. Gajapati Narayan Deo v. State of Orissa 1953 Colourable legislation.
  • M. Karunanidhi v. Union of India 1979 Repugnancy requires direct conflict; 4 propositions.
  • Mohit Minerals v. Union of India 2022 GST Council recommendations are not binding.
  • State of Rajasthan v. Union of India 1977 President's Rule on dissolution of Assemblies post-Emergency; Court refused to interfere.
  • Atiabari Tea Co. v. State of Assam 1961 Art 301 - interpretation of free movement.

  • TrapZonal Councils are statutory; Inter-State Council is constitutional (Art 263).
  • TrapArt 249 resolution must be by Rajya Sabha - not Lok Sabha.
  • TrapGST Council recommendations are persuasive, not binding.
