Protection in Criminal Proceedings, Right to Life and Personal Liberty, Arrest and Detention (Arts 20, 21, 21A, 22)

iOverview

The single most important cluster. Art 21's expansion through Maneka Gandhi, Puttaswamy and several 'rights included' cases is repeatedly asked. Art 22's numbers (24 hours, 3 months) are classic. Cross-refer CrPC/BNSS.

★Must know

  1. Art 20(1) - no ex post facto criminal law (conviction or greater penalty than law at the time); applies only to criminal laws (Rattan Lal v. State of Punjab, 1964); does not bar retrospective procedural/ civil laws. Art 20(2) - no double jeopardy (prosecution and punishment for same offence only once) - applies only to judicial proceedings (Maqbool Hussain, 1953) - departmental inquiry then trial is no bar. Art 20(3) - no person accused of an offence shall be compelled to be witness against himself.
  2. Art 20(3) requirements: accused person; compulsion; testimonial evidence. Samples like fingerprints, handwriting, thumb impression are not testimonial (State of Bombay v. Kathi Kalu Oghad, 1961, 11 judges). Nandini Satpathy v. P.L. Dani (1978): right to silence extends to police interrogation. Selvi v. State of Karnataka (2010): narco-analysis, polygraph and brain-mapping without consent violate Arts 20(3) and 21. Ritesh Sinha v. State of U.P. (2019): magistrate may order voice samples.
  3. Art 21: 'No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law.' A.K. Gopalan (1950) - narrow, procedure merely 'enacted by legislature'. Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978) - procedure must be just, fair and reasonable; Arts 14, 19, 21 form a 'golden triangle'; right to travel abroad is part of Art 21.
  4. Art 21 includes (illustrative): right to live with human dignity (Francis Coralie Mullin, 1981); livelihood (Olga Tellis, 1985); speedy trial (Hussainara Khatoon, 1979); free legal aid (M.H. Hoskot, 1978; Hussainara); against solitary confinement and bar fetters (Sunil Batra, 1978; Prem Shankar Shukla, 1980); privacy (K.S. Puttaswamy, 2017); health and emergency medical aid (Parmanand Katara, 1989; Paschim Banga Khet Mazdoor Samity, 1996); clean environment (Subhash Kumar, 1991; M.C. Mehta); shelter; education (Unni Krishnan, 1993); freedom from sexual harassment at work (Vishaka, 1997); reputation (Kiran Bedi); marriage of choice (Shafin Jahan, 2018; Shakti Vahini, 2018); passive euthanasia and living will (Common Cause, 2018; Aruna Shanbaug, 2011); against custodial violence and arrest guidelines (D.K. Basu, 1997); against delay in executing death sentence (Shatrughan Chauhan, 2014); right to be forgotten; right against climate change harms (M.K. Ranjitsinh v. Union of India, 2024); right to digital access for persons with disabilities and acid-attack survivors (Amar Jain v. Union of India, 2025); reproductive autonomy incl. for unmarried women (X v. Principal Secretary, Health, 2022); menstrual health reported recognised as part of Art 21 (Jan 2026) Verify · case title.
  5. Right to die: Gian Kaur v. State of Punjab (1996) - right to life does not include right to die; euthanasia (passive) permitted under strict guidelines (Common Cause). BNS 2023 has no counterpart of s.309 IPC (attempted suicide); Mental Healthcare Act 2017, s.115, presumes severe stress.
  6. Death penalty: Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab (1980) upheld constitutionality, 'rarest of rare' doctrine; Mithu v. State of Punjab (1983) struck down mandatory death penalty (s.303 IPC); Machhi Singh (1983) categories; Shatrughan Chauhan (2014) - inordinate delay can commute.
  7. Right to privacy: Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Retd.) v. Union of India (24 Aug 2017), 9 judges, unanimous: privacy is a fundamental right under Art 21 (and Part III as a whole); overruled M.P. Sharma (1954) and Kharak Singh (1962) in part; ADM Jabalpur (1976) declared wrongly decided. Puttaswamy-II (2018): Aadhaar Act upheld (4:1) with s.57 struck and limits.
  8. Art 21A (86th Amendment 2002; RTE Act 2009 in force 1 April 2010): free and compulsory education for children 6-14 years; RTE Act s.12(1)(c) 25% quota in private unaided schools upheld in Society for Unaided Private Schools of Rajasthan (2012); Pramati (2014): RTE Act not applicable to minority schools.
  9. Art 22(1)-(2) (arrest): right to be informed of grounds; consult and be defended by legal practitioner of choice; production before nearest Magistrate within 24 hours excluding journey time. Not available to enemy aliens and persons detained under preventive detention laws.
  10. Art 22(3)-(7) (preventive detention): no detention beyond 3 months unless Advisory Board (judges or persons qualified as HC judges) reports sufficient cause; grounds to be communicated; earliest opportunity of representation; Parliament may prescribe maximum period (e.g., 12 months in National Security Act 1980). 44th Amendment proposed 2 months and Advisory Board chaired by HC judge but is not yet brought into force. A.K. Roy v. Union of India (1982): NSA valid; no right to counsel before Advisory Board. Preventive detention is a concurrent subject (Entry 9 List I - defence/foreign; Entry 3 List III - others).
  11. Arrest law linkages: Joginder Kumar v. State of U.P. (1994), D.K. Basu v. State of W.B. (1997), Arnesh Kumar (2014), Hussainara; Court on handcuffing (Prem Shankar Shukla); bail under Art 21 (Satender Kumar Antil, 2022; Union of India v. K.A. Najeeb, 2021 - prolonged incarceration violates Art 21 even under UAPA).

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
Art 20(1)No conviction except for violation of law in force at the time; no penalty greater than that prescribed at the time.
Art 20(2)No person shall be prosecuted and punished for the same offence more than once.
Art 20(3)No accused shall be compelled to be witness against himself.
Art 21Protection of life and personal liberty.
Art 21ARight to education (6-14).
Art 22(2)Production before Magistrate within 24 hours.
Art 22(4)Preventive detention beyond three months only with Advisory Board report.
Art 359(1)Presidential order can suspend enforcement of FR during Emergency except Arts 20 and 21 (44th Amendment).

⚖Landmark cases

A.K. Gopalan v. State of Madras

1950

Narrow view: 'procedure established by law' = any procedure enacted by law.

Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India

1978

Procedure must be fair, just and reasonable; overruled Gopalan; passport impounding.

ADM Jabalpur v. Shivkant Shukla

1976

During Emergency, no habeas corpus - overruled by Puttaswamy (2017).

Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar

1979

Right to speedy trial and free legal aid; under-trial prisoners.

Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corporation

1985

Right to livelihood within right to life; pavement dwellers; no estoppel against FR.

Francis Coralie Mullin v. Administrator, UT of Delhi

1981

Right to life means to live with dignity.

Unni Krishnan v. State of A.P.

1993

Right to education up to 14 years flows from Art 21.

Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan

1997

Guidelines against sexual harassment (now POSH Act 2013); international conventions read into FR.

D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal

1997

Guidelines to be followed in arrest/detention.

Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India

2017

Right to privacy is fundamental.

Common Cause v. Union of India

2018

Right to die with dignity - passive euthanasia and advance directive recognised.

Selvi v. State of Karnataka

2010

Involuntary narco/polygraph/brain mapping violate Art 20(3), 21.

Kathi Kalu Oghad

1961

Giving specimen handwriting/fingerprints is not testimony.

Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab

1980

Death penalty valid; rarest of rare.

Mohd. Ahmed Khan v. Shah Bano Begum

1985

Muslim wife entitled to maintenance under s.125 CrPC; DPSP Art 44 referred.

Mohd. Abdul Samad v. State of Telangana

2024

Divorced Muslim woman can claim maintenance under s.125 CrPC (now s.144 BNSS).

M.K. Ranjitsinh v. Union of India

2024

Right to be free from adverse effects of climate change is part of Art 14 and 21.

Union of India v. K.A. Najeeb

2021

Prolonged incarceration without trial can justify bail even under statutory restrictions (UAPA).

✎Memory aids

  • Art 20 = 'EDS' - Ex post facto, Double jeopardy, Self-incrimination.
  • Art 22: '24-3-' = 24 hours production; 3 months detention before Advisory Board.

!Exam traps

  • TrapArt 20 and 21 cannot be suspended even in Emergency (44th Amendment).
  • TrapArt 21 applies to all persons, citizens or not.
  • TrapArt 22(1)-(2) rights are not available under preventive detention; Art 22(4)-(7) is only for preventive detention.
  • Trap'Procedure established by law' is different from 'due process' - but Maneka Gandhi brought it near to due process.
  • TrapArt 21 vs Art 21A: age bracket 6-14 only for 21A.
