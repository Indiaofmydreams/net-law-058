Union and its Territory; Citizenship (Arts 1-11)

iOverview

Short but factual. Art 3 mechanics, Art 2 vs Art 3, Citizenship Act 1955 modes and the CAA/Section 6A developments are the usual testing points.

★Must know

  1. Art 1: 'India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States'. Territory = States, Union Territories, and territories that may be acquired.
  2. Art 2: Parliament may admit into the Union or establish NEW States (not part of India) - e.g., Sikkim (1975, 35th and 36th Amendments).
  3. Art 3: Parliament may by law form new States, alter areas, boundaries or names of existing States. Procedure: Bill only on President's recommendation; President refers it to the State Legislature concerned for its views (not binding - Babulal Parante, 1960); simple majority; not an Art 368 amendment (Art 4).
  4. Art 4: laws under Arts 2 and 3 are not constitutional amendments under Art 368.
  5. Present position: 28 States and 8 Union Territories (J&K reorganised as UT in 2019; Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu merged in 2020). Telangana (2014) is the youngest State.
  6. Citizenship in Constitution: Art 5 (domicile at commencement), Art 6 (migrants from Pakistan), Art 7 (migrants to Pakistan who returned), Art 8 (persons of Indian origin abroad), Art 9 (voluntary acquisition of foreign citizenship = loss), Art 10 (continuance), Art 11 (Parliament's power to regulate citizenship by law).
  7. Citizenship Act 1955: acquisition by birth, descent, registration, naturalisation, incorporation of territory. Loss: renunciation, termination (voluntary acquisition of foreign citizenship), deprivation.
  8. Citizenship (Amendment) Act 2019: gives fast-track path (residence 5 years) to Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist, Jain, Parsi and Christian migrants from Afghanistan, Bangladesh and Pakistan who entered India on or before 31 December 2014. Rules notified in March 2024.
  9. Section 6A (Assam Accord migrants who entered between 1 Jan 1966 and 25 Mar 1971) was upheld by a Constitution Bench in Oct 2024 (4:1).

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
Art 3 (proviso)Bill needs President's recommendation + reference to State Legislature (for effect on a State's area/boundaries/name).
Art 11Parliament has plenary power to make law on acquisition and termination of citizenship.

⚖Landmark cases

Berubari Union (1960)

1960

Art 3 does not cover cession of territory to a foreign State; Art 368 amendment required.

Babulal Parante v. State of Bombay

1960

State Legislature's views under Art 3 are not binding on Parliament; fresh reference not needed after amendments to the Bill.

State of Arunachal Pradesh v. Khudiram Chakma

1994

Chakma refugees are entitled to life and personal liberty; State must protect them.

Sarbananda Sonowal v. Union of India

2005

IMDT Act struck down; Assam faced 'external aggression' through illegal migration.

In re Section 6A, Citizenship Act

2024

Constitution Bench (4:1) upheld Section 6A as a valid legislative solution for Assam.

!Exam traps

  • TrapArt 3 changes do not need a special majority or State ratification.
  • TrapCitizenship under Part II is a Union subject (List I, Entry 17); States have no say.
