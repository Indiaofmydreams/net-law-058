Fundamental Rights: Arts 12 and 13 (State, Law, Doctrines)

iOverview

Gateway to Part III. 'State' under Art 12 and doctrines of eclipse, severability, waiver and pre/post-constitutional laws are frequent MCQ material.

★Must know

  1. Art 12: 'State' includes Government and Parliament of India, Government and Legislature of each State, and all local or other authorities within the territory of India or under the control of the Government of India.
  2. 'Other authorities' (Rajasthan SEB v. Mohan Lal, 1967): authorities exercising governmental or sovereign functions/with statutory power to make rules. Sukhdev Singh v. Bhagatram (1975): statutory corporations (LIC, ONGC) are State. Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Mujib (1981): functional-institutional tests - deep and pervasive State control, financial aid, monopoly status, governmental functions. Pradeep Kumar Biswas v. Indian Institute of Chemical Biology (2002): 'dominant control' test.
  3. Judiciary: courts acting in judicial capacity are not 'State' for Art 12 for writ of certiorari against judicial orders (Rupa Ashok Hurra, 2002; Naresh Shridhar Mirajkar, 1966) - but administrative functions of courts are covered.
  4. BCCI is not 'State' (Zee Telefilms v. Union of India, 2005) but its public functions can be reviewed under Art 226. Private banks are not 'State' (Federal Bank v. Sagar Thomas, 2003) though writ may lie for public duty.
  5. Art 13(1): pre-Constitution laws inconsistent with FR are void to the extent of inconsistency (doctrine of eclipse: they become dormant, not dead - Bhikaji Narain Dhakras v. State of M.P., 1955). Art 13(2): State cannot make law violating FR; such a law is void to the extent (ab initio - Deep Chand v. State of U.P., 1959). Post-Constitution law violating FR guaranteed only to citizens is void only vis-a-vis citizens and operative for non-citizens (State of Gujarat v. Ambica Mills, 1974).
  6. Art 13(3): 'law' includes ordinance, order, bye-law, rule, regulation, notification, custom or usage having force of law. Art 13(4): constitutional amendments under Art 368 are not 'law' (24th Amendment).
  7. Doctrine of severability: only the unconstitutional part is struck if it is severable (A.K. Gopalan, 1950; R.M.D.C. v. Union of India, 1957).
  8. Doctrine of waiver: Fundamental rights cannot be waived (Behram Khurshed Pesikaka v. State of Bombay, 1955; Basheshar Nath v. CIT, 1959; Olga Tellis, 1985).
  9. Rights available only to citizens: Art 15, 16, 19, 29, 30. Available to all persons: Art 14, 20, 21, 21A (children), 22, 23, 24, 25-28 (religion), 32.
  10. Rights enforceable against private persons: Art 15(2), 17, 23, 24 (horizontal effect). Kaushal Kishor v. State of U.P. (2023): FRs under Arts 19 and 21 can also be enforced against non-State actors in appropriate cases.

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
Art 12Definition of 'State' for Part III.
Art 13(1)Pre-constitutional laws, to the extent of inconsistency, are void.
Art 13(2)Post-constitutional laws, to the extent of contravention, are void.
Art 13(3)'Law' defined; 'laws in force' defined.
Art 13(4)Amendment under Art 368 is not 'law' under Art 13.

⚖Landmark cases

Rajasthan SEB v. Mohan Lal

1967

Wide interpretation of 'other authorities'.

Sukhdev Singh v. Bhagat Ram

1975

Statutory corporations are 'State'.

Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Mujib Sehravardi

1981

Five-factor test for 'instrumentality or agency of State'.

Pradeep Kumar Biswas v. IICB

2002

Overruled the narrow Sabhajit Tewary; 'dominant control' test.

Zee Telefilms v. Union of India

2005

BCCI not 'State', but writ may lie for public duties.

Bhikaji Narain Dhakras v. State of M.P.

1955

Doctrine of eclipse - pre-constitution law not dead but dormant.

Behram Khurshed Pesikaka v. State of Bombay

1955

No waiver of FR.

Deep Chand v. State of U.P.

1959

Post-constitution law violating Art 13(2) is stillborn/void ab initio.

Kaushal Kishor v. State of U.P.

2023

FR under Arts 19/21 are enforceable against private persons; no additional restrictions on Art 19(1)(a) beyond Art 19(2).

!Exam traps

  • TrapEclipse vs severability: eclipse deals with timing (revival when obstruction removed); severability deals with extent (part void).
  • TrapDoctrine of eclipse for post-constitution laws applies only in respect of non-citizens.
