Parliament: Composition, Procedure, Privileges, Money Bills and Anti-Defection (Tenth Schedule)

iOverview

Composition numbers, joint sitting, Money Bill, privileges (Sita Soren) and anti-defection law (Kihoto Hollohan, Speaker timelines) are consistently tested.

★Must know

  1. Art 79: Parliament = President + Rajya Sabha + Lok Sabha. Rajya Sabha (Art 80): maximum 250 (238 elected by State/UT legislatures + 12 nominated); present strength 245 (233 elected + 12 nominated); term 6 years; one-third retire every second year; permanent House; not subject to dissolution. Lok Sabha (Art 81): maximum 550 (530 from States + 20 from UTs); present strength 543; term 5 years (Art 83(2)); can be extended by Parliament for one year at a time during a proclamation of Emergency (not beyond 6 months after Emergency ends).
  2. Qualifications (Art 84): citizen; age 25 (LS), 30 (RS); other qualifications by law (RP Act 1951). Disqualifications (Art 102): office of profit (Jaya Bachchan v. Union of India, 2006; 'Office of Profit' (Prevention of Disqualification) Act), unsound mind, undischarged insolvent, not a citizen, disqualified by law or Tenth Schedule. Decision on disqualification: President on the opinion of Election Commission (Art 103), except Tenth Schedule cases decided by the Speaker/Chairman.
  3. RP Act 1951 s.8(3): conviction with imprisonment of 2 years or more = disqualification for term + 6 years. Lily Thomas v. Union of India (2013): s.8(4) (3-month protection) struck down - disqualification is immediate on conviction. Lok Prahari v. Union of India (2018). Public Interest Foundation v. Union of India (2019): Court refused to add disqualification at the charge-framing stage.
  4. Art 85: sessions - not more than six months between two sessions. Art 87: special address by President. Art 93-96: Speaker and Deputy Speaker - elected by Lok Sabha, resign to each other; removal by resolution passed by an effective majority of all then members after 14 days' notice. Speaker has a casting vote only (Art 100(1)); presiding officer does not vote at first instance. Quorum = one-tenth of total membership (Art 100(3)). Deputy Speaker has been vacant in the 17th and 18th Lok Sabha - a debated violation of constitutional spirit Verify · current.
  5. Legislative procedure: ordinary Bill - passes both Houses; Art 108 joint sitting for deadlock (if other House rejects, or disagrees on amendments, or six months lapse without passing) - presided by Speaker; applicable to ordinary and financial (non-money) Bills, NOT Money Bills or Constitution Amendment Bills. Joint sittings held: 1961 (Dowry Prohibition Bill), 1978 (Banking Service Commission Repeal Bill), 2002 (POTA).
  6. Money Bill (Art 110): introduced only in Lok Sabha on President's recommendation; Speaker's certificate is final (Art 110(3)); Rajya Sabha can only recommend amendments within 14 days (Art 109); LS may accept or reject. Financial Bill (Art 117) - Category I (money-bill-like provisions + others) and Category II. Aadhaar Act, 2016 passed as Money Bill: validity upheld in Puttaswamy (2018) majority, doubted by minority; Rojer Mathew v. South Indian Bank (2019) referred the issue of Money Bill certification to a larger bench - pending Verify.
  7. Budget process: Art 112 (annual financial statement), 113 (voting on demands for grants - only Lok Sabha), 114 (Appropriation Bill), 115 (supplementary), 116 (vote on account), 117; 265 - no tax except by authority of law; 266 - Consolidated Fund of India/State and Public Account; 267 - Contingency Fund of India (corpus Rs 500 crore, earlier Rs 50 crore) at disposal of the President.
  8. Privileges: Art 105(1) freedom of speech in Parliament; 105(2) no liability in any court for anything said or any vote given in Parliament or its committees, and for publication under authority of a House; 105(3) other privileges defined by law (not yet codified) as of 44th Amendment commencement. Art 122 - validity of proceedings cannot be questioned on irregularity of procedure. Raja Ram Pal v. Hon'ble Speaker, Lok Sabha (2007): expulsion of MPs in 'cash for query' sting upheld; privilege action is subject to limited judicial review. Keshav Singh (1965) - Art 143 reference on conflict between Assembly and HC; Pandit M.S.M. Sharma v. Sri Krishna Sinha (1959) - privileges not subject to Art 19(1)(a).
  9. P.V. Narasimha Rao v. State (CBI/SPE) (1998) (3:2) gave MPs immunity from prosecution for taking bribes for votes; OVERRULED by the 7-judge Bench in Sita Soren v. Union of India (4 March 2024): bribery is not protected by Art 105(2)/194(2); no immunity from prosecution for receiving bribe to vote or speak in a certain way.
  10. Tenth Schedule (52nd Amendment 1985; modified by 91st Amendment 2003 to omit split exception): disqualification if a member voluntarily gives up membership of party or votes/abstains contrary to party whip without permission (unless condoned within 15 days); independent members joining a party; nominated members joining a party after 6 months. Exception: merger of two-thirds of legislature party with another party (para 4). Decision by the Speaker/Chairman (para 6).
  11. Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu (1992): Tenth Schedule valid; para 7 (ousting court jurisdiction) struck down for lacking ratification under Art 368(2)(ii); Speaker's decision is a tribunal decision subject to judicial review on limited grounds (jurisdictional errors, mala fides, perversity, violation of natural justice) and cannot be reviewed before the final decision except for interim disqualification/suspension. Ravi S. Naik (1994): 'voluntarily giving up membership' includes conduct inferred; Rajendra Singh Rana (2007); Keisham Meghachandra Singh v. Speaker, Manipur Legislative Assembly (2020): Speaker must decide within 3 months ordinarily; Parliament should consider vesting the power in an independent tribunal. Padi Kaushik Reddy v. State of Telangana (July 2025): Speaker directed to decide within 3 months Verify. Subhash Desai v. Principal Secretary, Maharashtra (2023, 5 judges): Governor cannot call a floor test without objective material; whip of a political party (not legislature party) is the relevant authority; Nabam Rebia (2016) reference to 7 judges left to be examined.
  12. Women's reservation: Constitution (106th Amendment) Act 2023 (Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam) - one-third reservation of seats for women in Lok Sabha, State Assemblies and Delhi Assembly (Arts 330A, 332A, 239AA(2)(b)); to come into force after the first census and delimitation exercise after commencement; 15-year duration, extendable. The Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill 2026 on delimitation and operationalisation was defeated in the Lok Sabha (April 2026) for want of two-thirds majority Verify · details.

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
Art 105(2)No member liable in any court for anything said or vote given in Parliament - bribery is not protected (Sita Soren, 2024).
Art 108Joint sitting - not for Money Bills or Constitution Amendment Bills.
Art 110Money Bill definition and certification by the Speaker.
Tenth Schedule para 4Merger exception - 2/3 of legislature party.
Tenth Schedule para 6Decision by Chairman or Speaker.

⚖Landmark cases

Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu

1992

Tenth Schedule valid; para 7 struck down; Speaker's decision reviewable.

Raja Ram Pal v. Speaker, Lok Sabha

2007

Expulsion of MPs valid; privileges reviewable to limited extent.

Lily Thomas v. Union of India

2013

s.8(4) RP Act struck down; immediate disqualification on conviction.

Jaya Bachchan v. Union of India

2006

'Office of profit' - actual receipt of pecuniary gain is irrelevant; potential is enough.

P.V. Narasimha Rao v. State

1998

Bribery immunity (overruled in 2024).

Sita Soren v. Union of India

2024

7 judges: no immunity for bribery by legislators.

Keisham Meghachandra Singh v. Speaker

2020

Speaker to decide disqualification within 3 months.

Subhash Desai v. Principal Secretary, Govt of Maharashtra

2023

Governor's floor test power; Speaker's powers; whip.

Rojer Mathew v. South Indian Bank

2019

Money Bill reference to larger bench (also tribunals).

✎Memory aids

  • Art 108 joint sitting - 'No for M-C': Money Bill and Constitution amendment Bill.
  • LS 543 = 530 + 13 UTs; RS 245 = 233 + 12.

!Exam traps

  • TrapMoney Bill cannot be introduced in Rajya Sabha; only LS decides what is a Money Bill (Speaker).
  • TrapSpeaker is NOT removed by impeachment but by a resolution with 14 days' notice.
  • TrapThe nominee limit of 12 in Rajya Sabha is under Art 80(3), not Art 80(2).
  • TrapAnti-defection cannot be invoked for 'merger' of 2/3 of the legislature party.
