iOverview

Writ jurisdiction is a constant AIBE theme (nature of each writ, scope of Art 32 vs 226). Property after 1978 is a constitutional right under Art 300A.

★Must know

  1. Art 32: right to move the Supreme Court for enforcement of FR - itself a fundamental right; Dr Ambedkar called it 'the heart and soul of the Constitution'; part of basic structure. Supreme Court may issue directions, orders or writs including habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, quo warranto and certiorari. Art 32(3): Parliament can empower other courts to issue writs. Art 32(4): right cannot be suspended except as provided by the Constitution.
  2. Art 226: High Courts may issue writs to any person or authority (including Government) within its territorial jurisdiction - for enforcement of FR AND 'for any other purpose' - wider than Art 32. 226(1A): jurisdiction on the basis of cause of action arising wholly or partly in the territory (15th Amendment). Alternative remedy is a rule of prudence, not an absolute bar (Whirlpool Corporation v. Registrar of Trade Marks, 1998 - exceptions: FR violation, natural justice breach, lack of jurisdiction, challenge to vires of law).
  3. Habeas corpus ('produce the body'): against illegal detention by State or private persons; can be filed by any person; res judicata not applicable (Ghulam Sarwar, 1967). Mandamus ('we command'): to perform public duty; not against private individuals (generally), the President/Governors personally (Art 361), or for enforcing non-statutory directives; the petitioner must have legal right. Prohibition: issued to lower court/tribunal to prevent excess of jurisdiction - issued BEFORE the order is final (vs certiorari after order). Certiorari ('to be certified'): quash orders of inferior courts and quasi-judicial authorities for jurisdictional error, violation of natural justice, error of law apparent on face of record (Hari Vishnu Kamath, 1955; Syed Yakoob, 1964). Quo warranto ('by what authority'): against usurpation of public office of substantive character; any person can file (University of Mysore v. C.D. Govinda Rao, 1965).
  4. Art 227: superintendence of High Courts over all courts and tribunals in its territory - basic structure (L. Chandra Kumar, 1997); Art 227 is wider (supervisory), non-writ.
  5. Res judicata: dismissal of Art 226 petition on merits bars Art 32 petition on same facts (Daryao v. State of U.P., 1961); dismissal in limine without speaking order does not.
  6. Locus standi relaxed for PIL: S.P. Gupta v. Union of India (1981); Bandhua Mukti Morcha (1984); Judges transfer case. 'Epistolary jurisdiction' - letters treated as petitions (Sunil Batra). Safeguards: Balwant Singh Chaufal (2010); misuse - 'publicity interest litigation' - costs imposed. [Cross-ref: Public Interest Litigation.]
  7. Art 33: Parliament may restrict/abrogate FR of armed forces, police, intelligence, etc. Art 34: restrictions on FR while martial law is in force; Parliament can indemnify. Art 35: only Parliament (not States) can make laws for giving effect to Arts 16(3), 32(3), 33, 34 and offences under Part III.
  8. Art 31 (compulsory acquisition) deleted by the 44th Amendment (1978); Art 19(1)(f) deleted; right to property now an ordinary constitutional right under Art 300A: 'No person shall be deprived of property save by authority of law.' Judicial review is still available; compensation not guaranteed explicitly, but see Jilubhai Nanbhai Khachar (1995) and Kolkata Municipal Corporation v. Bimal Kumar Shah (2024) - Art 300A includes: duty to inform, hear objections, reasoned decision, public purpose, compensation, efficient acquisition, conclusion; it is also a human right.
  9. Art 31A: saves laws on acquisition of estates, management of property, amalgamation of companies, rights of managing agents/mining leases from challenge under Arts 14, 19 (1st Amendment 1951; 4th, 17th, 25th Amendments). Art 31B and Ninth Schedule: laws listed are protected (see Section 1.3). Art 31C: see Section 1.3.
  10. Mineral Area Development Authority v. SAIL (25 July 2024), 9 judges, 8:1: States have legislative competence to tax mineral rights (Entry 50, List II) - royalty is not a tax; Parliament's limits under Entry 54 List I MMDR Act. [Cross-ref: Taxation.]

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
Art 32(2)Writs: habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, quo warranto, certiorari.
Art 226(1)HC writ power 'for the enforcement of any of the rights conferred by Part III and for any other purpose'.
Art 300ARight to property - no deprivation save by authority of law.
Art 31BLaws in Ninth Schedule - saved from invalidity on grounds of violation of FR (subject to I.R. Coelho).

✎Memory aids

  • Writs: 'HM-PCQ' - Habeas, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo warranto.
  • Prohibition = prevent (before order); Certiorari = cancel (after order).

!Exam traps

  • TrapArt 32 is for FR only; Art 226 for FR + any other legal right.
  • TrapArt 32 is itself a FR; Art 226 is not a FR.
  • TrapRight to property today is NOT a fundamental right but a constitutional (and human) right.
  • TrapHabeas corpus can be filed by a third person; quo warranto can be filed by any person (not only aggrieved).
