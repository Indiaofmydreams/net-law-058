iOverview

Ethics overlaps with contempt law, privilege and strike jurisprudence. Includes the October 2025 Supreme Court ruling on summoning advocates.

★Must know

  1. Contempt of Courts Act 1971 - definitions (s.2): 'civil contempt' - wilful disobedience to any judgment, decree, direction, order, writ or other process of a court or wilful breach of an undertaking given to a court; 'criminal contempt' - publication (words spoken or written, signs, visible representations) which (i) scandalises or tends to scandalise, or lowers or tends to lower the authority of any court; (ii) prejudices or interferes or tends to interfere with the due course of any judicial proceeding; or (iii) interferes or tends to interfere with, or obstructs or tends to obstruct, the administration of justice in any other manner. Defences: innocent publication and distribution (s.3); fair and accurate report of judicial proceedings (s.4); fair criticism of a judicial act after the case is finally decided (s.5); complaint against presiding officers of subordinate courts made in good faith to a superior court (s.6); publication of proceedings in chambers or in camera (s.7); other defences (s.8); truth as a defence if justified in public interest and the request is bona fide (s.13(b), inserted by the 2006 Amendment). s.12 - punishment: simple imprisonment for a term up to six months, or fine up to Rs 2,000, or both; the accused may be discharged or the punishment remitted on apology if the court is satisfied that the apology is bona fide; the court may withhold punishment if the contempt does not substantially interfere with the administration of justice (s.13(a)). s.14 - procedure where contempt is in the face of the Supreme Court or a High Court; s.15 - cognisance of criminal contempt (other than in the face of the court) by the Supreme Court or High Court on its own motion or on a motion by the Attorney-General/Solicitor-General (Supreme Court), Advocate-General (High Court) or any other person with the written consent of the Attorney-General/Advocate-General; s.17 - procedure after cognisance (notice, hearing, affidavit); s.19 - appeal as of right to the Supreme Court from an order punishing for contempt (within 30 days); s.20 - limitation: no court shall initiate any proceeding for contempt after the expiry of one year from the date on which the contempt is alleged to have been committed. Constitutional contempt power: Art 129 (Supreme Court as a court of record), Art 215 (High Courts). Cases: Re: Prashant Bhushan (2020) - fine of Re 1 (tweets); Baradakanta Mishra v. Registrar of Orissa High Court (1974) - scandalising; E.M. Sankaran Namboodiripad v. T. Narayanan Nambiar (1970); Perspective Publications v. State of Maharashtra (1969); In re: Arundhati Roy (2002); Dr. D.C. Saxena v. Hon'ble the Chief Justice of India (1996); Re: Vijay Kurle (2020) - advocates who made allegations against judges were held guilty of contempt.
  2. Advocates and contempt: an advocate is an officer of the court; the judge-advocate relationship rests on mutual respect (BCI Rule 2-3); remarks against judges outside court may amount to contempt; the High Court may take action; the Bar Council's disciplinary power is independent (Pravin C. Shah; Vinay Chandra Mishra). Court's power to censure advocates, withdraw their pre-audience, restrain from appearing: Bar Council of India v. High Court of Kerala (2004). Remarks in court against the judge by an advocate are grounds for contempt and misconduct (M.B. Sanghi).
  3. Strikes by lawyers: Ex-Capt. Harish Uppal v. Union of India (2002/2003) - lawyers have no right to go on strike or give a call for boycott of courts, not even on a token strike; protests may be held by press statements, TV interviews, black or white armbands, and peaceful demonstration outside and away from court premises, not affecting court proceedings; any lawyer who abstains from work in breach of this is liable for professional misconduct and the Bar Council must take action; the courts may impose costs on the lawyers who strike (Ramon Services Pvt. Ltd. v. Subhash Kapoor, 2001 - a client may recover compensation from advocate who abstains); Common Cause v. Union of India (2006); Krishnakant Tamrakar v. State of M.P. (2018) - strike by lawyers is illegal; the High Courts to take cognisance; Bar Council of India's Rule 'no strike' (BCI Resolution); the Court said that the BCI must act against defaulters.
  4. Advocate-client privilege: Evidence Act ss.126-129 now BSA ss.132-134: s.132 BSA (s.126 Evidence Act): no advocate shall at any time be permitted, unless with his client's express consent, to disclose any communication made to him in the course and for the purpose of his employment as such advocate by or on behalf of his client, or to state the contents or condition of any document with which he has become acquainted in the course and for the purpose of his professional employment, or to disclose any advice given by him to his client in the course and for the purpose of such employment; exceptions: (1) any such communication made in furtherance of any illegal purpose; (2) any fact observed by any advocate in the course of his employment showing that any crime or fraud has been committed since the commencement of his employment; the obligation continues after the employment has ceased. s.133 BSA (s.127): same protection for interpreters, clerks and servants of advocates; s.134 BSA (s.129): no one shall be compelled to disclose to the court any confidential communication between himself and his legal professional adviser unless he offers himself as a witness (then only as necessary). Waiver: privilege is the client's, not the advocate's; privilege is not waived merely by the client giving evidence.
  5. In re: Summoning Advocates who give legal opinion or represent parties during investigation of cases and related issues (31 October 2025; 2025 INSC 1275; suo motu proceedings after the Enforcement Directorate's summons to a senior advocate): the Supreme Court held that investigating agencies and police cannot summon advocates to disclose the legal advice given to clients or to disclose communications with clients, except within the exceptions of s.132 BSA (illegal purpose/crime or fraud); any summons to an advocate must have the prior written approval of a superior officer (not below the rank of Superintendent of Police) recording reasons that the case falls within the exceptions; the advocate may challenge the summons before the court under s.528 BNSS or Art 226; production of digital devices of the advocate can be ordered only by a court (not by the investigating officer), and the advocate has the right to have the privilege decided by a judicial forum; the Court clarified that in-house counsel employed full-time by a company are not 'advocates' for the purposes of s.132 BSA (their communication with the employer is not protected by that section, though s.134 BSA - the protection for confidential communications with a legal adviser - may apply in respect of communications to the company's legal advisers) Verify · details. This decision was followed by the observations that legal professional privilege is a facet of the right to a fair trial (Art 21).
  6. Legal aid and public duty: advocates are expected to provide legal aid to the poor as part of their obligation (BCI Rules; Legal Services Authorities Act); State-paid legal aid advocates; duty of counsel to defend an accused (Rule 15); Mohammed Ajmal Mohammad Amir Kasab v. State of Maharashtra (2012) - duty of the Court to appoint a competent lawyer; Khatri; Suk Das. Lawyers' duty not to refuse a brief because the client is unpopular (A.S. Mohammed Rafi).
  7. Consumer Protection and advocates: Bar of Indian Lawyers (through its President Jasbir Singh Malik) v. D.K. Gandhi PS Rao, Deputy Director, National Institute of Communicable Diseases (2024): the Supreme Court held (May 2024) that the legal profession is sui generis; advocates' services are not 'service' under s.2(42) of the Consumer Protection Act 2019 (and corresponding provisions of the 1986 Act); the NCDRC decision in Indian Medical Association v. V.P. Shantha line did not apply; consumer fora cannot entertain complaints on deficiency in services of advocates; remedies lie in disciplinary proceedings or a civil suit for professional negligence Verify. Jacob Mathew v. State of Punjab (2005) - professional negligence standard.
  8. Other ethical/ regulatory developments: BCI's Rules on advocates' welfare; Advocates' Welfare Fund; BCI's decisions on non-practising advocates and on the AIBE; BCI's resolutions on the conduct of Bar elections; the Bar Council of India (Verification) Rules 2015; Law Commission 266th report; the Government's Advocates (Amendment) Bill 2025 draft (withdrawn) Verify; the Bar Council elections and the cap on tenure; use of AI and ethical standards (BCI and High Courts have issued advisories on AI-generated pleadings and citation of non-existent precedents - misconduct if filed carelessly) Verify; social-media advertising and the BCI's 2008 resolution.

⚖Landmark cases

Ex-Capt. Harish Uppal v. Union of India

2002

No lawyer's right to strike; protests only in permitted forms; strike amounts to misconduct.

Re: Prashant Bhushan

2020

Criminal contempt for tweets; fine of Re 1.

Baradakanta Mishra v. Registrar of Orissa High Court

Scandalising the court; scope of contempt.

In re: Summoning Advocates who give legal opinion or represent parties during investigation

2025

Advocates cannot be summoned to disclose client communications except under s.132 BSA exceptions; prior approval required; in-house counsel not covered by s.132.

Ramon Services Pvt. Ltd. v. Subhash Kapoor

2001

Client can claim compensation from advocates for loss due to strike.

Bar of Indian Lawyers v. D.K. Gandhi PS Rao

2024

Advocates' services are outside the Consumer Protection Act Verify.

A.S. Mohammed Rafi v. State of Tamil Nadu

2011

Advocates cannot be prevented by Bar resolution from representing clients.

✎Memory aids

  • Contempt Act: s.12 (punishment 6 months/Rs 2,000), s.13 (truth defence), s.15 (cognisance), s.19 (appeal in 30 days), s.20 (1 year).
  • BSA 132-134 = Evidence Act 126-129.

!Exam traps

  • TrapUnder s.13(b) truth is a defence to criminal contempt only if bona fide and in public interest.
  • TrapLimitation for contempt: one year from the date of the alleged contempt.
  • TrapPrivilege belongs to the client; the advocate cannot waive it.
  • TrapIn-house counsel are not 'advocates' for s.132 BSA purposes (2025).

  3. Which BSA section protects advocate-client communications? (s.132)
