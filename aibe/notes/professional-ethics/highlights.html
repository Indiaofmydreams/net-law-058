1

Advocates Act, 1961: Bar Councils, Enrolment, Right to Practise, Senior Advocates, Right of Audience

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Advocates Act s.16Senior advocates and other advocates.
Advocates Act s.23Right of pre-audience: AG, SG, Additional SG, Advocates-General, senior advocates, other advocates.
Advocates Act s.24Qualifications for admission: citizen, 21 years, law degree, enrolment fee.
Advocates Act s.24ADisqualifications for enrolment: moral turpitude conviction etc.
Advocates Act s.29-30Single class of advocates; right to practise throughout India.
Advocates Act s.45Penalty for illegal practice: up to six months.

Cases in one line

  • Indira Jaising v. Supreme Court of India 2017 Guidelines for designation of senior advocates; point-based system; Permanent Committee.
  • Bar Council of India v. A.K. Balaji 2018 Foreign law firms cannot practise in India (fly-in fly-out allowed for foreign law/ international arbitration).
  • Gaurav Kumar v. Union of India 2024 State Bar Councils cannot charge enrolment fees beyond the statutory limit.
  • Bar Council of India v. Bonnie Foi Law College 2023 BCI's power to conduct the All India Bar Examination as post-enrolment test upheld Verify.

Memory aids

  • Pre-audience: AG - SG - ASG - Advocates-General - Seniors - Others.
  • State Bar Council: 15 / 20 / 25 members by electorate size 5,000 / 10,000 / above.

Exam traps

  • TrapOnly advocates (not pleaders/vakils) are recognised after the Advocates Act 1961.
  • TrapRight to practise under s.30 came into force only on 15 June 2011.
  • TrapA senior advocate cannot file a vakalatnama or take direct instructions from clients.
2

Professional and Other Misconduct (s.35), Disciplinary Committees, Procedure, Punishments, Appeals and Leading Misconduct Cases

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Advocates Act s.35Misconduct: punishments - reprimand, suspension, removal from roll.
Advocates Act s.36BState Bar Council Disciplinary Committee to dispose within one year; else transferred to BCI.
Advocates Act s.37Appeal to BCI within 60 days.
Advocates Act s.38Appeal to Supreme Court within 60 days.
Advocates Act s.42Disciplinary Committees have civil-court powers.

Cases in one line

  • V.C. Rangadurai v. D. Gopalan 1979 Misconduct: advocate-client fiduciary relationship; Bar Council's role in discipline.
  • Bar Council of Maharashtra v. M.V. Dabholkar 1975 Bar Council's disciplinary jurisdiction; standard of conduct expected of advocates.
  • In re: Vinay Chandra Mishra 1995 Advocate suspended for three years for contempt of court; advocate as officer of the court.
  • Pravin C. Shah v. K.A. Mohd. Ali 2001 High Court cannot, in contempt, debar an advocate from practice; only the Bar Council can.
  • R.D. Saxena v. Balram Prasad Sharma 2000 Advocate has no lien over client's papers; non-return is misconduct.
  • Ex-Capt. Harish Uppal v. Union of India 2002 Lawyers have no right to strike or boycott courts; strike can be misconduct.
  • R.K. Anand v. Registrar, Delhi High Court 2009 Senior advocate suspended for four months for unethical conduct in the BMW trial.
  • P.D. Gupta v. Ram Murti 1997 Misappropriation of client's money is grave misconduct; removal from roll.
  • Bar of Indian Lawyers v. D.K. Gandhi PS Rao 2024 Advocates are not providers of 'service' under the Consumer Protection Act Verify.

Memory aids

  • Punishments under s.35: 'D-R-S-R' - Dismiss complaint, Reprimand, Suspend, Remove from roll.
  • Appeals: 60 days to BCI (s.37); 60 days to Supreme Court (s.38).

Exam traps

  • TrapOnly the Bar Council (not the High Court) can debar an advocate; the High Court may punish for contempt.
  • TrapAn advocate does not have a lien over the client's papers.
  • TrapDisciplinary appeals go: State Bar Council DC -> BCI -> Supreme Court.
  • TrapStrike by lawyers is misconduct; protest is allowed outside court hours/ premises through peaceful means.
3

Bar Council of India Rules (Part VI, Chapter II): Duty to Court, Client, Opponent and Colleagues; Fees and Accounts; Advertising; Other Employment; Senior Advocates; Dress

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

BCI Rule 20No contingent fee; no sharing of proceeds.
BCI Rule 36No soliciting or advertising.
BCI Rules 47-49No business; director allowed (not Managing Director); no full-time salaried employment.
BCI Rule 6Not to appear before a relative.
BCI Rule 11-12Duty to accept briefs; no withdrawal without notice.

Cases in one line

  • V.C. Rangadurai v. D. Gopalan 1979 Duty to client; advocate cannot give away client's trust.
  • R.D. Saxena v. Balram Prasad Sharma 2000 No lien on client's papers; duty to return documents and account.
  • Dr. Haniraj L. Chulani v. Bar Council of Maharashtra and Goa 1996 Cannot practise law and medicine simultaneously; no dual enrolment.
  • Sushma Suri v. Government (NCT of Delhi) 1999 Law officer/ legal adviser who acts/pleads is an advocate under BCI rules.
  • A.S. Mohammed Rafi v. State of Tamil Nadu 2011 A Bar Association cannot ban its members from representing a party; advocates must defend even unpopular accused.
  • M.B. Sanghi v. High Court of Punjab and Haryana 1991 Advocate who abused a judge guilty of contempt; advocates must be respectful.
  • Harish Uppal v. Union of India 2002 No right to strike; remedy for clients.

Memory aids

  • Duties: 'C-C-O-C' - Court, Client, Opponent, Colleagues. Prohibitions: contingent fee (20), actionable claims (21), purchase at execution sale (22), advertising (36), business (47).

Exam traps

  • TrapAdvocate may be a director of a company but not its Managing Director or Secretary.
  • TrapA full-time salaried employee must stop practising and intimate the Bar Council.
  • TrapContingent fee is prohibited, but advocates may agree a fixed fee and advance court fees (Rule 32 Explanation).
  • TrapAn advocate cannot communicate directly with a represented opponent.
4

Contempt of Court, Strikes, Professional Privilege (BSA ss.132-134), Legal Aid, Duties as Officer of the Court and Recent Rulings (2024-2026)

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Contempt of Courts Act s.12Penalty: up to six months or fine up to Rs 2,000 or both; apology may be accepted if bona fide.
Contempt of Courts Act s.13(b)Truth is a defence if bona fide and in public interest.
Contempt of Courts Act s.20Limitation: one year.
BSA s.132 (Evidence Act s.126)Advocate-client privilege; exceptions: illegal purpose; crime or fraud after employment.
BSA s.134 (Evidence Act s.129)Confidential communications with legal adviser.

Cases in one line

  • Ex-Capt. Harish Uppal v. Union of India 2002 No lawyer's right to strike; protests only in permitted forms; strike amounts to misconduct.
  • Re: Prashant Bhushan 2020 Criminal contempt for tweets; fine of Re 1.
  • Baradakanta Mishra v. Registrar of Orissa High Court 1974 Scandalising the court; scope of contempt.
  • In re: Summoning Advocates who give legal opinion or represent parties during investigation 2025 Advocates cannot be summoned to disclose client communications except under s.132 BSA exceptions; prior approval required; in-house counsel not covered by s.132.
  • Ramon Services Pvt. Ltd. v. Subhash Kapoor 2001 Client can claim compensation from advocates for loss due to strike.
  • Bar of Indian Lawyers v. D.K. Gandhi PS Rao 2024 Advocates' services are outside the Consumer Protection Act Verify.
  • A.S. Mohammed Rafi v. State of Tamil Nadu 2011 Advocates cannot be prevented by Bar resolution from representing clients.

Memory aids

  • Contempt Act: s.12 (punishment 6 months/Rs 2,000), s.13 (truth defence), s.15 (cognisance), s.19 (appeal in 30 days), s.20 (1 year).
  • BSA 132-134 = Evidence Act 126-129.

Exam traps

  • TrapUnder s.13(b) truth is a defence to criminal contempt only if bona fide and in public interest.
  • TrapLimitation for contempt: one year from the date of the alleged contempt.
  • TrapPrivilege belongs to the client; the advocate cannot waive it.
  • TrapIn-house counsel are not 'advocates' for s.132 BSA purposes (2025).
