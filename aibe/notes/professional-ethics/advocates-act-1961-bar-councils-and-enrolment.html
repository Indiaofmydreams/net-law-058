iOverview

The statutory machinery that regulates the legal profession. The Act has a regular set of testable sections.

★Must know

  1. The Advocates Act 1961 (on the recommendation of the Law Commission's 14th Report and the All India Bar Committee) amends and consolidates the law relating to legal practitioners and provides for the constitution of Bar Councils and an All-India Bar; it abolished the old classes of legal practitioners (pleaders, vakils, attorneys) and created a single class - 'advocates' (s.29). Chapters: II Bar Councils (ss.3-15); III Admission and enrolment of advocates (ss.16-28); IV Right to practise (ss.29-34); V Conduct of advocates (ss.35-44); VI Miscellaneous (ss.45-60). In force from 19 May 1961 (the Act was brought into force progressively).
  2. State Bar Councils (s.3): a Bar Council for each State (and for Delhi and the Union Territories); composition: the Advocate-General of the State as ex officio member and 15 members (where the electorate is up to 5,000), 20 members (5,001-10,000) or 25 members (above 10,000) elected by advocates on the State roll from amongst themselves (proportional representation by single transferable vote); term of five years (s.8); Chairman and Vice-Chairman elected by the Council Verify · section. Functions (s.6): admit persons as advocates on its roll; prepare and maintain such roll; entertain and determine cases of misconduct against advocates on its roll; safeguard the rights, privileges and interests of advocates; promote and support law reform; manage and invest its funds; provide for the election of its members; organise legal aid to the poor in the prescribed manner; promote legal education and lay down standards in consultation with the BCI and universities.
  3. Bar Council of India (s.4): consists of the Attorney-General of India and the Solicitor-General of India (ex officio) and one member elected by each State Bar Council from among its members; Chairman and Vice-Chairman elected by the Council; term of five years Verify · the extension of terms in recent years. Functions (s.7): lay down standards of professional conduct and etiquette for advocates; lay down procedure to be followed by its disciplinary committee and those of each State Bar Council; safeguard rights, privileges and interests of advocates; promote and support law reform; deal with and dispose of any matter arising under the Act referred to it by a State Bar Council; exercise general supervision and control over State Bar Councils; promote legal education and lay down standards in consultation with the universities and State Bar Councils; recognise universities whose degree in law shall be a qualification for enrolment (and visit and inspect them); conduct seminars; organise legal aid; recognise foreign qualifications in law on a reciprocal basis; manage and invest funds; provide for the election of its members. s.49 - the BCI's general power to make rules, including on standards of professional conduct and etiquette (s.49(1)(c)), on dress to be worn by advocates (s.49(1)(gg)), conditions subject to which an advocate may practise, and the disciplinary procedure. Committees: the Disciplinary Committee (three persons: two elected from members and one co-opted advocate; the senior-most is the Chairman), the Executive Committee, the Legal Aid Committee and others (ss.9-10B).
  4. Senior and other advocates (s.16): two classes - senior advocates and other advocates; an advocate may with his consent be designated as a senior advocate if the Supreme Court or a High Court is of the opinion that by virtue of his ability, standing at the Bar or special knowledge or experience in law he is deserving of such distinction; senior advocates are subject to restrictions specified by the BCI in the interests of the profession. Indira Jaising v. Supreme Court of India (2017): guidelines for designation - a Permanent Committee (Chief Justice, two senior-most judges, Attorney-General and a member of the Bar nominated), a point-based system (100 points - years of practice, reported judgments, publications, personality test), consideration by the Full Court; in 2023 (Indira Jaising v. Supreme Court of India, Jan 2023) the Court reviewed the point system and clarified its application, relying on a holistic assessment Verify. A senior advocate cannot file vakalatnama or accept instructions directly from a client; must appear with an Advocate-on-Record in the Supreme Court; cannot draft pleadings (BCI Rules, Part VI Chapter I). Advocates-on-Record (Supreme Court Rules 2013, Order IV): only an AoR (who has passed the AoR examination and trained for one year under an AoR) can file matters in the Supreme Court.
  5. Admission and enrolment: s.17 - State Bar Council prepares and maintains a roll (State roll) of advocates; s.18 - transfer of name from one State roll to another; s.22 - certificate of enrolment; s.24 - qualifications: (a) a citizen of India (nationals of other countries admitted on reciprocal basis); (b) has completed 21 years of age; (c) has obtained a degree in law from a recognised university (three-year course after a graduate degree, or a five-year integrated course) or a foreign qualification recognised by the BCI; (d) fulfils such other conditions as the State Bar Council may specify; (e) has paid the enrolment fee (not exceeding Rs 600 to the State Bar Council and Rs 150 to the BCI; Rs 125 and Rs 25 for SC/ST - total Rs 750 general / Rs 125 per Verify) - Gaurav Kumar v. Union of India (30 July 2024): State Bar Councils cannot charge enrolment fee beyond the statutory fee. s.24A - disqualifications for enrolment: (a) conviction for an offence involving moral turpitude; (b) conviction for an offence under the Untouchability (Offences) Act 1955 (now Protection of Civil Rights Act); (c) dismissal or removal from employment or office under the State on a charge involving moral turpitude; disqualification (a) and (b) ceases after two years from release Verify. s.26 - disposal of applications; s.26A - removal of names from the roll (on death, or on the request of the advocate, or for any other reason); s.30 - right to practise (see below). BCI Verification Rules 2015: Certificate of Practice and Place of Practice; All India Bar Examination (AIBE) - mandatory for enrolled advocates to obtain a Certificate of Practice (BCI Rules, Part VI; AIBE upheld in Bar Council of India v. Bonnie Foi Law College, 2023: the BCI has the power to conduct the AIBE after enrolment, as a post-enrolment test for competence and a pre-condition to the certificate; the Court reiterated that the exam does not violate Art 19(1)(g)).
  6. Right to practise (Chapter IV): s.29 - only advocates are recognised as a class of persons entitled to practise law; s.30 - every advocate whose name is entered in the State roll is entitled as of right to practise throughout the territories to which the Act extends in (i) all courts including the Supreme Court; (ii) before any tribunal or person legally authorised to take evidence; (iii) before any other authority or person before whom such advocate is by or under any law for the time being in force entitled to practise (s.30 came into force on 15 June 2011); s.32 - power of the court to permit appearances by non-advocates in a particular case; s.33 - advocates alone are entitled to practise (no person unless enrolled as an advocate can practise in any court or before any authority, subject to the Act or other law); s.34 - High Court's power to make rules laying down conditions subject to which an advocate shall be permitted to practise in the High Court and the courts subordinate to it; s.35 - misconduct (see 9.E2). Pre-audience (s.23): (1) the Attorney-General of India has pre-audience over all other advocates; (2) the Solicitor-General; (3) Additional Solicitors-General; (4) the Advocates-General of States (in their respective States; in the order of seniority of the States for outside courts); (5) senior advocates; (6) other advocates (inter se seniority according to date of enrolment). A senior advocate gets precedence over other advocates.
  7. Persons illegally practising (s.45): any person who practises in any court or before any other authority or person, in contravention of s.33, is punishable with imprisonment up to six months. Advocates Welfare Fund Act 2001; Bar Council of India Rules on legal education (Rules of Legal Education 2008; 3-year and 5-year courses; Standards).
  8. Foreign lawyers: Bar Council of India v. A.K. Balaji (13 March 2018): foreign law firms and foreign lawyers cannot practise litigation or non-litigation in India (the practice of law includes non-litigious work) but may visit on a 'fly-in fly-out' basis for a temporary period to advise on foreign law and international arbitration; the BCI Rules for Registration and Regulation of Foreign Lawyers and Foreign Law Firms in India 2022 (as amended in 2025) permit registration of foreign lawyers and firms to practise foreign law and international legal matters on a reciprocal basis, excluding litigation and matters requiring Indian enrolment Verify · 2025 amendments.
  9. Legal education and bar: Law Commission 266th Report (2017) on the Advocates Act (suggestions on regulation of legal profession, strikes, and disciplinary system); Advocates (Amendment) Bill 2025 (draft, circulated February 2025; withdrawn after protests) Verify.

⚖Landmark cases

Indira Jaising v. Supreme Court of India

2017

Guidelines for designation of senior advocates; point-based system; Permanent Committee.

Bar Council of India v. A.K. Balaji

2018

Foreign law firms cannot practise in India (fly-in fly-out allowed for foreign law/ international arbitration).

Gaurav Kumar v. Union of India

2024

State Bar Councils cannot charge enrolment fees beyond the statutory limit.

Bar Council of India v. Bonnie Foi Law College

2023

BCI's power to conduct the All India Bar Examination as post-enrolment test upheld Verify.

✎Memory aids

  • Pre-audience: AG - SG - ASG - Advocates-General - Seniors - Others.
  • State Bar Council: 15 / 20 / 25 members by electorate size 5,000 / 10,000 / above.

!Exam traps

  • TrapOnly advocates (not pleaders/vakils) are recognised after the Advocates Act 1961.
  • TrapRight to practise under s.30 came into force only on 15 June 2011.
  • TrapA senior advocate cannot file a vakalatnama or take direct instructions from clients.
