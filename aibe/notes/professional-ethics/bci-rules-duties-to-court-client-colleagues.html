iOverview

The core of the ethics paper. Learn each duty and a handful of rule numbers. (Rule numbers vary in published compilations; the substance is what is tested.)

★Must know

  1. Source and nature: the BCI Rules, Part VI, 'Rules Governing Advocates', framed under s.49(1)(c) of the Advocates Act, 'Standards of Professional Conduct and Etiquette'; Chapter I - Rules regarding senior advocates; Chapter II - Standards of Professional Conduct and Etiquette (Sections I-VII); Chapter III - Section on conditions for practising; Chapter IV - Dress; Rules are binding and breach can amount to professional misconduct under s.35.
  2. Duty to the Court (Section I; Rules 1-10 Verify · numbers): an advocate shall, during the presentation of his case and while acting before a court, conduct himself with dignity and self-respect, and shall use his best efforts to restrain and prevent his client from resorting to sharp or unfair practices (Rule 1); shall maintain a respectful attitude towards the courts, bearing in mind that the dignity of the judicial office is essential for a free community (Rule 2); shall not influence the decision of a court by any illegal or improper means; private communications with a judge relating to a pending case are forbidden (Rule 3); shall use his best efforts to restrain and prevent his client from resorting to unfair practices, and refuse to represent a client who persists (Rule 4); shall appear in court in the prescribed dress and presentable (Rule 5); shall not enter appearance, act, plead or practise before a court, tribunal or authority if the sole or any member thereof is related to the advocate as father, grandfather, son, grandson, uncle, brother, nephew, first cousin, husband, wife, mother, daughter, grandmother, aunt, sister, niece, father-in-law, etc. (Rule 6 - appearing before a relative); shall not wear bands or gowns in public places other than in courts except on ceremonial occasions (Rule 7); shall not appear for or against an organisation or institution, society or corporation if he is a member of the Executive Committee (Rule 8); shall not act or plead in any matter in which he is himself pecuniarily interested (Rule 9); shall not stand surety or certify the soundness of a surety for his client (Rule 10).
  3. Duty to the Client (Section II; Rules 11-32 Verify · numbers): an advocate is bound to accept any brief in the courts or tribunals or before any other authority in or before which he professes to practise at a fee consistent with his standing at the Bar and the nature of the case, subject to special circumstances justifying refusal (Rule 11); shall not ordinarily withdraw from engagements once accepted without sufficient cause and unless reasonable and sufficient notice is given to the client; he shall refund any part of the fee not earned (Rule 12); shall not accept a brief or appear in a case in which he has reason to believe that he will be a witness, and if he becomes a witness he shall not continue to appear except as to formal matters (Rule 13); shall, at the commencement of his engagement and during its progress, make full and frank disclosure to his client relating to his connection with the parties and any interest in or about the controversy as are likely to affect the client's judgment (Rule 14); shall uphold the interest of his client fearlessly by all fair and honourable means without regard to any unpleasant consequences to himself or any other; he shall defend a person accused of a crime regardless of his personal opinion as to the guilt of the accused (Rule 15 - the counsel's duty is to ensure fair trial); shall not act on the instructions of any person other than his client or his authorised agent (Rule 16); shall not do anything whereby he abuses or takes advantage of the confidence reposed in him by his client (Rule 24); shall not stipulate for a fee contingent on the results of litigation or agree to share the proceeds thereof (Rule 20); shall not buy or traffic in or stipulate for or agree to receive any share or interest in any actionable claim (Rule 21); shall not, directly or indirectly, bid for or purchase, either in his own name or in any other name, any property sold in execution of a decree in a proceeding in which he was professionally engaged (Rule 22); keep accounts of the client's money (Rules 26-27); shall not apply moneys received for expenses to fees and vice versa except with express instructions (Rule 27); after termination of the proceeding, is at liberty to appropriate towards the settled fee due to him any sum remaining with him out of the amount paid or sent for expenses (Rule 28) and, where the fee is unsettled, to deduct the fee payable under the Court rules and refund the balance (Rule 29); shall furnish a copy of accounts to the client on demand on payment of the cost (Rule 30); shall not enter into arrangements whereby funds in his hands are converted into loans (Rule 31); shall not lend money to his client for the purpose of any action or legal proceedings in which he is engaged (but may agree to advance court fees, expenses of witnesses and other disbursements) (Rule 32).
  4. Duty to the opponent (Section III; Rules 33-34 Verify): an advocate shall not in any way communicate or negotiate or call upon the opposite party who is represented by an advocate except through that advocate; shall do his best to carry out all legitimate promises made to the opposite party even though not reduced to writing or enforceable under the rules of the court; shall not appear for the opposite party in a case where he previously advised or acted for the other party in connection with the same matter.
  5. Duty to colleagues (Section IV; Verify · numbers): an advocate shall not solicit work or advertise, either directly or indirectly, whether by circulars, advertisements, touts, personal communications, interviews not warranted by personal relations, furnishing or inspiring newspaper comments or producing his photographs to be published in connection with cases in which he has been engaged or concerned (Rule 36); his sign-board or nameplate should be of reasonable size and shall not indicate that he is or has been president or member of a Bar Council or Association, or that he has been Government Advocate, or that he is a specialist; shall not permit his professional services or his name to be used in aid of, or to make possible, the unauthorised practice of law by any law agency (Rule 37); shall not enter appearance in any case in which there is already another advocate engaged for the party except with the consent of such advocate (or, if consent is not produced, only after giving reasons) (Rule 38-ish); shall not accept a vakalatnama without the previous advocate's fee being cleared where the earlier engagement was for the same case unless it is shown that the party has paid or the advocate has been discharged Verify. The BCI relaxed the advertising bar in 2008 to allow advocates to provide information on websites (name, contact details, area of practice, qualifications) without soliciting.
  6. Other employment (Section VII; Rules 47-52 Verify): an advocate shall not personally engage in any business; but he may be a sleeping partner in a firm doing business provided that, in the opinion of the Bar Council, the nature of the business is not inconsistent with the dignity of the profession (Rule 47); an advocate may be a Director or Chairman of the Board of Directors of a company with no objection if he does not practise as a Managing Director or Secretary (Rule 48); an advocate shall not be a full-time salaried employee of any person, Government, firm, corporation or concern so long as he continues to practise, and shall on taking up such employment intimate the fact to the Bar Council whose roll he is borne and shall thereupon cease to practise as an advocate so long as he continues in such employment (Rule 49); an advocate who is a Law Officer of the Central Government or of a State or of any public body or authority and is designated as such by the terms of his appointment and is required to act or plead in courts is exempted (Rule 50). Dr. Haniraj L. Chulani v. Bar Council of Maharashtra and Goa (1996): a person practising another profession (medicine) cannot simultaneously be enrolled; Sushma Suri v. Government (NCT of Delhi) (1999): a legal adviser or law officer who acts or pleads in courts may continue to practise; Satish Kumar Sharma v. Bar Council of H.P. (2001): a full-time salaried employee cannot practise.
  7. Fees: the fee is a matter of agreement; a contingent fee is prohibited (champerty) (Rule 20); advocates cannot claim a share in the decretal amount; a fee agreement must not be unconscionable; an advocate cannot demand fees from a client without rendering service; non-payment does not allow withholding of papers (R.D. Saxena). Retainer; refund of unearned fee (Rule 12). The Supreme Court in Re: Fees (V.C. Rangadurai) and in has said that the bar should not become a trade.
  8. Senior advocates (Part VI Chapter I): a senior advocate shall not (a) file a vakalatnama or act in any court, tribunal or before any person or authority mentioned in s.30; (b) appear without an Advocate-on-Record in the Supreme Court or without another advocate in other courts (rules for 'junior'); (c) accept instructions to appear except from an advocate; (d) accept directly from a client any brief to appear or draft pleadings; he may, however, advise on legal questions or settle drafts; he shall not do conveyancing, drafting of pleadings or affidavits (the 2023 BCI amendments relaxed these in some respects Verify); a senior advocate in the Supreme Court must have a prescribed 'practice' and can be designated by the Court under s.16.
  9. Dress (Part VI, Chapter IV): advocates appearing in the Supreme Court, High Courts, subordinate courts, tribunals and authorities: black buttoned-up coat, chapkan, achkan, black sherwani and white bands with advocates' gown; women advocates - black and full-sleeve jacket or blouse, white collar-band, saree or long skirt or churidar-kurta or salwar-kameez or traditional dress in white or black or any subdued colour; bands and gowns not to be worn in public places except courts and ceremonial occasions; advocates in summer may wear white shirt or blouse with bands (without the coat) in subordinate courts. (Regulation under s.49(1)(gg).) Neckties/ bands: rule of dress is not tested beyond these basics.
  10. Additional duties: legal aid and pro bono (Art 39A; Legal Services Authorities Act); duty to maintain the dignity of the profession; duty not to misrepresent the court; duty to disclose adverse authority and to assist the court; duty to keep client secrets (BSA s.132 - see 9.E4); duty not to disclose; duty of fairness to witnesses; duty not to coach or tamper with witnesses (misconduct under BNS ss.229-232); duty not to engage in sexual misconduct or harassment (POSH Act extends to Bar associations).

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
BCI Rule 20No contingent fee; no sharing of proceeds.
BCI Rule 36No soliciting or advertising.
BCI Rules 47-49No business; director allowed (not Managing Director); no full-time salaried employment.
BCI Rule 6Not to appear before a relative.
BCI Rule 11-12Duty to accept briefs; no withdrawal without notice.

⚖Landmark cases

V.C. Rangadurai v. D. Gopalan

1979

Duty to client; advocate cannot give away client's trust.

R.D. Saxena v. Balram Prasad Sharma

2000

No lien on client's papers; duty to return documents and account.

Dr. Haniraj L. Chulani v. Bar Council of Maharashtra and Goa

1996

Cannot practise law and medicine simultaneously; no dual enrolment.

Sushma Suri v. Government (NCT of Delhi)

1999

Law officer/ legal adviser who acts/pleads is an advocate under BCI rules.

A.S. Mohammed Rafi v. State of Tamil Nadu

2011

A Bar Association cannot ban its members from representing a party; advocates must defend even unpopular accused.

M.B. Sanghi v. High Court of Punjab and Haryana

1991

Advocate who abused a judge guilty of contempt; advocates must be respectful.

Harish Uppal v. Union of India

2002

No right to strike; remedy for clients.

✎Memory aids

  • Duties: 'C-C-O-C' - Court, Client, Opponent, Colleagues. Prohibitions: contingent fee (20), actionable claims (21), purchase at execution sale (22), advertising (36), business (47).

!Exam traps

  • TrapAdvocate may be a director of a company but not its Managing Director or Secretary.
  • TrapA full-time salaried employee must stop practising and intimate the Bar Council.
  • TrapContingent fee is prohibited, but advocates may agree a fixed fee and advance court fees (Rule 32 Explanation).
  • TrapAn advocate cannot communicate directly with a represented opponent.

?Likely questions

  1. Which BCI Rule prohibits advertising? (Rule 36)
  2. May an advocate accept a fee contingent on the result? (No - Rule 20)
  3. May an advocate appear before a judge who is his relative? (No - Rule 6)
  4. May an advocate be a full-time salaried employee while practising? (No - Rule 49)
