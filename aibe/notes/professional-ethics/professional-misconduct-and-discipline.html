iOverview

Chapter V of the Act. Know the procedure, who decides, what penalties exist, and the cases on what counts as misconduct.

★Must know

  1. s.35 - Punishment of advocates for professional or other misconduct: where, on receipt of a complaint or otherwise, a State Bar Council has reason to believe that any advocate on its roll has been guilty of professional or other misconduct, it shall refer the case for disposal to its Disciplinary Committee; the Disciplinary Committee after giving the advocate concerned and the Advocate-General of the State an opportunity of being heard may (a) dismiss the complaint or, where the proceedings were initiated at the instance of the State Bar Council, direct that the proceedings be filed; (b) reprimand the advocate; (c) suspend the advocate from practice for such period as it may deem fit; (d) remove the name of the advocate from the State roll of advocates. s.35(4): where an advocate is suspended from practice, he is debarred during the period of suspension from practising in any court or before any authority or person in India. The Bar Council of India's Disciplinary Committee has parallel powers in cases referred to it, and original powers over advocates of different States (s.36).
  2. Meaning of 'misconduct': the Act does not define it. It covers professional misconduct and 'other' misconduct (conduct unbecoming even outside professional work). Bar Council of Maharashtra v. M.V. Dabholkar (1975): misconduct includes conduct that disgraces the profession; the Bar Council is the proper body to judge; Noratanmal Chouraria v. M.R. Murli (2004): the court should presume only if misconduct is established; V.C. Rangadurai v. D. Gopalan (1979): professional misconduct arises when an advocate breaches his duty to a client; the advocate-client relationship is fiduciary; the Supreme Court upheld the Bar Council's punishment although the complainant was not the client; In re: Vinay Chandra Mishra (1995): a senior advocate was held guilty of contempt of the Supreme Court and suspended from practice for three years - the Court held it may suspend an advocate's right to practise as part of contempt jurisdiction (Art 129) and that an advocate is an officer of the court and must maintain its dignity; Pravin C. Shah v. K.A. Mohd. Ali (2001): the High Court cannot, in contempt proceedings, deprive an advocate of his right to practise (this lies exclusively with the Bar Council under s.35); the court may refer the matter to the Bar Council; Bar Council of India v. High Court of Kerala (2004): the High Court can prevent an advocate from appearing in a court by an order but has no power to debar from practice; R.D. Saxena v. Balram Prasad Sharma (2000): an advocate has no lien over papers/documents of a client for unpaid fees and cannot withhold them - failure to return is professional misconduct; the Court also held that an advocate who refuses to return documents is guilty and was suspended for six months.
  3. Illustrative misconduct under s.35 and BCI Rules: (1) negligence or non-appearance after accepting a brief (Chandra Shekhar Soni v. Bar Council of Rajasthan, 1983; Harish Chandra Tiwari v. Baiju, 2002 - gross negligence); (2) misappropriation or non-accounting of client's money (P.D. Gupta v. Ram Murti, 1997); (3) acting against a former client; (4) fraudulent or dishonest conduct or forging documents; (5) bribery, influencing a judge or presiding officer or communicating privately with the judge (BCI Rules); (6) misbehaviour with court or judge (M.B. Sanghi v. High Court of Punjab and Haryana, 1991 - advocate abusing a judge; O.P. Sharma v. High Court of Punjab and Haryana, 2011); (7) striking and abstaining from courts (Ex-Capt. Harish Uppal v. Union of India, 2002; Krishnakant Tamrakar v. State of M.P., 2018); (8) soliciting work, touting and advertising (BCI Rule 36; Re: Rule 36); (9) charging a contingent fee/champerty; (10) appearing for a party whom the advocate previously advised for the opposite party; (11) engaging in business or employment in violation of BCI Rules; (12) fabrication of evidence; (13) improper conduct in the media (R.K. Anand v. Registrar, Delhi High Court, 2009 - senior advocate suspended for four months for attempting to subvert justice in the BMW hit-and-run case (sting operation); disqualified from being designated senior advocate for some time; the Court held that professional misconduct and contempt are different); (14) assault or insulting the judge/ witness; (15) conviction for moral turpitude offence; (16) conflict of interest; (17) conduct as a Law Officer in violation of rules (Sushma Suri v. Government of NCT of Delhi, 1999 - whether a salaried legal adviser is an advocate).
  4. Standard of proof and procedure: disciplinary proceedings are quasi-judicial/ quasi-criminal; the Disciplinary Committee has the powers of a civil court for summoning, discovery and production of documents, receiving evidence on affidavits, issuing commissions (s.42); proceedings are judicial proceedings for the purposes of ss.193, 219 and 228 IPC (ss.229, 258 and 267 BNS) (s.42(2)); the complainant is not a party but his interest is considered; s.43 - costs of proceedings; s.44 - review of orders by the Disciplinary Committee (within 60 days). Procedure per Bar Council of India Rules Part VII: complaint with affidavit; reply; evidence; hearing; reasoned order; State Bar Council cannot hold proceedings in secret. Complaint may be made by a client, any person, a court (reference), or the Council itself suo motu; the Advocate-General is entitled to notice. The Disciplinary Committee should dispose of complaints within one year (s.36B).
  5. s.36B (disposal of disciplinary proceedings): the Disciplinary Committee of a State Bar Council must dispose of a complaint within one year from the date of receipt of the complaint or initiation of proceedings suo motu; failing which the proceedings stand transferred to the BCI which shall dispose of them as if originally instituted; s.36A - changes in constitution of Disciplinary Committees; s.37 - appeal to the Bar Council of India: any person aggrieved by an order of the Disciplinary Committee of a State Bar Council made under s.35 (or the Advocate-General) may within 60 days of the date of communication of the order prefer an appeal to the BCI; the appeal is heard by a Disciplinary Committee of the BCI which may dismiss, modify, or set aside, with power to enhance the punishment; s.38 - appeal to the Supreme Court: any person aggrieved by an order of the Disciplinary Committee of the BCI made under s.35 or s.37 (or the Attorney-General or Advocate-General) may within 60 days prefer an appeal to the Supreme Court; the Supreme Court may pass such order as it deems fit; s.40 - stay of order; s.41 - alteration in the roll of advocates (when a name is removed etc.).
  6. Reinstatement: an advocate removed from the roll may apply for restoration; the Disciplinary Committee may, after considering conduct, allow; s.26A (removal of names from roll) gives power to remove names of persons deceased or who have requested; the State Bar Council may restore.
  7. Remedies for clients: complaint to the Bar Council; claim of negligence before the civil court (Jacob Mathew v. State of Punjab (2005) on professional negligence principle; consumer forum - services rendered by advocates are not 'service' under the Consumer Protection Act (Bar of Indian Lawyers v. D.K. Gandhi (2024): the Supreme Court held in May 2024 that advocates/legal profession are not within the ambit of 'service' under the Consumer Protection Act 1986/2019); the earlier NCDRC decision in D.K. Gandhi PS Rao v. Bar of Indian Lawyers (2007) stayed Verify · details.

⚖Landmark cases

V.C. Rangadurai v. D. Gopalan

1979

Misconduct: advocate-client fiduciary relationship; Bar Council's role in discipline.

Bar Council of Maharashtra v. M.V. Dabholkar

1975

Bar Council's disciplinary jurisdiction; standard of conduct expected of advocates.

In re: Vinay Chandra Mishra

1995

Advocate suspended for three years for contempt of court; advocate as officer of the court.

Pravin C. Shah v. K.A. Mohd. Ali

2001

High Court cannot, in contempt, debar an advocate from practice; only the Bar Council can.

R.D. Saxena v. Balram Prasad Sharma

2000

Advocate has no lien over client's papers; non-return is misconduct.

Ex-Capt. Harish Uppal v. Union of India

2002

Lawyers have no right to strike or boycott courts; strike can be misconduct.

R.K. Anand v. Registrar, Delhi High Court

2009

Senior advocate suspended for four months for unethical conduct in the BMW trial.

P.D. Gupta v. Ram Murti

1997

Misappropriation of client's money is grave misconduct; removal from roll.

Bar of Indian Lawyers v. D.K. Gandhi PS Rao

2024

Advocates are not providers of 'service' under the Consumer Protection Act Verify.

✎Memory aids

  • Punishments under s.35: 'D-R-S-R' - Dismiss complaint, Reprimand, Suspend, Remove from roll.
  • Appeals: 60 days to BCI (s.37); 60 days to Supreme Court (s.38).

!Exam traps

  • TrapOnly the Bar Council (not the High Court) can debar an advocate; the High Court may punish for contempt.
  • TrapAn advocate does not have a lien over the client's papers.
  • TrapDisciplinary appeals go: State Bar Council DC -> BCI -> Supreme Court.
  • TrapStrike by lawyers is misconduct; protest is allowed outside court hours/ premises through peaceful means.

?Likely questions

  1. Which section of the Advocates Act punishes professional misconduct? (s.35)
  2. Appeal against the order of a State Bar Council's Disciplinary Committee lies to? (Bar Council of India under s.37)
  3. Time for appeal to Supreme Court under s.38? (60 days)
  4. Which case held that advocates have no right to strike? (Harish Uppal)
