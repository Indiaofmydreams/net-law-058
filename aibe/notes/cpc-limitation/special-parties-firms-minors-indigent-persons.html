Special Parties: Corporations, Firms, Minors and Persons of Unsound Mind, Indigent Persons (Orders XXIX, XXX, XXXII, XXXIII)

iOverview

Next friend/ guardian ad litem, compromise by guardian (Order 32 Rule 7), suits against firms in firm name (Order 30), indigent person rules (Order 33 Rule 5).

★Must know

  1. ORDER XXIX - SUITS BY OR AGAINST CORPORATIONS: Rule 1: in suits by or against a corporation, any pleading may be signed and verified on behalf of the corporation by the secretary or by any director or other principal officer of the corporation who is able to depose to the facts of the case. Rule 2: service on corporation: subject to any statutory provision regulating service of process, where the suit is against a corporation, the summons may be served (a) on the secretary, or on any director, or other principal officer of the corporation, or (b) by leaving it or sending it by post addressed to the corporation at the registered office, or if there is no registered office then at the place where the corporation carries on business. Rule 3: power to require personal appearance of officer of the corporation.
  2. ORDER XXX - SUITS BY OR AGAINST FIRMS AND PERSONS CARRYING ON BUSINESS IN NAMES OTHER THAN THEIR OWN: Rule 1: any two or more persons claiming or being liable as partners and carrying on business in India may sue or be sued in the name of the firm (if any) of which such persons were partners at the time of the accruing of the cause of action, and any party to a suit may in such case apply to the court for a statement of the names and addresses of the persons who were, at the time of the accruing of the cause of action, partners in such firm, to be furnished and verified in such manner as the court may direct. Rule 2: disclosure of partners' names - where persons sue or are sued as partners in the name of their firm, the plaintiffs or their pleader shall on demand in writing by or on behalf of any defendant, forthwith declare in writing the names and places of residence of all the persons constituting the firm. Rule 3: service - where persons are sued as partners in the name of their firm, the summons shall be served either (a) upon any one or more of the partners, or (b) at the principal place at which the partnership business is carried on within India upon any person having, at the time of service, the control or management of the partnership business there, as the court may direct, and such service shall be deemed good service upon the firm so sued, whether all or any of the partners are within or without India. Rule 4: right of suit on death of partner (it is not necessary to join the legal representatives of the deceased partner). Rule 5: notice in what capacity served - where a summons is issued to a firm and is served in the manner provided by Rule 3, every person upon whom it is served shall be informed by notice in writing given at the time of such service whether he is served as a partner or as a person having the control or management of the partnership business, or in both characters, and, in default of such notice, the person served shall be deemed to be served as a partner. Rule 6: appearance of partners individually; Rule 7: no appearance except by partners (a person served as a person in control of business cannot appear except as a partner); Rule 8: appearance under protest - any person served as a partner may appear under protest denying that he is a partner; Rule 9: suits between co-partners (the Order applies to suits between a firm and one or more of its partners and between firms having one or more common partners); Rule 10: suits against persons carrying on business in name other than their own - the person may be sued in such name. A firm is not a legal entity; a decree against a firm can be executed against the firm's property or the partners' personal property if they were served or appeared as partners (Order 21 Rule 50). Registration under the Partnership Act Sec 69 bars suits by unregistered firms for contractual rights (not applicable to suits against the firm).
  3. ORDER XXXII - SUITS BY OR AGAINST MINORS AND PERSONS OF UNSOUND MIND: Rule 1: every suit by a minor shall be instituted in his name by a person who in such suit shall be called the NEXT FRIEND of the minor. Rule 2: where a suit is instituted by or on behalf of a minor without a next friend, the defendant may apply to have the plaint taken off the file, with costs to be paid by the pleader or other person by whom it was presented; Rule 3: GUARDIAN FOR THE SUIT - where the defendant is a minor, the court, on being satisfied of the fact of his minority, shall appoint a proper person to be guardian for the suit for such minor (the court may appoint the court officer or a person who has no adverse interest); service on the guardian ad litem; a decree passed against a minor without a guardian being appointed is voidable (not a nullity) unless prejudice - Walian v Banke Behari (1903). Rule 3A: no decree shall be set aside merely on the ground that the next friend or guardian for the suit of a minor had an interest in the subject-matter of the suit adverse to that of the minor, but the fact that such next friend or guardian had such an adverse interest shall be a ground for revising the decree if the interests of the minor were prejudiced (revision/ review/ appeal). Rule 4: WHO MAY ACT AS NEXT FRIEND OR BE APPOINTED GUARDIAN FOR THE SUIT: (1) any person who is of sound mind and has attained majority may act as next friend of a minor or as his guardian for the suit, provided that the interest of such person is not adverse to that of the minor and that he is not, in the case of a next friend, a defendant, or, in the case of a guardian for the suit, a plaintiff. (2) where a minor has a guardian appointed or declared by competent authority, no person other than such guardian shall act as the next friend of the minor or be appointed his guardian for the suit unless the court considers, for reasons to be recorded, that it is for the minor's welfare that another person be permitted to act or be appointed. Rule 5: representation of minor by next friend or guardian for the suit - every application to the court on behalf of a minor other than an application under Rule 10(2) shall be made by his next friend or by his guardian for the suit; Rule 6: receipt by next friend or guardian for the suit of property under a decree for the minor - without the leave of the court; Rule 7 - AGREEMENT OR COMPROMISE BY NEXT FRIEND OR GUARDIAN FOR THE SUIT: (1) no next friend or guardian for the suit shall, without the leave of the court expressly recorded in the proceedings, enter into any agreement or compromise on behalf of a minor with reference to the suit in which he acts as next friend or guardian. (2) any such agreement or compromise entered into without the leave of the court so recorded shall be VOIDABLE against all parties other than the minor. Rule 11: removal of guardian for the suit; Rule 12: retirement/ removal/ death of next friend; Rule 15: Rules 1 to 14 apply to persons of unsound mind (and to persons who by reason of mental infirmity are incapable of protecting their interests). A minor is bound by a decree properly passed after due representation; The court must be satisfied that a compromise is for the benefit of the minor.
  4. ORDER XXXIII - SUITS BY INDIGENT PERSONS: Rule 1: subject to the following provisions, any suit may be instituted by an indigent person. Explanation I: a person is an 'indigent person' if he is not possessed of sufficient means (other than property exempt from attachment in execution of a decree and the subject-matter of the suit) to enable him to pay the fee prescribed by law for the plaint in such suit, or, where no such fee is prescribed, if he is not entitled to property worth one thousand rupees other than the property exempt from attachment in execution of a decree, and the subject-matter of the suit. Explanation II: any property which is acquired by a person after the presentation of his application for permission to sue as an indigent person, and before the decision of the application, shall be taken into account in considering the question whether or not the applicant is an indigent person. Rule 2: contents of application; Rule 3: presentation of application by the applicant in person (unless exempted); Rule 4: examination of applicant; Rule 5 - REJECTION OF APPLICATION: the court shall reject an application for permission to sue as an indigent person (a) where it is not framed and presented in the manner prescribed by Rules 2 and 3; (b) where the applicant is not an indigent person; (c) where he has, within two months next before the presentation of the application, disposed of any property fraudulently or in order to be able to apply for permission to sue as an indigent person; provided that no application shall be rejected if, even after the value of the property disposed of by the applicant is taken into account, the applicant would be entitled to sue as an indigent person; (d) where his allegations do not show a cause of action; (e) where he has entered into any agreement with reference to the subject-matter of the proposed suit under which any other person has obtained an interest in such subject-matter; (f) where the allegations made by the applicant in the application show that the suit would be barred by any law for the time being in force; (g) where any other person has entered into an agreement with him to finance the litigation. Rule 7: procedure at the hearing - the court shall examine the witnesses and decide; Rule 8: procedure if application admitted - it shall be numbered and registered and shall be deemed the plaint in the suit, and the suit shall proceed in all other respects as a suit instituted in the ordinary manner, except that the plaintiff shall not be liable to pay any court-fee (other than fees payable for service of process) in respect of any petition, appointment of a pleader or other proceeding connected with the suit; Rule 9: removal from indigency (court may order the plaintiff to pay the fee if he becomes possessed of means - Rule 9: the court may withdraw the permission to sue as an indigent person where (a) he is guilty of vexatious or improper conduct; (b) it appears that his means are such that he ought not to continue to sue as an indigent person; or (c) he has entered into an agreement with another person under which such person has obtained an interest in the subject-matter); Rule 10: costs; Rule 11: procedure where indigent person succeeds - the court shall calculate the amount of court-fees which would have been paid by the plaintiff if he had not been permitted to sue as an indigent person; such amount shall be a first charge on the subject-matter of the suit; Rule 12: where the plaintiff fails - the court shall order him to pay the court fees; Rule 15: REFUSAL TO ALLOW APPLICANT TO SUE AS INDIGENT PERSON BARS SUBSEQUENT APPLICATION OF LIKE NATURE - an order refusing to allow the applicant to sue as an indigent person shall be a bar to any subsequent application of the like nature by him in respect of the same right to sue, but the applicant shall be at liberty to institute a suit in the ordinary manner in respect of such right, provided that he first pays the costs (if any) incurred by the State Government and by the opposite party in opposing his application for leave to sue as an indigent person. Rule 18: Government may provide pleader to an unrepresented indigent person (legal aid); Order 44: appeals by indigent persons (Order 44 Rule 1: any person entitled to prefer an appeal who is unable to pay the fee required for the memorandum of appeal may present an application accompanied by a memorandum of appeal, and may be allowed to appeal as an indigent person subject to the provisions relating to suits by indigent persons).

!Exam traps

  • TrapOrder 32 Rule 7: compromise on behalf of a minor without the leave of the court is VOIDABLE against all parties other than the minor.
  • TrapOrder 32 Rule 3A: adverse interest of next friend/ guardian - a ground for revising the decree.
  • TrapOrder 33 Rule 5: application is rejected where the applicant is not indigent, no cause of action, financing by another, etc.
  • TrapOrder 30: a firm is sued in the firm's name; service on any partner or person in control suffices; Rule 8: appearance under protest.
  • TrapOrder 33 Rule 15: refusal bars a subsequent application, but not an ordinary suit upon payment of costs.

?Likely questions

  1. Suit by a minor: through a next friend (Order 32 Rule 1).
  2. Compromise on behalf of a minor needs: leave of the court (Rule 7).
  3. Indigent person: Order 33; rejection grounds: Rule 5.
  4. Suits by or against firms: Order 30.
