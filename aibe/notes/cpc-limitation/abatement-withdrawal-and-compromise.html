Death, Marriage and Insolvency of Parties; Withdrawal and Compromise (Orders XXII and XXIII)

iOverview

Abatement, substitution of legal representatives, limitation for substitution (90 days) and setting aside abatement (60 days), withdrawal with liberty (Order 23 Rule 1) and compromise decrees (Rule 3) are routine questions.

★Must know

  1. ORDER XXII Rule 1: the death of a plaintiff or defendant shall not cause the suit to abate if the right to sue survives. Rule 2: where there are more than one plaintiff or defendant, and any of them dies, and where the right to sue survives against or to the surviving defendant or defendants alone, or to the surviving plaintiff or plaintiffs alone, the court shall cause an entry to that effect to be made on the record and the suit shall proceed at the instance of the surviving plaintiff(s) or against the surviving defendant(s). Rule 3 - PROCEDURE IN CASE OF DEATH OF ONE OF SEVERAL PLAINTIFFS OR OF SOLE PLAINTIFF: (1) where one of two or more plaintiffs dies and the right to sue does not survive to the surviving plaintiff or plaintiffs alone, or a sole plaintiff or sole surviving plaintiff dies and the right to sue survives, the court, on an application made in that behalf, shall cause the legal representative of the deceased plaintiff to be made a party and shall proceed with the suit. (2) where within the time limited by law no application is made under sub-rule (1), the suit shall ABATE so far as the deceased plaintiff is concerned, and, on the application of the defendant, the court may award to him the costs which he may have incurred in defending the suit, to be recovered from the estate of the deceased plaintiff. Rule 4 - PROCEDURE IN CASE OF DEATH OF ONE OF SEVERAL DEFENDANTS OR OF SOLE DEFENDANT: (1) where one of two or more defendants dies and the right to sue does not survive against the surviving defendant or defendants alone, or a sole defendant or sole surviving defendant dies and the right to sue survives, the court, on an application made in that behalf, shall cause the legal representative of the deceased defendant to be made a party and shall proceed with the suit. (2) any person so made a party may make any defence appropriate to his character as legal representative of the deceased defendant. (3) where within the time limited by law no application is made under sub-rule (1), the suit shall abate as against the deceased defendant. (4) the court whenever it thinks fit, may exempt the plaintiff from the necessity of substituting the legal representatives of any such defendant who has failed to file a written statement or who, having filed it, has failed to appear and contest the suit at the hearing; and judgment may, in such case, be pronounced against the said defendant notwithstanding the death of such defendant and shall have the same force and effect as if it has been pronounced before death took place. (5) where the question arises as to whether any person is or is not the legal representative of a deceased plaintiff or deceased defendant, such question shall be determined by the court (Rule 5).
  2. Rule 6 - NO ABATEMENT BY REASON OF DEATH AFTER HEARING: notwithstanding anything contained in the foregoing rules, whether the cause of action survives or not, there shall be no abatement by reason of the death of either party between the conclusion of the hearing and the pronouncing of the judgment, but judgment may in such case be pronounced notwithstanding the death and shall have the same force and effect as if it had been pronounced before the death took place. Rule 7: marriage of a female party (no abatement; suit continues); Rule 8: insolvency of plaintiff - the suit shall not abate by reason only of the insolvency of the plaintiff, but the assignee or receiver may continue the suit or give security for costs; if he fails, the suit may be dismissed on the application of the defendant. RULE 9 - EFFECT OF ABATEMENT OR DISMISSAL: (1) where a suit abates or is dismissed under this Order, no fresh suit shall be brought on the same cause of action. (2) the plaintiff or the person claiming to be the legal representative of a deceased plaintiff or the assignee or the receiver in insolvency, where the suit has abated or has been dismissed under this Order, may apply for an order to set aside the abatement or dismissal; and if it is proved that he was prevented by any sufficient cause from continuing the suit, the court shall set aside the abatement or dismissal upon such terms as to costs or otherwise as it thinks fit. (3) the provisions of Sec 5 of the Limitation Act, 1963 shall apply to applications under sub-rule (2). Rule 10 - procedure in case of assignment before final order in suit: the suit may be continued by or against the person to or upon whom the interest has devolved with the leave of the court (an assignee/ transferee pendente lite); Rule 10A: duty of pleader to communicate to the court the death of a party; Rule 11: application of Order to appeals (the word 'plaintiff' includes appellant, 'defendant' includes respondent); Rule 12: Order not to apply to proceedings in execution of a decree or order (Rules 3, 4, 8 do not apply to execution proceedings).
  3. LIMITATION (Limitation Act 1963): application to implead legal representatives of a deceased plaintiff/ appellant/ defendant/ respondent - NINETY days from the date of death (Art 120); application to set aside abatement - SIXTY days from the date of abatement (Art 121) - abatement takes place on the expiry of the 90 days, automatically, without any order of the court; application for condonation of delay under Sec 5 along with Rule 9(2). Principles: (1) abatement is automatic and the suit stands abated once the period lapses; no order is needed; (2) where one of the several plaintiffs/ appellants dies and the right to sue survives to the others, there is no abatement unless the interest is joint and indivisible; (3) in JOINT AND INDIVISIBLE decree appeals, if the legal representatives of one deceased respondent are not brought on record, the entire appeal abates (State of Punjab v Nathu Ram, 1962; Sardar Amarjit Singh Kalra v Pramod Gupta, 2003: where the decree is joint and inseverable, abatement of the appeal against one respondent leads to abatement of the whole appeal; the test being whether the decree could be inconsistent or contradictory if the appeal proceeds); (4) substitution of legal heirs can be sought even after notice; the court should be liberal in setting aside abatement as the delay is for sufficient cause (Mithailal Dalsangar Singh v Annabai Devram Kini, 2003: the court should take a liberal view in setting aside abatement, since procedural technicalities should not defeat substantive rights); (5) Order 22 Rule 4 requires the legal representatives of a deceased defendant to be substituted; where the court exempts the plaintiff (Rule 4(4)); (6) The Supreme Court in a 2025 ruling explained the correct procedure for an application to substitute legal heirs, set aside the abatement and condone delay under Order XXII Rules 3, 4 and 9 (the court should ordinarily treat the application as a composite application) - verify. A decree passed against a dead person without substitution is a nullity unless Rule 4(4)/ Rule 6 applies (Order 22 Rule 4(4) cures when the defendant failed to file a written statement).
  4. ORDER XXIII - WITHDRAWAL AND ADJUSTMENT OF SUITS: Rule 1 - WITHDRAWAL OF SUIT OR ABANDONMENT OF PART OF CLAIM: (1) at any time after the institution of a suit, the plaintiff may as against all or any of the defendants abandon his suit or abandon a part of his claim; provided that where the plaintiff is a minor or other person to whom the provisions of Rules 1 to 14 of Order XXXII extend, neither the suit nor any part of the claim shall be abandoned without the leave of the court. (2) an application for leave under the proviso to sub-rule (1) shall be accompanied by an affidavit of the next friend and also, if the minor or such other person is represented by a pleader, by a certificate of the pleader to the effect that the abandonment proposed is, in his opinion, for the benefit of the minor or such other person. (3) where the court is satisfied (a) that a suit must fail by reason of some formal defect, or (b) that there are sufficient grounds for allowing the plaintiff to institute a fresh suit for the subject-matter of a suit or part of a claim, it may, on such terms as it thinks fit, grant the plaintiff permission to withdraw from such suit or such part of the claim with liberty to institute a fresh suit in respect of the subject-matter of such suit or such part of the claim. (4) where the plaintiff (a) abandons any suit or part of claim under sub-rule (1), or (b) withdraws from a suit or part of a claim without the permission referred to in sub-rule (3), he shall be liable for such costs as the court may award and shall be precluded from instituting any fresh suit in respect of such subject-matter or such part of the claim. (5) nothing in this rule shall be deemed to authorise the court to permit one of several plaintiffs to abandon a suit or part of a claim under sub-rule (1), or to withdraw, under sub-rule (3), any suit or part of a claim, without the consent of the other plaintiff. Rule 1A: when transposition of defendants as plaintiffs may be permitted. Rule 2: limitation law not affected by first suit - in any fresh suit instituted on permission granted under Rule 1, the plaintiff shall be bound by the law of limitation in the same manner as if the first suit had not been instituted. Formal defect = a defect of the nature which is not related to merits (Sec 14 Limitation Act might apply).
  5. RULE 3 - COMPROMISE OF SUIT: where it is proved to the satisfaction of the court that a suit has been adjusted wholly or in part by any lawful agreement or compromise in writing and signed by the parties, or where the defendant satisfies the plaintiff in respect of the whole or any part of the subject-matter of the suit, the court shall order such agreement, compromise or satisfaction to be recorded, and shall pass a decree in accordance therewith so far as it relates to the parties to the suit, whether or not the subject-matter of the agreement, compromise or satisfaction is the same as the subject-matter of the suit; provided that where it is alleged by one party and denied by the other that an adjustment or satisfaction has been arrived at, the court shall decide the question; but no adjournment shall be granted for the purpose of deciding the question, unless the court, for reasons to be recorded, thinks fit to grant such adjournment. Explanation: an agreement or compromise which is void or voidable under the Indian Contract Act 1872 shall not be deemed to be lawful within the meaning of this rule. RULE 3A - BAR TO SUIT: no suit shall lie to set aside a decree on the ground that the compromise on which the decree is based was not lawful. Rule 3B: no agreement or compromise in a representative suit (Sec 92 suits, Order 1 Rule 8 suits) shall be entered into without the leave of the court expressly recorded in the proceedings; and the court shall give notice to persons interested. Rule 4: proceedings in execution of decree or order not affected. Consent decree: Pushpa Devi Bhagat v Rajinder Singh (2006): (1) no appeal lies against a consent decree (Sec 96(3)); (2) the only remedy of a party who claims that the consent decree was not lawful is to approach the same court which recorded the compromise (under the proviso to Rule 3 - to contend that no compromise was arrived at); (3) no separate suit (Rule 3A); (4) Order 43 Rule 1(m) appeal lies against an order refusing to record a compromise. A consent decree operates as an estoppel; its terms are contractual; the court must be satisfied that the compromise is lawful and the parties have authority (a pleader's compromise requires express authority - Byram Pestonji Gariwala v Union Bank of India, 1992; Banwari Lal v Chando Devi, 1993: compromise in a suit by minors/ representatives - court's duty to examine). A compromise decree isn't a decision on merits so it doesn't constitute res judicata strictly but binds by estoppel.

⚖Landmark cases

State of Punjab v Nathu Ram

1962

Abatement of appeal against one respondent where the decree is joint and indivisible leads to abatement of the whole appeal.

Mithailal Dalsangar Singh v Annabai Devram Kini

2003

Liberal approach to setting aside abatement; sufficient cause.

Pushpa Devi Bhagat v Rajinder Singh

2006

Remedies against a consent/ compromise decree: no appeal, no separate suit; approach the court that recorded the compromise.

Byram Pestonji Gariwala v Union Bank of India

1992

Compromise - written, signed, lawful; authority of counsel.

!Exam traps

  • TrapSubstitution: 90 days from death; setting aside abatement: 60 days from abatement.
  • TrapAbatement is automatic; no order necessary.
  • TrapWithdrawal WITH permission and liberty (Rule 1(3)) - fresh suit allowed; withdrawal WITHOUT permission - fresh suit barred (Rule 1(4)).
  • TrapRule 3A: no suit to set aside a compromise decree; no appeal (Sec 96(3)).
  • TrapOrder 22 does not apply to execution proceedings (Rule 12).
  • TrapNo abatement if death occurs after the conclusion of the hearing (Rule 6).

?Likely questions

  1. Limitation for substitution of legal representatives: 90 days.
  2. Limitation for setting aside abatement: 60 days.
  3. Withdrawal of suit with liberty to file fresh suit: Order 23 Rule 1(3).
  4. Compromise of suit: Order 23 Rule 3; bar to suit: Rule 3A.
