Execution: General Principles, Courts and Section 47 (Secs 36-54)

iOverview

Execution is consistently among the highest-yield areas. Know which court executes (Secs 37-46), what Sec 47 covers, who is liable (Secs 49-53) and the Supreme Court's 2025-26 timelines.

★Must know

  1. EXECUTION = the enforcement of decrees and orders by process of court so as to enable the decree-holder to realise the fruits of the decree. Provisions: Sections 36-74 and Order XXI (the longest Order, with 106 Rules). Sec 36: the provisions of the Code relating to the execution of decrees (including provisions relating to payment under a decree) shall, so far as they are applicable, be deemed to apply to the execution of orders (including payment under an order). Sec 37: 'COURT WHICH PASSED A DECREE' - includes (a) where the decree to be executed has been passed in the exercise of appellate jurisdiction, the court of first instance, and (b) where the court of first instance has ceased to exist or to have jurisdiction to execute it, the court which, if the suit wherein the decree was passed were instituted at the time of making the application for the execution of the decree, would have jurisdiction to try such suit. Sec 38: COURT BY WHICH DECREE MAY BE EXECUTED - a decree may be executed either by the court which passed it, or by the court to which it is sent for execution. Sec 39: TRANSFER OF DECREE FOR EXECUTION - (1) the court which passed a decree may, on the application of the decree-holder, send it for execution to another court, of competent jurisdiction, (a) if the person against whom the decree is passed actually and voluntarily resides, or carries on business, or personally works for gain, within the local limits of the jurisdiction of such other court; or (b) if such person has not property within the local limits of the jurisdiction of the court which passed the decree sufficient to satisfy such decree and has property within the local limits of the jurisdiction of such other court; or (c) if the decree directs the sale or delivery of immovable property situate outside the local limits of the jurisdiction of the court which passed it; or (d) if the court which passed the decree considers for any other reason, which it shall record in writing, that the decree should be executed by such other court. (4) nothing in this section shall be deemed to authorise the court which passed a decree to execute such decree against any person or property outside the local limits of its jurisdiction (2002 amendment - to overcome the earlier practice). Sec 40: transfer of decree to court in another State; Sec 41: result of execution proceedings to be certified by the transferee court; Sec 42: POWERS OF COURT IN EXECUTING TRANSFERRED DECREE - the court executing a decree sent to it shall have the same powers in executing such decree as if it had been passed by itself; Sec 43: execution of decrees passed by Civil Courts in places to which the Code does not extend; Sec 44: execution of decrees passed by Revenue Courts; Sec 44A: reciprocating territories (see 1.4); Sec 45: execution of decrees outside India (the court may send the decree to a court outside India under Central Government order); Sec 46: PRECEPTS - upon the application of the decree-holder the court which passed the decree may, whenever it thinks fit, issue a precept to any other court which would be competent to execute such decree to attach any property belonging to the judgment-debtor and specified in the precept; the court shall proceed to attach the property in the manner prescribed in regard to the attachment of property in execution of a decree; but no attachment under a precept shall continue for more than TWO MONTHS unless the period of attachment is extended by an order of the court which passed the decree or unless before the determination of such attachment the decree has been transferred to the court by which the attachment has been made and the decree-holder has applied to such court for an order for the sale of such property.
  2. Sec 47 - QUESTIONS TO BE DETERMINED BY THE COURT EXECUTING DECREE: (1) all questions arising between the parties to the suit in which the decree was passed, or their representatives, and relating to the execution, discharge or satisfaction of the decree, shall be determined by the court executing the decree and not by a separate suit. [(2) omitted by the 1976 amendment]. (3) where a question arises as to whether any person is or is not the representative of a party, such question shall, for the purposes of this section, be determined by the court. Explanation I: for the purposes of this section, a plaintiff whose suit has been dismissed and a defendant against whom a suit has been dismissed are parties to the suit. Explanation II: (a) for the purposes of this section, a purchaser of property at a sale in execution of a decree shall be deemed to be a party to the suit in which the decree is passed; and (b) all questions relating to the delivery of possession of such property to such purchaser or his representative shall be deemed to be questions relating to the execution, discharge or satisfaction of the decree within the meaning of this section. ESSENTIALS: (1) the question must arise between the PARTIES to the suit or their representatives; (2) the question must relate to execution, discharge or satisfaction of the decree; (3) the question must arise in the execution proceedings. Third parties (not parties) must be dealt with under Order 21 Rules 58 (claims), 97-101 (obstruction) or by separate suits (Order 21 Rule 63). The executing court CANNOT GO BEHIND THE DECREE - it must execute the decree as it stands; it cannot question its correctness or add to or vary it (Vasudev Dhanjibhai Modi v Rajabhai Abdul Rehman, 1970; Topanmal Chhotamal v Kundomal Gangaram, 1960) - EXCEPTION: where the decree is a NULLITY (passed without inherent jurisdiction, or against a dead person, or violating a statutory prohibition), the executing court can decline execution on that ground (Sunder Dass v Ram Prakash, 1977; Rafique Bibi v Sayed Waliuddin, 2004; Hasham Abbas Sayyad v Usman Abbas Sayyad, 2007). Also: executing court can interpret an ambiguous decree; can determine whether the decree is satisfied; can decide whether the JD's property is attachable; can decide limitation for execution. Sec 47 questions are determined by the executing court even if they involve questions of title between parties (but not those between a decree-holder and a stranger). Sec 47 vs. Order 21 Rule 97-101: in a possession decree, any person (including a stranger) resisting or obstructing is dealt with under Rules 97-103; the question of the right, title and interest are to be determined by that court (Rule 101) - the Supreme Court in Periyammal v V. Rajamani (2025) emphasised these provisions to prevent multiplicity of proceedings.
  3. Sec 49: transferee - every transferee of a decree shall hold the same subject to the equities (if any) which the judgment-debtor might have enforced against the original decree-holder. Sec 50: LEGAL REPRESENTATIVE - (1) where a judgment-debtor dies before the decree has been fully satisfied, the holder of the decree may apply to the court which passed it to execute the same against the legal representative of the deceased. (2) where the decree is executed against such legal representative, he shall be liable only to the extent of the property of the deceased which has come to his hands and has not been duly disposed of; and, for the purpose of ascertaining such liability, the court executing the decree may, of its own motion or on the application of the decree-holder, compel such legal representative to produce such accounts as it thinks fit. Sec 51: POWERS OF COURT TO ENFORCE EXECUTION: subject to such conditions and limitations as may be prescribed, the court may, on the application of the decree-holder, order execution of the decree (a) by delivery of any property specifically decreed; (b) by attachment and sale or by the sale without attachment of any property; (c) by arrest and detention in prison for such period not exceeding the period specified in Sec 58, where arrest and detention is permissible under that section; (d) by appointing a receiver; or (e) in such other manner as the nature of the relief granted may require; PROVISO (2002): where the decree is for the payment of money, execution by detention in prison shall not be ordered unless, after giving the judgment-debtor an opportunity of showing cause why he should not be committed to prison, the court, for reasons recorded in writing, is satisfied (a) that the judgment-debtor, with the object or effect of obstructing or delaying the execution of the decree, (i) is likely to abscond or leave the local limits of the jurisdiction of the court, or (ii) has, after the institution of the suit in which the decree was passed, dishonestly transferred, concealed, or removed any part of his property, or committed any other act of bad faith in relation to his property, or (b) that the judgment-debtor has, or has had since the date of the decree, the means to pay the amount of the decree or some substantial part thereof and refuses or neglects or has refused or neglected to pay the same, or (c) that the decree is for a sum for which the judgment-debtor was bound in a fiduciary capacity to account. Explanation: in the calculation of the means of the JD, there shall be left out of account any property which, by or under any law or custom having the force of law for the time being in force, is exempt from attachment in execution of the decree. Sec 52: enforcement of decree against legal representative - where a decree is passed against a party as the legal representative of a deceased person, and the decree is for the payment of money out of the property of the deceased, it may be executed by the attachment and sale of any such property; and if no such property remains in the possession of the JD and he fails to satisfy the court that he has duly applied such property, the decree may be executed against the JD to the extent of the property in respect of which he has failed so to satisfy the court in the same manner as if the decree had been against him personally. Sec 53: liability of ancestral property in hands of son or other descendant - for the purposes of Secs 50 and 52, property in the hands of a son or other descendant which is liable under Hindu law to be attached and sold in execution of a decree against his father, grandfather or great-grandfather, shall be deemed to be property of the deceased which has come to the hands of the son or other descendant as his legal representative. Sec 54: partition of estates or separation of share (execution of decrees for partition of revenue-paying estates by the Collector).
  4. WHO CAN EXECUTE: the decree-holder, his legal representative or transferee (Order 21 Rule 16 - transfer of decree: assignee may apply for execution with notice to the transferor and JD); WHO CAN BE PROCEEDED AGAINST: the judgment-debtor and his legal representative. Joint decree-holders: Order 21 Rule 15 - any one of the joint decree-holders may apply for execution of the whole decree for the benefit of all, if the decree is joint; the court may impose conditions. Execution of a decree against a firm: Order 21 Rule 50. LIMITATION FOR EXECUTION: Art 136 of the Limitation Act 1963 - TWELVE YEARS from the date when the decree or order becomes enforceable (or, where the decree directs payment at a certain date, from that date); for a decree of any civil court (not being a decree granting a mandatory injunction); successive execution applications may be filed within the 12 years; where the decree directs payment at a certain date or on certain conditions, time runs from the date when it becomes enforceable. Sec 48 CPC (the earlier 12-year bar and 'new application after 12 years'), was repealed in 1976. A decree for mandatory injunction: 3 years (Art 135). Execution proceedings are not a suit (Sec 141 CPC applies the CPC provisions to proceedings other than suits as far as possible). Execution proceedings continue as a continuation of the suit (Sec 141). Res judicata applies to execution proceedings (Explanation VII to Sec 11).
  5. RECENT SUPREME COURT DIRECTIONS ON EXECUTION (2025-26): PERIYAMMAL (dead) through LRs v V. RAJAMANI (2025 INSC 329, 6 March 2025): the Supreme Court, alarmed by the pendency of execution petitions, directed all High Courts to ensure that execution petitions already pending (as at March 2025) are disposed of within SIX MONTHS and that all executing courts dispose of execution proceedings within six months of the date of filing, extendable only for reasons recorded in writing; the presiding officer of the executing court is answerable for default; the Supreme Court issued guidelines including that executing courts should not allow withdrawal and refiling of execution petitions without valid reasons (Nov 2025), that the decree-holder be put in possession without delay, and that High Courts collect and submit data on pendency; further directions on monitoring and periodic reporting by the High Courts were issued in later hearings (verify the details). By 2026 the number of pending execution petitions stood at approximately 8 lakh (a reported 7.96 lakh as of April 2026; the Court in 2026 expressed concern that merely seeking figures every six months was inadequate). The Court has repeatedly cited Rahul S. Shah v Jinendra Kumar Gandhi (2021) (guidelines for execution - the executing court to examine the JD on oath regarding assets under Order 21 Rule 41, direct disclosure by affidavit of assets, and dispose of execution within six months) and held that Order 21 Rules 97-101 and Sec 47 together enable the executing court to deal with obstruction by third parties and resolve all issues in one go. These are important 'current affairs' hooks.

⚖Landmark cases

Vasudev Dhanjibhai Modi v Rajabhai Abdul Rehman

1970

Executing court cannot go behind the decree; only a nullity can be challenged in execution.

Sunder Dass v Ram Prakash

1977

A decree passed without jurisdiction is a nullity and can be declined execution.

Rahul S. Shah v Jinendra Kumar Gandhi

2021

Guidelines for speedy execution - examine the JD on assets; dispose within six months.

Periyammal v V. Rajamani

2025 INSC 329

Execution petitions to be disposed of within six months; presiding officers accountable; High Courts to monitor.

!Exam traps

  • TrapSec 47(2) was OMITTED in 1976; Sec 47(1) and (3) remain.
  • TrapExecuting court cannot go behind the decree unless the decree is a nullity.
  • TrapSec 46 precept attachment lasts TWO months unless extended.
  • TrapSec 39(4): the transferring court cannot execute outside its local limits.
  • TrapLimitation for execution: 12 years (Art 136).
  • TrapSec 51(c) proviso: arrest of JD for money decree only on grounds specified.

?Likely questions

  1. Questions relating to execution, discharge or satisfaction of the decree are determined by: the executing court (Sec 47).
  2. Court which passed a decree can transfer it for execution: Sec 39.
  3. Precept: Sec 46; attachment valid for 2 months.
  4. Execution petitions to be disposed within six months: Periyammal v V. Rajamani (2025).
  5. Limitation for execution of a decree: 12 years.
