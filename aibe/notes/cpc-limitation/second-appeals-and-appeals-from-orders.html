Second Appeals (Secs 100-103) and Appeals from Orders (Secs 104-106, Order XLIII)

iOverview

Substantial question of law and its tests; Sec 100A; and the list of appealable orders.

★Must know

  1. SEC 100 - SECOND APPEAL: (1) unless otherwise expressly provided in the body of this Code or by any other law for the time being in force, an appeal shall lie to the High Court from every decree passed in appeal by any court subordinate to the High Court, if the High Court is satisfied that the case involves a SUBSTANTIAL QUESTION OF LAW. (2) an appeal may lie under this section from an appellate decree passed ex parte. (3) in an appeal under this section, the memorandum of appeal shall precisely state the substantial question of law involved in the appeal. (4) where the High Court is satisfied that a substantial question of law is involved in any case, it shall formulate that question. (5) the appeal shall be heard on the question so formulated and the respondent shall, at the hearing of the appeal, be allowed to argue that the case does not involve such question; provided that nothing in this sub-section shall be deemed to take away or abridge the power of the court to hear, for reasons to be recorded, the appeal on any other substantial question of law, not formulated by it, if it is satisfied that the case involves such question. Sec 101: no second appeal shall lie except on the ground mentioned in Sec 100. Sec 102: NO SECOND APPEAL IN CERTAIN SUITS - no second appeal shall lie from any decree, when the subject-matter of the original suit is for recovery of money not exceeding TWENTY-FIVE THOUSAND rupees (as amended in 2002). Sec 103: POWER OF HIGH COURT TO DETERMINE ISSUES OF FACT - in any second appeal, the High Court may, if the evidence on the record is sufficient, determine any issue necessary for the disposal of the appeal, (a) which has not been determined by the lower appellate court or both by the court of first instance and the lower appellate court, or (b) which has been wrongly determined by such court or courts by reason of a decision on such question of law as is referred to in Sec 100. Limitation: NINETY days (Art 116).
  2. SUBSTANTIAL QUESTION OF LAW: not defined in the Code. Tests: Sir Chunilal V. Mehta & Sons Ltd v Century Spinning & Manufacturing Co (1962): the proper test for determining whether a question of law raised in the case is substantial would be whether it is of general public importance or whether it directly and substantially affects the rights of the parties and if so whether it is either an open question in the sense that it is not finally settled by this Court or by the Privy Council or by the Federal Court or is not free from difficulty or calls for discussion of alternative views. Santosh Hazari v Purushottam Tiwari (2001): a point of law which admits of no two opinions may be a proposition of law but cannot be a substantial question of law; the High Court must formulate the substantial question of law at the time of admission; and the proposition must be 'substantial' - a question is substantial if it is debatable, not previously settled, and has a material bearing on the decision of the case. Hero Vinoth v Seshammal (2006): the High Court cannot interfere with findings of fact; concurrent findings of fact are not to be disturbed unless perverse, based on no evidence or on misreading/ misappreciation of evidence, or contrary to law; Kondiba Dagadu Kadam v Savitribai Sopan Gujar (1999): the High Court in second appeal cannot re-appreciate evidence merely because another view is possible; Ramchandra v Ramalingam (1963); Municipal Committee Hoshiarpur v Punjab State Electricity Board (2010): when the High Court can interfere in second appeal; Gurdev Kaur v Kaki (2007): HC's function is to ensure the correct application of law, not to be a third fact-finding court. In 2026 the Supreme Court again set aside a High Court's interference with concurrent findings of fact in a second appeal (2026 INSC 900 - verify). A finding of fact based on inadmissible evidence or ignoring material evidence is a question of law (misreading of documents, wrong burden of proof, perverse findings are questions of law). Sec 100 conditions are mandatory; a second appeal not on a substantial question of law is incompetent; a decree upon a compromise isn't appealable (Sec 96(3)).
  3. SEC 100A - NO FURTHER APPEAL IN CERTAIN CASES (inserted 2002): notwithstanding anything contained in any Letters Patent for any High Court or in any other instrument having the force of law or in any other law for the time being in force, where any appeal from an original or appellate decree or order is heard and decided by a Single Judge of a High Court, no further appeal shall lie from the judgment, decision or order of such Single Judge in such appeal or from any decree passed in such appeal (it ousts the Letters Patent appeal against the Single Judge's decision in a first or second appeal from an order/ decree; Sec 100A applies only where the Single Judge's decision is in an appeal from decree/ order (not in original jurisdiction)). Sec 100A was upheld in Salem Advocate Bar Association (2003, 'Salem I') and Sec 100A didn't bar a Letters Patent appeal against a Single Judge's decision in exercise of original jurisdiction or in writ jurisdiction (Subal Paul v Malina Paul, 2003 - Letters Patent appeal against writ orders unaffected). Letters Patent appeals: Clause 15 (Calcutta, Bombay, Madras) / Clause 10 (Allahabad, Patna, etc.) - an appeal lies from a Judge of the High Court to a Bench of two judges from the 'judgment'; limitation: 30 days (Art 117).
  4. SEC 104 - ORDERS FROM WHICH APPEAL LIES: (1) an appeal shall lie from the following orders, and save as otherwise expressly provided in the body of this Code or by any law for the time being in force, from no other orders: (ff) an order under Sec 35A; (g) an order under Sec 95; (h) an order under any of the provisions of this Code imposing a fine or directing the arrest or detention in civil prison of any person except where such arrest or detention is in execution of a decree; (i) any order made under rules from which an appeal is expressly allowed by rules. (2) no appeal shall lie from any order passed in appeal under this section. Sec 105: OTHER ORDERS: (1) save as otherwise expressly provided, no appeal shall lie from any order made by a court in the exercise of its original or appellate jurisdiction; but where a decree is appealed from, any error, defect or irregularity in any order, affecting the decision of the case, may be set forth as a ground of objection in the memorandum of appeal. (2) notwithstanding anything contained in sub-section (1), where any party aggrieved by an order of remand from which an appeal lies does not appeal therefrom, he shall thereafter be precluded from disputing its correctness. Sec 106: WHAT COURTS TO HEAR APPEALS - where an appeal from any order is allowed it shall lie to the court to which an appeal would lie from the decree in the suit in which such order was made, or where such order is made by a court (not being a High Court) in the exercise of appellate jurisdiction, then to the High Court. ORDER XLIII RULE 1 - APPEALS FROM ORDERS (key items): (a) an order under Rule 10 of Order VII returning a plaint to be presented to the proper court except where the procedure specified in Rule 10A of Order VII has been followed; (c) an order under Rule 9 of Order IX rejecting an application (in a case open to appeal) for an order to set aside the dismissal of a suit; (d) an order under Rule 13 of Order IX rejecting an application (in a case open to appeal) for an order to set aside a decree passed ex parte; (f) an order under Rule 21 of Order XI; (j) an order under Rule 72 or Rule 92 of Order XXI setting aside or refusing to set aside a sale; (k) an order under Rule 10 of Order XXII giving or refusing to give leave; (m) an order under Rule 2 of Order XXV rejecting an application (in a case open to appeal) for an order to set aside the dismissal of a suit; (n) an order under Rule 5 or Rule 7 of Order XXXIII rejecting an application for permission to sue as an indigent person; (p) orders in interpleader-suits under Rule 3, Rule 4 or Rule 6 of Order XXXV; (q) an order under Rule 2, Rule 3 or Rule 6 of Order XXXVIII (arrest/ attachment before judgment); (r) an order under Rule 1, Rule 2, Rule 2A, Rule 4 or Rule 10 of Order XXXIX (temporary injunctions, disobedience, etc.); (s) an order under Rule 1 or Rule 4 of Order XL (receiver); (t) an order under Rule 16 of Order XLI (return of appeal memorandum); (u) an order under Rule 23 or Rule 23A of Order XLI remanding a case, where an appeal would lie from the decree of the appellate court; (w) an order under Rule 4 of Order XLVII granting an application for review. Order 43 Rule 2: procedure - Order 41 rules apply to appeals from orders so far as may be. NOT appealable: rejection of an application under Order 7 Rule 11 (not a decree - revision only), order granting/ refusing amendment, order on admissibility of documents, interlocutory orders on adjournments, transfer orders, order rejecting application to implead under Order 1 Rule 10 (no appeal; revision/ Art 227), order refusing to frame an additional issue.
  5. APPEALS TO THE SUPREME COURT: Sec 109 - when appeals lie to the Supreme Court: subject to the provisions in Chapter IV of Part V of the Constitution and such rules as may, from time to time, be made by the Supreme Court regarding appeals from the Courts of India, and to the provisions hereinafter contained, an appeal shall lie to the Supreme Court from any judgment, decree or final order in a civil proceeding of a High Court if the High Court certifies under Art 134A (a) that the case involves a substantial question of law of general importance; and (b) that in the opinion of the High Court the said question needs to be decided by the Supreme Court. Sec 110 (omitted). Sec 112: savings - nothing in this Code shall be deemed to affect the powers of the Supreme Court under Art 136 or any other provision of the Constitution (special leave). Order XLV: appeals to the Supreme Court - Rule 1: definition of 'decree'; Rule 3: certificate by the High Court; Rule 7: admission of appeal... Art 133 (appeal in civil matters with certificate), Art 132 (appeals involving substantial question of law as to interpretation of the Constitution), Art 136 (special leave to appeal against any judgment, decree, determination, sentence or order passed by any court or tribunal - no appeal as of right but discretionary); limitation for SLP: 90 days from the date of the judgment of the High Court (60 days from the date of refusal of the certificate under Art 134A); Art 137: review of Supreme Court judgments; Art 142: complete justice. Order 44: appeals by indigent persons.

⚖Landmark cases

Sir Chunilal V. Mehta & Sons v Century Spinning & Manufacturing Co

1962

Test of a 'substantial question of law'.

Santosh Hazari v Purushottam Tiwari

2001

High Court must formulate the substantial question of law; scope of second appeal; concurrent findings.

Kondiba Dagadu Kadam v Savitribai Sopan Gujar

1999

High Court cannot re-appreciate evidence in a second appeal.

Subal Paul v Malina Paul

2003

Sec 100A doesn't bar Letters Patent appeals against Single Judge's writ decisions.

!Exam traps

  • TrapSecond appeal lies ONLY on a substantial question of law, which must be formulated by the High Court (Sec 100(4)).
  • TrapSec 102: no second appeal in money suits up to Rs 25,000 (small value).
  • TrapSec 100A: no further appeal against the Single Judge's decision in a first or second appeal from decree/ order.
  • TrapOrders are appealable only if listed in Sec 104/ Order 43 Rule 1.
  • TrapOrder 43 Rule 1(w): order granting review is appealable; order refusing review is not.
  • TrapRejection of an application under Order 7 Rule 11: no appeal (not a decree).

?Likely questions

  1. Second appeal: Sec 100 - substantial question of law.
  2. Which case laid down the test for 'substantial question of law'? Sir Chunilal V. Mehta.
  3. No second appeal in small suits up to Rs 25,000: Sec 102.
  4. Appeal from an order refusing a temporary injunction: Order 43 Rule 1(r).
  5. Sec 100A: no Letters Patent appeal.
