1

Scheme of the Code and Key Definitions (Section 2)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Shah Babulal Khimji v Jayaben D. Kania 1981 'Judgment' under Letters Patent - intermediary/ interlocutory judgments; test of finality of the right of a party.
  • Hansraj Gupta v Dehra Dun-Mussoorie Electric Tramway 1933, PC A suit is a civil proceeding commenced by the presentation of a plaint.
  • Hasham Abbas Sayyad v Usman Abbas Sayyad 2007 A decree passed without inherent jurisdiction is a nullity and can be challenged even in execution.

Exam traps

  • TrapRejection of a plaint IS a decree (deemed); dismissal for default is NOT a decree.
  • TrapOrders under Order 21 Rules 98 and 100 are treated as decrees (Rule 103); since the 1976 amendment, 'determination of any question within Sec 47' is no longer part of the definition of 'decree'.
  • TrapA preliminary decree cannot be an 'order'; orders are never preliminary.
  • TrapMesne profits exclude profits due to improvements by the wrongdoer; they include interest.

Jurisdiction of Civil Courts and Place of Suing (Secs 9, 15-25)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Dhulabhai v State of MP 1968 Principles on exclusion of civil courts' jurisdiction by statute.
  • Kiran Singh v Chaman Paswan 1954 Decree passed without jurisdiction is a nullity; Sec 11 of the Suits Valuation Act.
  • Hakam Singh v Gammon (India) Ltd 1971 Parties may choose one among several competent courts; ouster of all courts is void.
  • A.B.C. Laminart v A.P. Agencies 1989 Exclusive jurisdiction clause; cause of action principles.
  • Swastik Gases v Indian Oil Corporation 2013 'Subject to ... jurisdiction' clause read as exclusive.
  • Maneka Sanjay Gandhi v Rani Jethmalani 1979 Transfer petitions under Sec 25 - ends of justice; apprehension of unfair trial.

Exam traps

  • TrapObjection to territorial and pecuniary jurisdiction: waived unless taken at the earliest possible opportunity, before settlement of issues, and failure of justice shown (Sec 21). Subject-matter jurisdiction: cannot be waived.
  • TrapSec 16 proviso: suits for relief obtainable by personal obedience may also be filed where the defendant resides.
  • TrapSec 19 gives the plaintiff an option; Sec 20 is the residuary provision (defendant's residence or cause of action).
  • TrapA corporation carries on business at its principal office and, for causes arising there, also at a subordinate office (Explanation to Sec 20).
  • TrapSec 24: High Court or District Court; Sec 25: only the Supreme Court.
3

Res Sub Judice (Sec 10), Res Judicata (Sec 11) and Constructive Res Judicata

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Daryao v State of UP 1961 Res judicata applies to writ petitions under Art 32 on the same facts.
  • Satyadhyan Ghosal v Deorajin Debi 1960 Res judicata applies to different stages of the same proceeding; policy - finality.
  • Forward Construction Co v Prabhat Mandal 1986 Constructive res judicata: matters that might and ought to have been raised.
  • Sheodan Singh v Daryao Kunwar 1966 Where the appeal is dismissed on a preliminary ground, the decision on merits by the trial court still operates; conditions for res judicata with two courts.
  • Mathura Prasad Bajoo Jaiswal v Dossibai N.B. Jeejeebhoy 1970 Pure question of law unrelated to rights of parties does not operate as res judicata.
  • National Institute of Mental Health v C. Parameshwara 2005 Test for Sec 10: whether decision in the first suit would operate as res judicata in the second.
  • B.S. Lalitha v Bhuvanesh 2026 INSC 499 Interlocutory res judicata - second Order VII Rule 11 application on the same ground barred.

Memory aids

  • Res judicata conditions = M-P-T-C-D: Matter, Parties, Title, Competent court, Decided finally.

Exam traps

  • TrapRes sub judice bars TRIAL (stay), not INSTITUTION; res judicata bars trial of suit/ issue.
  • TrapConstructive res judicata (Explanation IV) covers grounds that MIGHT AND OUGHT to have been taken.
  • TrapPure question of law (no connection with rights) does not operate as res judicata.
  • TrapSec 11 applies to execution proceedings (Explanation VII).
  • TrapPendency of a suit in a FOREIGN court does not bar an Indian court (Explanation to Sec 10).

Foreign Courts, Foreign Judgments and Foreign States (Secs 13, 14, 44A, 83-87B)

Medium-high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • R. Viswanathan v Rukn-ul-Mulk Syed Abdul Wajid 1963 Competence of a foreign court; jurisdiction in personam; Sec 13.
  • Y. Narasimha Rao v Y. Venkata Lakshmi 1991 Recognition of foreign matrimonial judgments.
  • Moloji Nar Singh Rao v Shankar Saran 1962 Appearance under protest is not submission to jurisdiction.
  • Alcon Electronics v Celem SA 2017 Enforcement of foreign decrees: reciprocating vs non-reciprocating territory.

Memory aids

  • Sec 13 exceptions = C-M-I-N-F-B.

Exam traps

  • TrapSec 44A: only decrees of SUPERIOR courts of RECIPROCATING territories can be executed directly; USA decrees need a fresh suit.
  • TrapForeign arbitral awards do not come under Sec 44A (Arbitration Act Part II).
  • TrapSec 13 applies to 'conclusive' foreign judgments - it is a rule of res judicata with six exceptions.
  • TrapSec 86 requires Central Government consent for suing a foreign State.

Suits by or against the Government and Public Officers (Secs 79-82, Order XXVII)

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Bihari Chowdhary v State of Bihar 1984 Sec 80 is mandatory; intended to give Government an opportunity to settle claims.
  • State of Andhra Pradesh v Pioneer Builders 2006 Notice under Sec 80 - mandatory; applies subject to Sec 80(3) on substantial compliance.
  • Ghanshyam Dass v Dominion of India 1984 Strict compliance with Sec 80; notice must be given to the right authority.

Exam traps

  • TrapSec 80 notice: two months; suit against Government or public officer for official acts; Sec 80(2) allows urgent relief suits with leave.
  • TrapSec 80(3): errors in notice are not fatal if the plaintiff and cause of action are identifiable.
  • TrapSec 82: decree against Government cannot be executed until three months from decree.
  • TrapSuits by the Government do not need notice.
  • TrapOrder 27A: notice to Attorney-General/ Advocate-General on constitutional questions.

Institution of Suit, Plaint, Return and Rejection of Plaint (Sec 26, Orders IV and VII)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • T. Arivandandam v T.V. Satyapal 1977 Court must read the plaint meaningfully; if it is manifestly vexatious and meritless, reject it at the first hearing.
  • Dahiben v Arvindbhai Kalyanji Bhanusali 2020 Scope of Order VII Rule 11; plaint read as a whole; limitation as mixed question.
  • Saleem Bhai v State of Maharashtra 2003 Only the plaint and documents filed with it are examined under Rule 11.
  • Sejal Glass Ltd v Navilan Merchants 2018 No partial rejection of plaint under Order VII Rule 11.
  • B.S. Lalitha v Bhuvanesh 2026 INSC 499 A second Order VII Rule 11 application on the same issue is barred by interlocutory res judicata.

Exam traps

  • TrapRejection of plaint: a DECREE (appealable); order refusing to reject: not a decree, not appealable.
  • TrapUnder Rule 11 the court looks only at the PLAINT - not at the written statement.
  • TrapPartial rejection of a plaint is not allowed.
  • TrapRes judicata is generally NOT a ground under Rule 11(d); a limitation bar apparent on the plaint's face is.
  • TrapReturn of plaint: appeal lies under Order 43 Rule 1(a), unless Rule 10A procedure was followed.
  • TrapOrder 7 Rule 6: ground for exemption from limitation must be stated in the plaint.

Parties to Suits and Joinder (Order I, Order II) and Representative Suits

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Udit Narain Singh Malpaharia v Additional Member, Board of Revenue 1963 Distinction between necessary and proper parties; two tests for necessary party.
  • Mumbai International Airport v Regency Convention Centre 2010 Plaintiff is dominus litis; party can be added only if necessary or proper; scope of Order 1 Rule 10(2).
  • Gurbux Singh v Bhooralal 1964 Order 2 Rule 2: conditions for the bar; defendant must prove the earlier plaint and the same cause of action.
  • Kasturi v Iyyamperumal 2005 Who is a necessary party in a suit for specific performance.
  • Janki Vashdeo Bhojwani v Indusind Bank 2005 Power of attorney holder's limits in giving evidence.

Exam traps

  • TrapNon-joinder of a NECESSARY party is fatal; of a proper party is not.
  • TrapMisjoinder/ non-joinder objections must be taken before settlement of issues (Rule 13).
  • TrapOrder 2 Rule 2 covers omission of RELIEFS and CLAIMS of the same cause of action; the bar applies to a subsequent SUIT.
  • TrapRepresentative suit under Order 1 Rule 8 needs court's permission AND notice to all interested.
  • TrapProceedings against an added defendant are deemed to begin on service of summons (limitation).

Summons, Service, Written Statement, Set-off and Counter-claim (Sec 27, Orders V and VIII)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Kailash v Nanhku 2005 The 90-day period in Order 8 Rule 1 proviso is directory in ordinary suits.
  • SCG Contracts India v K.S. Chamankar Infrastructure 2019 In commercial suits the 120-day outer limit for the written statement is mandatory.
  • Desh Raj v Balkishan 2020 Time limits for the written statement; courts should not extend liberally - importance of expeditious trial.
  • Balraj Taneja v Sunil Madan 1999 Court cannot pass a decree mechanically when no written statement is filed; it must examine the plaint.
  • Ramesh Chand Ardawatiya v Anil Panjwani 2003 Counter-claim may be allowed after the written statement but before issues are framed.

Exam traps

  • TrapWritten statement: 30 days + up to 90 days (ordinary suits; extension beyond is directory); 120 days (commercial suits; mandatory).
  • TrapRule 5: allegations not specifically denied are deemed admitted.
  • TrapRule 2: defences like fraud, limitation, release must be specifically pleaded.
  • TrapCounter-claim can survive dismissal of the plaintiff's suit (Rule 6D).
  • TrapSubstituted service: Order 5 Rule 20; service on an adult member of family (not a servant): Rule 15.

Pleadings: Fundamental Rules, Amendment and Verification (Order VI)

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Revajeetu Builders & Developers v Narayanaswamy & Sons 2009 Detailed principles for allowing amendments.
  • Vidyabai v Padmalatha 2009 Due diligence; meaning of 'commencement of trial' for Order 6 Rule 17 proviso.
  • Modi Spinning & Weaving Mills v Ladha Ram & Co 1976 An admission cannot be withdrawn by way of amendment.
  • Udhav Singh v Madhav Rao Scindia 1977 Meaning of material facts in pleadings.
  • B.K. Narayana Pillai v Parameswaran Pillai 2000 Liberal approach to amendment to decide the real controversy.

Exam traps

  • TrapPleadings contain MATERIAL FACTS - not evidence and not law.
  • TrapOrder 6 Rule 17 proviso: no amendment after commencement of trial unless due diligence shown.
  • TrapOrder 6 Rule 18: amendment to be carried out within the time fixed or 14 days.
  • TrapDeparture (Rule 7): no new ground except by amendment.
  • TrapOrder 6 Rule 4: particulars of fraud, undue influence, misrepresentation must be specifically pleaded.

Appearance, Non-appearance and Ex Parte Decrees (Order IX)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Sangram Singh v Election Tribunal, Kotah 1955 Defendant appearing without written statement may still participate; principles of ex parte procedure.
  • Arjun Singh v Mohindra Kumar 1964 Order 9 Rule 7 - defendant's appearance and good cause for previous non-appearance.
  • Bhanu Kumar Jain v Archana Kumar 2005 Remedies against ex parte decree: appeal and Rule 13; scope of appeal.

Exam traps

  • TrapDismissal under Rule 3 does NOT bar a fresh suit; dismissal under Rule 8 DOES (Rule 9).
  • TrapRule 13 limitation: 30 days from decree (or knowledge if summons not duly served).
  • TrapIrregularity in service is not enough if defendant had notice (Proviso 2).
  • TrapAfter an appeal against the ex parte decree is decided on merits, Rule 13 application does not lie.
  • TrapRule 5: seven days to apply for fresh summons.

From First Hearing to Judgment: Orders X to XX (Case Management and Trial)

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Salem Advocate Bar Association v Union of India 2005 Upheld the 2002 amendments including evidence on affidavit and Sec 89; guidelines for adjournments and commissioners.
  • Karam Kapahi v Lal Chand Public Charitable Trust 2010 Judgment on admission - the admission must be clear and unambiguous; Order 12 Rule 6 is discretionary.
  • Major S.S. Khanna v Brig F.J. Dillon 1964 Preliminary issues under Order 14 Rule 2 - only jurisdiction and statutory bar issues are tried first.
  • Shiv Cotex v Tirgun Auto Plast 2011 Adjournments - court's duty to discourage unnecessary adjournments.
  • Vadiraj Naggappa Vernekar v Sharadchandra Prabhakar Gogate 2009 Power to recall a witness is not to be used to fill lacunae.

Exam traps

  • TrapAdjournments: maximum THREE to a party (Order 17 Rule 1).
  • TrapJudgment: ordinarily within 30 days of hearing; extendable to 60 days in exceptional cases (Order 20 Rule 1).
  • TrapDecree to be drawn up within 15 days (Order 20 Rule 6A).
  • TrapExamination-in-chief is on AFFIDAVIT (Order 18 Rule 4); cross-examination before the court/ Commissioner.
  • TrapPreliminary issue under Order 14 Rule 2: only jurisdiction/ bar of law.
  • TrapMesne profits under Order 20 Rule 12: up to three years after the decree.

Supplemental Proceedings and Interim Reliefs (Secs 94-95, Orders XXXVIII, XXXIX, XL, XXVI)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Dalpat Kumar v Prahlad Singh 1992 The triple test: prima facie case, balance of convenience and irreparable injury.
  • Gujarat Bottling Co v Coca Cola Co 1995 Interim injunction principles; protection of the defendant by undertaking/ security.
  • Wander Ltd v Antox India 1990 Limited interference by appellate court with discretionary interim orders.
  • Raman Tech & Process Engineering Co v Solanki Traders 2008 Attachment before judgment is a drastic remedy to be exercised sparingly.
  • Morgan Stanley Mutual Fund v Kartick Das 1994 Ex parte injunctions - exceptional cases and reasons recorded.
  • T. Krishnaswamy Chetty v C. Thangavelu Chetty 1955 Appointment of receiver is a harsh remedy.

Exam traps

  • TrapOrder 39 Rule 2A: detention up to THREE months; attachment valid for ONE year.
  • TrapOrder 39 Rule 3A: ex parte injunction to be decided within THIRTY days.
  • TrapOrder 38 Rule 5(4): an attachment before judgment without compliance with Rule 5(1) is void.
  • TrapSec 95 compensation: not exceeding Rs 50,000 or pecuniary limits; no separate suit.
  • TrapLocal commissioner cannot collect evidence.

Summary Procedure and Commercial Summary Judgment (Order XXXVII, Order XIII-A)

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • IDBI Trusteeship Services v Hubtown Ltd 2017 Principles for granting or refusing leave to defend in summary suits.
  • Mechelec Engineers & Manufacturers v Basic Equipment Corporation 1976 Original summary suit principles on triable issues and conditional leave.
  • B.L. Kashyap & Sons v JMS Steels & Power Corporation 2022 Scope of Order 37 - liquidated demand; written contracts.

Exam traps

  • TrapOrder 37: appearance within 10 days of service of summons; leave to defend within 10 days of service of summons for judgment.
  • TrapWhere part of the claim is admitted, the admitted amount must be deposited.
  • TrapOrder 37 is for 'liquidated demand' - not for unliquidated damages.
  • TrapOrder 13A is under the Commercial Courts Act - 'no real prospect of succeeding'.
  • TrapRule 4: decree can be set aside under special circumstances.

Death, Marriage and Insolvency of Parties; Withdrawal and Compromise (Orders XXII and XXIII)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • State of Punjab v Nathu Ram 1962 Abatement of appeal against one respondent where the decree is joint and indivisible leads to abatement of the whole appeal.
  • Mithailal Dalsangar Singh v Annabai Devram Kini 2003 Liberal approach to setting aside abatement; sufficient cause.
  • Pushpa Devi Bhagat v Rajinder Singh 2006 Remedies against a consent/ compromise decree: no appeal, no separate suit; approach the court that recorded the compromise.
  • Byram Pestonji Gariwala v Union Bank of India 1992 Compromise - written, signed, lawful; authority of counsel.

Exam traps

  • TrapSubstitution: 90 days from death; setting aside abatement: 60 days from abatement.
  • TrapAbatement is automatic; no order necessary.
  • TrapWithdrawal WITH permission and liberty (Rule 1(3)) - fresh suit allowed; withdrawal WITHOUT permission - fresh suit barred (Rule 1(4)).
  • TrapRule 3A: no suit to set aside a compromise decree; no appeal (Sec 96(3)).
  • TrapOrder 22 does not apply to execution proceedings (Rule 12).
  • TrapNo abatement if death occurs after the conclusion of the hearing (Rule 6).

Special Parties: Corporations, Firms, Minors and Persons of Unsound Mind, Indigent Persons (Orders XXIX, XXX, XXXII, XXXIII)

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Exam traps

  • TrapOrder 32 Rule 7: compromise on behalf of a minor without the leave of the court is VOIDABLE against all parties other than the minor.
  • TrapOrder 32 Rule 3A: adverse interest of next friend/ guardian - a ground for revising the decree.
  • TrapOrder 33 Rule 5: application is rejected where the applicant is not indigent, no cause of action, financing by another, etc.
  • TrapOrder 30: a firm is sued in the firm's name; service on any partner or person in control suffices; Rule 8: appearance under protest.
  • TrapOrder 33 Rule 15: refusal bars a subsequent application, but not an ordinary suit upon payment of costs.

Public Nuisance and Public Charities (Secs 91-93), Interpleader (Sec 88, Order XXXV), Mortgage Suits (Order XXXIV) and Other Special Orders

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Swami Paramatmanand Saraswati v Ramji Tripathi 1974 Scope of Sec 92 - the suit must be for vindicating the right of the public trust; personal rights not covered.
  • Vidyodaya Trust v Mohan Prasad R. 2008 Sec 92 - leave of court and nature of suit.

Exam traps

  • TrapSec 91: Advocate-General, or two or more persons WITH LEAVE; a person with SPECIAL DAMAGE can sue independently.
  • TrapSec 92: only for public charitable/ religious trusts; breach of trust or directions; the suit must be representative.
  • TrapInterpleader: plaintiff must be a mere stakeholder claiming no interest other than charges or costs.
  • TrapOrder 34: preliminary decree then final decree; deficiency decree for balance (Rule 6).
  • TrapSec 88: no interpleader where a pending suit can decide the rights of all.

Alternative Dispute Resolution in the CPC (Sec 89, Order X Rules 1A-1C), Mediation Act 2023 and Pre-Institution Mediation

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Salem Advocate Bar Association v Union of India 2005 Sec 89 valid; drafting error of Sec 89(2)(c)-(d) corrected by interchanging mediation and judicial settlement; model rules.
  • Afcons Infrastructure v Cherian Varkey Construction 2010 Consent not required for mediation/ conciliation/ Lok Adalat; list of cases suitable and unsuitable for ADR.
  • Patil Automation v Rakheja Engineers 2022 Pre-institution mediation under Sec 12A is mandatory in commercial suits (prospective).

Exam traps

  • TrapSec 89 lists FOUR modes: arbitration, conciliation, judicial settlement (including Lok Adalat), mediation.
  • TrapConsent of parties is needed only for ARBITRATION referral; not for mediation/ conciliation/ Lok Adalat (Afcons).
  • TrapSec 12A Commercial Courts Act: pre-institution mediation mandatory unless urgent interim relief is sought.
  • TrapLok Adalat award is deemed a decree and is final - no appeal.

Execution: General Principles, Courts and Section 47 (Secs 36-54)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Vasudev Dhanjibhai Modi v Rajabhai Abdul Rehman 1970 Executing court cannot go behind the decree; only a nullity can be challenged in execution.
  • Sunder Dass v Ram Prakash 1977 A decree passed without jurisdiction is a nullity and can be declined execution.
  • Rahul S. Shah v Jinendra Kumar Gandhi 2021 Guidelines for speedy execution - examine the JD on assets; dispose within six months.
  • Periyammal v V. Rajamani 2025 INSC 329 Execution petitions to be disposed of within six months; presiding officers accountable; High Courts to monitor.

Exam traps

  • TrapSec 47(2) was OMITTED in 1976; Sec 47(1) and (3) remain.
  • TrapExecuting court cannot go behind the decree unless the decree is a nullity.
  • TrapSec 46 precept attachment lasts TWO months unless extended.
  • TrapSec 39(4): the transferring court cannot execute outside its local limits.
  • TrapLimitation for execution: 12 years (Art 136).
  • TrapSec 51(c) proviso: arrest of JD for money decree only on grounds specified.

Modes of Execution, Arrest and Detention, and Attachable Property (Secs 51, 55-61)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Exam traps

  • TrapSalary: the first Rs 1,000 and two-thirds of the remainder are exempt in execution of ordinary money decrees (clause (i)); in execution of maintenance decrees one-third of the salary is exempt (clause (ia)).
  • TrapHouses of agriculturist/ labourer/ domestic servant occupied by him are exempt, but not for decrees for rent of such house (Sec 60(2)).
  • TrapWomen cannot be arrested/ detained for money decrees (Sec 56).
  • TrapSec 64: private transfer after attachment is void only against the attaching creditors (relative voidness).
  • TrapLife insurance money payable on JD's life is exempt; a mere right to sue for damages is not attachable; books of account are exempt.

Order XXI: Application for Execution, Attachment and Sale Procedure

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Rahul S. Shah v Jinendra Kumar Gandhi 2021 Effective execution: affidavit of assets (Rule 41), disposal within six months, steps for obstructions.

Exam traps

  • TrapRule 22: notice to show cause where execution applied after TWO years from the decree, or against legal representative, or assignee/ receiver.
  • TrapRule 68: 15 days for immovable; 7 days for movable property.
  • TrapRule 84: 25% immediately; Rule 85: balance within 15 days.
  • TrapRule 89: 60 days; deposit 5% to purchaser plus the decretal amount.
  • TrapRule 90 needs 'material irregularity or fraud' AND 'substantial injury'; irregularity alone is not enough.
  • TrapDecree-holder cannot bid without permission (Rule 72); officers cannot bid (Rule 73).
  • TrapRule 92(3): no suit to set aside an order under Rule 92.

Obstruction, Resistance and Third-Party Claims (Order XXI Rules 97-106) and Related Sections

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Brahmdeo Chaudhary v Rishikesh Prasad Jaiswal 1997 Executing court must adjudicate third-party obstruction claims under Rules 97-101; separate suit barred.
  • Silverline Forum v Rajiv Trust 1998 Third party obstructions; scope of Rule 97-101 and who may raise.
  • Bhanwar Lal v Satyanarain 1995 Obstruction by a person not bound by the decree; executing court's duty.

Exam traps

  • TrapRules 97-103: all questions of right, title or interest of the applicant/ obstructor are decided by the executing court itself (Rule 101), not by a separate suit.
  • TrapRule 102: a transferee pendente lite from the JD cannot invoke Rules 98 and 100.
  • TrapOrders under Rules 98 and 100 are treated as DECREES (Rule 103) - appealable.
  • TrapRule 98(2): detention up to 30 days for obstruction without just cause.
  • TrapRule 58 (claim to attached property) applies to attachment; Rule 97 to possession decrees.
22

First Appeals from Original Decrees (Secs 96-99A, Order XLI)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Garikapati Veeraya v N. Subbiah Choudhry 1957 The right of appeal is a vested substantive right governed by the law at the date of institution of the suit.
  • Baldev Singh v Surinder Mohan Sharma 2003 A non-party can appeal with leave if prejudicially affected.
  • Union of India v Ibrahim Uddin 2012 Additional evidence in appeal under Order 41 Rule 27 is exceptional.
  • Mahant Dhangir v Madan Mohan 1987 Order 41 Rule 33 - relief to non-appealing parties in the interests of justice.
  • Abdur Rahman v Athifa Begum 1996 Appeal cannot be decided on merits when the appellant is absent.

Exam traps

  • TrapSec 96(3): no appeal against a consent decree; Sec 96(4): no appeal in small cause suits up to Rs 10,000 except on a question of law.
  • TrapAppeal limitation: 90 days to High Court; 30 days to other courts.
  • TrapCross-objection: one month from service of notice of the hearing (Rule 22).
  • TrapRule 27: additional evidence only in specified situations; not to fill gaps.
  • TrapRemand under Rule 23 (preliminary point) vs Rule 23A (other cases); Order 43 Rule 1(u) appeal against remand.
23

Second Appeals (Secs 100-103) and Appeals from Orders (Secs 104-106, Order XLIII)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Sir Chunilal V. Mehta & Sons v Century Spinning & Manufacturing Co 1962 Test of a 'substantial question of law'.
  • Santosh Hazari v Purushottam Tiwari 2001 High Court must formulate the substantial question of law; scope of second appeal; concurrent findings.
  • Kondiba Dagadu Kadam v Savitribai Sopan Gujar 1999 High Court cannot re-appreciate evidence in a second appeal.
  • Subal Paul v Malina Paul 2003 Sec 100A doesn't bar Letters Patent appeals against Single Judge's writ decisions.

Exam traps

  • TrapSecond appeal lies ONLY on a substantial question of law, which must be formulated by the High Court (Sec 100(4)).
  • TrapSec 102: no second appeal in money suits up to Rs 25,000 (small value).
  • TrapSec 100A: no further appeal against the Single Judge's decision in a first or second appeal from decree/ order.
  • TrapOrders are appealable only if listed in Sec 104/ Order 43 Rule 1.
  • TrapOrder 43 Rule 1(w): order granting review is appealable; order refusing review is not.
  • TrapRejection of an application under Order 7 Rule 11: no appeal (not a decree).
24

Reference, Review and Revision (Secs 113-115; Orders XLVI and XLVII)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Aribam Tuleshwar Sharma v Aribam Pishak Sharma 1979 Error apparent on the face of the record - meaning; review is not an appeal.
  • Kamlesh Verma v Mayawati 2013 Summary of the principles of review under Order 47.
  • Chhajju Ram v Neki 1922 'Any other sufficient reason' means a reason analogous to the specified grounds.
  • Shiv Shakti Co-operative Housing Society v Swaraj Developers 2003 Effect of amendments to Sec 115 - interlocutory orders.
  • Surya Dev Rai v Ram Chander Rai 2003 Remedies against orders of civil courts: revision or Art 227; later modified by Radhey Shyam v Chhabi Nath (2015).
  • Radhey Shyam v Chhabi Nath 2015 Orders of civil courts are not subject to Art 226 writs of certiorari; Art 227 available.

Exam traps

  • TrapReview: 30 days; revision: 90 days; reference: no limitation (made by the court itself).
  • TrapReview lies in the same court; revision in the High Court; there is no appeal against an order rejecting review.
  • Trap'Error apparent on the face of the record' does not include a wrong decision requiring a long process of reasoning.
  • TrapA subsequent change in law by a superior court's decision is NOT a ground for review (Explanation to Rule 1).
  • TrapSec 115(2): HC cannot revise where an appeal lies.

Judgments, Interest, Costs and Inherent Powers (Secs 33-35B, 144, 148-153B, Order XX)

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • South Eastern Coalfields v State of MP 2003 Doctrine of restitution under Sec 144; actus curiae neminem gravabit.
  • Manohar Lal Chopra v Rai Bahadur Rao Raja Seth Hiralal 1962 Inherent powers cannot override express provisions of the Code.
  • Padam Sen v State of UP 1961 Scope of inherent powers under Sec 151.

Exam traps

  • TrapSec 34: future interest for non-commercial transactions: not exceeding 6% p.a.; commercial: not exceeding contractual rate/ nationalised bank rate.
  • TrapSec 35A: compensatory costs not exceeding Rs 3,000; appealable; no appeal on Sec 35B costs.
  • TrapSec 148A caveat: valid for 90 days.
  • TrapSec 144 restitution - by application, not by suit (Sec 144(2)).
  • TrapSec 151 cannot be used when a specific provision exists.
  • TrapSec 152: only clerical or arithmetical errors; accidental slips - 'at any time'.
