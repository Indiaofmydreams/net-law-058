Public Nuisance and Public Charities (Secs 91-93), Interpleader (Sec 88, Order XXXV), Mortgage Suits (Order XXXIV) and Other Special Orders

iOverview

Sections 91-92 (who may sue and with what leave) and the structure of Order 34 mortgage decrees are the main targets.

★Must know

  1. Sec 91 - PUBLIC NUISANCES AND OTHER WRONGFUL ACTS AFFECTING THE PUBLIC: (1) in the case of a public nuisance or other wrongful act affecting, or likely to affect, the public, a suit for a declaration and injunction or for such other relief as may be appropriate in the circumstances of the case may be instituted, (a) by the Advocate-General, or (b) with the leave of the court, by two or more persons, even though no special damage has been caused to such persons by reason of such public nuisance or other wrongful act. (2) nothing in sub-section (1) shall be deemed to limit or otherwise affect any right of suit which may exist independently of its provisions. Hence a private person who suffers SPECIAL DAMAGE (damage over and above that suffered by the public) can sue independently without leave (common law right preserved by Sec 91(2) - Rule that a private individual may sue for public nuisance only if he proves special damage). Sec 91 applies to public nuisance, such as obstruction of a highway, pollution; the relief is declaration and injunction (not damages).
  2. Sec 92 - PUBLIC CHARITIES: (1) in the case of any alleged breach of any express or constructive trust created for public purposes of a charitable or religious nature, or where the direction of the court is deemed necessary for the administration of any such trust, the Advocate-General, or two or more persons having an interest in the trust and having obtained the leave of the court, may institute a suit, whether contentious or not, in the principal civil court of original jurisdiction or in any other court empowered in that behalf by the State Government within the local limits of whose jurisdiction the whole or any part of the subject-matter of the trust is situate, to obtain a decree - (a) removing any trustee; (b) appointing a new trustee; (c) vesting any property in a trustee; (cc) directing a trustee who has been removed or a person who has ceased to be a trustee, to deliver possession of any trust property in his possession to the person entitled to the possession of such property; (d) directing accounts and inquiries; (e) declaring what proportion of the trust property or of the interest therein shall be allocated to any particular object of the trust; (f) authorising the whole or any part of the trust property to be let, sold, mortgaged or exchanged; (g) settling a scheme; or (h) granting such further or other relief as the nature of the case may require. (2) subject to the provisions of the Religious Endowments Act 1863, no suit claiming any of the reliefs specified in sub-section (1) shall be instituted in respect of any such trust as is therein referred to except in conformity with the provisions of that sub-section. Essentials for Sec 92: (i) the trust is public (not private) and for charitable or religious purposes; (ii) there is an alleged breach of trust or direction of the court is necessary for administration; (iii) the suit is for one of the reliefs in sub-section (1); (iv) the suit is by the Advocate-General or two or more persons having an interest with leave of court. Where the suit is to vindicate the personal/ individual right of the plaintiff, Sec 92 doesn't apply (it must be representative - Vidyodaya Trust v Mohan Prasad R., 2008; Swami Paramatmanand Saraswati v Ramji Tripathi, 1974: the true nature of the suit; Charan Singh v Darshan Singh, 1975). The leave is a safeguard against frivolous suits; permission must be applied for and the court must be satisfied prima facie about the breach; the plaintiffs must have an interest in the trust. Sec 93: exercise of powers of the Advocate-General outside presidency towns - by the Collector or by any officer specially appointed. Public interest litigation in the High Court and Supreme Court (Art 226/32) supplements Secs 91-92.
  3. INTERPLEADER SUITS - Sec 88: where two or more persons claim adversely to one another the same debt, sum of money or other property, movable or immovable, from another person, who claims no interest therein other than for charges or costs and who is ready to pay or deliver it to the rightful claimant, such other person may institute a suit of interpleader against all the claimants for the purpose of obtaining a decision as to the person to whom the payment or delivery shall be made and of obtaining indemnity for himself: provided that where any suit is pending in which the rights of all the parties can properly be decided, no such suit of interpleader shall be instituted. ORDER XXXV - Rule 1: plaint in an interpleader-suit - in every plaint in an interpleader suit, in addition to the other statements necessary for plaints, it must be stated (a) that the plaintiff claims no interest in the subject-matter in dispute other than for charges or costs; (b) the claims made by the defendants severally; and (c) that there is no collusion between the plaintiff and any of the defendants. Rule 2: payment of thing claimed into court; Rule 3: where the thing claimed is capable of being paid into court or placed in the custody of the court, the plaintiff may be required to so deposit before obtaining any order in the suit; Rule 4: procedure where defendant is suing plaintiff - the court may stay the suit and decide in the interpleader; Rule 5: procedure at the first hearing - the court may declare that the plaintiff is discharged from all liability to the claimants in respect of the thing claimed, award him his costs, and dismiss him from the suit; Rule 6: charges of plaintiff. Interpleader essentials: (1) two or more persons claiming adversely the same debt or property; (2) from a stakeholder who claims no interest (other than charges or costs); (3) stakeholder is ready to deliver to the rightful claimant; (4) no collusion; (5) no pending suit in which the rights of all can be decided. Order 36: SPECIAL CASE - parties to any suit may agree to state the questions of law or fact for the opinion of the court in the form of a special case (Rule 1), and the court shall hear and determine (Rule 3) and judgment shall be pronounced as in other suits; Order 24: payment into court - Rule 1: the defendant in any suit to recover a debt or damages may, at any stage of the suit, deposit in court such sum of money as he considers a satisfaction in full of the claim; Rule 2: notice of deposit to the plaintiff; Rule 3: interest on deposit not allowed to plaintiff after the notice; Rule 4: procedure where the plaintiff accepts as satisfaction in full or part. Order 25: security for costs - Rule 1: at any stage of a suit the court may, either of its own motion or on the application of any defendant, order the plaintiff to give security for the payment of all costs incurred and likely to be incurred by any defendant where it appears to the court that the sole plaintiff is, or (when there are more than one) that all the plaintiffs are, residing out of India, and that such plaintiff does not possess any sufficient immovable property within India other than the property in suit,; failure to furnish security results in dismissal of the suit (Rule 2). Order 28: suits by or against military men, Order 31: suits by or against trustees, executors and administrators (Rule 1: trustee/ executor/ administrator may sue or be sued without joining the persons beneficially interested; Rule 3: the beneficiaries must be joined where the trustee has an interest adverse).
  4. ORDER XXXIV - SUITS RELATING TO MORTGAGES OF IMMOVABLE PROPERTY: Rule 1: PARTIES TO SUITS FOR FORECLOSURE, SALE AND REDEMPTION - subject to the provisions of any other law for the time being in force, all persons having an interest either in the mortgage-security or in the right of redemption shall be joined as parties to any suit relating to the mortgage (the mortgagee/ mortgagor and persons deriving title from them - all necessary parties); Rule 2: preliminary decree in a foreclosure suit - the court shall pass a preliminary decree (a) ordering that an account be taken of what was due to the plaintiff at the date of such decree for principal and interest on the mortgage, costs of suit and other costs, charges and expenses properly incurred by the mortgagee; (b) directing that, if the defendant pays into court the amount so found due on or before a date within six months from the date on which the court confirms and countersigns the account taken, or such date as the court may fix, the plaintiff shall deliver up all documents in his possession or power relating to the mortgaged property and re-transfer the property; and (c) that if payment is not made on or before such date, the plaintiff shall be entitled to apply for a final decree that the defendant be debarred from all right to redeem the property (foreclosure). Rule 3: final decree in foreclosure suit - upon application by the plaintiff after the expiry of the period; Rule 4: preliminary decree in a suit for sale (similar; the final decree for sale of property or part sufficient to satisfy); Rule 5: final decree in a suit for sale - on application, the court shall pass a final decree directing that the mortgaged property or a sufficient part thereof be sold, and that the proceeds of the sale be applied (a) in payment of the costs of the sale; (b) in payment of what is due to the plaintiff; and (c) the balance (if any) be paid to the defendant or other persons entitled to receive the same. Rule 6: RECOVERY OF BALANCE DUE ON MORTGAGE IN SUIT FOR SALE - where the net proceeds of any sale are insufficient to pay the amount due to the plaintiff, the court may, on application, pass a personal decree against the defendant for the balance (the DEFICIENCY DECREE) provided the balance is legally recoverable from the defendant otherwise than out of the property sold (Sec 90 TPA). Rule 7: preliminary decree in a redemption suit; Rule 8: final decree in a redemption suit - where payment is made, the court shall direct the delivery of documents and re-transfer or, on default, foreclosure or sale; Rule 14: a mortgagee who has obtained a decree for the payment of money in satisfaction of a claim arising under the mortgage shall not be entitled to bring the mortgaged property to sale otherwise than by instituting a suit for sale in enforcement of the mortgage, and he may institute such suit notwithstanding anything contained in Order II Rule 2. Since 1976, the court may order sale in mortgage decrees; the Transfer of Property Act Secs 85-90 and Order 34 operate together; the decrees are preliminary (account of dues; time to pay) and final (foreclosure/ sale). Sale of mortgaged property in the final decree is conducted as in execution (Order 34 Rule 5).

⚖Landmark cases

Swami Paramatmanand Saraswati v Ramji Tripathi

1974

Scope of Sec 92 - the suit must be for vindicating the right of the public trust; personal rights not covered.

Vidyodaya Trust v Mohan Prasad R.

2008

Sec 92 - leave of court and nature of suit.

!Exam traps

  • TrapSec 91: Advocate-General, or two or more persons WITH LEAVE; a person with SPECIAL DAMAGE can sue independently.
  • TrapSec 92: only for public charitable/ religious trusts; breach of trust or directions; the suit must be representative.
  • TrapInterpleader: plaintiff must be a mere stakeholder claiming no interest other than charges or costs.
  • TrapOrder 34: preliminary decree then final decree; deficiency decree for balance (Rule 6).
  • TrapSec 88: no interpleader where a pending suit can decide the rights of all.

?Likely questions

  1. Public nuisance suits: Sec 91.
  2. Public charities: Sec 92 - Advocate-General or two or more persons with leave.
  3. Interpleader suit: Sec 88/ Order 35.
  4. Preliminary decree in mortgage suits: Order 34.
