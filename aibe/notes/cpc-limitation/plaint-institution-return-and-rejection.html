Institution of Suit, Plaint, Return and Rejection of Plaint (Sec 26, Orders IV and VII)

iOverview

Order VII Rule 11 (rejection of plaint) is among the most litigated and most frequently examined provisions; with recent 2025-26 Supreme Court rulings it is a high-probability topic.

★Must know

  1. Sec 26: every suit shall be instituted by the presentation of a plaint or in such other manner as may be prescribed. In every plaint, facts shall be proved by affidavit (Sec 26(2), added 1999). Order IV Rule 1: every suit shall be instituted by presenting a plaint in duplicate to the court or such officer as it appoints; every plaint shall comply with the rules contained in Orders VI and VII; the plaint shall not be deemed duly instituted unless it complies with these requirements (Order IV Rule 1(3)). The date of institution = the date of presentation of a proper plaint (important for limitation). Order IV Rule 2: register of suits.
  2. Order VII Rule 1 - PARTICULARS TO BE CONTAINED IN THE PLAINT: (a) the name of the court in which the suit is brought; (b) the name, description and place of residence of the plaintiff; (c) the name, description and place of residence of the defendant, so far as they can be ascertained; (d) where the plaintiff or the defendant is a minor or a person of unsound mind, a statement to that effect; (e) the facts constituting the cause of action and when it arose; (f) the facts showing that the court has jurisdiction; (g) the relief which the plaintiff claims; (h) where the plaintiff has allowed a set-off or relinquished a portion of his claim, the amount so allowed or relinquished; and (i) a statement of the value of the subject-matter of the suit for the purposes of jurisdiction and of court-fees, so far as the case admits. Rule 2: suits for money - the precise amount claimed (except where the plaintiff sues for mesne profits, or for an amount which will be found due on taking unsettled accounts, or for movables of which valuation is not possible); Rule 3: suits for immovable property - the description must be sufficient to identify it (boundaries, survey number); Rule 4: where the plaintiff sues as a representative, the plaint must show that he has an actual existing interest in the subject-matter and has taken steps necessary to enable him to institute a suit concerning it; Rule 5: the defendant's interest and liability to be shown; Rule 6: GROUNDS OF EXEMPTION FROM LIMITATION LAW - where the suit is instituted after the expiration of the period prescribed by the law of limitation, the plaint shall show the ground upon which exemption from such law is claimed (Sec 14, 15 etc. of the Limitation Act); Rule 7: relief to be specifically stated (the court may grant any other relief to which the plaintiff is entitled - a prayer for 'such further or other relief' is sufficient for relief not specifically asked); Rule 8: relief founded on separate grounds to be stated separately; Rule 9: procedure on admitting plaint - the plaintiff must present as many copies as defendants, along with prescribed fee for service of summons (as amended 1999/ 2002 - copies on plain paper; delivery within 7 days); Rule 14: production of document on which plaintiff sues - where a plaintiff sues upon a document in his possession or power, he shall produce it in court when the plaint is presented, and shall at the same time deliver the document or a copy thereof to be filed with the plaint. A document not produced with the plaint cannot be received in evidence without leave of the court (Rule 14(3)); documents in plaintiff's possession but not relied upon in the suit (for cross-examination/ rebuttal) may be produced later (Rule 14(4)); Rule 15: where the document is not in the plaintiff's possession, he shall state in whose possession it is; Rule 17: production of shop-book.
  3. VALUATION: for court fees (Court Fees Act 1870 and State laws) and for jurisdiction (Suits Valuation Act 1887); the plaintiff is the best judge of valuation (dominus litis) but the court may examine if manifestly undervalued (Order 7 Rule 11(b)). Nature of cause of action: 'cause of action' = bundle of facts necessary for the plaintiff to prove to get a decree - every fact which is material to be proved to entitle the plaintiff to succeed (Rule 11(a)). A plaint cannot be amended to introduce a fresh cause of action after limitation (subject to Order 6 Rule 17).
  4. RETURN OF PLAINT - Order VII Rule 10: subject to the provisions of Rule 10A, the plaint shall at any stage of the suit be returned to be presented to the court in which the suit should have been instituted; (2) procedure on returning a plaint: the judge shall endorse on it the date of its presentation and return, the name of the party presenting it, and a brief statement of the reasons for returning it. Rule 10A: where the plaint is returned the court shall fix a date for the appearance of the parties in the court in which the plaint is proposed to be presented, and give notice (to avoid multiplicity - 2002 amendment). Return of plaint is for lack of territorial/ pecuniary jurisdiction (not for any other defect). The plaint, when re-presented, is treated as a fresh institution in the proper court, but Sec 14 of the Limitation Act (exclusion of time spent bona fide in a court without jurisdiction) applies; An order returning a plaint is appealable (Order 43 Rule 1(a)) except where the procedure under Rule 10A has been followed.
  5. REJECTION OF PLAINT - Order VII Rule 11: the plaint shall be rejected in the following cases: (a) where it does not disclose a cause of action; (b) where the relief claimed is undervalued, and the plaintiff, on being required by the court to correct the valuation within a time to be fixed by the court, fails to do so; (c) where the relief claimed is properly valued, but the plaint is returned upon paper insufficiently stamped, and the plaintiff, on being required by the court to supply the requisite stamp-paper within a time to be fixed by the court, fails to do so; (d) where the suit appears from the statement in the plaint to be barred by any law; (e) where it is not filed in duplicate; (f) where the plaintiff fails to comply with the provisions of Rule 9. PROVISO: the time fixed by the court for the correction of the valuation or supplying of the requisite stamp-paper shall not be extended unless the court, for reasons to be recorded, is satisfied that the plaintiff was prevented by any cause of an exceptional nature from correcting the valuation or supplying the requisite stamp-paper, as the case may be, within the time fixed by the court and that refusal to extend such time would cause grave injustice to the plaintiff. Rule 12: where a plaint is rejected the judge shall record an order to that effect with the reasons for such order. Rule 13: the rejection of the plaint on any of the grounds hereinbefore mentioned shall not of its own force preclude the plaintiff from presenting a fresh plaint in respect of the same cause of action.
  6. PRINCIPLES (Supreme Court): (1) the power under Rule 11 is drastic; the court looks ONLY at the averments in the PLAINT (and documents filed with it) and assumes them to be true; the defence, written statement or the defendant's documents cannot be looked at (Saleem Bhai v State of Maharashtra, 2003; Liverpool & London S.P. & I Association v M.V. Sea Success I, 2004; Dahiben v Arvindbhai Kalyanji Bhanusali, 2020 - the plaint must be read as a whole; clever drafting creating an illusion of cause of action must be detected and nipped in the bud: T. Arivandandam v T.V. Satyapal, 1977); (2) the question of 'bar of limitation' can be a ground under Rule 11(d) only if the plaint on its face shows the suit to be time-barred - mixed questions of law and fact require trial (the Supreme Court in N. Asha Devi v R. Aravind Kumar, 2026 INSC 908, reiterated that a plaint can be rejected where the limitation bar is apparent on the face of the plaint - verify); (3) RES JUDICATA ordinarily cannot be a ground for rejection under Rule 11(d) as it involves mixed questions of fact and law (SC, July 2025); (4) the court-fee/ valuation question: Rule 11(b)-(c) requires the court to call upon the plaintiff to correct valuation; where the question of valuation requires evidence, a plaint cannot be rejected at the threshold and the issue can be decided after evidence (Neelam Sharma v Amita Passan, 2026 INSC 913 - verify); (5) PARTIAL REJECTION is not permissible - the plaint must be rejected as a whole, not in part (Sejal Glass Ltd v Navilan Merchants, 2018; Madhav Prasad Aggarwal v Axis Bank, 2019); (6) an application under Rule 11 can be made at any stage of the suit - even before issues, even after the written statement - but a second application on the same ground is barred by interlocutory res judicata (B.S. Lalitha v Bhuvanesh, 2026 INSC 499); (7) a plaint rejected under Rule 11 results in a DECREE (Sec 2(2)), appealable under Sec 96; an order REFUSING to reject is not a decree and is not appealable (it may be challenged by a revision or under Art 227 - and as the Supreme Court noted in a 2026 matter 'no appeal lies against the rejection of an application under Order VII Rule 11'); (8) 'barred by any law' - includes bar by limitation, by a statute (e.g., Sec 80 non-compliance is not a bar as such but a defect), by Sec 34 SARFAESI, Sec 9 CPC ouster, Benami Act, etc.; the bar must appear from the plaint; (9) vexatious litigation; 'meritless' suits - illusory cause of action; (10) Order VII Rule 11 and Sec 151: the court has inherent power to reject plaint in the interest of justice only within Rule 11's grounds.
  7. AMENDMENT AND VERIFICATION: Order 6 Rule 17 (see 2.4) allows amendment of plaint; the plaint can be amended even after limitation if the amendment is merely formal or does not change the nature of the suit or cause of action. Plaint verification (Order 6 Rule 15) and an affidavit (Sec 26(2)) are mandatory; non-verification is a curable defect. Signature and pleadings: Order 6 Rule 14. Return and Rejection: difference - return is for want of territorial or pecuniary jurisdiction; rejection is for substantive defects; return does not terminate the suit; rejection does and also results in a decree (deemed), though a fresh plaint is allowed (Rule 13).

⚖Landmark cases

T. Arivandandam v T.V. Satyapal

1977

Court must read the plaint meaningfully; if it is manifestly vexatious and meritless, reject it at the first hearing.

Dahiben v Arvindbhai Kalyanji Bhanusali

2020

Scope of Order VII Rule 11; plaint read as a whole; limitation as mixed question.

Saleem Bhai v State of Maharashtra

2003

Only the plaint and documents filed with it are examined under Rule 11.

Sejal Glass Ltd v Navilan Merchants

2018

No partial rejection of plaint under Order VII Rule 11.

B.S. Lalitha v Bhuvanesh

2026 INSC 499

A second Order VII Rule 11 application on the same issue is barred by interlocutory res judicata.

!Exam traps

  • TrapRejection of plaint: a DECREE (appealable); order refusing to reject: not a decree, not appealable.
  • TrapUnder Rule 11 the court looks only at the PLAINT - not at the written statement.
  • TrapPartial rejection of a plaint is not allowed.
  • TrapRes judicata is generally NOT a ground under Rule 11(d); a limitation bar apparent on the plaint's face is.
  • TrapReturn of plaint: appeal lies under Order 43 Rule 1(a), unless Rule 10A procedure was followed.
  • TrapOrder 7 Rule 6: ground for exemption from limitation must be stated in the plaint.

?Likely questions

  1. Under which provision is a plaint rejected? Order VII Rule 11.
  2. A plaint can be rejected where it does not disclose a cause of action (Rule 11(a)).
  3. Rejection of plaint amounts to: a decree (Sec 2(2)).
  4. Particular documents on which plaintiff sues must be produced: Order VII Rule 14.
