Summons, Service, Written Statement, Set-off and Counter-claim (Sec 27, Orders V and VIII)

iOverview

Timelines for written statement (30 + 90 days; 120 days in commercial suits), modes of service, substituted service, set-off vs counter-claim are standard questions.

★Must know

  1. Sec 27: where a suit has been duly instituted, a summons may be issued to the defendant to appear and answer the claim and may be served in the manner prescribed on such day not beyond thirty days from the date of the institution of the suit. Order V Rule 1: when a suit has been duly instituted, a summons may be issued to the defendant to appear and answer the claim and to file the written statement of his defence, if any, within THIRTY DAYS from the date of service of summons on that defendant; proviso (as inserted in 2002): where the defendant fails to file the written statement within the said period of 30 days, he shall be allowed to file the same on such other day, as may be specified by the court, for reasons to be recorded in writing, but which shall not be later than NINETY DAYS from the date of service of summons. Rule 2: copy of plaint to accompany summons.
  2. MODES OF SERVICE (Order V): Rule 9 - delivery of summons by the court: summons shall be served by delivering or tendering a copy thereof signed by the judge or an officer appointed by him and sealed with the seal of the court, either by the court's own officer or by registered post acknowledgment due, or speed post, or by an approved courier service, or by any other means of transmission of documents (including fax message or electronic mail service) provided by the rules made by the High Court; also by the plaintiff (Rule 9(4): the court may permit the plaintiff to effect service by registered post/ courier at his own expense - plaintiff's service). Rule 9(5): when an acknowledgment or any other receipt purporting to be signed by the defendant or his agent is received by the court or the postal article containing the summons is received back by the court with an endorsement purporting to have been made by a postal employee or by any person authorised by the courier service to the effect that the defendant or his agent had refused to take delivery of the postal article containing the summons or had refused to accept the summons by any other means, the court issuing the summons shall declare that the summons had been duly served on the defendant. Rule 12: service to be on the defendant in person, when practicable, or on his agent; Rule 13: service on agent by whom defendant carries on business; Rule 15: where the defendant is absent and has no agent, service may be made on any adult member of the family who is residing with him (not a servant); Rule 16: person served to sign acknowledgment; Rule 17: procedure when defendant refuses to accept service or cannot be found - the serving officer shall affix a copy of the summons on the outer door or some other conspicuous part of the house in which the defendant ordinarily resides or carries on business or personally works for gain, and shall then return the original to the court from which it was issued with a report stating that he has so affixed the copy, the circumstances under which he did so, and the name and address of the person (if any) by whom the house was identified and in whose presence the copy was affixed; Rule 19: examination of the serving officer; Rule 20 - SUBSTITUTED SERVICE: (1) where the court is satisfied that there is reason to believe that the defendant is keeping out of the way for the purpose of avoiding service, or that for any other reason the summons cannot be served in the ordinary way, the court shall order the summons to be served by affixing a copy thereof in some conspicuous place in the court-house, and also upon some conspicuous part of the house (if any) in which the defendant is known to have last resided or carried on business or personally worked for gain, or in such other manner as the court thinks fit. (1A) where the court acting under sub-rule (1) orders service by an advertisement in a newspaper, the newspaper shall be a daily newspaper circulating in the locality in which the defendant is last known to have actually and voluntarily resided, carried on business or personally worked for gain. (2) service substituted by order of the court shall be as effectual as if it had been made on the defendant personally. Rule 21: service of summons where defendant resides within the jurisdiction of another court; Rule 22: service within presidency towns; Rule 24: service on a defendant in prison; Rule 25: service on a defendant residing out of India with no agent in India (Order V Rule 25 - sent by post/ means to the defendant). Summons to witnesses: Order 16.
  3. Content of summons: it must specify the date for appearance and, as per Order 5 Rule 5, the court may fix a day for settlement of issues and final disposal (summons for settlement of issues/ final disposal - Rule 5: summons may be for the settlement of issues only or for the final disposal of the suit) and must be accompanied by a copy of the plaint. Defendant may appear in person or by a pleader (Order 5 Rule 3 - personal appearance if the defendant is within the court's local limits and not exempt). A decree passed without service of summons on the defendant is a nullity (void) if the defendant had no notice. Presumption of service: registered post with proper address - presumption of service under Sec 27 of the General Clauses Act and Sec 114 Evidence Act (now BSA Sec 119).
  4. ORDER VIII - WRITTEN STATEMENT: Rule 1: the defendant shall, within 30 days from the date of service of summons on him, present a written statement of his defence; provided that where the defendant fails to file the written statement within the said period of thirty days, he shall be allowed to file the same on such other day, as may be specified by the court, for reasons to be recorded in writing, but which shall not be later than ninety days from the date of service of summons. The 90-day limit in an ordinary civil suit is DIRECTORY, not mandatory, and the court may allow the filing beyond 90 days in exceptional cases for reasons recorded (Kailash v Nanhku, 2005; Salem Advocate Bar Association v Union of India (2005)); BUT in COMMERCIAL DISPUTES under the Commercial Courts Act 2015 (as amended 2018) the outer limit is 120 days from service (30 + 90) and is MANDATORY - the court has no power to extend time beyond 120 days and the right to file the written statement is forfeited (SCG Contracts India v K.S. Chamankar Infrastructure, 2019; Desh Raj v Balkishan, 2020 - recognises the exceptional discretion in non-commercial cases but emphasises the need for speed). Rule 1A: duty of defendant to produce documents upon which he relies and to enter in a list the documents in his possession which he does not rely on; a document not produced at that stage cannot be received in evidence without leave. Rule 2: NEW FACTS MUST BE SPECIALLY PLEADED: the defendant must raise by his pleading all matters which show the suit not to be maintainable, or that the transaction is either void or voidable in point of law, and all such grounds of defence as, if not raised, would be likely to take the opposite party by surprise, or would raise issues of fact not arising out of the plaint, as, for instance, fraud, limitation, release, payment, performance, or facts showing illegality. Rule 3: denial to be specific - it shall not be sufficient for a defendant in his written statement to deny generally the grounds alleged by the plaintiff, but the defendant must deal specifically with each allegation of fact of which he does not admit the truth, except damages. Rule 4: evasive denial - where a defendant denies an allegation of fact in the plaint, he must not do so evasively, but answer the point of substance. Rule 5: SPECIFIC DENIAL - every allegation of fact in the plaint, if not denied specifically or by necessary implication, or stated to be not admitted in the pleading of the defendant, shall be taken to be admitted, except as against a person under disability; but the court may in its discretion require any fact so admitted to be proved otherwise than by such admission. Rule 10: procedure when party fails to present written statement called for by the court - the court shall pronounce judgment against him, or make such order in relation to the suit as it thinks fit (the court is not bound to pass a decree automatically - Balraj Taneja v Sunil Madan, 1999: the court must apply its mind to the plaint; Rule 10 judgment must be on merits of the plaintiff's case where the defendant is ex parte or in default of WS). Rule 9: no subsequent pleading other than a defence to a set-off or counter-claim shall be presented except by leave of the court. Rule 8: new ground of defence arising after institution.
  5. SET-OFF (Rule 6): (1) where in a suit for the recovery of money the defendant claims to set off against the plaintiff's demand any ascertained sum of money legally recoverable by him from the plaintiff, not exceeding the pecuniary limits of the jurisdiction of the court, and both parties fill the same character as they fill in the plaintiff's suit, the defendant may, at the first hearing of the suit, but not afterwards unless permitted by the court, present a written statement containing the particulars of the debt sought to be set off. (2) effect of set-off: the written statement shall have the same effect as a plaint in a cross-suit so as to enable the court to pronounce a final judgment in respect both of the original claim and of the set-off, but this shall not affect the lien, upon the amount decreed, of any pleader in respect of the costs payable to him under the decree. LEGAL SET-OFF: (i) the suit must be for recovery of money; (ii) the sum must be ascertained; (iii) legally recoverable (not time-barred); (iv) recoverable by the defendant from the plaintiff (or the person whom the plaintiff represents); (v) within the pecuniary limits of the court; (vi) both parties must be in the same character. EQUITABLE SET-OFF: allowed where claims arise from the same transaction even if the sum is unascertained (not available as of right; court discretion; Order 8 Rule 6 does not bar). A set-off is a statutory defence to the plaintiff's claim (not an independent action); the defendant cannot claim relief beyond the plaintiff's claim in set-off (he can in counter-claim).
  6. COUNTER-CLAIM (Rule 6A): (1) a defendant in a suit may, in addition to his right of pleading a set-off under Rule 6, set up, by way of counter-claim against the claim of the plaintiff, any right or claim in respect of a cause of action accruing to the defendant against the plaintiff either before or after the filing of the suit but before the defendant has delivered his defence or before the time limited for delivering his defence has expired, whether such counter-claim is in the nature of a claim for damages or not; provided that such counter-claim shall not exceed the pecuniary limits of the jurisdiction of the court. (2) such counter-claim shall have the same effect as a cross-suit so as to enable the court to pronounce a final judgment in the same suit, both on the original claim and on the counter-claim. (3) the plaintiff shall be at liberty to file a written statement in answer to the counter-claim of the defendant within such period as may be fixed by the court. (4) the counter-claim shall be treated as a plaint and governed by the rules applicable to plaints. Rule 6B: counter-claim to be stated; Rule 6C: exclusion of counter-claim (where the plaintiff contends that it ought not to be disposed of by way of counter-claim - application to exclude); Rule 6D: effect of discontinuance of suit - the counter-claim may be proceeded with notwithstanding that the suit has been stayed, discontinued or dismissed; Rule 6E: default of plaintiff to reply to counter-claim - the court may pronounce judgment against him; Rule 6F: relief to defendant where counter-claim succeeds - where in any suit a set-off or counter-claim is established as a defence against the plaintiff's claim and any balance is found due to the plaintiff or the defendant, as the case may be, the court may give judgment to the party entitled to such balance. Principles: a counter-claim is a cross-suit; it can be filed even AFTER the written statement but before issues are framed - Ramesh Chand Ardawatiya v Anil Panjwani (2003: the court has discretion to permit a counter-claim after the filing of the written statement but before the framing of issues; no strict bar on counter-claim being filed along with or after WS); Bollepanda P. Poonacha v K.M. Madapa (2008): counter-claim must relate to cause of action that arose before the defendant delivered his defence; also Rohit Singh v State of Bihar (2006): counter-claim can be maintained even if the plaintiff's suit is dismissed; Jag Mohan Chawla v Dera Radha Swami Satsang (1996): counter-claim as an independent claim; Sec 151.
  7. COMPARISON - SET-OFF vs COUNTER-CLAIM: (1) set-off is a statutory defence - ascertained sum; counter-claim is a cross-action for any claim (damages, injunction, declaration) arising from any cause of action; (2) set-off must be within the pecuniary limits of the court and only for money; counter-claim also within pecuniary limits but not limited to money; (3) set-off is a shield (defence) but results in a decree in respect of balance; counter-claim is a sword; (4) set-off must be claimed at the first hearing; counter-claim before the defendant delivers his defence (court may allow later); (5) set-off - parties in the same character; counter-claim - cause of action against the plaintiff; (6) both are treated as a plaint (cross-suit) and the court pronounces final judgment on both.

⚖Landmark cases

Kailash v Nanhku

2005

The 90-day period in Order 8 Rule 1 proviso is directory in ordinary suits.

SCG Contracts India v K.S. Chamankar Infrastructure

2019

In commercial suits the 120-day outer limit for the written statement is mandatory.

Desh Raj v Balkishan

2020

Time limits for the written statement; courts should not extend liberally - importance of expeditious trial.

Balraj Taneja v Sunil Madan

1999

Court cannot pass a decree mechanically when no written statement is filed; it must examine the plaint.

Ramesh Chand Ardawatiya v Anil Panjwani

2003

Counter-claim may be allowed after the written statement but before issues are framed.

!Exam traps

  • TrapWritten statement: 30 days + up to 90 days (ordinary suits; extension beyond is directory); 120 days (commercial suits; mandatory).
  • TrapRule 5: allegations not specifically denied are deemed admitted.
  • TrapRule 2: defences like fraud, limitation, release must be specifically pleaded.
  • TrapCounter-claim can survive dismissal of the plaintiff's suit (Rule 6D).
  • TrapSubstituted service: Order 5 Rule 20; service on an adult member of family (not a servant): Rule 15.

?Likely questions

  1. Time for filing written statement: 30 days; outer limit 90 days (ordinary suit).
  2. In commercial suits the outer limit for written statement: 120 days.
  3. Counter-claim is dealt with in: Order 8 Rule 6A.
  4. Substituted service is provided in: Order 5 Rule 20.
