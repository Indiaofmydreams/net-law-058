First Appeals from Original Decrees (Secs 96-99A, Order XLI)

Very high yield1–2 Qs expected14 min readSec 96-99A; Order 41 Rules 1-6, 11, 17, 19, 22, 23-25, 27, 31, 33; Art 116 Limitation Act

iOverview

Right of appeal as a creature of statute, bars (Sec 96(3), (4)), preliminary decrees (Sec 97), cross-objections (Order 41 Rule 22), additional evidence (Rule 27), remand (Rules 23, 23A) and Rule 33 powers.

★Must know

  1. A RIGHT OF APPEAL is a substantive, statutory right - it is neither inherent nor automatic; it must be conferred by statute and is governed by the law in force on the date of institution of the suit (not of the decision) (Garikapati Veeraya v N. Subbiah Choudhry, 1957: the right of appeal is a vested right from the date of institution of the original proceeding). Appeal = a continuation of the suit (the appellate court re-hears on facts and law). Appeals: (1) first appeal (Sec 96 - on facts and law); (2) second appeal (Sec 100 - only on substantial question of law); (3) appeals from orders (Sec 104, Order 43); (4) appeals to the Supreme Court (Sec 109, Art 132-136); (5) Letters Patent appeals. Appellate court has the powers of the trial court (Sec 107).
  2. SEC 96 - APPEAL FROM ORIGINAL DECREE: (1) save where otherwise expressly provided in the body of this Code or by any other law for the time being in force, an appeal shall lie from every decree passed by any court exercising original jurisdiction to the court authorised to hear appeals from the decisions of such court. (2) an appeal may lie from an original decree passed ex parte. (3) NO APPEAL SHALL LIE from a decree passed by the court with the CONSENT OF PARTIES. (4) no appeal shall lie, except on a question of law, from a decree in any suit of the nature cognizable by Courts of Small Causes, when the amount or value of the subject-matter of the original suit does not exceed TEN THOUSAND RUPEES. Sec 97: APPEAL FROM FINAL DECREE WHERE NO APPEAL FROM PRELIMINARY DECREE - where any party aggrieved by a preliminary decree passed after the commencement of this Code does not appeal from such decree, he shall be precluded from disputing its correctness in any appeal which may be preferred from the final decree (so an appeal from a preliminary decree is not mandatory - but if not made, its correctness cannot be raised later; there is no bar to arguing the final decree on its own). Sec 98: DECISION WHERE APPEAL HEARD BY TWO OR MORE JUDGES - (1) where an appeal is heard by a Bench of two or more Judges, the appeal shall be decided in accordance with the opinion of such Judges or of the majority (if any) of such Judges. (2) where there is no such majority which concurs in a judgment varying or reversing the decree appealed from, such decree shall be confirmed; provided that where the Bench consists of two Judges belonging to a Court consisting of more than two Judges and the Judges composing the Bench differ in opinion on a point of law, they may state the point of law upon which they differ and the appeal shall then be heard upon that point only by one or more of the other Judges, and such point shall be decided according to the opinion of the majority of the Judges who have heard the appeal, including those who first heard it. Sec 99: NO DECREE TO BE REVERSED OR MODIFIED FOR ERROR OR IRREGULARITY NOT AFFECTING MERITS OR JURISDICTION - no decree shall be reversed or substantially varied, nor shall any case be remanded, in appeal on account of any misjoinder or non-joinder of parties or causes of action or any error, defect or irregularity in any proceedings in the suit, not affecting the merits of the case or the jurisdiction of the court; provided that nothing in this section shall apply to non-joinder of a necessary party. Sec 99A: no order under Sec 47 shall be reversed or substantially varied, on account of any error, defect or irregularity in any proceeding relating to the execution of a decree, unless such error, defect or irregularity has, in the opinion of the court, substantially affected the decision of the case.
  3. WHO CAN APPEAL: a party to the suit adversely affected by the decree (aggrieved person), or a person claiming under him; a stranger can appeal only with the leave of the appellate court if he is bound by the decree and has an interest (an 'aggrieved person' - Baldev Singh v Surinder Mohan Sharma, 2003: a person not a party to the suit may appeal with leave if he is prejudicially affected by the decree). A party who has consented to the decree cannot appeal (Sec 96(3)). An appeal does not lie against a judgment, only against a decree (but where a decree is not drawn up, the appeal is entertained on the judgment). Limitation (Limitation Act 1963, Art 116): appeal to the High Court from a decree or order - NINETY days; to any other court - THIRTY days. Sec 5 Limitation Act applies. Order 41 Rule 1: FORM OF APPEAL - every appeal shall be preferred in the form of a memorandum signed by the appellant or his pleader and presented to the court or to such officer as it appoints in this behalf; the memorandum shall be accompanied by a copy of the decree appealed from and (unless the appellate court dispenses therewith) of the judgment on which it is founded; the memorandum shall set forth, concisely and under distinct heads, the grounds of objection to the decree appealed from without any argument or narrative; and such grounds shall be numbered consecutively. Rule 1(3): where the appeal is against a decree for payment of money, the appellant shall, within the time allowed for presenting the appeal, deposit the amount disputed in the appeal or furnish such security in respect thereof as the court may think fit (verify applicability in your State rules). Rule 2: grounds which may be taken in appeal - the appellant shall not, except by leave of the court, urge or be heard in support of any ground of objection not set forth in the memorandum of appeal; but the appellate court, in deciding the appeal, shall not be confined to the grounds of objection set forth in the memorandum of appeal or taken by leave of the court under this rule; provided that the court shall not rest its decision on any other ground unless the party who may be affected thereby has had a sufficient opportunity of contesting the case on that ground. Rule 3: rejection or amendment of memorandum; Rule 3A: application for condonation of delay - when an appeal is presented after the expiry of the period of limitation, it shall be accompanied by an application supported by affidavit setting forth the facts on which the appellant relies to satisfy the court that he had sufficient cause for not preferring the appeal within such period; Rule 4: one of several plaintiffs or defendants may obtain reversal of whole decree where it proceeds on ground common to all.
  4. STAY: Rule 5: STAY OF PROCEEDINGS AND OF EXECUTION - (1) an appeal shall not operate as a stay of proceedings under a decree or order appealed from except so far as the appellate court may order, nor shall execution of a decree be stayed by reason only of an appeal having been preferred from the decree; but the appellate court may for sufficient cause order stay of execution of such decree. (3) no order for stay of execution shall be made under sub-rule (1) or sub-rule (2) unless the court making it is satisfied (a) that substantial loss may result to the party applying for stay of execution unless the order is made; (b) that the application has been made without unreasonable delay; and (c) that security has been given by the applicant for the due performance of such decree or order as may ultimately be binding upon him. Rule 6: security in case of order for execution of decree appealed from. Rule 11: POWER TO DISMISS APPEAL WITHOUT SENDING NOTICE TO LOWER COURT - the appellate court after fixing a day for hearing the appellant may dismiss the appeal (after hearing him) without serving notice on the respondent; Rule 12: day for hearing appeal; Rule 14: publication and service of notice of day for hearing appeal; Rule 17: DISMISSAL OF APPEAL FOR APPELLANT'S DEFAULT - (1) where on the day fixed, or on any other day to which the hearing may be adjourned, the appellant does not appear when the appeal is called on for hearing, the court may make an order that the appeal be dismissed (the court cannot decide the appeal on merits in default - Abdur Rahman v Athifa Begum, 1996: where the appellant is absent, the appeal must be dismissed for default, not on merits); Rule 19: READMISSION OF APPEAL DISMISSED FOR DEFAULT - where an appeal is dismissed under Rule 11(2) or Rule 17(1), the appellant may apply to the appellate court for the readmission of the appeal; and, where it is proved that he was prevented by any sufficient cause from appearing when the appeal was called on for hearing or from depositing the sum so required, the court shall readmit the appeal on such terms as to costs or otherwise as it thinks fit. Rule 21: rehearing on application of respondent against whom ex parte decree made.
  5. RULE 22 - UPON HEARING, RESPONDENT MAY OBJECT TO DECREE AS IF HE HAD PREFERRED A SEPARATE APPEAL: (1) any respondent, though he may not have appealed from any part of the decree, may not only support the decree on any of the grounds decided against him in the court below, but take any cross-objection to the decree which he could have taken by way of appeal, provided he has filed in the appellate court within ONE MONTH from the date of service on him or his pleader of notice of the day fixed for hearing the appeal, or within such further time as the appellate court may see fit to allow, a memorandum setting forth the grounds of objection to the decree which he could have taken by way of appeal. (4) where, in any case in which any respondent has under this rule filed a memorandum of objection, the original appeal is withdrawn or is dismissed for default, the objection so filed may nevertheless be heard and determined after such notice to the other parties as the court thinks fit. Rule 23: REMAND OF CASE BY APPELLATE COURT - where the court from whose decree an appeal is preferred has disposed of the suit upon a preliminary point and the decree is reversed in appeal, the appellate court may, if it thinks fit, by order remand the case, and may further direct what issue or issues shall be tried in the case so remanded, and shall send a copy of its judgment and order to the court from which the appeal is preferred, with directions to readmit the suit under its original number in the register of civil suits, and proceed to determine the suit; and the evidence (if any) recorded during the original trial shall, subject to all just exceptions, be evidence during the trial after remand. Rule 23A: REMAND IN OTHER CASES - where the court from whose decree an appeal is preferred has disposed of the case otherwise than on a preliminary point, and the decree is reversed in appeal and a re-trial is considered necessary, the appellate court shall have the same powers as it has under Rule 23. Remand is exceptional - an appellate court should not remand where evidence on record is sufficient to decide (Rule 24); Rule 25: appellate court may frame issues and refer them for trial to the lower court (taking findings) - the evidence recorded and the findings are returned within a fixed period; the appellate court then decides. Remand against a remand order: appeal under Order 43 Rule 1(u).
  6. RULE 27 - PRODUCTION OF ADDITIONAL EVIDENCE IN APPELLATE COURT: (1) the parties to an appeal shall not be entitled to produce additional evidence, whether oral or documentary, in the appellate court. But if (a) the court from whose decree the appeal is preferred has refused to admit evidence which ought to have been admitted, or (aa) the party seeking to produce additional evidence, establishes that notwithstanding the exercise of due diligence, such evidence was not within his knowledge or could not, after the exercise of due diligence, be produced by him at the time when the decree appealed against was passed, or (b) the appellate court requires any document to be produced or any witness to be examined to enable it to pronounce judgment, or for any other substantial cause, the appellate court may allow such evidence or document to be produced, or witness to be examined. (2) wherever additional evidence is allowed to be produced by an appellate court, the court shall record the reason for its admission. Principles: additional evidence is allowed sparingly; it's not for filling lacunae; the 'requirement' of the court under clause (b) arises when the court finds that it cannot pronounce judgment satisfactorily without the document (K. Venkataramiah v A. Seetharama Reddy, 1963; Union of India v Ibrahim Uddin, 2012: additional evidence cannot be allowed to patch up weak points; clause (b) - the court must itself find a need). Rule 28: mode of taking additional evidence - by the appellate court or by the lower court at its direction. Rule 29: points to be defined and recorded. Rule 30: judgment to be pronounced; Rule 31: CONTENTS, DATE AND SIGNATURE OF JUDGMENT - the judgment of the appellate court shall be in writing and shall state (a) the points for determination; (b) the decision thereon; (c) the reasons for the decision; and (d) where the decree appealed from is reversed or varied, the relief to which the appellant is entitled, and shall at the time that it is pronounced be signed and dated by the judge or by the judges concurring therein. Rule 32: what judgment may direct; RULE 33 - POWER OF COURT OF APPEAL: the appellate court shall have power to pass any decree and make any order which ought to have been passed or made and to pass or make such further or other decree or order as the case may require, and this power may be exercised by the court notwithstanding that the appeal is as to part only of the decree and may be exercised in favour of all or any of the respondents or parties, although such respondents or parties may not have filed any appeal or objection; provided that the appellate court shall not make any order under Sec 35A in pursuance of any objection on which the court from whose decree the appeal is preferred has omitted or refused to make such order. The power under Rule 33 is to be exercised sparingly to do complete justice between parties and in cases where the relief is inextricably linked (Mahant Dhangir v Madan Mohan, 1987; Nirmala Bala Ghose v Balai Chand Ghose, 1965: Rule 33 allows giving relief to a non-appealing party where the decree is a joint or the reliefs are interconnected).

⚖Landmark cases

Garikapati Veeraya v N. Subbiah Choudhry

1957

The right of appeal is a vested substantive right governed by the law at the date of institution of the suit.

Baldev Singh v Surinder Mohan Sharma

2003

A non-party can appeal with leave if prejudicially affected.

Union of India v Ibrahim Uddin

2012

Additional evidence in appeal under Order 41 Rule 27 is exceptional.

Mahant Dhangir v Madan Mohan

1987

Order 41 Rule 33 - relief to non-appealing parties in the interests of justice.

Abdur Rahman v Athifa Begum

1996

Appeal cannot be decided on merits when the appellant is absent.

!Exam traps

  • TrapSec 96(3): no appeal against a consent decree; Sec 96(4): no appeal in small cause suits up to Rs 10,000 except on a question of law.
  • TrapAppeal limitation: 90 days to High Court; 30 days to other courts.
  • TrapCross-objection: one month from service of notice of the hearing (Rule 22).
  • TrapRule 27: additional evidence only in specified situations; not to fill gaps.
  • TrapRemand under Rule 23 (preliminary point) vs Rule 23A (other cases); Order 43 Rule 1(u) appeal against remand.

?Likely questions

  1. Appeal from original decree: Sec 96.
  2. No appeal from a consent decree: Sec 96(3).
  3. Cross-objection: Order 41 Rule 22.
  4. Additional evidence in appeal: Order 41 Rule 27.
  5. Power of the appellate court to grant relief to non-appealing respondents: Rule 33.
