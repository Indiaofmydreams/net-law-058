Res Sub Judice (Sec 10), Res Judicata (Sec 11) and Constructive Res Judicata

iOverview

Perennial favourites: essentials of Secs 10 and 11, Explanations I-VIII, constructive res judicata (Explanation IV), res judicata in different contexts, and the 2026 Supreme Court ruling on interlocutory res judicata.

★Must know

  1. Sec 10 STAY OF SUIT (RES SUB JUDICE): no court shall proceed with the trial of any suit in which the matter in issue is also directly and substantially in issue in a previously instituted suit between the same parties, or between parties under whom they or any of them claim, litigating under the same title, where such suit is pending in the same or any other Court in India having jurisdiction to grant the relief claimed, or in any Court beyond the limits of India established or continued by the Central Government and having like jurisdiction, or before the Supreme Court. Explanation: the pendency of a suit in a foreign court does not preclude the courts in India from trying a suit founded on the same cause of action. ESSENTIALS: (1) two suits - one previously instituted, one subsequently instituted; (2) the matter in issue in the subsequent suit must be directly and substantially in issue in the previous suit (the test: whether a decision in the previous suit would operate as res judicata in the subsequent suit - National Institute of Mental Health & Neuro Sciences v C. Parameshwara, 2005); (3) same parties or parties under whom they claim, litigating under the same title; (4) the previously instituted suit is pending in the same or another court competent to grant the relief claimed; (5) the previous suit must be a SUIT - not applications or execution proceedings. Object: to prevent courts of concurrent jurisdiction from simultaneously trying two parallel suits, avoid conflicting decisions and multiplicity. Sec 10 is MANDATORY (the court has no discretion to refuse stay), but it only bars the TRIAL, not the institution of the subsequent suit; the stay applies to the later suit; a decree passed in contravention of Sec 10 is not a nullity (it is an irregularity - Pukhraj D. Jain v G. Gopalakrishna, 2004). Where Sec 10 doesn't strictly apply, the court may stay under its inherent power (Sec 151) in the interest of justice (Indian Bank v Maharashtra State Co-operative Marketing Federation, 1998; Aspi Jal v Khushroo Rustom Dadyburjor, 2013 - Sec 10 applies where the matter in issue is substantially the same; otherwise consolidation or a stay under Sec 151 may be considered).
  2. Sec 11 RES JUDICATA: no court shall try any suit or issue in which the matter directly and substantially in issue has been directly and substantially in issue in a former suit between the same parties, or between parties under whom they or any of them claim, litigating under the same title, in a court competent to try such subsequent suit or the suit in which such issue has been subsequently raised, and has been heard and finally decided by such court. Maxims: 'nemo debet bis vexari pro una et eadem causa' (no man should be vexed twice for the same cause); 'interest reipublicae ut sit finis litium' (it is in the interest of the State that there be an end to litigation); 'res judicata pro veritate occipitur' (a judicial decision is accepted as correct). Based on public policy (not only on the technicalities of Sec 11; the principle applies even where Sec 11 isn't strictly applicable - e.g., writ petitions (Daryao v State of UP, 1961), industrial disputes, taxation (limited), and between two stages of the same litigation).
  3. ESSENTIALS (conditions) of Sec 11: (1) the matter directly and substantially in issue in the subsequent suit must have been directly and substantially in issue in the former suit; (2) the former suit must have been between the same parties or parties under whom they or any of them claim; (3) the parties must have litigated under the same title; (4) the matter must have been heard and finally decided by the former court; (5) the former court must have been competent to try the subsequent suit or the suit in which the issue is subsequently raised. 'Directly and substantially in issue' = the main point on which the decision turned; contrast 'collaterally or incidentally in issue' (not barred). A 'former suit' means a suit which has been decided prior to the suit in question whether or not it was instituted prior thereto (Explanation I). Competence of the court is determined irrespective of any provisions as to a right of appeal from the decision (Explanation II). The matter must have been alleged by one party and either denied or admitted, expressly or impliedly, by the other (Explanation III).
  4. EXPLANATION IV - CONSTRUCTIVE RES JUDICATA: any matter which might and ought to have been made a ground of defence or attack in such former suit shall be deemed to have been a matter directly and substantially in issue in such suit. Illustration: A sues B for rent for a year and B pleads nothing; a later suit by B contending that rent was not due for want of title is barred - B could and should have taken that ground. Forward Construction Co v Prabhat Mandal (Regd), 1986 - constructive res judicata applies to writ petitions/ interlocutory proceedings and to defences that could have been raised. EXPLANATION V: any relief claimed in the plaint, which is not expressly granted by the decree, shall for the purposes of Sec 11 be deemed to have been refused. EXPLANATION VI: where persons litigate bona fide in respect of a public right or of a private right claimed in common for themselves and others, all persons interested in such right shall, for the purposes of Sec 11, be deemed to claim under the persons so litigating. EXPLANATION VII: the provisions of Sec 11 apply to a proceeding for the execution of a decree and references in this section to any suit, issue or former suit shall be construed as references, respectively, to any proceeding for the execution of the decree, question arising in such proceeding and a former proceeding for the execution of that decree. EXPLANATION VIII: an issue heard and finally decided by a court of limited jurisdiction, competent to decide such issue, shall operate as res judicata in a subsequent suit, notwithstanding that such court of limited jurisdiction was not competent to try such subsequent suit or the suit in which such issue has been subsequently raised (added 1976).
  5. SPECIAL ASPECTS: (a) Res judicata applies to ISSUES (decision on issue in an earlier suit binds in a later suit even on a different cause of action - 'issue estoppel'); (b) A DECISION ON A PURE QUESTION OF LAW unconnected with the right of the parties does not operate as res judicata; but where a question of law relates to the right of the parties it does (Mathura Prasad Bajoo Jaiswal v Dossibai N.B. Jeejeebhoy, 1970; Mohanlal Goenka v Benoy Kishna Mukherjee, 1953: a decision on a jurisdiction question - the principle that a wrong decision on jurisdiction is binding if the court had jurisdiction); (c) A JUDGMENT BY CONSENT/ COMPROMISE decree operates as estoppel between the parties but is not strictly res judicata as nothing is 'heard and decided' (Pulavarthi Venkata Subba Rao v Valluri Jagannadha Rao, 1967: consent decree is in the nature of a contract - but binds); (d) Decisions of appellate courts: where the trial court decides a matter and the appeal is dismissed on a preliminary ground, the trial court's decision on merits still operates as res judicata (Sheodan Singh v Daryao Kunwar, 1966); (e) Interlocutory orders: Satyadhyan Ghosal v Deorajin Debi (1960): the principle of res judicata applies to successive stages of the same proceeding - a matter decided at an earlier stage of the same litigation cannot be reopened at a later stage; (f) Ex parte decrees operate as res judicata; (g) The plea of res judicata must be raised and proved; a party may waive it; (h) Res judicata vs estoppel: res judicata is a bar to the court's jurisdiction to try; estoppel is a rule of evidence; res judicata applies to decisions of competent courts; estoppel arises from the party's conduct; (i) Res judicata and Order 2 Rule 2: Sec 11 relates to a matter decided; Order 2 Rule 2 relates to claims not made in the same cause of action (Gurbux Singh v Bhooralal, 1964).
  6. RECENT (2025-26): (1) The Supreme Court held in July 2025 that res judicata ordinarily cannot be a ground for rejecting a plaint under Order VII Rule 11, since it involves a mixed question of fact and law that must be decided on pleadings and evidence at trial; (2) B.S. LALITHA v BHUVANESH (2026 INSC 499, decided 15 May 2026): the Supreme Court explained INTERLOCUTORY RES JUDICATA - the principle applies between two stages of the same litigation; where an earlier application under Order VII Rule 11 CPC has been decided and attained finality, a second application seeking rejection of the plaint on substantially the same issue is barred; the defendant cannot circumvent the bar by invoking different sub-clauses of Rule 11 in the second application as any such ground could and ought to have been raised in the first application (Explanation IV to Sec 11). The same case dealt with daughters' rights in a partition suit (Sec 6 Hindu Succession Act, as amended in 2005, as a saving clause rather than a jurisdictional bar).

⚖Landmark cases

Daryao v State of UP

1961

Res judicata applies to writ petitions under Art 32 on the same facts.

Satyadhyan Ghosal v Deorajin Debi

1960

Res judicata applies to different stages of the same proceeding; policy - finality.

Forward Construction Co v Prabhat Mandal

1986

Constructive res judicata: matters that might and ought to have been raised.

Sheodan Singh v Daryao Kunwar

1966

Where the appeal is dismissed on a preliminary ground, the decision on merits by the trial court still operates; conditions for res judicata with two courts.

Mathura Prasad Bajoo Jaiswal v Dossibai N.B. Jeejeebhoy

1970

Pure question of law unrelated to rights of parties does not operate as res judicata.

National Institute of Mental Health v C. Parameshwara

2005

Test for Sec 10: whether decision in the first suit would operate as res judicata in the second.

B.S. Lalitha v Bhuvanesh

2026 INSC 499

Interlocutory res judicata - second Order VII Rule 11 application on the same ground barred.

✎Memory aids

  • Res judicata conditions = M-P-T-C-D: Matter, Parties, Title, Competent court, Decided finally.

!Exam traps

  • TrapRes sub judice bars TRIAL (stay), not INSTITUTION; res judicata bars trial of suit/ issue.
  • TrapConstructive res judicata (Explanation IV) covers grounds that MIGHT AND OUGHT to have been taken.
  • TrapPure question of law (no connection with rights) does not operate as res judicata.
  • TrapSec 11 applies to execution proceedings (Explanation VII).
  • TrapPendency of a suit in a FOREIGN court does not bar an Indian court (Explanation to Sec 10).

?Likely questions

  1. Principle: 'nemo debet bis vexari pro una et eadem causa' - res judicata.
  2. Constructive res judicata is in: Explanation IV to Sec 11.
  3. Sec 10 is mandatory and bars: the trial of the subsequent suit.
  4. Interlocutory res judicata: B.S. Lalitha v Bhuvanesh (2026).
