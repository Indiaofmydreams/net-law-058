Modes of Execution, Arrest and Detention, and Attachable Property (Secs 51, 55-61)

Very high yield1–2 Qs expected15 min readSec 51; Sec 55-59; Sec 60-64; Sec 135, 135A; Order 21 Rules 30-36

iOverview

The list of property exempt from attachment (Sec 60) and the rules on arrest/ detention (Secs 55-59) are regularly framed as 'which of these cannot be attached?'

★Must know

  1. Modes under Sec 51: (a) delivery of property specifically decreed; (b) attachment and sale or sale without attachment; (c) arrest and detention in civil prison; (d) appointment of a receiver; (e) any other manner as the nature of the relief requires (e.g., for a mandatory injunction - Order 21 Rule 32). Order 21 Rule 30: decree for payment of money - execution by detention in prison of the judgment-debtor, or by attachment and sale of his property, or both. Rule 31: decree for specific movable property - by seizure and delivery, or by detention, or attachment, or both; Rule 32: decree for specific performance for restitution of conjugal rights, or for an injunction - (1) where the party against whom a decree for the specific performance of a contract, or for restitution of conjugal rights, or for an injunction, has been passed, has had an opportunity of obeying the decree and has wilfully failed to obey it, the decree may be enforced in the case of a decree for restitution of conjugal rights by the attachment of his property or, in the case of a decree for the specific performance of a contract or for an injunction by his detention in the civil prison, or by the attachment of his property, or by both. (5) where a decree for the specific performance of a contract or for an injunction has not been obeyed, the court may, in lieu of or in addition to the above processes, direct that the act required to be done may be done so far as practicable by the decree-holder or some other person appointed by the court, at the cost of the judgment-debtor, and upon the act being done the expenses incurred may be ascertained in such manner as the court may direct and may be recovered as if they were included in the decree. Rule 34: decree for execution of document or endorsement of negotiable instrument - where a decree is for the execution of a document or for the endorsement of a negotiable instrument, and the JD neglects or refuses to obey the decree, the decree-holder may prepare a draft of the document or endorsement in accordance with the terms of the decree and deliver the same to the court; the court shall cause the draft to be served on the JD and, on objections, settle the document and execute it. Rule 35: DECREE FOR IMMOVABLE PROPERTY - (1) where a decree is for the delivery of any immovable property, possession thereof shall be delivered to the party to whom it has been adjudged, or to such person as he may appoint to receive delivery on his behalf, and, if necessary, by removing any person bound by the decree who refuses to vacate the property. (2) where a decree is for the joint possession of immovable property, such possession shall be delivered by affixing a copy of the warrant in some conspicuous place on the property and proclaiming, by beat of drum or other customary mode, at some convenient place, the substance of the decree. (3) where possession of any building or enclosure is to be delivered and the person in possession, being bound by the decree, does not afford free access, the court, through its officers, may, after giving reasonable warning and facility to any woman not appearing in public according to the customs of the country to withdraw, remove or open any lock or bolt or break open any door or do any other act necessary for putting the decree-holder in possession. Rule 36: delivery of property in the occupancy of tenant - symbolic possession by affixing the warrant and proclaiming (where the property is in the occupancy of a tenant or other person entitled to occupy and not bound by the decree to relinquish).
  2. ARREST AND DETENTION (Secs 55-59): Sec 55: (1) a judgment-debtor may be arrested in execution of a decree at any hour and in any place, except (a) no arrest shall be made after sunset and before sunrise; (c) no dwelling-house shall be entered after sunset and before sunrise; (d) no outer door of a dwelling-house shall be broken open unless such dwelling-house is in the occupancy of the judgment-debtor and he is not himself present therein, or, being present, refuses or in any way prevents access thereto, but when the officer authorised to make the arrest has duly gained access to any dwelling-house, he may break open the door of any room in which he has reason to believe the JD is to be found; (e) where the room is in the actual occupancy of a woman who is not the JD and who according to the customs of the country does not appear in public, the officer shall give notice to her that she is at liberty to withdraw, and, after allowing a reasonable time for her to withdraw and giving her reasonable facility for withdrawing, may enter the room for the purpose of making the arrest; (f) where the decree in execution of which a JD is arrested is a decree for the payment of money and the JD pays the amount of the decree and the costs of the arrest to the officer arresting him, such officer shall at once release him. (2) where a judgment-debtor is arrested in execution of a money decree and brought before the court, the court shall inform him that he may apply to be declared an insolvent and that he may be discharged if he has not acted in bad faith. Separately, Sec 135 exempts parties, witnesses and pleaders from arrest under civil process while attending court, and Sec 135A exempts members of legislative bodies. Sec 56: PROHIBITION OF ARREST OR DETENTION OF WOMEN IN EXECUTION OF DECREE FOR PAYMENT OF MONEY - notwithstanding anything in this Code, the court shall not order the arrest or detention in the civil prison of a woman in execution of a decree for the payment of money. Sec 57: subsistence allowance - the State Government may fix scales, graded according to the rank, race and nationality of the JD, of monthly allowances payable for the subsistence of JDs; the allowance is payable by the decree-holder. Sec 58 - DETENTION AND RELEASE: (1) every person detained in the civil prison in execution of a decree shall be so detained, (a) where the decree is for the payment of a sum of money exceeding five thousand rupees, for a period up to THREE MONTHS; and (b) where the decree is for the payment of a sum of money exceeding two thousand rupees, but not exceeding five thousand rupees, for a period up to SIX WEEKS: provided that he shall be released from such detention before the expiry of the said period of detention (i) on the amount mentioned in the warrant for his detention being paid to the officer in charge of the civil prison, or (ii) on the decree against him being otherwise fully satisfied, or (iii) on the request of the person at whose instance he has been detained, or (iv) on the omission by the person, at whose instance he has been detained, to pay subsistence allowance, or (v) on the order of the court. (1A) for the removal of doubts, it is hereby declared that no order for detention of the JD in civil prison in execution of a decree for the payment of money shall be made where the total amount of the decree does not exceed two thousand rupees. (2) a judgment-debtor released from detention under this section shall not merely by reason of his release be discharged from his debt, but he shall not be liable to be re-arrested under the decree in execution of which he was detained in the civil prison. Sec 59: release on ground of illness - may be released on the ground of serious illness or in case of an infectious disease prevailing in the prison.
  3. SEC 60 - PROPERTY LIABLE TO ATTACHMENT AND SALE IN EXECUTION OF DECREE: (1) the following property is liable to attachment and sale in execution of a decree, namely, lands, houses or other buildings, goods, money, bank-notes, cheques, bills of exchange, hundis, promissory notes, Government securities, bonds or other securities for money, debts, shares in a corporation and, save as hereinafter mentioned, all other saleable property, movable or immovable, belonging to the judgment-debtor, or over which, or the profits of which, he has a disposing power which he may exercise for his own benefit, whether the same be held in the name of the judgment-debtor or by another person in trust for him or on his behalf: PROVIDED THAT THE FOLLOWING PARTICULARS SHALL NOT BE LIABLE TO SUCH ATTACHMENT OR SALE, namely: (a) the necessary wearing apparel, cooking vessels, beds and bedding of the judgment-debtor, his wife and children, and such personal ornaments as, in accordance with religious usage, cannot be parted with by any woman; (b) tools of artisans, and, where the JD is an agriculturist, his implements of husbandry and such cattle and seed-grain as may, in the opinion of the court, be necessary to enable him to earn his livelihood as such, and such portion of agricultural produce or of any class of agricultural produce as may have been declared to be free from liability under the provisions of the next following section; (c) houses and other buildings (with the materials and the sites thereof and the land immediately appurtenant thereto and necessary for their enjoyment) belonging to an agriculturist or a labourer or a domestic servant and occupied by him; (d) books of account; (e) a mere right to sue for damages; (f) any right of personal service; (g) stipends and gratuities allowed to pensioners of the Government or of a local authority or of any other employer, or payable out of any service family pension fund notified in the Official Gazette by the Central Government or the State Government in this behalf, and political pensions; (h) the wages of labourers and domestic servants, whether payable in money or in kind; (i) SALARY to the extent of the first one thousand rupees and two-thirds of the remainder in execution of any decree other than one for maintenance (proviso: where any part of such portion of the salary as is liable to attachment has been, for a period of twenty-four months, attached, whether continuously or intermittently, in execution of one and the same decree, such portion shall, for a further period of twelve months, be exempt from attachment); (ia) one-third of the salary in execution of any decree for maintenance; (j) the pay and allowances of persons to whom the Air Force Act 1950, the Army Act 1950, or the Navy Act 1957 applies; (k) all compulsory deposits and other sums in or derived from any fund to which the Provident Funds Act 1925 applies in so far as they are declared by the said Act not to be liable to attachment; (ka) all deposits and other sums in or derived from any fund to which the Public Provident Funds Act 1968 applies in so far as they are declared by the said Act as not to be liable to attachment; (kb) all moneys payable under a policy of insurance on the life of the judgment-debtor; (kc) the interest of a lessee of a residential building to which the provisions of law for the time being in force relating to control of rents and accommodation apply; (l) any allowance forming part of the emoluments of any servant of the Government or of any servant of a railway company or local authority which the appropriate Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, declare to be exempt from attachment, and any subsistence grant or allowance made to a public officer while under suspension; (m) an expectancy of succession by survivorship or other merely contingent or possible right or interest; (n) a right to future maintenance; (o) any allowance declared by any Indian law to be exempt from liability to attachment or sale in execution of a decree; and (p) where the judgment-debtor is a person liable for the payment of land-revenue, any movable property which, under any law for the time being applicable to him, is exempt from sale for the recovery of an arrear of such revenue. Explanation I: the moneys payable in respect of the allowances mentioned in clauses (g), (h), (i), (ia), (j), (l) and (o) are exempt from attachment or sale, whether before or after they are actually payable, and the liability of such moneys to attachment or sale shall be determined with reference to the character thereof at the time of attachment. Explanation II: in clauses (i) and (ia), 'salary' means the total monthly emoluments, excluding any allowance declared exempt from attachment, derived by a person from his employment whether on duty or on leave. Explanation III: in clauses (j), (l) and (o), 'appropriate Government' means (i) as respects any person in the service of the Central Government, or any servant of a railway administration or of a cantonment authority or of the port authority of a major port, the Central Government; and (ii) as respects any other servant of the Government or a servant of any other local authority, the State Government. Explanation IV: for the purposes of this proviso, wages include wages payable in kind including the portion of agricultural produce payable to the labourers in lieu of their wages. (2) nothing in this section shall be deemed to exempt houses and other buildings (with the materials and the sites thereof and the lands immediately appurtenant thereto and necessary for their enjoyment) from attachment or sale in execution of decrees for rent of any such house, building, site or land.
  4. Sec 61: partial exemption of agricultural produce - the State Government may, by general or special order published in the Official Gazette, declare that such portion of agricultural produce, or of any class of agricultural produce, as may appear to the State Government to be necessary for the purpose of providing for the due cultivation of the land and for the support of the JD and his family until the next harvest, shall, as long as it is in the possession of an agriculturist, be exempt from liability to attachment or sale in execution of a decree. Sec 62: SEIZURE OF MOVABLE PROPERTY IN DWELLING-HOUSE - no person executing any process under this Code directing or authorising seizure of movable property shall enter any dwelling-house after sunset and before sunrise; no outer door shall be broken open unless the house is in the occupancy of the JD and he is not present or refuses access; and the officer shall give due notice to women not appearing in public. Sec 63: property attached in execution of decrees of several courts (the court of the highest grade/ the court that first attached - where property not in custody of any court is attached in execution of decrees of more than one court, the court which shall receive or realise such property and determine claims thereto and objections to the attachment thereof shall be the court of the highest grade, or, where there is no difference in grade between such courts, the court which first attached the property). Sec 64: PRIVATE ALIENATION OF PROPERTY AFTER ATTACHMENT TO BE VOID - (1) where an attachment has been made, any private transfer or delivery of the property attached or of any interest therein and any payment to the judgment-debtor of any debt, dividend or other moneys contrary to such attachment, shall be void as against all claims enforceable under the attachment. (2) nothing in this section shall apply to any private transfer or delivery of the property attached or of any interest therein, made in pursuance of any contract for such transfer or delivery entered into and registered before the attachment, or to any payment made in pursuance of any contract entered into and registered before the attachment. Explanation: for the purposes of this section, claims enforceable under an attachment include claims for the rateable distribution of assets. The alienation is void only against the attaching decree-holder (and others claiming rateable distribution); it is valid between the parties (Sec 64 is relative).

!Exam traps

  • TrapSalary: the first Rs 1,000 and two-thirds of the remainder are exempt in execution of ordinary money decrees (clause (i)); in execution of maintenance decrees one-third of the salary is exempt (clause (ia)).
  • TrapHouses of agriculturist/ labourer/ domestic servant occupied by him are exempt, but not for decrees for rent of such house (Sec 60(2)).
  • TrapWomen cannot be arrested/ detained for money decrees (Sec 56).
  • TrapSec 64: private transfer after attachment is void only against the attaching creditors (relative voidness).
  • TrapLife insurance money payable on JD's life is exempt; a mere right to sue for damages is not attachable; books of account are exempt.

?Likely questions

  1. Which of the following is not attachable under Sec 60? book of account / right to sue for damages / wages of labourers / personal ornaments of woman.
  2. Detention in civil prison: up to 3 months (decree above Rs 5,000).
  3. Arrest of a woman in execution of a money decree: prohibited (Sec 56).
  4. Alienation after attachment: void under Sec 64.
