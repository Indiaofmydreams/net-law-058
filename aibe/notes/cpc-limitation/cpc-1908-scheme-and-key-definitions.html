Scheme of the Code and Key Definitions (Section 2)

iOverview

The CPC 1908 is procedural law: 158 sections (the substantive body) and 51 Orders with Rules (First Schedule). Definitions of decree, judgment, order, mesne profits and legal representative are tested year after year, usually in the form of 'which of the following is a decree?'

★Must know

  1. STRUCTURE: Part I - Sections 1-158 (the body of the Code: substantive principles such as res judicata, jurisdiction, execution framework, appeals); Part II - Sections 121-158 deal with rules and miscellaneous matters; First Schedule - Orders I to LI with Rules (procedural details which a High Court may amend under Sec 122-125). Sections cannot be altered by the High Court; Orders and Rules can be amended by the High Courts (Sec 122, 129). Where a rule made by a High Court conflicts with the Code, the Code prevails. The Code was substantially amended in 1976, 1999 (Act 46 of 1999) and 2002 (Act 22 of 2002, in force 1 July 2002), and by the Commercial Courts Act 2015 (as amended in 2018) for commercial disputes.
  2. Procedural law is meant to be the handmaid of justice: procedural rules are to be liberally construed; but rules relating to jurisdiction and limitation are strictly applied. Sec 4: savings of special or local laws; Sec 5: application of the Code to revenue courts; Sec 7: Provincial Small Cause Courts; Sec 8: Presidency small cause courts; Sec 3: subordination of courts (District Court subordinate to HC; every civil court of lower grade subordinate to District Court); Sec 6: pecuniary jurisdiction - no court has jurisdiction over suits whose value exceeds its pecuniary limits.
  3. Sec 2(2) DECREE: the formal expression of an adjudication which, so far as regards the court expressing it, conclusively determines the rights of the parties with regard to all or any of the matters in controversy in the suit and may be either preliminary or final. It shall be deemed to include the rejection of a plaint and the determination of any question within Sec 144, but shall not include (a) any adjudication from which an appeal lies as an appeal from an order, or (b) any order of dismissal for default. Explanation: a decree is preliminary when further proceedings have to be taken before the suit can be completely disposed of; it is final when such adjudication completely disposes of the suit; it may be partly preliminary and partly final. ESSENTIALS: (1) an adjudication; (2) in a suit; (3) conclusive determination of rights; (4) formal expression. Types: preliminary (e.g., partition, mortgage, administration, possession with mesne profits - Order 20 Rules 12, 13, 15, 18); final; partly preliminary-partly final; deemed decrees (rejection of plaint; determination of questions under Sec 144; and orders under Order 21 Rules 98 and 100, which Order 21 Rule 103 treats as decrees); ex parte decree; consent decree. A decree can be passed only in a suit, and a decree may be appealed as an appeal from decree (Sec 96). See Shyam Sundar Sarma v Pannalal Jaiswal (2005) on the meaning of 'decree'.
  4. Sec 2(9) JUDGMENT: the statement given by the judge of the grounds of a decree or order. Sec 2(14) ORDER: the formal expression of any decision of a civil court which is not a decree. Difference between decree and order: (a) decree is passed in a suit commenced by presentation of a plaint; an order may be passed in a suit or in any other proceeding (application, execution etc.); (b) a decree conclusively determines the rights of parties on matters in controversy; an order may or may not; (c) a decree may be preliminary or final; an order cannot be preliminary; (d) every decree is appealable unless expressly barred; an order is appealable only if specified in Sec 104 and Order 43 Rule 1; (e) a decree has a judgment; an order may or may not have; (f) a second appeal lies on a decree (Sec 100) but not on an order. Judgment vs decree: a judgment contains reasons; a decree follows the judgment and gives the formal relief (Order 20 Rule 6). Under the Letters Patent, 'judgment' has a wider meaning (Shah Babulal Khimji v Jayaben D. Kania, 1981: an order which affects the merits of the controversy or decides a valuable right of the party - 'intermediary or interlocutory judgments').
  5. Sec 2(12) MESNE PROFITS: those profits which the person in wrongful possession of such property actually received or might with ordinary diligence have received therefrom, together with interest on such profits, but shall not include profits due to improvements made by the person in wrongful possession. Mesne profits are awarded as a part of a decree for possession (Order 20 Rule 12): for the period before suit (ascertained or inquiry), and for the period after suit until delivery of possession or relinquishment or three years from the date of the decree, whichever is earlier. Sec 2(11) LEGAL REPRESENTATIVE: a person who in law represents the estate of a deceased person, and includes any person who intermeddles with the estate of the deceased and where a party sues or is sued in a representative character the person on whom the estate devolves on the death of the party so suing or sued. Sec 2(10) JUDGMENT-DEBTOR: any person against whom a decree has been passed or an order capable of execution has been made; Sec 2(3) DECREE-HOLDER: any person in whose favour a decree has been passed or an order capable of execution has been made. Sec 2(17) PUBLIC OFFICER: includes a judge, a person in the service or pay of the Government or entrusted with public duties, officer of a court of justice, person authorised to perform any duty in connection with the administration of justice, etc. Sec 2(4) DISTRICT; Sec 2(5) FOREIGN COURT: a court situated outside India and not established or continued by the authority of the Central Government; Sec 2(6) FOREIGN JUDGMENT: the judgment of a foreign court; Sec 2(15) PLEADER.
  6. Related concepts: 'suit' is not defined in the Code - a civil proceeding instituted by the presentation of a plaint (Hansraj Gupta v Dehra Dun-Mussoorie Electric Tramway, 1933 PC). A 'suit of a civil nature': one in which the principal question relates to a civil right (Sec 9 and Explanations). A 'cause of action': the bundle of facts which it is necessary for the plaintiff to prove in order to succeed (Cooke v Gill; Gurdit Singh v Munsha Singh, 1977). A 'plaint' and 'written statement' are pleadings (Order 6 Rule 1). 'Sue' vs 'apply': suits - plaint; applications - petition.

⚖Landmark cases

Shah Babulal Khimji v Jayaben D. Kania

1981

'Judgment' under Letters Patent - intermediary/ interlocutory judgments; test of finality of the right of a party.

Hansraj Gupta v Dehra Dun-Mussoorie Electric Tramway

1933, PC

A suit is a civil proceeding commenced by the presentation of a plaint.

Hasham Abbas Sayyad v Usman Abbas Sayyad

2007

A decree passed without inherent jurisdiction is a nullity and can be challenged even in execution.

!Exam traps

  • TrapRejection of a plaint IS a decree (deemed); dismissal for default is NOT a decree.
  • TrapOrders under Order 21 Rules 98 and 100 are treated as decrees (Rule 103); since the 1976 amendment, 'determination of any question within Sec 47' is no longer part of the definition of 'decree'.
  • TrapA preliminary decree cannot be an 'order'; orders are never preliminary.
  • TrapMesne profits exclude profits due to improvements by the wrongdoer; they include interest.

?Likely questions

  1. Which of the following is a decree? rejection of a plaint / order of dismissal for default.
  2. Sec 2(12) defines: mesne profits.
  3. Judgment is defined in: Sec 2(9).
  4. Which kind of decree can be partly preliminary and partly final? Explanation to Sec 2(2).
