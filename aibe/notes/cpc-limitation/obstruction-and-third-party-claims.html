Obstruction, Resistance and Third-Party Claims (Order XXI Rules 97-106) and Related Sections

iOverview

Rules 97-103 (resistance/ dispossession) and Rule 102 (transferee pendente lite) are among the most litigated and frequently tested; the Supreme Court has repeatedly reaffirmed that these are meant to avoid multiplicity of proceedings.

★Must know

  1. RULE 97 - RESISTANCE OR OBSTRUCTION TO POSSESSION OF IMMOVABLE PROPERTY: (1) where the holder of a decree for the possession of immovable property or the purchaser of any such property sold in execution of a decree is resisted or obstructed by any person in obtaining possession of the property, he may make an application to the court complaining of such resistance or obstruction. (2) where any application is made under sub-rule (1), the court shall proceed to adjudicate upon the application in accordance with the provisions herein contained. Rule 98 - ORDERS AFTER ADJUDICATION: (1) upon the determination of the questions referred to in Rule 101, the court shall, in accordance with such determination and subject to the provisions of sub-rule (2), (a) make an order allowing the application and directing that the applicant be put into possession of the property or dismissing the application; or (b) pass such other order as, in the circumstances of the case, it may deem fit. (2) where, upon such determination, the court is satisfied that the resistance or obstruction was occasioned without any just cause by the judgment-debtor or by some other person at his instigation or on his behalf, or by any transferee, where such transfer was made during the pendency of the suit or execution proceedings, it shall direct that the applicant be put into possession of the property, and where the applicant is still resisted or obstructed in obtaining possession, the court may also, at the instance of the applicant, order the judgment-debtor, or any person acting at his instigation or on his behalf, to be detained in civil prison for a term which may extend to THIRTY days. Rule 99 - DISPOSSESSION BY DECREE-HOLDER OR PURCHASER: (1) where any person other than the judgment-debtor is dispossessed of immovable property by the holder of a decree for the possession of such property or, where such property has been sold in execution of a decree, by the purchaser thereof, he may make an application to the court complaining of such dispossession. (2) where any such application is made, the court shall proceed to adjudicate upon the application in accordance with the provisions herein contained. Rule 100 - ORDER TO BE PASSED UPON APPLICATION COMPLAINING OF DISPOSSESSION: upon the determination of the questions referred to in Rule 101, the court shall, in accordance with such determination, (a) make an order allowing the application and directing that the applicant be put into the possession of the property or dismissing the application; or (b) pass such other order as, in the circumstances of the case, it may deem fit. RULE 101 - QUESTION TO BE DETERMINED: all questions (including questions relating to right, title or interest in the property) arising between the parties to a proceeding on an application under Rule 97 or Rule 99 or their representatives, and relevant to the adjudication of the application, shall be determined by the court dealing with the application, and not by a separate suit, and for this purpose, the court shall, notwithstanding anything to the contrary contained in any other law for the time being in force, be deemed to have jurisdiction to decide such questions. RULE 102 - RULES NOT APPLICABLE TO TRANSFEREE PENDENTE LITE: nothing in Rules 98 and 100 shall apply to resistance or obstruction in execution of a decree for the possession of immovable property by a person to whom the judgment-debtor has transferred the property after the institution of the suit in which the decree was passed or to the dispossession of any such person. Explanation: in this rule, 'transfer' includes a transfer by operation of law. RULE 103 - ORDERS TO BE TREATED AS DECREES: where any application has been adjudicated upon under Rule 98 or Rule 100, the order made thereon shall have the same force and be subject to the same conditions as to an appeal or otherwise as if it were a decree (so appealable under Sec 96; the Code treats it as a decree). Rule 104: order under Rule 101 or Rule 103 to be subject to the result of any suit (pending suit); Rules 105-106: hearing of the application; where a party does not appear, the court may dismiss the application or proceed ex parte, and the aggrieved party may apply within 30 days to set aside such order. Sec 146: proceedings by or against representatives; Sec 47 explanation; Order 21 Rule 35 delivery of possession.
  2. PRINCIPLES: (1) Rules 97-103 provide a COMPLETE CODE for adjudication of claims of third parties (and others) resisting or obstructing execution of a decree for possession - all questions of right, title and interest are decided in the execution proceedings itself (Rule 101), not by a separate suit; Brahmdeo Chaudhary v Rishikesh Prasad Jaiswal (1997): the executing court can adjudicate upon the claim of a third party and whether such person is bound by the decree; the court must not dismiss the application on the basis that it must be tried by a separate suit; Bhanwar Lal v Satyanarain (1995): Rule 97 'any person' includes the judgment-debtor and third parties; Noorduddin v Dr. K.L. Anand (1995): the executing court's order under Rule 98 must be after a proper adjudication; Silverline Forum (P) Ltd v Rajiv Trust (1998): an obstructionist who is a stranger claiming independent title can be heard under Rule 97; after adjudication the order is appealable (Rule 103). (2) A TRANSFEREE PENDENTE LITE cannot be heard as a third-party claimant under Rules 98 and 100 because he is bound by the decree under Sec 52 TPA (lis pendens): the person who purchased from the judgment-debtor after the institution of the suit is bound; Rule 102 exclusion applies; however, in 2025 the Supreme Court clarified that Rule 102 does not bar a bona fide purchaser who claims through an independent third party and not from the judgment-debtor (verify the exact ruling) - the court looks at whether the person claims through the JD. (3) Rule 58 vs Rules 97-101: Rule 58 for attachment claims; Rules 97-101 for possession. (4) Rule 99-100: a person dispossessed (not the JD) can apply; a person who claims independently and was not a party to the suit may also resist. (5) Third party remedies where a decree for money is executed: Rule 58 (claim), Rule 63 (suit). (6) Auction purchaser's rights: Rule 94 certificate; Rule 95: delivery of possession in the occupancy of JD; Order 21 Rule 96 - symbolic delivery where in the possession of a tenant. (7) Periyammal v V. Rajamani (2025 INSC 329): on Rules 97-103 the Court stressed that frivolous third-party obstruction should be dealt with firmly; costs; and a six-month timeline.
  3. OTHER EXECUTION-RELATED PROVISIONS: Sec 144 (restitution) - see Module 6; Sec 146 - proceedings by or against representatives: where the provisions of this Code require anything to be done by or against any person, such provisions shall be deemed to include a reference to any person claiming under him; Sec 150: transfer of business (the court's business transferred is to be done by the court to which it is transferred); Sec 141: miscellaneous proceedings - the procedure provided in the Code in regard to suits shall be followed, as far as it can be made applicable, in all proceedings in any court of civil jurisdiction (does not include proceedings under Art 226 - explanation: the Explanation added in 1976 states that 'proceedings' doesn't include any proceeding under Art 226 of the Constitution); Sec 145: enforcement of liability of surety (the surety's liability under a bond can be enforced as if the surety had become liable as a party to the decree, without a separate suit: Sec 145); Sec 148: enlargement of time (see Module 6); Sec 149: power to make up deficiency of court-fees. Execution against the Government: Sec 82; Sec 60(1) exemptions; The Supreme Court has noted that Sec 60 allows attachment of JD's property and that the decree-holder may seek assistance of the court to discover assets (Rule 41).

⚖Landmark cases

Brahmdeo Chaudhary v Rishikesh Prasad Jaiswal

1997

Executing court must adjudicate third-party obstruction claims under Rules 97-101; separate suit barred.

Silverline Forum v Rajiv Trust

1998

Third party obstructions; scope of Rule 97-101 and who may raise.

Bhanwar Lal v Satyanarain

1995

Obstruction by a person not bound by the decree; executing court's duty.

!Exam traps

  • TrapRules 97-103: all questions of right, title or interest of the applicant/ obstructor are decided by the executing court itself (Rule 101), not by a separate suit.
  • TrapRule 102: a transferee pendente lite from the JD cannot invoke Rules 98 and 100.
  • TrapOrders under Rules 98 and 100 are treated as DECREES (Rule 103) - appealable.
  • TrapRule 98(2): detention up to 30 days for obstruction without just cause.
  • TrapRule 58 (claim to attached property) applies to attachment; Rule 97 to possession decrees.

?Likely questions

  1. Resistance or obstruction to possession of immovable property: Order 21 Rule 97.
  2. Questions of title in obstruction proceedings are decided under Rule 101.
  3. Rule 102: Rules 98 and 100 not applicable to transferee pendente lite.
  4. Orders under Rule 98/100 are treated as decrees: Rule 103.
