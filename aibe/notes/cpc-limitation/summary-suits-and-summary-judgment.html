Summary Procedure and Commercial Summary Judgment (Order XXXVII, Order XIII-A)

High yield1–2 Qs expected7 min readOrder 37 Rules 1-7; Order 13A (Commercial Courts Act); Order 15A

iOverview

Order 37 (leave to defend) with the IDBI Trusteeship principles is tested regularly; summary judgment under the Commercial Courts Act is a newer hook.

★Must know

  1. ORDER XXXVII applies to (a) High Courts, City Civil Courts and Courts of Small Causes, and (b) other courts (the High Court may restrict its operation to specified categories). Rule 1(2): it applies to (a) suits upon bills of exchange, hundis and promissory notes; (b) suits in which the plaintiff seeks only to recover a debt or liquidated demand in money payable by the defendant, with or without interest, arising (i) on a written contract; or (ii) on an enactment, where the sum sought to be recovered is a fixed sum of money or in the nature of a debt other than a penalty; or (iii) on a guarantee, where the claim against the principal is in respect of a debt or liquidated demand only. Rule 2: INSTITUTION OF SUMMARY SUITS: a suit to which this Order applies may, if the plaintiff desires to proceed hereunder, be instituted by presenting a plaint which shall contain (a) a specific averment to the effect that the suit is filed under this Order; (b) that no relief which does not fall within the ambit of this rule has been claimed in the plaint; (c) the inscription, immediately below the number of the suit in the title of the suit, that the suit is under Order XXXVII of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908.
  2. Rule 3 - PROCEDURE FOR THE APPEARANCE OF DEFENDANT: (1) in a suit to which this Order applies, the plaintiff shall, together with the summons under Rule 2 of Order V, serve on the defendant a copy of the plaint and annexures thereto and the defendant may, at any time within TEN DAYS of such service, enter an appearance either in person or by pleader and, in either case, he shall file in court an address for service of notices on him. (4) if the defendant enters an appearance, the plaintiff shall thereafter serve on the defendant a SUMMONS FOR JUDGMENT in Form No. 4A in Appendix B, returnable not less than 10 days from the date of service supported by an affidavit verifying the cause of action and the amount claimed and stating that in his belief there is no defence to the suit. (5) the defendant may, at any time within TEN DAYS of service of such summons for judgment, by affidavit or otherwise disclosing such facts as may be deemed sufficient to entitle him to defend, apply on such summons for LEAVE TO DEFEND such suit, and leave to defend may be granted to him unconditionally or subject to such terms as may appear to the court or judge to be just: provided that leave to defend shall not be refused unless the court is satisfied that the facts disclosed by the defendant do not indicate that he has a substantial defence to raise or that the defence intended to be put up by the defendant is frivolous or vexatious: provided further that, where a part of the amount claimed by the plaintiff is admitted by the defendant to be due from him, leave to defend the suit shall not be granted unless the amount so admitted to be due is deposited by the defendant in court. (6) at the hearing of such summons for judgment, (a) if the defendant has not applied for leave to defend, or if such application has been made and is refused, the plaintiff shall be entitled to judgment forthwith; or (b) if the defendant is permitted to defend as to the whole or any part of the claim, the court or judge may direct him to give such security, and within such time as may be fixed by the court or judge, and that, on failure to give such security within the time specified by the court or judge or to carry out such other directions as may have been given by the court or judge, the plaintiff shall be entitled to judgment forthwith. Rule 4: POWER TO SET ASIDE DECREE - after decree the court may, under special circumstances, set aside the decree, and if necessary stay or set aside execution, and may give leave to the defendant to appear to the summons and to defend the suit, if it seems reasonable to the court so to do and on such terms as the court thinks fit. Rule 7: save as provided in the Order, the procedure in ordinary suits applies.
  3. PRINCIPLES FOR LEAVE TO DEFEND (IDBI Trusteeship Services Ltd v Hubtown Ltd, 2017, summarising Mechelec Engineers & Manufacturers v Basic Equipment Corporation, 1976): (a) if the defendant satisfies the court that he has a good defence to the claim on its merits, the defendant is entitled to unconditional leave to defend; (b) if the defendant raises a triable issue indicating that he has a fair or bona fide or reasonable defence, although not a positively good defence, the defendant is ordinarily entitled to unconditional leave to defend; (c) even if the defendant raises triable issues, if the court has reasonable doubt about the bona fides of the defence, or genuineness of the defence, the court may impose conditions at the time of granting leave to defend (e.g., deposit of the whole or part of the claimed amount or furnishing security); (d) if the defendant has no defence or the defence set up is illusory/ sham/ practically moonshine, the court may refuse leave or grant conditional leave; (e) the court cannot dismiss the application simply because the defence is 'weak' - a plausible defence is enough. Where the defendant admits part of the claim, he must deposit it. Order 37 is not available for unliquidated damages, specific performance, injunction. Limitation for Rule 4 application: 30 days (Art 123 by analogy). The summary suit is a means of expeditious recovery for a liquidated claim; B.L. Kashyap & Sons v JMS Steels & Power Corporation (2022): the claim must be a debt or liquidated demand - and Order 37 applies to a written contract containing price; Milkhiram (India) v Chamanlal Bros (1965): scope of 'written contract'.
  4. ORDER XIII-A - SUMMARY JUDGMENT (Commercial Courts Act 2015, as amended in 2018): applies to commercial disputes; the court may give a summary judgment against a plaintiff or defendant on a claim if it considers that (a) the plaintiff has no real prospect of succeeding on the claim or the defendant has no real prospect of successfully defending the claim, as the case may be; and (b) there is no other compelling reason why the claim should not be disposed of before recording of oral evidence (Rule 3). The application may be made after the summons has been served and before the issues are framed (Rule 2); the court may grant conditional orders (deposit, security) - Rule 7; costs - Rule 8. Distinguish: Order 37 is for liquidated money claims in all courts notified; Order 13A is for all commercial disputes of specified value irrespective of nature of claim and applies even for defendants. Case management hearing (Order 15-A - commercial courts): the court frames issues, fixes timelines for filing affidavits, schedules the trial (Order 15-A Rule 6). Courts also emphasise time-bound trials: Sec 16 and Sec 12A (pre-institution mediation) of the Commercial Courts Act.

!Exam traps

  • TrapOrder 37: appearance within 10 days of service of summons; leave to defend within 10 days of service of summons for judgment.
  • TrapWhere part of the claim is admitted, the admitted amount must be deposited.
  • TrapOrder 37 is for 'liquidated demand' - not for unliquidated damages.
  • TrapOrder 13A is under the Commercial Courts Act - 'no real prospect of succeeding'.
  • TrapRule 4: decree can be set aside under special circumstances.

?Likely questions

  1. Summary suit: Order 37.
  2. Time for appearance in a summary suit: 10 days.
  3. Unconditional leave to defend where the defendant shows a triable issue.
  4. Summary judgment in commercial disputes: Order XIII-A.
