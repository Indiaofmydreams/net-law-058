Appearance, Non-appearance and Ex Parte Decrees (Order IX)

iOverview

Order IX Rules 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 13 and the limitation for setting aside are examined nearly every year.

★Must know

  1. Rule 1: the parties shall attend the court in person or by their pleaders on the day fixed in the summons for the defendant to appear and answer, and the suit shall then be heard unless the hearing is adjourned to a future day fixed by the court. Rule 2: dismissal of suit where summons not served in consequence of the plaintiff's failure to pay costs (the court may dismiss when the failure to serve is due to non-payment of process fee). Rule 3: WHERE NEITHER PARTY APPEARS, the court may make an order that the suit be dismissed. Rule 4: PLAINTIFF MAY BRING FRESH SUIT OR COURT MAY RESTORE SUIT TO FILE - where a suit is dismissed under Rule 2 or Rule 3, the plaintiff may (subject to the law of limitation) bring a fresh suit; or he may apply for an order to set the dismissal aside, and if he satisfies the court that there was sufficient cause for his not paying the court-fee and postal charges (if any) required within the time fixed before the issue of the summons, or for his non-appearance, as the case may be, the court shall make an order setting aside the dismissal and shall appoint a day for proceeding with the suit. Rule 5: dismissal of suit where plaintiff, after summons returned unserved, fails for SEVEN DAYS to apply for fresh summons (the 2002 amendment reduced the period to seven days).
  2. Rule 6 - PROCEDURE WHEN ONLY PLAINTIFF APPEARS: (1) where the plaintiff appears and the defendant does not appear when the suit is called on for hearing, then (a) if it is proved that the summons was duly served, the court may proceed ex parte; (b) if it is not proved that the summons was duly served, the court shall direct a second summons to be issued and served on the defendant; (c) if it is proved that the summons was served on the defendant, but not in sufficient time to enable him to appear and answer on the day fixed in the summons, the court shall postpone the hearing of the suit to a future day to be fixed by the court, and shall direct notice of such day to be given to the defendant. (2) where it is owing to the plaintiff's neglect or omission that the summons was not duly served or was not served in sufficient time, the court shall order the plaintiff to pay the costs occasioned by the postponement. Rule 7: procedure where defendant appears on the day of adjourned hearing and assigns good cause for previous non-appearance - the court may, upon such terms as to costs or otherwise as it directs, permit him to appear and answer the claim as if he had appeared on the day fixed for his appearance. Rule 8: PROCEDURE WHERE DEFENDANT ONLY APPEARS: where the defendant appears and the plaintiff does not appear when the suit is called on for hearing, the court shall make an order that the suit be dismissed, unless the defendant admits the claim or part thereof, in which case the court shall pass a decree against the defendant upon such admission, and, where part only of the claim has been admitted, shall dismiss the suit so far as it relates to the remainder. Rule 9: DECREE AGAINST PLAINTIFF BY DEFAULT BARS FRESH SUIT - where a suit is wholly or partly dismissed under Rule 8, the plaintiff shall be precluded from bringing a fresh suit in respect of the same cause of action. But he may apply for an order to set the dismissal aside, and if he satisfies the court that there was sufficient cause for his non-appearance when the suit was called on for hearing, the court shall make an order setting aside the dismissal upon such terms as to costs or otherwise as it thinks fit, and shall appoint a day for proceeding with the suit. (Contrast: dismissal under Rule 3 does NOT bar fresh suit (Rule 4) - dismissal under Rule 8 does (Rule 9).) Rules 10-12: consequences of non-attendance of one of several plaintiffs/ defendants; the court may proceed against others or dismiss; Rule 12: consequences of non-compliance with order (the plaintiff failing to appear is subject to dismissal; defendant is subject to ex parte).
  3. RULE 13 - SETTING ASIDE DECREE EX PARTE AGAINST DEFENDANT: in any case in which a decree is passed ex parte against a defendant, he may apply to the court by which the decree was passed for an order to set it aside; and if he satisfies the court that the summons was not duly served, or that he was prevented by any sufficient cause from appearing when the suit was called on for hearing, the court shall make an order setting aside the decree as against him upon such terms as to costs, payment into court or otherwise as it thinks fit, and shall appoint a day for proceeding with the suit. PROVISO 1: where the decree is of such a nature that it cannot be set aside as against such defendant only it may be set aside as against all or any of the other defendants also. PROVISO 2 (1976): no court shall set aside a decree passed ex parte merely on the ground that there has been an irregularity in the service of summons, if it is satisfied, on the evidence before it, that the defendant had notice of the date of hearing and had sufficient time to appear and answer the plaintiff's claim. EXPLANATION (2002): where there has been an appeal against a decree passed ex parte under this rule, and the appeal has been disposed of on any ground other than the ground that the appellant has withdrawn the appeal, no application shall lie under this rule for setting aside that ex parte decree. Rule 14: no decree shall be set aside on any such application as aforesaid unless notice thereof has been served on the opposite party.
  4. REMEDIES AGAINST AN EX PARTE DECREE: (1) application under Order 9 Rule 13 before the same court; (2) appeal under Sec 96(2) on merits (against ex parte decree); (3) review under Order 47; (4) suit on the ground of fraud (Sec 44 Evidence Act; now BSA); (5) revision/ Art 227. The defendant can pursue Rule 13 AND appeal concurrently; but the Explanation to Rule 13 bars Rule 13 once an appeal has been disposed of on merits (Rani Choudhury v Suraj Jit Choudhury, 1982: both remedies are concurrent; Bhanu Kumar Jain v Archana Kumar, 2005: in an appeal against an ex parte decree the defendant can only argue that the plaintiff's case was not proved but cannot argue facts that he could have proved if he had appeared; he must separately demonstrate sufficient cause under Rule 13). LIMITATION: application under Rule 13 - THIRTY DAYS from the date of the decree, or, where the summons was not duly served, from the date when the applicant had knowledge of the decree (Art 123 Limitation Act); Sec 5 applies. Sufficient cause: bona fide reasons, liberal approach (Parimal v Veena, 2011: the 'sufficient cause' to be liberally construed; Rafiq v Munshilal, 1981: a party shouldn't suffer for the default of his advocate - the Court held that the party is not to suffer for the negligence of the counsel in appropriate cases, though later cases (Salil Dutta v T.M. & M.C. Pvt Ltd, 1993) caution that the party must be diligent).
  5. Sangram Singh v Election Tribunal, Kotah (1955): the defendant who has not filed a written statement but appears can participate in the proceedings (cross-examine and address) - the court should not treat as ex parte; Arjun Singh v Mohindra Kumar (1964): Order 9 Rule 7 - appearance of the defendant at the adjourned hearing, with good cause for the previous non-appearance; Ex parte decree is not a nullity but an irregularity; it is valid until set aside. Where a defendant appears and then absents himself, the court may proceed ex parte under Order 17 Rule 2 and Order 9 Rule 6. Order 9 Rule 13 vs Rule 7: Rule 7 applies before the ex parte hearing begins and Rule 13 after the decree.

!Exam traps

  • TrapDismissal under Rule 3 does NOT bar a fresh suit; dismissal under Rule 8 DOES (Rule 9).
  • TrapRule 13 limitation: 30 days from decree (or knowledge if summons not duly served).
  • TrapIrregularity in service is not enough if defendant had notice (Proviso 2).
  • TrapAfter an appeal against the ex parte decree is decided on merits, Rule 13 application does not lie.
  • TrapRule 5: seven days to apply for fresh summons.

?Likely questions

  1. Setting aside an ex parte decree: Order 9 Rule 13.
  2. Limitation for setting aside ex parte decree: 30 days.
  3. Dismissal where neither party appears: Order 9 Rule 3; fresh suit allowed.
  4. Procedure when only the plaintiff appears: Rule 6.
