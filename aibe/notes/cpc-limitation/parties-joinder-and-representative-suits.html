Parties to Suits and Joinder (Order I, Order II) and Representative Suits

iOverview

Joinder of parties, necessary vs proper parties, misjoinder/ non-joinder, representative suits (Order I Rule 8) and the rule against splitting claims (Order II Rule 2).

★Must know

  1. ORDER I RULE 1 - JOINDER OF PLAINTIFFS: all persons may be joined in one suit as plaintiffs in whom any right to relief in respect of or arising out of the same act or transaction or series of acts or transactions is alleged to exist, whether jointly, severally or in the alternative, where, if such persons brought separate suits, any common question of law or fact would arise. Rule 3 - JOINDER OF DEFENDANTS: all persons may be joined as defendants against whom any right to relief in respect of or arising out of the same act or transaction or series of acts or transactions is alleged to exist, whether jointly, severally, or in the alternative, where, if separate suits were brought against such persons, any common question of law or fact would arise. Rule 3A: separate trials - if it appears that joinder may embarrass or delay trial, the court may order separate trials. Rule 4: judgment may be given for or against one or more of joint parties. Rule 5: defendant need not be interested in all the relief claimed. Rule 6: joint contractors - plaintiff may join. Rule 7: when plaintiff in doubt from whom redress is to be obtained (alternative defendants).
  2. Rule 9 - MISJOINDER AND NON-JOINDER: no suit shall be defeated by reason of the misjoinder or non-joinder of parties, and the court may in every suit deal with the matter in controversy so far as regards the rights and interests of the parties actually before it. Proviso: nothing in this rule shall apply to non-joinder of a NECESSARY PARTY. Rule 10 - SUIT IN NAME OF WRONG PLAINTIFF; ADDITION AND STRIKING OUT OF PARTIES: (1) where a suit has been instituted in the name of the wrong person as plaintiff, or where it is doubtful whether it has been instituted in the name of the right plaintiff, the court may at any stage of the suit, if satisfied that the suit has been instituted through a bona fide mistake, and that it is necessary for the determination of the real matter in dispute so to do, order any other person to be substituted or added as plaintiff upon such terms as the court thinks just. (2) the court may at any stage of the proceedings, either upon or without the application of either party, and on such terms as may appear to the court to be just, order that the name of any party improperly joined, whether as plaintiff or defendant, be struck out, and that the name of any person who ought to have been joined, whether as plaintiff or defendant, or whose presence before the court may be necessary in order to enable the court effectually and completely to adjudicate upon and settle all the questions involved in the suit, be added. (3) no person shall be added as a plaintiff suing without a next friend or as the next friend of a plaintiff under any disability without his consent. (4) where a defendant is added the plaint shall, unless the court otherwise directs, be amended in such manner as may be necessary, and amended copies of the summons and of the plaint shall be served on the new defendant and, if the court thinks fit, on the original defendants. (5) subject to the provisions of the Limitation Act 1963, Sec 21, the proceedings as against any person added as defendant shall be deemed to have begun only on the service of the summons. Rule 13: all objections on the ground of non-joinder or misjoinder of parties shall be taken at the earliest possible opportunity and, in all cases where issues are settled, at or before such settlement, unless the ground of objection has subsequently arisen, and any such objection not so taken shall be deemed to have been waived.
  3. NECESSARY PARTY vs PROPER PARTY: Udit Narain Singh Malpaharia v Additional Member, Board of Revenue (1963): a NECESSARY party is one without whom no order can be made effectively; a PROPER party is one in whose absence an effective order can be made but whose presence is necessary for a complete and final decision on the question involved. TWO TESTS for a necessary party: (1) there must be a right to some relief against such party in respect of the matter involved in the proceeding in question; (2) it should not be possible to pass an effective decree in the absence of such a party. Kasturi v Iyyamperumal (2005): in a suit for specific performance of an agreement for sale, a person who claims title adverse to the vendor is not a necessary party as the only issue is whether the agreement is enforceable against the vendor; Mumbai International Airport (P) Ltd v Regency Convention Centre & Hotels (2010): the plaintiff is the dominus litis and cannot be compelled to sue a person against whom he claims no relief - the court may add a party only if he is a necessary or proper party; a person who has no direct interest in the subject-matter cannot be added merely because he may be affected indirectly; Sumtibai v Paras Finance Co (2007); Ramesh Hirachand Kundanmal v Municipal Corporation of Greater Bombay (1992): the court has wide discretion to add a person whose presence is necessary to adjudicate effectively. Transposition of parties: the court may transpose a defendant to plaintiff (Order 23 Rule 1A).
  4. ORDER I RULE 8 - REPRESENTATIVE SUITS: (1) where there are numerous persons having the same interest in one suit, one or more of such persons may, with the permission of the court, sue or be sued, or may defend such suit, on behalf of, or for the benefit of, all persons so interested; but the court shall in such case give, at the plaintiff's expense, notice of the institution of the suit to all such persons either by personal service or, where from the number of persons or any other cause such service is not reasonably practicable, by public advertisement, as the court in each case may direct. (2) the court may direct that one or more of the persons may sue or be sued... (4) any person on whose behalf or for whose benefit a suit is instituted, or defended, under sub-rule (1), may apply to the court to be made a party to such suit. (5) where any person suing or defending in any such suit does not proceed with due diligence in the suit or defence, the court may substitute in his place any other person having the same interest in the suit. (6) a decree passed in a suit under this rule shall be binding on all persons on whose behalf, or for whose benefit, the suit is instituted, or defended, as the case may be (Explanation: the persons not having the same interest as the representative are not bound). Rule 8(6) and Sec 11 Explanation VI. Rule 8 requires 'same interest' (Kumaravelu Chettiar v T.P. Ramaswamy Ayyar, 1933 - test: same interest need not be identical but a common interest); Rule 8 applies to suits concerning rights of many persons, such as members of a community, public rights, or a sect; in a representative suit, withdrawal/ compromise needs the court's permission and notice (Order 23 Rule 3B). Class action: Order I Rule 8 and Sec 91-92 for public interest; Companies Act Sec 245 for class actions in company law.
  5. ORDER II - FRAMING OF SUIT: Rule 1: every suit shall as far as practicable be so framed as to afford ground for final decision upon the subjects in dispute and to prevent further litigation concerning them. RULE 2 - SUIT TO INCLUDE THE WHOLE CLAIM: (1) every suit shall include the whole of the claim which the plaintiff is entitled to make in respect of the cause of action; but a plaintiff may relinquish any portion of his claim in order to bring the suit within the jurisdiction of any court. (2) where a plaintiff omits to sue in respect of, or intentionally relinquishes, any portion of his claim, he shall not afterwards sue in respect of the portion so omitted or relinquished. (3) a person entitled to more than one relief in respect of the same cause of action may sue for all or any of such reliefs; but if he omits, except with the leave of the court, to sue for all such reliefs, he shall not afterwards sue for any relief so omitted. Explanation: an obligation and a collateral security for its performance and successive claims arising under the same obligation shall be deemed respectively to constitute but one cause of action. PRINCIPLES: the bar applies where the later suit is based on the SAME CAUSE OF ACTION as the earlier suit (Gurbux Singh v Bhooralal, 1964: the defendant must plead and prove the bar by producing the earlier plaint; the question whether the cause of action is the same is determined by comparing the pleadings; the bar does not apply where the plaintiff could not have claimed the omitted relief (e.g., relief not available at the time of the first suit)); Sec 11 and Order 2 Rule 2: res judicata is based on decision; Order 2 Rule 2 on omission to claim; Order 2 Rule 2 does not apply to execution proceedings, nor to a case where the first suit is withdrawn with permission (Order 23 Rule 1), nor to suit for possession after a suit for declaration where leave is obtained. Rule 3: joinder of causes of action - the plaintiff may unite in the same suit several causes of action against the same defendant or the same defendants jointly; Rule 4: only certain claims to be joined for recovery of immovable property (mesne profits or arrears of rent, damages for breach of contract under which the property is held, claims for which the plaintiff is entitled to relief arising out of the same transaction); Rule 6: court may order separate trials; Rule 7: objections for misjoinder of causes of action to be taken at the earliest opportunity.
  6. Related: ORDER III - recognised agents and pleaders: Rule 1: any appearance, application or act in or to any court, required or authorised by law to be made or done by a party in such court, may, except where otherwise expressly provided by any law for the time being in force, be made or done by the party in person, or by his recognised agent, or by a pleader appearing, applying or acting, as the case may be, on his behalf; Rule 2: recognised agents - persons holding powers-of-attorney authorising them to make such appearances and applications and do such acts on behalf of parties, and persons carrying on trade or business for and in the names of parties not resident within the local limits of the court; Rule 4: appointment of a pleader (vakalatnama); no pleader shall act for any person in any court unless he has been appointed for the purpose by such person by a document in writing signed by such person; Rule 4(2): the appointment shall be filed in court and shall remain in force until determined with the leave of the court by a writing signed by the client or the pleader; Rule 6: service of process on recognised agent. Order III Rule 1-2 and the power of attorney holder: a power of attorney holder can depose only about what he has personal knowledge of (Janki Vashdeo Bhojwani v Indusind Bank, 2005); he cannot depose for the principal on matters in the personal knowledge of the principal.

⚖Landmark cases

Udit Narain Singh Malpaharia v Additional Member, Board of Revenue

1963

Distinction between necessary and proper parties; two tests for necessary party.

Mumbai International Airport v Regency Convention Centre

2010

Plaintiff is dominus litis; party can be added only if necessary or proper; scope of Order 1 Rule 10(2).

Gurbux Singh v Bhooralal

1964

Order 2 Rule 2: conditions for the bar; defendant must prove the earlier plaint and the same cause of action.

Kasturi v Iyyamperumal

2005

Who is a necessary party in a suit for specific performance.

Janki Vashdeo Bhojwani v Indusind Bank

2005

Power of attorney holder's limits in giving evidence.

!Exam traps

  • TrapNon-joinder of a NECESSARY party is fatal; of a proper party is not.
  • TrapMisjoinder/ non-joinder objections must be taken before settlement of issues (Rule 13).
  • TrapOrder 2 Rule 2 covers omission of RELIEFS and CLAIMS of the same cause of action; the bar applies to a subsequent SUIT.
  • TrapRepresentative suit under Order 1 Rule 8 needs court's permission AND notice to all interested.
  • TrapProceedings against an added defendant are deemed to begin on service of summons (limitation).

?Likely questions

  1. Necessary party test: Udit Narain Singh Malpaharia.
  2. Order 1 Rule 10(2): court can add parties at any stage.
  3. A suit shall include the whole of the claim: Order 2 Rule 2.
  4. Representative suit: Order 1 Rule 8.
  5. Dominus litis: plaintiff.
