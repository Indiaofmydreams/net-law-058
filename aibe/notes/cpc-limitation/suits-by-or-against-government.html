Suits by or against the Government and Public Officers (Secs 79-82, Order XXVII)

High yield2–3 Qs expected8 min readSec 79; Sec 80; Sec 81-82; Order 27; Order 27A; Sec 15(2) Limitation Act

  3. Landmark cases (3)
  4. Exam traps
  5. Likely questions

iOverview

Section 80 notice (two months), Section 79 (parties), Section 82 (execution against Government) and Order 27 are the frequent questions; the 2025 Supreme Court ruling on non-service of Sec 80 notice is a recent hook.

★Must know

  1. Sec 79: in a suit by or against the Government, the authority to be named as plaintiff or defendant shall be (a) in the case of a suit by or against the Central Government, the Union of India; and (b) in the case of a suit by or against a State Government, the State. (See Art 300 of the Constitution.)
  2. Sec 80 - NOTICE: (1) subject to sub-section (2), no suit shall be instituted against the Government (including the Government of the State of Jammu and Kashmir - since 2019, this reference is no longer relevant) or against a public officer in respect of any act purporting to be done by such public officer in his official capacity, until the expiration of TWO MONTHS next after notice in writing has been delivered to, or left at the office of - (a) in the case of a suit against the Central Government, except where it relates to a railway, a Secretary to that Government; (b) in the case of a suit against the Central Government where it relates to a railway, the General Manager of that railway; (c) in the case of a suit against any other State Government, a Secretary to that Government or the Collector of the district; and, in the case of a public officer, delivered to him or left at his office, stating the cause of action, the name, description and place of residence of the plaintiff and the relief which he claims; and the plaint shall contain a statement that such notice has been so delivered or left. (2) A suit to obtain an URGENT OR IMMEDIATE RELIEF against the Government or any public officer in respect of any act purporting to be done by such public officer in his official capacity, may be instituted, with the leave of the court, without serving any notice as required by sub-section (1); but the court shall not grant relief in the suit, whether interim or otherwise, except after giving to the Government or public officer, as the case may be, a reasonable opportunity of showing cause in respect of the relief prayed for in the suit: provided that the court shall, if satisfied, after hearing the parties, that no urgent or immediate relief need be granted in the suit, return the plaint for presentation to it after complying with the requirements of sub-section (1). (3) No suit instituted against the Government or against a public officer in respect of any act purporting to be done by such public officer in his official capacity shall be dismissed merely by reason of any error or defect in the notice referred to in sub-section (1), if in such notice (a) the name, description and residence of the plaintiff had been so given as to enable the appropriate authority or the public officer to identify the person serving the notice and such notice had been delivered or left at the office of the appropriate authority specified in sub-section (1), and (b) the cause of action and the relief claimed by the plaintiff had been substantially indicated.
  3. Object of Sec 80: to give the Government an opportunity to consider the claim and settle it without litigation, avoid unnecessary litigation and public funds. The section is MANDATORY and its terms are to be strictly complied with, subject to Sec 80(3) substantial compliance (Bihari Chowdhary v State of Bihar, 1984: the notice requirement is mandatory; Sec 80 is not an empty formality; State of Andhra Pradesh v Pioneer Builders, 2006). Sec 80 does not apply to (a) suits against Government for injunction or relief that is urgent (Sec 80(2)); (b) suits not 'against Government or public officer' such as suits by Government; (c) writ petitions; (d) ordinarily, suits against statutory corporations or government companies that are not 'the Government'; however, in 2025 the Supreme Court held that failure to serve the mandatory Sec 80 notice before suing a State-owned entity covered by the requirement rendered the resulting decree a nullity, open to challenge even at a later stage (verify the exact ruling). A notice under Sec 80 isn't needed where the suit is against a public officer for an act NOT done in his official capacity. Delay: two months must lapse; a suit filed before the expiry is premature; the defect may be cured by return of plaint and re-presentation. Limitation does not stop running during the notice period (Sec 15(2) Limitation Act - the period of notice is excluded if the notice is required by law: Sec 15(2) Limitation Act: in computing the period of limitation for any suit of which notice has been given, in accordance with the requirements of any enactment in force, the period of such notice shall be excluded).
  4. Sec 81: exemption from arrest and personal appearance - a public officer shall not be arrested under Sec 55 or Order XXI and is exempt from personal appearance in respect of acts in official capacity, unless the court requires. Sec 82: EXECUTION OF DECREE AGAINST GOVERNMENT: (1) where, in a suit by or against the Government or against a public officer in respect of any such act as aforesaid, a decree is passed against the Union of India or a State or, as the case may be, the public officer, such decree shall not be executed unless it remains unsatisfied for the period of THREE MONTHS computed from the date of such decree; Verify the remaining sub-sections of Sec 82 in the bare Code.
  5. ORDER XXVII - SUITS BY OR AGAINST THE GOVERNMENT OR PUBLIC OFFICERS IN THEIR OFFICIAL CAPACITY: Rule 1: persons authorised to act for the Government - the plaint shall be signed and verified by any person whom the Government may, by general or special order, appoint in this behalf, and shall be sufficient for any person so appointed to be acquainted with the facts; Rule 2: persons authorised to act for the Government in respect of suits; Rule 3: persons to be named as plaintiffs or defendants in suits by or against the Government; Rule 4: the Government pleader to be agent of the Government for the purpose of receiving processes (Rule 4: Government pleader is agent for service); Rule 5: appearance of Government - the court shall allow a reasonable time for the Government pleader to receive instructions; Rule 5A: where the suit is against a public officer for damages or other relief and the Government not joined, the Government shall be joined as a party (Rule 5A: where a suit is instituted against a public officer for damages in respect of an act alleged to be done by him in his official capacity, the Government shall be joined as a party to the suit); Rule 5B: duty of the court in suits against the Government or a public officer to assist in arriving at a settlement (Rule 5B: in every suit or proceeding to which the Government or a public officer acting in his official capacity is a party, the court shall make an endeavour at the first hearing of the suit, where it is possible according to the nature and circumstances of the case, to assist the parties in arriving at a settlement in respect of the subject-matter of the suit); Rule 8A: no security shall be required from the Government or from a public officer sued in an official capacity (no security for costs or costs of appeal required); Rule 8B: Government pleader; Order XXVII-A: notice to the Attorney-General or Advocate-General in suits involving substantial question of law as to the interpretation of the Constitution or as to the validity of any statutory instrument (Rule 1: in any suit in which it appears to the court that any such question is involved, the court shall not proceed to determine that question until after notice has been given to the Attorney-General for India (where the question relates to the Constitution of India or validity of a statutory instrument) or the Advocate-General of the State). Government can be sued in tort subject to sovereign immunity doctrine (Kasturi Lal; Nilabati Behera).

⚖Landmark cases

Bihari Chowdhary v State of Bihar

1984

Sec 80 is mandatory; intended to give Government an opportunity to settle claims.

State of Andhra Pradesh v Pioneer Builders

2006

Notice under Sec 80 - mandatory; applies subject to Sec 80(3) on substantial compliance.

Ghanshyam Dass v Dominion of India

1984

Strict compliance with Sec 80; notice must be given to the right authority.

!Exam traps

  • TrapSec 80 notice: two months; suit against Government or public officer for official acts; Sec 80(2) allows urgent relief suits with leave.
  • TrapSec 80(3): errors in notice are not fatal if the plaintiff and cause of action are identifiable.
  • TrapSec 82: decree against Government cannot be executed until three months from decree.
  • TrapSuits by the Government do not need notice.
  • TrapOrder 27A: notice to Attorney-General/ Advocate-General on constitutional questions.

?Likely questions

  1. Notice before suing the Government: two months (Sec 80).
  2. Suit for urgent relief without notice: with the leave of court (Sec 80(2)).
  3. In suits by or against the Central Government, the party named is: Union of India (Sec 79).
  4. Order 27A: notice to Attorney-General for India/ Advocate-General.
