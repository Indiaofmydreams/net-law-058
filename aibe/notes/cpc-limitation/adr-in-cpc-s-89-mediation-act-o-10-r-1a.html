Alternative Dispute Resolution in the CPC (Sec 89, Order X Rules 1A-1C), Mediation Act 2023 and Pre-Institution Mediation

High yield1–2 Qs expected6 min readSec 89; Order 10 Rules 1A-1C; Mediation Act 2023; Commercial Courts Act Sec 12A; Legal Services Authorities Act Sec 20-21

iOverview

Sec 89 and Afcons Infrastructure are standard; newer questions test the Mediation Act 2023 and Sec 12A of the Commercial Courts Act.

★Must know

  1. Sec 89 - SETTLEMENT OF DISPUTES OUTSIDE THE COURT: (1) where it appears to the court that there exist elements of a settlement which may be acceptable to the parties, the court shall formulate the terms of settlement and give them to the parties for their observations and, after receiving the observations of the parties, the court may reformulate the terms of a possible settlement and refer the same for (a) arbitration; (b) conciliation; (c) judicial settlement including settlement through Lok Adalat; or (d) mediation. (2) where a dispute has been referred (a) for arbitration or conciliation, the provisions of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996 shall apply as if the proceedings for arbitration or conciliation were referred for settlement under the provisions of that Act; (b) to Lok Adalat, the court shall refer the same to the Lok Adalat in accordance with the provisions of sub-section (1) of Sec 20 of the Legal Services Authorities Act 1987 and all other provisions of that Act shall apply in respect of the dispute so referred; (c) for judicial settlement, the court shall refer the same to a suitable institution or person and such institution or person shall be deemed to be a Lok Adalat and all the provisions of the Legal Services Authorities Act 1987 shall apply as if the dispute were referred to a Lok Adalat under the provisions of that Act; (d) for mediation, the court shall effect a compromise between the parties and shall follow such procedure as may be prescribed.
  2. ORDER X RULES 1A-1C: Rule 1A: after recording the admissions and denials, the court shall direct the parties to the suit to opt either mode of the settlement outside the court as specified in sub-section (1) of Sec 89; on the option of the parties, the court shall fix the date of appearance before such forum or authority as may be opted by the parties. Rule 1B: where a suit is referred to a forum or authority, the parties shall appear before such forum or authority for conciliation of the suit. Rule 1C: where the suit is referred under Rule 1A and the presiding officer of the conciliation forum or authority is satisfied that it would not be proper in the interest of justice to proceed with the matter further, then, it shall refer the matter again to the court and parties shall appear before the court on the date fixed by the court.
  3. SALEM ADVOCATE BAR ASSOCIATION v UNION OF INDIA (2005): the Supreme Court upheld the validity of Sec 89 (and the 2002 amendments) and, to remedy a drafting error, directed that the definitions of 'judicial settlement' and 'mediation' in Sec 89(2) be read as interchanged - i.e., Sec 89(2)(c) and (d) read as: (c) for mediation (the court shall effect a compromise between the parties and follow the procedure prescribed) and (d) for judicial settlement (the court shall refer to a suitable institution or person, deemed to be a Lok Adalat). The Court also framed Model Rules for mediation and directed High Courts to frame rules. AFCONS INFRASTRUCTURE v CHERIAN VARKEY CONSTRUCTION (2010): (1) the court is not required to obtain the consent of parties for referring a case to mediation, conciliation or Lok Adalat - consent is necessary only for ARBITRATION (Sec 89 vs Arbitration Act Sec 7); (2) the court should not apply Sec 89 mechanically; (3) cases normally NOT suitable for ADR: representative suits under Order 1 Rule 8 (public interest); disputes relating to election to public offices; cases involving grant of probate/ letters of administration; cases involving serious and specific allegations of fraud, fabrication of documents, forgery, impersonation, coercion; cases requiring protection of courts (claims against minors, deities and mentally challenged persons); cases involving prosecution for criminal offences; (4) cases normally suitable for ADR: all cases relating to trade, commerce and contracts; all cases arising from strained or broken relationships (family disputes, partition, matrimonial); all cases where there is a need for continuation of the pre-existing relationship (e.g., disputes between landlord and tenant, neighbours, employers/ employees); all cases relating to tortious liability including motor accident claims; all consumer disputes including disputes relating to deficiency of service. Sec 89 reference does not oust the court; if ADR fails, the suit continues.
  4. MEDIATION ACT 2023: enacted in September 2023 (assent 14 September 2023) to promote mediation, including institutional mediation, pre-litigation mediation and online mediation; provides for community mediation, enforceability of mediated settlement agreements (MSA) as if they were a decree/ judgment (Sec 27), registration of MSA with the Mediation Council of India (MCI), time limit of 180 days (extendable by 180 days) for completion of mediation, confidentiality; Sec 5: parties may voluntarily and before filing any suit or proceedings in any court, attempt to settle by pre-litigation mediation; the Act excludes disputes involving prosecution for criminal offences, matters affecting rights of third parties, disputes relating to land acquisition, etc. (First Schedule - disputes not fit for mediation). The Act is being brought into force in phases (verify). COMMERCIAL COURTS ACT 2015, Sec 12A (inserted in 2018): a suit, which does not contemplate any urgent interim relief under the Act, shall not be instituted unless the plaintiff exhausts the remedy of pre-institution mediation in accordance with such manner and procedure as may be prescribed by rules made by the Central Government (Commercial Courts (Pre-Institution Mediation and Settlement) Rules 2018); Patil Automation (P) Ltd v Rakheja Engineers (P) Ltd (2022): Sec 12A is MANDATORY; a suit instituted without pre-institution mediation is liable to be rejected under Order 7 Rule 11 (but prospective from 20 August 2022). The Legal Services Authorities Act 1987 and Lok Adalats (award deemed a decree of a civil court, final - Sec 21; no appeal; challenge only by writ). Plea bargaining and arbitration (Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996) complete the framework; settlement through Sec 89 can also be a conciliation under Part III of the Arbitration Act; court-annexed mediation centres.

⚖Landmark cases

Salem Advocate Bar Association v Union of India

2005

Sec 89 valid; drafting error of Sec 89(2)(c)-(d) corrected by interchanging mediation and judicial settlement; model rules.

Afcons Infrastructure v Cherian Varkey Construction

2010

Consent not required for mediation/ conciliation/ Lok Adalat; list of cases suitable and unsuitable for ADR.

Patil Automation v Rakheja Engineers

2022

Pre-institution mediation under Sec 12A is mandatory in commercial suits (prospective).

!Exam traps

  • TrapSec 89 lists FOUR modes: arbitration, conciliation, judicial settlement (including Lok Adalat), mediation.
  • TrapConsent of parties is needed only for ARBITRATION referral; not for mediation/ conciliation/ Lok Adalat (Afcons).
  • TrapSec 12A Commercial Courts Act: pre-institution mediation mandatory unless urgent interim relief is sought.
  • TrapLok Adalat award is deemed a decree and is final - no appeal.

?Likely questions

  1. Settlement of disputes outside court: Sec 89.
  2. Sec 89(1): arbitration, conciliation, judicial settlement, mediation.
  3. Salem Advocate Bar Association v UoI (2005): validity of Sec 89.
  4. Pre-institution mediation: Sec 12A Commercial Courts Act.
