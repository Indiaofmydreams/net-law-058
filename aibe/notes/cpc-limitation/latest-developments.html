Code of Civil Procedure and Limitation

Latest developments

Recent judgments, amendments and institutional changes that are favourite current-affairs questions. Notes cover developments up to 18 Nov 2026; anything tagged Verify should be checked against the official source.

Execution petitions to be disposed of within six months

Mar 2025 - 2026

Periyammal v V. Rajamani (2025 INSC 329, 6 March 2025): the Supreme Court directed High Courts to ensure pending execution petitions are decided within six months and that executing courts finish execution within six months of filing, with presiding officers answerable for default. Later hearings in 2025-26 monitored compliance; reported pendency was about 8 lakh execution petitions; the Court also discouraged withdrawal and refiling of execution petitions without good reason and reinforced use of Order 21 Rule 41 (affidavit of assets) and Rules 97-101.

Why it matters: Execution timelines; Order 21 Rule 41; Rules 97-103; Rahul S. Shah (2021).

Interlocutory res judicata

15 May 2026

B.S. Lalitha v Bhuvanesh (2026 INSC 499): the principle of res judicata applies between two stages of the same litigation; a second application under Order 7 Rule 11 on the same issue is barred, and a defendant cannot raise different sub-clauses of Rule 11 in successive applications when they could have been raised earlier (Explanation IV to Sec 11).

Why it matters: Sec 11; constructive res judicata; Order 7 Rule 11.

Order 7 Rule 11 - boundaries

2025-26

Res judicata ordinarily cannot ground rejection of a plaint (July 2025); a plaint can be rejected where the bar of limitation is apparent from the plaint itself (N. Asha Devi v R. Aravind Kumar, 2026 INSC 908 - verify); valuation/ court-fee questions requiring evidence may be decided after trial (Neelam Sharma v Amita Passan, 2026 INSC 913 - verify); partial rejection of plaint remains impermissible (Sejal Glass, 2018); refusal to reject is not appealable.

Why it matters: Order 7 Rule 11(a)-(d); rejection of plaint as a decree.

Section 80 notice

2025

The Supreme Court held that failure to serve the mandatory Sec 80 notice before suing a State-owned entity covered by the requirement renders the resulting decree a nullity open to challenge even later (verify the entity and facts); Sec 80(2) leave route for urgent relief; Sec 80(3) substantial compliance.

Why it matters: Sec 80; two months; urgent relief with leave.

Order 21 Rule 102

2025

The Court clarified that Rule 102 (no remedy for transferee pendente lite from the judgment-debtor) does not bar a bona fide purchaser claiming through an independent third party and not through the judgment-debtor (verify).

Why it matters: Order 21 Rules 97-103; Sec 52 TPA.

Order 22 and abatement

2025

The Supreme Court explained the correct procedure for substituting legal heirs, setting aside abatement and condoning delay (Order 22 Rules 3, 4 and 9, read with Arts 120-121 Limitation Act); courts take a liberal view of setting aside abatement (Mithailal Dalsangar Singh, 2003) but joint and indivisible decrees abate wholly.

Why it matters: Order 22; 90 and 60 days.

Evidence, appeals and second appeals

Aug 2026

The Court emphasised that recall of witnesses under Order 18 Rule 17 is discretionary and not to fill lacunae; reiterated that High Courts should not disturb concurrent findings of fact in second appeals absent a substantial question of law (2026 INSC 900 - verify); and held that a composite appeal against decrees passed in a common judgment can be maintainable (verify).

Why it matters: Order 18 Rule 17; Sec 100; appeals.

Mediation and commercial litigation

2022-2026

Sec 12A of the Commercial Courts Act (pre-institution mediation) is mandatory (Patil Automation, 2022); the Mediation Act 2023 is being operationalised; Sec 89 CPC and Afcons Infrastructure (2010) remain the CPC anchor for court-referred ADR; commercial courts enforce the 120-day outer limit for written statements (SCG Contracts, 2019).

Why it matters: Sec 89; Order 10 Rules 1A-1C; Commercial Courts Act.
