iOverview

Why labour law looks the way it does, what the four Codes replaced, when they came into force and what remains outside them.

★Must know

  1. Constitutional basis: Fundamental Rights - Art 14 (equality; equal pay for equal work), Art 16 (public employment), Art 19(1)(c) (form unions, associations, cooperative societies; no fundamental right to strike), Art 21 (livelihood, dignity, health of workers), Art 23 (forced labour, begar), Art 24 (no child below 14 in hazardous work). DPSP - Art 38, 39(a),(d),(e),(f), 41 (right to work), 42 (just and humane conditions, maternity relief), 43 (living wage), 43A (workers' participation in management - 42nd Amendment), 43B (cooperatives). Seventh Schedule: Entry 22 (trade unions; industrial and labour disputes), Entry 23 (social security and social insurance; employment and unemployment), Entry 24 (welfare of labour incl. conditions of work, provident funds, employers' liability, workmen's compensation, invalidity and old-age pensions and maternity benefits) - all in the Concurrent List. Entry 55 List I (regulation of labour and safety in mines and oilfields); Entry 61 List I (industrial disputes concerning Union employees).
  2. 'Appropriate Government' under the Codes: Central Government for establishments such as railways, mines, oilfields, major ports, banking and insurance companies, central public-sector undertakings, air transport; State Governments for the rest.
  3. Historical landmarks: Trade Disputes Act 1929; Trade Unions Act 1926; Industrial Disputes Act 1947 (framework for conciliation, adjudication and strikes); Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act 1946; Second National Commission on Labour (2002) recommended consolidation into broad groups - leading to the four Codes.
  4. The four Labour Codes: (1) Code on Wages, 2019; (2) Industrial Relations Code, 2020; (3) Code on Social Security, 2020; (4) Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020. Passed in 2019-2020; Presidential assent 2019 (Wages) and 28 September 2020 (the other three). Brought into force by four notifications dated 21 November 2025, repealing 29 Central labour laws. Central Rules under all four Codes were notified on 8-9 May 2026; State rules are being notified in phases (several States have notified; some pending) Verify · State-wise status.
  5. Counting the repeals (29 Acts): Wages Code subsumes 4 (Payment of Wages 1936; Minimum Wages 1948; Payment of Bonus 1965; Equal Remuneration 1976). IR Code subsumes 3 (Trade Unions 1926; Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) 1946; Industrial Disputes 1947). Social Security Code subsumes 9 (EPF & MP 1952; ESI 1948; Employees' Compensation 1923; Maternity Benefit 1961; Payment of Gratuity 1972; Employment Exchanges (CNV) 1959; Cine Workers Welfare Fund 1981; BOCW Welfare Cess 1996; Unorganised Workers' Social Security 2008). OSH Code subsumes 13 (Factories 1948; Mines 1952; Dock Workers (SHW) 1986; BOCW (RECS) 1996; Plantations Labour 1951; Contract Labour (R&A) 1970; Inter-State Migrant Workmen 1979; Working Journalists Acts 1955 and 1958; Motor Transport Workers 1961; Sales Promotion Employees 1976; Beedi & Cigar Workers 1966; Cine-Workers and Cinema Theatre Workers 1981).
  6. Common features of the Codes: universal coverage (organised and unorganised sectors, with different thresholds); single registration and licence, online returns; Inspector-cum-Facilitator (a role of advice and inspection); web-based randomised inspections; fixed-term employment; written appointment letters; gender-neutral provisions; compounding of offences; decriminalisation of first offences in places; time-bound payment of wages; mandatory registration of gig and platform workers; national floor wage.
  7. Laws that remain outside the Codes (examples): Child and Adolescent Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act 1986; Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act 1976; Apprentices Act 1961; Employees' Pension Scheme 1995 (under the EPF scheme); Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (POSH) Act 2013; Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act 2016 (employment provisions); State Shops and Establishments Acts; Labour Welfare Fund Acts of States; Employees' Compensation is within the Social Security Code.
  8. How AIBE may frame questions: (a) on the Codes (definitions, thresholds, repeals); (b) on old-Act case law that is still treated as good law (Bangalore Water Supply, Uma Devi); (c) on constitutional rights of workers. Because the Codes became operative only a year before the exam, expect questions that test 'which Act has been repealed' and 'what is the new threshold'.
  9. Industrial jurisprudence principles: social justice and welfare orientation (Crown Aluminium Works v. Workmen, 1958); workers' right to collective bargaining; employer's right to manage; industrial peace through conciliation; 'hire and fire' limited by reasonableness and natural justice; reinstatement as ordinary relief for illegal termination.

⚖Landmark cases

Crown Aluminium Works v. Workmen

1958

Social justice; the concept of living wage and fair wage; industrial adjudication guided by equity.

People's Union for Democratic Rights v. Union of India (Asiad Workers)

1982

Non-payment of minimum wages is forced labour (Art 23); workers can seek writ remedy; Art 21 includes humane conditions.

Bandhua Mukti Morcha v. Union of India

1984

Bonded labour; PIL; State's duty to identify and rehabilitate.

Randhir Singh v. Union of India

1982

Equal pay for equal work is derived from Arts 14, 16 and 39(d).

T.K. Rangarajan v. Government of Tamil Nadu

2003

No fundamental right to strike.

✎Memory aids

  • 29 Acts = 4 (Wages) + 3 (IR) + 9 (Social Security) + 13 (OSH).
  • Entries 22-24 of List III = 'unions-disputes (22), social security (23), welfare of labour (24)'.

!Exam traps

  • TrapThe Codes are in force from 21 Nov 2025, but the Central Rules came only on 8-9 May 2026.
  • TrapChild labour and bonded labour statutes were not merged into the Codes.
  • TrapLabour is a Concurrent subject: both Parliament and State Legislatures can legislate.

?Likely questions

  1. How many Central labour laws were subsumed into the four Labour Codes? (29)
  2. The Codes came into effect on which date? (21 November 2025)
  3. Which Seventh Schedule entry deals with trade unions and industrial disputes? (Entry 22, List III)
