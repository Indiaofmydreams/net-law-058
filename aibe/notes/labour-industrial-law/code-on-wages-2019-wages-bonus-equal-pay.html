Code on Wages, 2019: Wages, Minimum and Floor Wage, Payment, Bonus, Equal Remuneration

iOverview

One law now covers payment of wages, minimum wages, bonus and equal pay for all employees. The 50% rule in 'wages', the floor wage and time-limits for payment are classic testing points.

★Must know

  1. Coverage: applies to all employees in all establishments (organised and unorganised); the earlier wage ceiling for the Payment of Wages Act (Rs 24,000 per month) has been removed - all employees are covered for timely payment of wages, deductions and minimum wages; bonus provisions apply to employees drawing wages up to Rs 21,000 per month.
  2. 'Wages' (s.2(y)): all remuneration whether by way of salary, allowances or otherwise, expressed in terms of money or capable of being so expressed, including basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance. Excluded: bonus payable under any law; value of house accommodation, supply of light, water, medical attendance; employer's contribution to pension or provident fund and social security contribution; conveyance allowance; special expenses entitlement; house rent allowance; remuneration payable under award or settlement; overtime allowance; commission; gratuity; retrenchment compensation or other terminal benefits; travelling concession. If the excluded components together exceed 50% of total remuneration, the excess is added back to wages ('the 50% rule') - so basic pay and DA must form at least 50% of total remuneration. This definition governs PF, gratuity and bonus calculations under the Codes.
  3. Equal remuneration (Chapter II, s.3): no employer shall discriminate on the ground of gender in matters of wages or recruitment, conditions of service and employment; includes transgender persons; employer shall not reduce the wages of any employee to comply. Randhir Singh v. Union of India (1982); State of Punjab v. Jagjit Singh (2016) - equal pay for equal work for temporary employees performing the same duties; Mackinnon Mackenzie & Co. v. Audrey D'Costa (1987) - Equal Remuneration Act principle; Air India v. Nargesh Meerza (1981).
  4. Minimum wage: employers shall pay not less than the minimum wage prescribed - the Code applies minimum wages to all employees (not only scheduled employment). The Central Government fixes a 'floor wage' (s.9) having regard to living standards; the appropriate Government cannot fix a minimum wage below the floor wage; different minimum rates for different regions/skills; time-work, piece-work; review at intervals not exceeding five years; Advisory Boards; minimum wage includes basic wage and DA. Payment to non-employees via contractors - principal employer ultimately liable. Cases: Bijay Cotton Mills v. State of Ajmer (1955) - statutory minimum wage is a reasonable restriction under Art 19(1)(g); Unichoyi v. State of Kerala (1962) - inability to pay is no defence; Workmen v. Reptakos Brett &amp; Co. (1992) - components of minimum wage; Sanjit Roy v. State of Rajasthan (1983) - minimum wages must be paid even in famine-relief works; PUDR (Asiad) (1982).
  5. Hours of work and overtime: the appropriate Government fixes normal working day; overtime shall be paid at not less than twice the normal rate of wages (s.14); weekly rest day.
  6. Payment of wages (Chapter III): wage period - daily, weekly, fortnightly or monthly (not exceeding one month); time-limit for payment (s.17): daily wage - at the end of the shift; weekly - on the last working day of the week; fortnightly - before the end of the second day after the end of the fortnight; monthly - before the expiry of the seventh day of the succeeding month; on removal, dismissal, retrenchment, resignation or superannuation - within two working days (s.17(2)); payment in coin/currency notes, cheque or credit to bank account or electronically ; responsibility for payment on the employer or a person designated; contractor/principal employer liability.
  7. Deductions (s.18): only authorised deductions - fines, absence from duty, damage to goods, house accommodation, amenities, recovery of advances, income-tax, court orders, cooperative society subscriptions, insurance premiums etc.; total deductions shall not exceed 50% of wages in any wage period (s.18(2)); fines limited and with show-cause; no deduction for damage unless caused by neglect/default.
  8. Bonus (Chapter V): every employee drawing wages not exceeding Rs 21,000 per month and who has worked for at least 30 days in an accounting year is entitled to bonus; minimum bonus is 8.33% of wages earned or Rs 100, whichever is higher; maximum 20% of wages; calculation ceiling of Rs 7,000 or the minimum wage, whichever is higher; allocable surplus (60% of available surplus for companies; 67% for others); set-on and set-off; payment within eight months from close of accounting year; disqualification for fraud, riotous or violent behaviour, theft, misappropriation or sabotage; establishments newly set up exempt for the first five accounting years unless profits; Mumbai Kamgar Sabha v. Abdulbhai Faizullabhai (1976) - bonus is not deferred wage but a share in profits; the Full Bench Formula governs the determination of allocable surplus in bonus disputes.
  9. Claims and adjudication: employee may apply to the Authority (appointed by the appropriate Government) for unpaid wages/bonus within three years from the date on which the claim arose Verify · section number; penalty up to ten times the amount in some cases; interest; appeals to Appellate Authority within 30 days; recovery as arrears of land revenue. 'Inspector-cum-Facilitator' appointed to supply information and advice and to inspect; web-based random inspection.
  10. Penalties and compounding: offence of paying less than the minimum wage - fine up to Rs 50,000 for first offence; repeat within five years - imprisonment up to three months and/or fine up to Rs 1 lakh Verify; Code offences compoundable by the authority with the specified amounts; no imprisonment for first-time minor offence.

⚖Landmark cases

Bijay Cotton Mills Ltd. v. State of Ajmer

1955

Minimum wage law is a reasonable restriction in public interest.

Unichoyi v. State of Kerala

1962

Employer must pay the statutory minimum even if the industry is unable to pay.

PUDR v. Union of India (Asiad Workers)

1982

Wages below minimum amount to forced labour.

Randhir Singh v. Union of India

1982

Equal pay for equal work - Art 14, 16, 39(d).

State of Punjab v. Jagjit Singh

2016

Temporary employees doing the same work as regular employees entitled to equal pay.

Mumbai Kamgar Sabha v. Abdulbhai Faizullabhai

1976

Bonus is linked to profits and is not an enforceable claim to deferred wage in the absence of statute.

Sanjit Roy v. State of Rajasthan

1983

Famine relief work - minimum wages must be paid; exemption unconstitutional.

✎Memory aids

  • Wages: 50% rule - excluded items beyond 50% are added back.
  • Bonus: 8.33% - 20%; ceiling Rs 21,000; 30 working days.

!Exam traps

  • TrapHouse rent allowance, bonus, gratuity, overtime and employer's PF contribution are excluded from 'wages' - but the 50% rule may add some back.
  • TrapTotal deductions are capped at 50% of wages.
  • TrapOn termination or resignation, wages must be paid within two working days.
  • TrapMinimum wage applies to all employees under the Code, not only scheduled employment.

?Likely questions

  1. Under the Code on Wages, overtime is payable at what rate? (Twice the normal rate)
  2. What is the minimum bonus percentage? (8.33%)
  3. Which Act dealt with 'equal remuneration' earlier? (Equal Remuneration Act 1976)
  4. Maximum deductions from wages? (50%)
