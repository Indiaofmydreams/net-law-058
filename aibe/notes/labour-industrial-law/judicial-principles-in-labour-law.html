iOverview

Principles you will need to apply to a fact scenario.

★Must know

  1. Regularisation: Secretary, State of Karnataka v. Umadevi (3) (2006, Constitution Bench): courts cannot order regularisation of employees appointed irregularly or without following the constitutional scheme of public employment (Arts 14, 16); 'illegal' v. 'irregular' appointments; one-time measure for those who had served 10 years or more against sanctioned posts. Later: Jaggo v. Union of India (Dec 2024) and Shripal v. Nagar Nigam, Ghaziabad (Jan 2025): the Supreme Court criticised the misuse of Umadevi to deny regularisation to long-serving workers on perennial work and directed regularisation; the principle that 'temporary' labels cannot disguise permanent needs Verify.
  2. Equal pay for equal work: Randhir Singh (1982); State of Punjab v. Jagjit Singh (2016) - temporary employees doing the same work as regular employees get minimum of regular pay scale; contrary caution in State of Haryana v. Charanjit Singh (2006).
  3. Disciplinary proceedings and domestic enquiry: principles of natural justice - notice, opportunity, impartial inquiry officer; no bias; findings supported by evidence; 'some evidence' and not strict rules of evidence (State of Haryana v. Rattan Singh, 1977); punishment proportional (Ranjit Thakur v. Union of India, 1987; Union of India v. G. Ganayutham, 1997 - proportionality as part of Wednesbury); Tribunal's power under s.11A ID Act; supply of enquiry report before punishment (Managing Director, ECIL v. B. Karunakar, 1993); suspension and subsistence allowance (State of Maharashtra v. Chandrabhan, 1983); termination simpliciter of probationers (Dipti Prakash Banerjee v. Satyendra Nath Bose National Centre, 1999 - stigma test).
  4. Standing Orders and misconduct: acts of misconduct defined in standing orders; 'misconduct' wider than enumerated list (Glaxo Laboratories v. Presiding Officer, 1984 - standing orders must be certified; employer cannot punish for acts not covered).
  5. Hire and fire: Delhi Transport Corporation v. DTC Mazdoor Congress (1991) - arbitrary termination clauses unconstitutional for State instrumentalities; Central Inland Water Transport Corp. v. Brojo Nath Ganguly (1986) - unconscionable contract of service void under s.23 Contract Act and Art 14.
  6. Reinstatement with back wages: Deepali Gundu Surwase (2013); J.K. Synthetics v. K.P. Agrawal (2007) (reinstatement not automatic); Gauri Shankar v. State of Rajasthan (2015) - compensation in lieu; Allahabad Jal Sansthan (2005).
  7. Strike: T.K. Rangarajan (2003) - no fundamental right; all strikes subject to statutory rules; illegal strike; wages not payable unless justified; Harish Uppal (2002); dismissals for participation in illegal strike - limited to violence.
  8. Constitutional rights of workers: Art 19(1)(c) - protects formation of unions, not recognition; Art 21 - livelihood (Olga Tellis; Delhi Transport Corp.); Art 23 - forced labour (PUDR); Art 14 - equal pay; Art 16 - public employment; Art 43A workers' participation in management; Art 300A and wages - PF/gratuity as property.
  9. Employer-employee relationship tests - control and supervision, integration, economic reality, multiple factors; 'contract of service' v. 'contract for services' (Dharangadhara Chemical Works v. State of Saurashtra, 1957; Silver Jubilee Tailoring House v. Chief Inspector of Shops, 1974); platform workers - emerging law: Karnataka and Rajasthan Acts; international: Uber (UK Supreme Court 2021) as a comparative marker.
  10. Compounding and prosecution: offences under labour law are triable by Magistrates; limitation for cognizance is as prescribed under the Code; a complaint by Inspector; 'sanction'.

⚖Landmark cases

Secretary, State of Karnataka v. Umadevi (3)

2006

No regularisation of irregular/illegal appointments; one-time measure.

Jaggo v. Union of India

2024

Umadevi cannot be used to exploit long-serving workers - regularisation directed Verify.

State of Haryana v. Rattan Singh

1977

Domestic enquiry - strict rules of evidence not applicable; 'some evidence' suffices.

Central Inland Water Transport Corp. v. Brojo Nath Ganguly

1986

Unconscionable terms in standard-form contracts of service are void.

Managing Director, ECIL v. B. Karunakar

1993

Copy of enquiry report must be furnished before punishment.

Deepali Gundu Surwase v. Kranti Junior Adhyapak Mahavidyalaya

2013

Full back wages is the normal rule.

✎Memory aids

  • 'Umadevi: no back-door regularisation; but long-serving perennial workers - Jaggo and Shripal'.

!Exam traps

  • TrapUmadevi (2006) is a Constitution Bench - binding; later two-judge benches only distinguish it.
  • TrapDomestic enquiry follows natural justice, not the Evidence Act.
  • TrapBack wages are not automatic in every case.

?Likely questions

  1. Which case denied regularisation of irregular appointments? (Umadevi)
  2. Which Article provides for workers' participation in management? (Art 43A)
