iOverview

Safety, health, working hours, contract labour and the constitutional rights cases (child labour, bonded labour, Vishaka, Bhopal, absolute liability).

★Must know

  1. OSH Code subsumes 13 Acts: Factories Act 1948; Mines Act 1952; Dock Workers (Safety, Health and Welfare) Act 1986; Building and Other Construction Workers (RECS) Act 1996; Plantations Labour Act 1951; Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Act 1970; Inter-State Migrant Workmen (RECS) Act 1979; Working Journalists and other Newspaper Employees (Conditions of Service) and Miscellaneous Provisions Act 1955; Working Journalists (Fixation of Rates of Wages) Act 1958; Motor Transport Workers Act 1961; Sales Promotion Employees (Conditions of Service) Act 1976; Beedi and Cigar Workers (Conditions of Employment) Act 1966; Cine-Workers and Cinema Theatre Workers (Regulation of Employment) Act 1981.
  2. Applicability: establishments with 10 or more workers (mines and docks irrespective of numbers); 'factory' means premises where 20 or more workers are employed (with power) or 40 or more (without power) (earlier 10 and 20 respectively; State Governments may raise the limit further); a single registration of an establishment; 'occupier' - the person with ultimate control over the affairs of the establishment (in a company, one of the directors); 'Inspector-cum-Facilitator'; 'Chief Inspector'; National and State Occupational Safety and Health Advisory Boards.
  3. Employer duties: provide a workplace free from hazards; appointment letters for all workers; free annual health check-up for workers above a certain age (45 years) Verify; Safety Committee in factories with 500 or more workers Verify; Welfare Officer in factories with 500 or more workers; canteen for 100 or more workers; crèche for 50 or more workers; first-aid; occupational disease notification; compensation for accidents is under the Social Security Code; notify accidents. Hazardous processes: duties for hazardous factories (Bhopal lessons - Chapter on hazardous industries; Environment Protection Act 1986; Public Liability Insurance Act 1991; National Green Tribunal Act 2010).
  4. Working hours and leave: 8 hours a day and 48 hours a week (more flexible hours if prescribed); spread-over of 12 hours; overtime wages at not less than twice the rate (Wages Code), overtime ceiling per quarter [125 hours - VERIFY]; weekly day of rest; annual leave with wages: one day for every 20 days worked (earlier 240 days eligibility is relaxed to 180 days of work in the preceding calendar year); women may work night shifts (7 pm - 6 am) with consent and prescribed safety measures (transport, security, CCTV, restrooms); women in mines and hazardous work allowed with safeguards.
  5. Contract labour (Chapter XI): the Code applies to establishments and contractors employing 50 or more contract workers (earlier 20); the contractor must hold a licence (a single, all-India licence valid for five years, with relaxations); contract labour is prohibited in core activities of an establishment except where (i) the normal functioning is such that the work is ordinarily done through contractors; (ii) the activities do not require full-time workers for the major part of the working hours in a day; or (iii) there is a sudden increase in volume of work; the principal employer is liable to pay wages and benefits if the contractor defaults; workers must be paid wages and have facilities (canteen, rest rooms, drinking water). Steel Authority of India Ltd. v. National Union Water Front Workers (2001, Constitution Bench): on abolition of contract labour, absorption of contract workers in the principal employer is not automatic; Air India Statutory Corporation v. United Labour Union (1996) overruled in part; Balwant Rai Saluja v. Air India (2014): 'control' test - contract labour is the principal employer's employee only if the contract is a sham; Gujarat Electricity Board v. Hind Mazdoor Sabha (1995).
  6. Inter-State migrant workers: includes a worker who migrates on his own and not only through a contractor; employer must pay an annual 'journey allowance' for to-and-fro travel to the native place; portability of benefits (ration, PDS); registration on the national portal; ISMW displacement allowance (as under old Act) is dropped in favour of journey allowance.
  7. Women at work: Maternity (Social Security Code); equal pay (Wages Code s.3); Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act 2013 - Internal Committee (10+ employees), Local Committee (district); complaint within 3 months (extendable); inquiry 90 days; Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan (1997); Medha Kotwal Lele v. Union of India (2013) - compliance; Apparel Export Promotion Council v. A.K. Chopra (1999); Aureliano Fernandes v. State of Goa (2023) - proper constitution of Internal Committee; Air India v. Nargesh Meerza (1981) - unequal service conditions for air hostesses (retirement on pregnancy) held arbitrary; Anuj Garg v. Hotel Association of India (2008) - ban on women employees in bars struck as stereotyping; Charu Khurana v. Union of India (2015) - no gender bias in make-up artists' union membership.
  8. Child labour: Art 24 (no child below 14 in factory/mine/hazardous employment); Child and Adolescent Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act 1986 as amended in 2016 - complete prohibition of employment of children below 14 in all occupations (with an exception for family enterprises and audio-visual entertainment outside school hours); prohibition of adolescents (14-18) in hazardous occupations/processes; penalties; Right to Education Act 2009 (Art 21A) - cross-ref Constitutional Law. Cases: M.C. Mehta v. State of Tamil Nadu (1996) - Sivakasi: directions on child labour (Rs 20,000 compensation per child; child labour rehabilitation-cum-welfare fund); Bachpan Bachao Andolan v. Union of India (2011) - circus children, trafficking; People's Union for Democratic Rights v. Union of India (1982).
  9. Bonded labour: Art 23; Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act 1976 abolishes the system and extinguishes debts; District Magistrate as implementing authority; vigilance committees; rehabilitation; offences punishable with up to 3 years' imprisonment and fine up to Rs 2,000; Bandhua Mukti Morcha v. Union of India (1984); Neerja Chaudhary v. State of M.P. (1984) - rehabilitation is part of liberation; Central Sector Scheme for Rehabilitation of Bonded Labourers (2021).
  10. Penalties and compounding in the OSH Code: higher fines for fatal accidents, with compensation to victims; first offence of certain kinds can be compounded; offences by companies attract liability of the officers in charge; a court of Metropolitan Magistrate or Judicial Magistrate of the First Class tries offences; cognizance only on a complaint by or with the authority of Inspector-cum-Facilitator.
  11. Absolute liability and workplace disasters: M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Oleum Gas Leak, 1987) - absolute liability for hazardous industry (departure from Rylands v. Fletcher); Union Carbide Corp. v. Union of India (1989) - Bhopal settlement; Union of India v. Union Carbide (1991) - review; Consumer Education and Research Centre v. Union of India (1995) - right to health of workers is a part of Art 21 (asbestos); Occupational diseases and ESI; Charan Lal Sahu v. Union of India (1990) - Bhopal Gas Leak (Processing of Claims) Act 1985 upheld.

✎Memory aids

  • Factory: 20/40. Contract labour: 50. Crèche: 50. Canteen: 100. Welfare officer: 500.
  • OSH Code merges 13 Acts: Factories-Mines-Docks-BOCW-Plantations-CLRA-ISMW-Journalists (2)-Motor-Sales-Beedi-Cine.

!Exam traps

  • TrapChild Labour Act 1986 (amended 2016) and Bonded Labour Act 1976 are NOT subsumed in the Codes.
  • TrapContract labour threshold is 50 (earlier 20).
  • TrapAbsolute liability (Oleum Gas) is stricter than strict liability (Rylands v. Fletcher).
  • TrapWomen night work is permitted only with consent and safety measures.
