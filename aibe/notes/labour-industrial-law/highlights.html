Architecture of Labour Law: Constitutional Basis, History and the Four Labour Codes

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Codes in force21 November 2025 (four notifications); Central Rules 8-9 May 2026.
Entries 22-24, List IIIConcurrent Legislative power over labour, social security and welfare.
Art 43Living wage and decent standard of life for workers.

Cases in one line

  • Crown Aluminium Works v. Workmen 1958 Social justice; the concept of living wage and fair wage; industrial adjudication guided by equity.
  • People's Union for Democratic Rights v. Union of India (Asiad Workers) 1982 Non-payment of minimum wages is forced labour (Art 23); workers can seek writ remedy; Art 21 includes humane conditions.
  • Bandhua Mukti Morcha v. Union of India 1984 Bonded labour; PIL; State's duty to identify and rehabilitate.
  • Randhir Singh v. Union of India 1982 Equal pay for equal work is derived from Arts 14, 16 and 39(d).
  • T.K. Rangarajan v. Government of Tamil Nadu 2003 No fundamental right to strike.

Memory aids

  • 29 Acts = 4 (Wages) + 3 (IR) + 9 (Social Security) + 13 (OSH).
  • Entries 22-24 of List III = 'unions-disputes (22), social security (23), welfare of labour (24)'.

Exam traps

  • TrapThe Codes are in force from 21 Nov 2025, but the Central Rules came only on 8-9 May 2026.
  • TrapChild labour and bonded labour statutes were not merged into the Codes.
  • TrapLabour is a Concurrent subject: both Parliament and State Legislatures can legislate.

Industrial Relations Code, 2020 (I): 'Industry', 'Worker', 'Industrial Dispute', Employer, Bi-partite Forums and Trade Unions

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

IR Code s.2(p)Definition of 'industry'; excludes sovereign functions, charitable services, domestic service.
IR Code s.2(zr)Definition of 'worker' - supervisory wage limit Rs 18,000.
IR Code s.2(q)Industrial dispute incl. individual disputes.
IR Code s.3-4Works Committee (100+ workers); Grievance Redressal Committee (20+ workers).
IR Code s.14Recognition: sole negotiating union (51%) or negotiating council (20% unions).

Cases in one line

  • Bangalore Water Supply & Sewerage Board v. A. Rajappa 1978 Triple test for industry; wide reading; sovereign functions alone excluded.
  • State of U.P. v. Jai Bir Singh 2026 Nine-judge Bench (20 Aug 2026): reference valid; BWSSB refined, not overruled; original test governs pending ID Act cases; IR Code s.2(p) not automatically governed.
  • Hospital Mazdoor Sabha v. State of Madras 1960 Hospitals can be industries.
  • Workmen of Dimakuchi Tea Estate v. Management 1958 Collective dispute must be espoused by union or substantial body of workmen.
  • Burmah Shell Oil Storage v. Burmah Shell Management Staff Association 1970 Dominant nature of duties determines whether a person is a workman.
  • Hussainbhai v. Alath Factory Thozhilali Union 1978 Control test; real employer behind the contractor.
  • All India Bank Employees' Association v. National Industrial Tribunal 1962 Art 19(1)(c) protects forming unions, not the right to strike or to recognition.
  • Balwant Rai Saluja v. Air India 2014 Contract labour not automatically employees of principal employer.

Memory aids

  • Triple test: 'S-O-P' - Systematic, Organised cooperation, Production/distribution to satisfy wants.
  • Works Committee 100, GRC 20, Standing Orders 300.

Exam traps

  • TrapThe wage limit of Rs 18,000 applies to supervisory employees to decide whether they are 'workers'.
  • TrapTrade union registration needs at least 7 members (and 10%/100 workers whichever is lesser).
  • TrapSole negotiating union = 51%; negotiating council requires unions with 20% each.
  • TrapArt 19(1)(c) does not include the right to strike (Rangarajan).
3

Industrial Relations Code, 2020 (II): Standing Orders, Dispute Resolution Machinery, Strikes and Lock-outs

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

IR Code s.62 (ID Act ss.22-23)Notice of strike/lock-out: 60 days; not within 14 days of notice; and bars during conciliation/tribunal/arbitration - now for ALL industrial establishments.
IR Code Chapter IV (Standing Orders)Applies to industrial establishments with 300 or more workers.
ID Act s.10AVoluntary reference of disputes to arbitration.
ID Act s.11ATribunal can modify punishment imposed by management.

Cases in one line

  • Workmen of Firestone Tyre & Rubber Co. v. Management 1973 Tribunal's power to examine misconduct and punishment; domestic enquiry principles.
  • Shankar Chakravarti v. Britannia Biscuit Co. 1979 If enquiry is vitiated, employer may adduce evidence before Tribunal; must plead and apply.
  • Delhi Transport Corp. v. D.T.C. Mazdoor Congress 1991 Struck down regulation 9(b) of DTC Regulations (termination without reasons) as arbitrary.
  • Deepali Gundu Surwase v. Kranti Junior Adhyapak Mahavidyalaya 2013 Full back wages and reinstatement is the normal rule after illegal termination.
  • Bank of India v. T.S. Kelawala 1990 Wages for strike period; legality and justification of strike.
  • T.K. Rangarajan v. Government of Tamil Nadu 2003 No fundamental right to strike.
  • Ex-Capt. Harish Uppal v. Union of India 2002 Strike by lawyers is illegal; reaffirmed that right to strike is not absolute.
  • Western India Match Co. v. Workmen 1973 Standing orders have statutory force.

Memory aids

  • Strike notice: '60 days before; not within 14 days; none during conciliation (+7), tribunal (+60), arbitration (+60), or while award/settlement operates'.

Exam traps

  • TrapNotice for strikes now applies to all industrial establishments, not only public utility services.
  • TrapConcerted casual leave by 50% or more workers is treated as a strike.
  • TrapA settlement in conciliation binds all workers; a bipartite settlement binds only signatories.
  • TrapStanding orders threshold 300 (not 100).

Industrial Relations Code, 2020 (III): Lay-off, Retrenchment, Closure, Fixed-term Employment, Re-skilling Fund, Unfair Labour Practices and Penalties

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

IR Code s.70 (ID Act s.25F)Retrenchment: one month notice (or wages), compensation 15 days' average pay per year, notice to Government.
IR Code s.77 (Chapter X)Prior permission for lay-off/retrenchment/closure in establishments with 300+ workers; deemed permission in 60 days.
ID Act s.25CLay-off compensation - 50% of basic wages and DA.
IR Code s.83 VerifyWorker Re-skilling Fund - 15 days' wages.

Cases in one line

  • Punjab Land Development & Reclamation Corporation v. Presiding Officer, Labour Court 1990 Constitution Bench - 'retrenchment' means every termination except those expressly excluded.
  • State Bank of India v. N. Sundara Money 1976 Retrenchment includes termination for any reason other than punishment; automatic termination covered.
  • Santosh Gupta v. State Bank of Patiala 1980 Termination in breach of s.25F is void; reinstatement follows.
  • Workmen of Meenakshi Mills Ltd. v. Meenakshi Mills Ltd. 1992 Validity of Chapter VB (ss.25K-25S) - reasonable restriction on employers' right.
  • Excel Wear v. Union of India 1978 Section 25-O (prior permission for closure) struck down at the time.
  • Delhi Cloth & General Mills Co. v. Shambhu Nath Mukherjee 1977 Retrenchment - broad meaning.
  • Mohan Lal v. Management of Bharat Electronics Ltd. 1981 Non-compliance with s.25F makes retrenchment void ab initio.

Memory aids

  • Retrenchment 'N-C-G': Notice (1 month) - Compensation (15 days per year) - Government notified.
  • Prior permission 300 workers; closure notice 60 days (50+ workers).

Exam traps

  • TrapCompensation for lay-off is 50% of basic + DA; for retrenchment 15 days' average pay per year.
  • TrapThe one-month notice for retrenchment can be replaced by wages in lieu.
  • TrapClosure compensation is limited to 3 months' average pay when due to unavoidable circumstances.
  • TrapPrior permission requirement now begins at 300 workers (earlier 100).
5

Code on Wages, 2019: Wages, Minimum and Floor Wage, Payment, Bonus, Equal Remuneration

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Wages Code s.2(y)Definition of 'wages' - 50% rule.
Wages Code s.3Prohibition of discrimination on the ground of gender.
Wages Code s.9Floor wage set by the Central Government.
Wages Code s.14Overtime at not less than twice the normal rate.
Wages Code s.17Time-limit for payment: monthly - before 7th day; on termination - within 2 working days.
Wages Code s.18Deductions; total not to exceed 50% of wages.
Wages Code Chapter VBonus: 8.33% minimum - 20% maximum; wage ceiling Rs 21,000.

Cases in one line

  • Bijay Cotton Mills Ltd. v. State of Ajmer 1955 Minimum wage law is a reasonable restriction in public interest.
  • Unichoyi v. State of Kerala 1962 Employer must pay the statutory minimum even if the industry is unable to pay.
  • PUDR v. Union of India (Asiad Workers) 1982 Wages below minimum amount to forced labour.
  • Randhir Singh v. Union of India 1982 Equal pay for equal work - Art 14, 16, 39(d).
  • State of Punjab v. Jagjit Singh 2016 Temporary employees doing the same work as regular employees entitled to equal pay.
  • Mumbai Kamgar Sabha v. Abdulbhai Faizullabhai 1976 Bonus is linked to profits and is not an enforceable claim to deferred wage in the absence of statute.
  • Sanjit Roy v. State of Rajasthan 1983 Famine relief work - minimum wages must be paid; exemption unconstitutional.

Memory aids

  • Wages: 50% rule - excluded items beyond 50% are added back.
  • Bonus: 8.33% - 20%; ceiling Rs 21,000; 30 working days.

Exam traps

  • TrapHouse rent allowance, bonus, gratuity, overtime and employer's PF contribution are excluded from 'wages' - but the 50% rule may add some back.
  • TrapTotal deductions are capped at 50% of wages.
  • TrapOn termination or resignation, wages must be paid within two working days.
  • TrapMinimum wage applies to all employees under the Code, not only scheduled employment.
6

Code on Social Security, 2020: EPF, ESI, Gratuity, Maternity Benefit, Employees' Compensation, Gig and Platform Workers

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

SS Code - Gratuity (Chapter V)5 years' service (1 year for fixed-term employees); 15 days' wages per year; cap Rs 20 lakh.
SS Code - Maternity (Chapter VI)26 weeks for first two children; 12 weeks for third or adopting/commissioning mother.
SS Code - Gig workers (Chapter IX)Aggregator contribution 1%-2% of turnover capped at 5% of payouts.
EPF threshold20 employees; ESI threshold 10; wage ceilings Rs 15,000 (EPF) / Rs 21,000 (ESI).

Cases in one line

  • Municipal Corporation of Delhi v. Female Workers (Muster Roll) 2000 Maternity benefit applies to casual/daily-wage women workers.
  • Deepika Singh v. Central Administrative Tribunal 2022 Maternity leave for a woman with a step-child; purposive reading of maternity law.
  • Vivekananda Vidya Mandir v. Regional PF Commissioner 2019 Special allowances paid universally are part of basic wage for PF.
  • Union Bank of India v. C.G. Ajay Babu 2018 Gratuity forfeiture needs conviction/offence involving moral turpitude.
  • Jeewanlal (1929) Ltd. v. Appellate Authority 1984 Gratuity is a statutory right of the employee, not dependent on the employer's profit.
  • Saurashtra Salt Manufacturing Co. v. Bai Valu Raja 1958 Notional extension of the employment: accident 'in the course of employment' includes the journey.
  • Neera Mathur v. LIC 1992 Pregnancy-related questions in service applications are invalid; privacy and dignity of women.

Memory aids

  • EPF 20 / ESI 10 / Gratuity 10 / Maternity 10 / Crèche 50.
  • Maternity: 26 weeks (1st & 2nd child); 12 weeks (3rd+, adoption, commissioning).

Exam traps

  • TrapGratuity requires 5 years' continuous service, but this is not needed in case of death or disablement.
  • TrapMaternity 26 weeks applies only to the first two children.
  • TrapEPF threshold is 20 employees; ESI 10 employees.
  • TrapThe 'Employees' Compensation Act' is now merged into the Code - but claims on pre-Code accidents may still use old provisions.
7

Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020; Contract Labour; Inter-State Migrants; Child and Bonded Labour; Women at Work

Medium-high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

OSH Code - factory20 or more workers with power; 40 or more without power.
OSH Code - contract labourApplies to establishments with 50 or more contract workers; core-activity prohibition with exceptions.
OSH Code - working time8 hours a day; 48 hours a week; overtime double; women night work with consent.
Art 24No child below 14 in hazardous employment.

Cases in one line

  • M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Oleum Gas Leak) 1987 Absolute liability of hazardous industries.
  • Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan 1997 Guidelines against workplace sexual harassment; now POSH Act 2013.
  • Steel Authority of India v. National Union Water Front Workers 2001 No automatic absorption of contract labour on abolition.
  • Balwant Rai Saluja v. Air India 2014 Test of control to decide the real employer in contract labour.
  • M.C. Mehta v. State of Tamil Nadu 1996 Child labour guidelines - Sivakasi; compensation fund.
  • Bandhua Mukti Morcha v. Union of India 1984 Bonded labour; PIL; Art 21 and 23.
  • Consumer Education & Research Centre v. Union of India 1995 Right to health and medical aid for workers is part of Art 21.
  • Air India v. Nargesh Meerza 1981 Service conditions requiring termination on first pregnancy are arbitrary.
  • Anuj Garg v. Hotel Association of India 2008 Gender stereotyping; ban on women employees in bars unconstitutional.

Memory aids

  • Factory: 20/40. Contract labour: 50. Crèche: 50. Canteen: 100. Welfare officer: 500.
  • OSH Code merges 13 Acts: Factories-Mines-Docks-BOCW-Plantations-CLRA-ISMW-Journalists (2)-Motor-Sales-Beedi-Cine.

Exam traps

  • TrapChild Labour Act 1986 (amended 2016) and Bonded Labour Act 1976 are NOT subsumed in the Codes.
  • TrapContract labour threshold is 50 (earlier 20).
  • TrapAbsolute liability (Oleum Gas) is stricter than strict liability (Rylands v. Fletcher).
  • TrapWomen night work is permitted only with consent and safety measures.
8

Judicial Principles in Labour Law: Regularisation, Termination, Contract Workers, Domestic Enquiry, Strikes and Constitutional Rights of Workers

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Art 16Equality in public employment - basis of Umadevi.
ID Act s.11ATribunal's power to substitute punishment.
Art 43AParticipation of workers in management.

Cases in one line

  • Secretary, State of Karnataka v. Umadevi (3) 2006 No regularisation of irregular/illegal appointments; one-time measure.
  • Jaggo v. Union of India 2024 Umadevi cannot be used to exploit long-serving workers - regularisation directed Verify.
  • State of Haryana v. Rattan Singh 1977 Domestic enquiry - strict rules of evidence not applicable; 'some evidence' suffices.
  • Central Inland Water Transport Corp. v. Brojo Nath Ganguly 1986 Unconscionable terms in standard-form contracts of service are void.
  • Managing Director, ECIL v. B. Karunakar 1993 Copy of enquiry report must be furnished before punishment.
  • Deepali Gundu Surwase v. Kranti Junior Adhyapak Mahavidyalaya 2013 Full back wages is the normal rule.

Memory aids

  • 'Umadevi: no back-door regularisation; but long-serving perennial workers - Jaggo and Shripal'.

Exam traps

  • TrapUmadevi (2006) is a Constitution Bench - binding; later two-judge benches only distinguish it.
  • TrapDomestic enquiry follows natural justice, not the Evidence Act.
  • TrapBack wages are not automatic in every case.
