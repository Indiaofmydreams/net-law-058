iOverview

The most numerically testable block: notice, compensation, thresholds, deemed permission and the new Worker Re-skilling Fund.

★Must know

  1. Lay-off (ID Act s.2(kkk); IR Code Chapter IX): the failure, refusal or inability of an employer on account of shortage of coal, power or raw materials, accumulation of stocks, breakdown of machinery, natural calamity or any other connected reason to give employment to a worker whose name is on the muster roll and who has not been retrenched. Lay-off compensation: 50% of the total of basic wages and dearness allowance for all days of lay-off (other than weekly holidays) (ID Act s.25C; IR Code Chapter IX); not payable if the worker refuses alternative employment, does not present himself daily, or the lay-off is due to a strike or slowing down in another part of the establishment; not for more than 45 days in a year (then the employer may retrench) Verify. Lay-off provisions apply to establishments with 50 or more workers (but not seasonal).
  2. Retrenchment (ID Act s.2(oo); IR Code): termination by the employer of the service of a worker for any reason whatsoever, otherwise than as a punishment inflicted by way of disciplinary action, except (a) voluntary retirement; (b) retirement on reaching the age of superannuation; (c) non-renewal of a contract of employment on its expiry (or termination under a stipulation in the contract); (d) termination due to continued ill-health (and, under the Code, non-renewal of fixed-term contract on expiry). The Constitution Bench in Punjab Land Development and Reclamation Corporation v. Presiding Officer, Labour Court (1990) read 'retrenchment' broadly to cover all terminations other than the stated exceptions (overruling the narrow view in Hariprasad Shivshankar Shukla v. A.D. Divelkar, 1957); State Bank of India v. N. Sundara Money (1976); Delhi Cloth & General Mills v. Shambhu Nath Mukherjee (1977).
  3. Conditions precedent to retrenchment (IR Code s.70; ID Act s.25F): (a) one month's written notice indicating the reasons, or wages in lieu of notice; (b) compensation equivalent to fifteen days' average pay for every completed year of continuous service or any part thereof in excess of six months; (c) notice to the appropriate Government. Applies to workers who have completed one year of continuous service (240 days in the preceding 12 months - ID Act s.25B; Code uses 'one year of continuous service'). Non-compliance renders the retrenchment void ab initio and the worker is entitled to reinstatement (Santosh Gupta v. State Bank of Patiala, 1980; Mohan Lal v. Management of Bharat Electronics Ltd., 1981; Anoop Sharma v. Executive Engineer, 2010). Procedure for retrenchment (ID Act s.25G - last come first go): follow the rule of seniority within the category unless recorded reasons; re-employment of retrenched workers (s.25H): employer must give workers retrenched in the preceding year an opportunity to offer themselves for re-employment, with preference.
  4. Worker Re-skilling Fund (IR Code s.83 Verify): employer shall credit 15 days' wages last drawn by the retrenched worker to the Worker Re-skilling Fund, from which the amount is paid to the worker's account within 45 days of retrenchment; in addition to retrenchment compensation.
  5. Transfer of undertaking (ID Act s.25FF; Code): a worker is entitled to notice and compensation as if retrenched, unless the service is not interrupted, the terms are not less favourable and the new employer is legally liable for compensation.
  6. Closure (ID Act s.2(cc); ss.25FFA, 25FFF): permanent closing down of a place of employment or part. Under ss.25FFA, an employer intending to close an undertaking employing 50 or more workers must give 60 days' notice to the appropriate Government; workers are entitled to notice and compensation as if retrenched (15 days' average pay for each year), but where the closure is due to unavoidable circumstances beyond the employer's control, compensation is capped at three months' average pay. Under Chapter X (special provisions - applicable to industrial establishments of 300 or more workers; earlier 100): prior permission of the appropriate Government is required for lay-off, retrenchment and closure (ID Act ss.25K-25S); application at least 90 days before closure; deemed permission if no response in 60 days; Government may grant permission, refuse or refer; effect of refusal; the Government can reduce or increase the 300-worker threshold by notification (IR Code s.77 Verify). The Code also provides that provisions of Chapter X apply to factory, mine or plantation - not to all industries.
  7. Case law on prior permission: Excel Wear v. Union of India (1978) (s.25-O struck down as violating Art 19(1)(g)) - later Chapter VB (ss.25K-25S) upheld in Workmen of Meenakshi Mills v. Meenakshi Mills Ltd. (1992) (upheld ss.25N as reasonable restriction); Meenakshi Mills confirms that Chapter V-B is valid.
  8. Fixed-term employment (IR Code s.2(o); ID Act and Standing Orders (Central) Rules amended 2018): a worker engaged on the basis of a written contract for a fixed period; the fixed-term worker has the same working hours, wages, allowances and other benefits as a permanent worker; entitled to gratuity pro rata even if service is less than five years (one year of service under SS Code); no notice or retrenchment compensation is payable on non-renewal at the end of the term; the employer may not use fixed-term contract to avoid regularisation of core work Verify. The Code adds flexibility; the judiciary examines abuse (e.g., Umadevi - see L8).
  9. Unfair labour practices (ID Act ss.25T-25U and Fifth Schedule; IR Code retains a list of unfair labour practices by employers and trade unions Verify): to interfere with, restrain or coerce workers in exercising rights; to dominate or support a union (company union); discriminatory discharge; failure to implement awards; victimisation; employing badlis as casuals for years; to threaten strikes without notice; to coerce workers to join or not join a union; to incite or force go-slow; sit-down strikes; to indulge in acts of force or violence. Penalty: imprisonment up to 6 months or fine up to Rs 1,000 under the ID Act s.25U; under the Code, higher fines.
  10. Offences and penalties: fines linked to contravention (strike/lock-out notice breaches, closure without notice, non-compliance with settlements); the Code decriminalises some offences with fines (Rs 10,000 to Rs 2 lakh in some cases, with imprisonment up to one month for aggravated cases Verify); compounding by the authority; offences by companies - officer in charge; courts of Metropolitan/ Judicial Magistrate; sections on cognizance only on complaint by or with the authority.
  11. Termination of probationers, temporary workers and casuals: temporary or ad hoc employees can be retrenched but only following s.25F; sham fixed-term/casual appointments are scrutinised for unfair practice; regularisation is not automatic (Uma Devi, 2006).

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
IR Code s.70 (ID Act s.25F)Retrenchment: one month notice (or wages), compensation 15 days' average pay per year, notice to Government.
IR Code s.77 (Chapter X)Prior permission for lay-off/retrenchment/closure in establishments with 300+ workers; deemed permission in 60 days.
ID Act s.25CLay-off compensation - 50% of basic wages and DA.
IR Code s.83 VerifyWorker Re-skilling Fund - 15 days' wages.

⚖Landmark cases

Punjab Land Development & Reclamation Corporation v. Presiding Officer, Labour Court

1990

Constitution Bench - 'retrenchment' means every termination except those expressly excluded.

State Bank of India v. N. Sundara Money

1976

Retrenchment includes termination for any reason other than punishment; automatic termination covered.

Santosh Gupta v. State Bank of Patiala

1980

Termination in breach of s.25F is void; reinstatement follows.

Workmen of Meenakshi Mills Ltd. v. Meenakshi Mills Ltd.

1992

Validity of Chapter VB (ss.25K-25S) - reasonable restriction on employers' right.

Excel Wear v. Union of India

1978

Section 25-O (prior permission for closure) struck down at the time.

Delhi Cloth & General Mills Co. v. Shambhu Nath Mukherjee

1977

Retrenchment - broad meaning.

Mohan Lal v. Management of Bharat Electronics Ltd.

1981

Non-compliance with s.25F makes retrenchment void ab initio.

✎Memory aids

  • Retrenchment 'N-C-G': Notice (1 month) - Compensation (15 days per year) - Government notified.
  • Prior permission 300 workers; closure notice 60 days (50+ workers).

!Exam traps

  • TrapCompensation for lay-off is 50% of basic + DA; for retrenchment 15 days' average pay per year.
  • TrapThe one-month notice for retrenchment can be replaced by wages in lieu.
  • TrapClosure compensation is limited to 3 months' average pay when due to unavoidable circumstances.
  • TrapPrior permission requirement now begins at 300 workers (earlier 100).

?Likely questions

  1. What compensation is a retrenched worker entitled to? (15 days' average pay per completed year)
  2. How much lay-off compensation? (50% of basic wages and DA)
  3. A fixed-term employee is eligible for gratuity after? (One year under the SS Code)
  4. Notice period for closure of an undertaking employing 50+ workers? (60 days)
