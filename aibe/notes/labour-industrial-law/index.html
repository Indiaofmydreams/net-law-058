Labour and Industrial Law carries about 4 of 100 questions. AIBE is a 100-question multiple-choice, open-book test; qualifying marks 45% (General/OBC) and 40% (SC/ST/PwD). Confirm format and languages in the AIBE-XXI notification Verify.

Question patterns

  • Direct recall of statute and section: 'The Industrial Relations Code replaces which Acts?'
  • Thresholds: 300 workers (standing orders / prior permission), 20 workers (EPF), 10 (ESI/gratuity/maternity), 50 (contract labour / crèche), 100 (works committee), 20 (grievance committee)
  • Percentages and periods: bonus 8.33%-20%, retrenchment compensation 15 days per year, strike notice 60 days, gratuity 5 years, maternity 26 weeks, advance tax instalments
  • Definitions: industry (triple test), worker/workman, wages, retrenchment, lay-off, supply, taxable person
  • What replaced what: Income-tax Act 1961 -> 2025; ID Act 1947 -> IR Code; GST rate slabs 2025

High-yield topics

  • Four Labour Codes: dates, number of Acts subsumed (29), rules (8-9 May 2026)
  • Industry definition and the 2026 nine-judge decision
  • Worker/workman definitions; supervisory wage ceiling Rs 18,000 (IR Code)
  • Strike and lock-out rules (60 days / 14 days notice) now applicable to all industrial establishments
  • Retrenchment: notice, compensation, re-skilling fund (15 days' wages)
  • Standing orders and prior-permission threshold: 300 workers
  • Wages Code: definition of wages (50% rule), floor wage, overtime double, deductions cap 50%
  • Gratuity, maternity benefit, EPF/ESI thresholds, gig and platform workers
  • OSH Code: factory definition, contract labour, women night shift, 8-hour day

Time strategy

  • These are mostly recall items. Spend about 60 seconds each; use the open book only to verify a threshold.
  • If a question uses an old Act name (e.g., Minimum Wages Act, ID Act), answer under the old law unless it asks about the new Code; if it says 'under the Code' use the Code.
  • For tax numbers, think about which law and which year the question is set in.

How to use these notes

  • The 8 marks split evenly: about 4 questions from Labour and Industrial Law and about 4 from Taxation. Prepare both blocks.
  • Labour block: first learn the architecture (4 Codes replacing 29 Acts), then the IR Code (highest yield), then Wages, Social Security and OSH. Keep the old Act names in mind because many questions are still framed on them.
  • Tax block: learn constitutional basics (Arts 265, 246, 246A, 279A), then the five or six Income-tax ideas that recur (residence, heads of income, exemptions, TDS, assessment, appeals), then GST (supply, registration, ITC, returns, appeals).
  • Study the 'key_numbers' table in rapid revision: AIBE loves thresholds and time-limits.
  • AIBE is open-book: use the old-to-new concordance to find the right Code quickly.
  • Overlaps: Constitutional Law (Arts 14, 19(1)(c), 21, 23, 24, 43, 246A, 265, 279A, 300A), CrPC/BNSS (prosecution procedure), Contract and NI Act, ADR (stamp duty), Torts and Consumer Protection, IP, PIL and Administrative Law (delegated legislation, natural justice).

Common traps

  • Both the Codes (21 Nov 2025) and Income-tax Act 2025 (1 April 2026) are in force, but proceedings for earlier periods continue under the old statutes.
  • Thresholds moved: standing orders and prior-permission to 300 workers (from 100); factory definition 20 with power / 40 without power (from 10/20); contract-labour applicability 50 (from 20).
  • IR Code strike notice rule now covers all industrial establishments, not only public utility services.
  • Gratuity: 5 years continuous service, but not required in case of death or disablement; fixed-term employees qualify after 1 year.
  • Wage ceilings: bonus eligibility Rs 21,000; ESI wage limit Rs 21,000; EPF wage ceiling Rs 15,000.
