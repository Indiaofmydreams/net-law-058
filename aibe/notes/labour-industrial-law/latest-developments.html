Labour and Industrial Law

Latest developments

Recent judgments, amendments and institutional changes that are favourite current-affairs questions. Notes cover developments up to 18 Nov 2026; anything tagged Verify should be checked against the official source.

Four Labour Codes in force; Central Rules notified

21 Nov 2025 (Codes); 8-9 May 2026 (Central Rules)

The Ministry of Labour and Employment brought the Code on Wages 2019, the Industrial Relations Code 2020, the Code on Social Security 2020 and the OSH Code 2020 into force on 21 November 2025, repealing 29 Central Acts. Central Rules under all four Codes were published in May 2026, after which compliance obligations began to bite; States are notifying their own rules in phases. Model Standing Orders were issued for the new regime. Trade unions have opposed the Codes and the legal validity of aspects (e.g., 300-worker thresholds) is contested in public debate Verify · pending challenges.

Why it matters: High-probability 'which Act has been repealed / which threshold now applies' questions.

Likely question: How many Central labour laws were merged into four Codes?

Answer: 29

State of U.P. v. Jai Bir Singh - meaning of 'industry'

20 Aug 2026 (nine-judge Bench; 2026 INSC 897)

By 6:3 the Court held the reference maintainable and by 5:4 refined the Bangalore Water Supply 'triple test' without a clear overruling; the 1978 test continues to govern pending disputes under the repealed Industrial Disputes Act and the refined test is prospective; the decision does not automatically fix the meaning of 'industry' in s.2(p) of the IR Code. Commentators note no clear majority on the reformulation (about four judges backed the new test) Verify.

Why it matters: A current-affairs favourite: bench strength, year, the test, the effect on hospitals/educational institutions.

Likely question: Which 1978 case's 'triple test' was reconsidered in 2026?

Answer: Bangalore Water Supply

Gig and platform workers

2023-2026

The Code on Social Security introduces aggregator contributions (1%-2% of turnover, capped at 5% of payouts); Rajasthan (2023) and Karnataka (2025) enacted State laws; Central Rules (2026) operationalise e-Shram registration and eligibility days Verify.

Why it matters: New protective regime; 'which Code' questions.

Likely question: Aggregators contribute what percentage of turnover?

Answer: 1%-2%

Regularisation of long-serving temporary workers

Dec 2024 - 2025

In Jaggo v. Union of India (Dec 2024) and Shripal v. Nagar Nigam, Ghaziabad (Jan 2025) the Supreme Court criticised the use of Umadevi to keep workers on temporary terms for decades and ordered regularisation Verify.

Why it matters: Umadevi vs Jaggo is a likely paired question.

Likely question: Which Constitution Bench decision restricts regularisation?

Answer: Umadevi
