iOverview

Thresholds, eligibility and benefits under the nine-Act merger. Gig and platform worker provisions are new and topical.

★Must know

  1. The Code subsumes: Employees' Compensation Act 1923; ESI Act 1948; EPF and Miscellaneous Provisions Act 1952; Employment Exchanges (Compulsory Notification of Vacancies) Act 1959; Maternity Benefit Act 1961; Payment of Gratuity Act 1972; Cine Workers Welfare Fund Act 1981; Building and Other Construction Workers' Welfare Cess Act 1996; Unorganised Workers' Social Security Act 2008. Administered by the EPFO, ESIC, National Social Security Board for unorganised, gig and platform workers, and the Social Security Fund.
  2. Employees' Provident Fund (Chapter III): applies to establishments with 20 or more employees (Central Government may lower the threshold or extend to others); mandatory contributions by employer and employee at 12% of wages (rate can be lower for certain establishments); statutory wage ceiling for compulsory coverage Rs 15,000 per month; three schemes - EPF Scheme 1952, Employees' Pension Scheme 1995 (EPS) and Employees' Deposit Linked Insurance Scheme 1976 (EDLI); Universal Account Number; interest on deposits declared annually; EPFO; damages for delayed payment; recovery as arrears of land revenue. 'Basic wage' for PF - Bridge and Roof Co. v. Union of India (1963); Manipal Academy of Higher Education v. Provident Fund Commissioner (2008); Vivekananda Vidya Mandir v. Regional Provident Fund Commissioner (2019) - special allowances that are universally paid are part of basic wage; Surya Roshni Ltd. v. EPF (2019).
  3. Employees' State Insurance (Chapter IV): applies to establishments with 10 or more employees (and the Code allows extension to hazardous employment even with fewer, and to establishments with less than 10 on a voluntary basis); employees drawing wages up to Rs 21,000 per month (Rs 25,000 for persons with disabilities); contributions - employer 3.25% and employee 0.75% of wages; benefits - sickness, maternity, disablement (temporary and permanent), dependants' benefit, medical benefit, funeral expenses, unemployment allowance (Atal Bimit Vyakti Kalyan Yojana); ESI Corporation. Under the Code, disputes concerning employer liability are heard by the Social Security Appellate Tribunal instead of ESI Courts Verify.
  4. Gratuity (Chapter V; formerly Payment of Gratuity Act 1972): payable on termination of employment after continuous service of five years or more - on superannuation, retirement or resignation, or death or disablement due to accident or disease (no minimum period needed in case of death or disablement). Applies to establishments with 10 or more employees (and to factories, mines, oilfields, plantations, ports, railways, shops and establishments). Fixed-term employees qualify on a pro-rata basis after one year of continuous service (Code). Formula: 15 days' wages (last drawn) for each completed year of service = (last drawn monthly wages x 15 / 26) x number of years (seasonal establishments: 7 days per season); employer's liability ceiling Rs 20 lakh (as notified). Gratuity must be paid within 30 days of becoming payable; simple interest on delay; nomination; exemption from attachment. Forfeiture: wholly or partly if services are terminated for wilful omission or negligence causing damage or loss to employer's property (to the extent of loss), or for riotous or disorderly conduct or an act of violence, or for an offence involving moral turpitude committed in the course of employment. Cases: Union Bank of India v. C.G. Ajay Babu (2018) - gratuity cannot be forfeited merely on departmental misconduct; offence involving moral turpitude must be established; Jeewanlal (1929) Ltd. v. Appellate Authority (1984) - gratuity payable irrespective of whether employer is making profits; the Controlling Authority decides disputes; appeal to Appellate Authority within 60 days.
  5. Maternity benefit (Chapter VI; formerly Maternity Benefit Act 1961 as amended in 2017): applies to every establishment employing 10 or more persons; woman must have worked at least 80 days in the 12 months preceding the expected delivery; benefit is paid at the average daily wage for the period of actual absence; duration: 26 weeks (not more than 8 weeks before the expected delivery) for the first two children; 12 weeks (6 weeks pre-delivery) for a third or subsequent child; 12 weeks for an adopting mother (child below three months) and a commissioning mother (from date child handed over); miscarriage or medical termination: 6 weeks; tubectomy: 2 weeks; illness arising out of pregnancy: 1 month; nursing breaks (two breaks of 15 minutes); work from home option after the benefit period if the nature of work permits; crèche mandatory for establishments with 50 or more employees (visits allowed four times a day); medical bonus of Rs 3,500 if no pre/post-natal care provided by employer Verify · amount; protection from dismissal during maternity. Cases: Municipal Corporation of Delhi v. Female Workers (Muster Roll) (2000) - daily wagers are entitled; Neera Mathur v. LIC (1992) - questions on pregnancy history in application invalid; Deepika Singh v. Central Administrative Tribunal (2022) - maternity leave (and child care leave) for women with step-children; Dr Kavita Yadav v. Secretary, Ministry of Health (Delhi HC, 2023) - maternity benefits to contractual employees.
  6. Employees' Compensation (Chapter VII; formerly Employees' Compensation Act 1923, earlier Workmen's Compensation Act): employer is liable to pay compensation when an employee suffers personal injury by accident arising out of and in the course of employment or contracts an occupational disease listed in Schedule III; compensation for death (50% of monthly wages x relevant factor, subject to a minimum), permanent total disablement (60% x relevant factor, minimum), partial disablement and temporary disablement; liability not arising if the disablement does not exceed three days (except death/serious), employee under influence of drugs/alcohol, wilful disobedience of safety order, wilful removal of a safety device; interest at 12% (or as notified) if compensation not paid within one month; penalty up to 50% for default without sufficient cause; claim to be made within two years of accident; Commissioner for Employees' Compensation adjudicates; appeal to High Court only on substantial question of law. Notional extension of employment: Saurashtra Salt Manufacturing Co. v. Bai Valu Raja (1958); Mackinnon Mackenzie & Co. v. Ibrahim Mahomed Issak (1969); Employees' State Insurance Corp. v. Francis De Costa (1996); Pratap Narain Singh Deo v. Srinivas Sabata (1976) - 'accident' includes injury arising from an event that is unexpected.
  7. Gig workers and platform workers (Chapter IX): 'gig worker' - person who performs work or participates in a work arrangement outside the traditional employer-employee relationship; 'platform worker' - a gig worker whose work is based on online platform; 'aggregator' - digital intermediary or marketplace for buyers and sellers (e.g., ride-hailing, food delivery). Aggregators must contribute 1% to 2% of their annual turnover (capped at 5% of the amount paid or payable to gig and platform workers) to the Social Security Fund; benefits via schemes - life and disability insurance, health and maternity benefits, old-age protection; registration on the e-Shram portal with Aadhaar-linked UAN; eligibility based on a minimum number of days of engagement (e.g., 90 days with one aggregator or 120 days across aggregators in the draft rules) Verify · Central Rules 2026. State laws: Rajasthan Platform Based Gig Workers (Registration and Welfare) Act 2023; Karnataka Platform-based Gig Workers (Social Security and Welfare) Act 2025 Verify. The Code does not decide whether gig workers are 'employees' for other labour laws.
  8. Unorganised workers and welfare: National Social Security Board; Welfare schemes (Pradhan Mantri Shram Yogi Maandhan; Ayushman Bharat; e-Shram); BOCW Welfare Boards - cess on cost of construction (1%-2%) funds the welfare of building and construction workers; Cine Workers Welfare Fund; Employment Exchanges (National Career Service portal) and mandatory notification of vacancies (now through National Career Service for establishments with 20+ employees Verify).
  9. Common features: the Code makes provision for social security for fixed-term workers, inter-state migrant workers (portability), and extends EPF/ESI to more sectors; 'wages' as in the Code on Wages; 'employee' defined broadly (includes contract and unorganised workers); Facilitator inspections; penalties for non-compliance (fines and imprisonment for diverting contributions); the Code allows aggrieved persons to appeal to the Appellate Tribunal.

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
SS Code - Gratuity (Chapter V)5 years' service (1 year for fixed-term employees); 15 days' wages per year; cap Rs 20 lakh.
SS Code - Maternity (Chapter VI)26 weeks for first two children; 12 weeks for third or adopting/commissioning mother.
SS Code - Gig workers (Chapter IX)Aggregator contribution 1%-2% of turnover capped at 5% of payouts.
EPF threshold20 employees; ESI threshold 10; wage ceilings Rs 15,000 (EPF) / Rs 21,000 (ESI).

⚖Landmark cases

Municipal Corporation of Delhi v. Female Workers (Muster Roll)

2000

Maternity benefit applies to casual/daily-wage women workers.

Deepika Singh v. Central Administrative Tribunal

2022

Maternity leave for a woman with a step-child; purposive reading of maternity law.

Vivekananda Vidya Mandir v. Regional PF Commissioner

2019

Special allowances paid universally are part of basic wage for PF.

Union Bank of India v. C.G. Ajay Babu

2018

Gratuity forfeiture needs conviction/offence involving moral turpitude.

Jeewanlal (1929) Ltd. v. Appellate Authority

1984

Gratuity is a statutory right of the employee, not dependent on the employer's profit.

Saurashtra Salt Manufacturing Co. v. Bai Valu Raja

1958

Notional extension of the employment: accident 'in the course of employment' includes the journey.

Neera Mathur v. LIC

1992

Pregnancy-related questions in service applications are invalid; privacy and dignity of women.

✎Memory aids

  • EPF 20 / ESI 10 / Gratuity 10 / Maternity 10 / Crèche 50.
  • Maternity: 26 weeks (1st & 2nd child); 12 weeks (3rd+, adoption, commissioning).

!Exam traps

  • TrapGratuity requires 5 years' continuous service, but this is not needed in case of death or disablement.
  • TrapMaternity 26 weeks applies only to the first two children.
  • TrapEPF threshold is 20 employees; ESI 10 employees.
  • TrapThe 'Employees' Compensation Act' is now merged into the Code - but claims on pre-Code accidents may still use old provisions.
