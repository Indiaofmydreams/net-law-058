iOverview

The definitions around which the whole of industrial law revolves. The nine-judge decision of 20 Aug 2026 on 'industry' is a high-probability current-affairs question.

★Must know

  1. The IR Code, 2020 consolidates and amends the law on trade unions, conditions of employment in industrial establishments or undertakings, and investigation and settlement of industrial disputes. It replaces the Trade Unions Act 1926, the Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act 1946 and the Industrial Disputes Act 1947.
  2. 'Industry' (IR Code s.2(p); ID Act s.2(j)): any systematic activity carried on by cooperation between an employer and worker (whether or not capital invested or profit motive) for production, supply or distribution of goods or services with a view to satisfy human wants or wishes (not spiritual or religious); includes any activity of the Dock Labour Board and activities relating to promotion of sales or business. Excluded: institutions owned or managed by organisations wholly or substantially engaged in charitable, social or philanthropic service; any activity of the appropriate Government relatable to sovereign functions (including defence research, atomic energy and space); domestic service; and any other activity notified. Compare s.2(j) ID Act - which was wide and the courts decided its limits.
  3. Bangalore Water Supply and Sewerage Board v. A. Rajappa (1978, seven judges): 'triple test' - (i) systematic activity; (ii) organised by cooperation between employer and employee; (iii) for the production and/or distribution of goods and services calculated to satisfy human wants and wishes. Dominant nature test for composite institutions; hospitals, educational institutions, clubs, research institutes, charities with commercial activity may be industries; only strictly sovereign functions (those which only a sovereign can perform: legislation, administration of justice, maintenance of law and order, defence) and some charitable/ spiritual activities are excluded. It overruled the narrower view in Safdarjung Hospital v. Kuldip Singh Sethi (1970) and University of Delhi v. Ram Nath (1963). Earlier: Hospital Mazdoor Sabha (1960) (hospitals are industries); Corporation of City of Nagpur v. Employees (1960).
  4. State of U.P. v. Jai Bir Singh (20 Aug 2026; 2026 INSC 897; nine judges led by CJI Surya Kant): the Court held by 6:3 that the reference was valid and, by 5:4, that the 1978 triple test needed refinement without overruling Bangalore Water Supply as a whole; the original 1978 test continues to govern all pending disputes under the repealed Industrial Disputes Act; the refined test will operate prospectively; and the Court said the 1978 expansive interpretation will not automatically govern s.2(p) of the IR Code, which has its own definition. Justice Nagarathna (reference not maintainable; definition should remain broad) and Justice Bagchi (reference valid; affirmed Bangalore Water Supply) wrote separate opinions; the outcome has been described as 4-4-1 on the merits Verify · the exact holdings from the judgment.
  5. 'Worker' (IR Code s.2(zr); ID Act 'workman' s.2(s)): any person (including apprentice - see exclusions; working journalists and sales promotion employees are included) employed in any industry to do any manual, unskilled, skilled, technical, operational, clerical or supervisory work for hire or reward, whether the terms are express or implied; includes a person dismissed, discharged or retrenched in connection with or as a consequence of an industrial dispute or whose dismissal, discharge or retrenchment has led to it; excludes members of the Armed Forces, police service or prison staff, persons mainly in managerial or administrative capacity, and persons in supervisory capacity drawing wages exceeding Rs 18,000 per month (or as notified) or exercising mainly managerial functions. The 'dominant nature of duties' test decides category (Burmah Shell Oil Storage v. Burmah Shell Management Staff Association, 1970; S.K. Maini v. Carona Sahu Co., 1994; Arkal Govind Raj Rao v. Ciba Geigy, 1985).
  6. Employer-employee relationship: control and supervision test; integration test; economic reality; 'real employer' behind contractor (Hussainbhai v. Alath Factory Thozhilali Union, 1978; Balwant Rai Saluja v. Air India, 2014 - in contract labour, a contractor's workers become employees of principal employer only if the contract is a sham). 'Employer' under the Code includes the contractor, the legal representative of a deceased employer and, in a factory, the occupier. 'Industrial establishment' means an establishment where an industry is carried on.
  7. 'Industrial dispute' (IR Code s.2(q); ID Act s.2(k)): any dispute or difference between employers and employers, employers and workers or workers and workers connected with employment, non-employment, terms of employment or conditions of labour of any person; includes a dispute about discharge, dismissal, retrenchment or termination of an individual worker (individual dispute deemed an industrial dispute). A dispute must be espoused by a union or a substantial number of workers to be an 'industrial dispute' in the case of non-individual disputes (Workmen of Dimakuchi Tea Estate v. Dimakuchi Tea Estate, 1958). Dispute between an employee and an employer who is not within the definition of industry is not covered.
  8. Bi-partite forums: Works Committee (IR Code s.3) in industrial establishments with 100 or more workers - equal number of employer and worker representatives, to promote measures for good relations; Grievance Redressal Committee (s.4) in every industrial establishment with 20 or more workers - up to ten members, equal representation of employer and workers, women proportionally; grievance must be raised within one year of cause; decision within 30 days; an aggrieved worker may seek conciliation within 30 days of the committee's decision Verify · section numbers.
  9. Trade unions (Chapter III; replaces Trade Unions Act 1926): any combination of workers or employers for regulating relations or imposing restrictive conditions on conduct of trade or business, including federation. Registration: at least 7 members, and (in the case of an establishment) not less than 10% of workers or 100 workers, whichever is less - subject to a minimum of 7; Registrar of Trade Unions (State/Central); certificate of registration; union is a body corporate; immunity from criminal conspiracy (for agreement to do an act in furtherance of trade dispute) and civil suit for acts done in furtherance of a trade dispute; objects and funds (general and political fund - political activity only through a separate political fund and contributing members' consent); not more than one-third of office-bearers or five (whichever is less) may be outsiders; cancellation of registration by Registrar subject to appeal to Industrial Tribunal; unions may amalgamate.
  10. Recognition of negotiating union (s.14): the union having 51% or more of the workers on the muster roll is the sole negotiating union; if no union has 51%, a negotiating council is constituted from unions with at least 20% membership, seats proportionate; the negotiating union/council negotiates with the employer on matters like wages and conditions and may be consulted on standing orders. A 'sole negotiating union' recognised by the employer from verification of membership by the employer or the appropriate Government.
  11. Right to unionise: Art 19(1)(c) protects the right to form unions but not the right to recognition or to strike (All India Bank Employees' Association v. National Industrial Tribunal, 1962; Kameshwar Prasad v. State of Bihar, 1962; B.R. Singh v. Union of India, 1989). Union leaders enjoy limited protection; Rangarajan (2003) - no right to strike even for government employees.
  12. Other basic terms: 'wages' for the Code (Section 2) - same as Code on Wages; 'appropriate Government'; 'average pay'; 'conciliation proceeding'; 'lay-off'; 'lock-out'; 'retrenchment'; 'strike'; 'trade union'; 'unfair labour practice' (appearing in the Code with a list in the Schedule Verify); 'fixed term employment' (see L4); 'standing orders'.

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says

IR Code s.2(zr)Definition of 'worker' - supervisory wage limit Rs 18,000.

⚖Landmark cases

Bangalore Water Supply & Sewerage Board v. A. Rajappa

1978

Triple test for industry; wide reading; sovereign functions alone excluded.

State of U.P. v. Jai Bir Singh

2026

Nine-judge Bench (20 Aug 2026): reference valid; BWSSB refined, not overruled; original test governs pending ID Act cases; IR Code s.2(p) not automatically governed.

Hospital Mazdoor Sabha v. State of Madras

1960

Hospitals can be industries.

Workmen of Dimakuchi Tea Estate v. Management

1958

Collective dispute must be espoused by union or substantial body of workmen.

Burmah Shell Oil Storage v. Burmah Shell Management Staff Association

1970

Dominant nature of duties determines whether a person is a workman.

Hussainbhai v. Alath Factory Thozhilali Union

1978

Control test; real employer behind the contractor.

All India Bank Employees' Association v. National Industrial Tribunal

1962

Art 19(1)(c) protects forming unions, not the right to strike or to recognition.

Balwant Rai Saluja v. Air India

2014

Contract labour not automatically employees of principal employer.

✎Memory aids

  • Triple test: 'S-O-P' - Systematic, Organised cooperation, Production/distribution to satisfy wants.
  • Works Committee 100, GRC 20, Standing Orders 300.

!Exam traps

  • TrapThe wage limit of Rs 18,000 applies to supervisory employees to decide whether they are 'workers'.
  • TrapTrade union registration needs at least 7 members (and 10%/100 workers whichever is lesser).
  • TrapSole negotiating union = 51%; negotiating council requires unions with 20% each.
  • TrapArt 19(1)(c) does not include the right to strike (Rangarajan).
