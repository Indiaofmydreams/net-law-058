iOverview

Statutory accountability bodies and the Right to Information Act 2005 - timelines and exemptions are exam favourites.

★Must know

  1. LOKPAL AND LOKAYUKTAS ACT 2013 (in force 16 January 2014): Lokpal at the Centre and Lokayuktas in States (Sec 63 required States to establish Lokayuktas within 365 days of commencement). LOKPAL: a Chairperson and not more than eight members, of whom fifty per cent shall be judicial members; at least fifty per cent of members from SC, ST, OBC, minorities and women. The Chairperson: a person who is or has been the Chief Justice of India or a Judge of the Supreme Court or an eminent person with special knowledge of anti-corruption policy, public administration, vigilance, finance, law and management. Selection Committee: PM (Chair), Speaker of Lok Sabha, Leader of Opposition in Lok Sabha, CJI or a Judge of the Supreme Court nominated by the CJI, and an eminent jurist nominated by the President on the recommendation of the first four. Term: five years or until 70 years of age (Chairperson/ members). Jurisdiction (Sec 14): includes the Prime Minister (except in matters relating to international relations, external and internal security, public order, atomic energy and space - and an inquiry against the PM must be held in camera and the decision is by 2/3 of full Bench of Lokpal), Ministers, MPs, Group A-D officers and officers of Central Government, officials of bodies/ boards funded by the Government, and persons receiving foreign donations above a specified amount (Rs 10 lakh per year) - the Prevention of Corruption Act 1988 offences. The limitation: no complaint after seven years from the date of the alleged offence (Sec 53). Lokpal has an Inquiry Wing and Prosecution Wing; may direct CBI. First Lokpal Chairperson: Justice Pinaki Chandra Ghose (March 2019). Recent: In 2025 the Lokpal assumed jurisdiction to examine complaints against a sitting High Court Judge, and the Supreme Court stayed that order (February 2025) - verify the latest position.
  2. LOKAYUKTA (State): created by State laws (Maharashtra 1971 first). CENTRAL VIGILANCE COMMISSION (CVC): initially by executive resolution (Santhanam Committee 1964); statutory status under the Central Vigilance Commission Act 2003 following Vineet Narain v Union of India (1998) (Jain Hawala case): CBI independence, CVC to supervise CBI, directions to insulate from political influence; CVC consists of a Central Vigilance Commissioner and not more than two Vigilance Commissioners; appointed by the President on recommendation of a committee (PM, Home Minister, Leader of Opposition). CBI: Delhi Special Police Establishment Act 1946; Sec 6A (single directive requiring prior approval for inquiry against officers of Joint Secretary level and above) was struck down in Subramanian Swamy v Director, CBI (2014) as violative of Art 14; Prevention of Corruption (Amendment) Act 2018 introduced Sec 17A - prior approval for inquiry/ investigation into offences relating to decisions/ recommendations of a public servant (a split verdict in 2023 referred the validity to a larger Bench - verify).
  3. RIGHT TO INFORMATION ACT 2005: Sec 2(h) 'public authority'; Sec 2(f) information; Sec 3: all citizens have right to information; Sec 4: proactive disclosure (suo motu); Sec 5: Public Information Officers (PIOs) and Assistant PIOs; Sec 6: request for information - application to PIO with the prescribed fee (Central: Rs 10), no reason need be given (Sec 6(2)); Sec 7(1): the PIO shall dispose of the request within THIRTY DAYS; where the information concerns the life or liberty of a person - within FORTY-EIGHT HOURS; transfer under Sec 6(3) - 5 days; Sec 7(5): no fee for persons below the poverty line; Sec 8: EXEMPTIONS - (a) information that would prejudicially affect sovereignty and integrity of India, security, strategic, scientific or economic interests of the State, relation with foreign State or lead to incitement of an offence; (b) information forbidden to be published by any court; (c) breach of privilege of Parliament or State Legislature; (d) commercial confidence, trade secrets or intellectual property, the disclosure of which would harm the competitive position of a third party, unless the competent authority is satisfied that larger public interest warrants the disclosure; (e) information available to a person in his fiduciary relationship, unless larger public interest; (f) information received in confidence from a foreign government; (g) information which would endanger the life or physical safety of any person or identify the source of information given in confidence for law enforcement; (h) information which would impede the process of investigation or apprehension or prosecution of offenders; (i) cabinet papers including records of deliberations of the Council of Ministers, Secretaries and other officers (after decision, material to be made public); (j) information which relates to personal information the disclosure of which has no relationship to any public activity or interest, or which would cause unwarranted invasion of the privacy of the individual unless the PIO is satisfied that larger public interest justifies disclosure - provided that information which cannot be denied to Parliament or State Legislature shall not be denied to any person. Sec 8(2): public interest override - information may be disclosed if public interest in disclosure outweighs harm to the protected interests (even for the Official Secrets Act 1923 or exemptions); Sec 8(3): information relating to any occurrence, event or matter which has taken place, occurred or happened twenty years before the date of request shall be provided (subject to exceptions in 8(1)(a), (c), (i)); Sec 9: copyright infringement; Sec 10: severability; Sec 11: third party information; Sec 24: exemption of certain organisations (Second Schedule - intelligence and security organisations such as IB, RAW and similar agencies - but allegations of corruption and human rights violations must be disclosed (with approval of the Information Commission within 45 days for human rights; information on corruption allegations - no approval).
  4. APPEALS AND PENALTIES: Sec 19(1): first appeal to the First Appellate Authority (an officer senior in rank to the PIO) within THIRTY DAYS from the expiry of the period within which the decision should have been made or the receipt of the decision; Sec 19(3): second appeal to the Central/ State Information Commission within NINETY DAYS; Sec 18: complaints to the Information Commission; the burden of proving that denial was justified lies on the PIO (Sec 19(5)); Sec 20: penalty - the Information Commission may impose a penalty of Rs 250 per day till the information is furnished, subject to a maximum of Rs 25,000 on the PIO for refusing to receive an application, not furnishing information within the time, malafide denial, incorrect/ incomplete/ misleading information, or destroying information; recommends disciplinary action against persistent defaulters (Sec 20(2)); Sec 22: the Act overrides inconsistent provisions of the Official Secrets Act 1923; Sec 23: bar of jurisdiction of courts (but Art 32/226 remain); Sec 12-15: constitution of the CIC (Chief Information Commissioner and not more than ten Information Commissioners) and State Information Commissions; RTI (Amendment) Act 2019 changed the term, status and salary of the Information Commissioners (made them as prescribed by the Central Government - challenged as eroding independence). Landmark: Secretary General, Supreme Court of India v Subhash Chandra Agarwal (2019, Constitution Bench): Office of the Chief Justice of India is a 'public authority' under RTI; judicial independence is not compromised by disclosure; information on judges' assets and collegium decisions subject to balancing (privacy and public interest); CBSE v Aditya Bandopadhyay (2011): examinees' answer books are accessible; fiduciary exemption; Girish Ramchandra Deshpande v Central Information Commissioner (2013): personal information such as service records and assets are generally personal; Reserve Bank of India v Jayantilal N. Mistry (2015): RBI cannot withhold bank inspection reports citing fiduciary relationship - public interest; Anjali Bhardwaj v Union of India (2019): vacancies in Information Commissions must be filled in a time-bound manner.
  5. DPDP ACT 2023 AND RTI (HOT): Sec 44(3) of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 amended Sec 8(1)(j) of the RTI Act so that exemption for 'personal information' applies without the earlier public interest balancing proviso and without the qualifier that it must have 'no relationship to any public activity or interest'; the amendment was notified into force with the DPDP Rules in November 2025 (14 November 2025); challenges are pending before the Supreme Court and the debate on RTI dilution continues (verify). Digital initiatives: the Right to Services legislation in States (time-bound public services - e.g., Madhya Pradesh Lok Sewa Guarantee Act 2010), citizens' charters.

!Exam traps

  • TrapRTI: PIO reply in 30 days (48 hours for life/ liberty); first appeal 30 days; second appeal 90 days.
  • TrapSec 20 penalty: Rs 250 per day, maximum Rs 25,000.
  • TrapSec 8(1)(j): personal information exemption; now altered by DPDP Act.
  • TrapLokpal: 8 members + Chairperson; 50% judicial; PM covered subject to restrictions; 7-year limit for complaints.

?Likely questions

  1. Time for PIO to respond under RTI: 30 days.
  2. First appeal under RTI: 30 days; second appeal: 90 days.
  3. Lokpal composition: Chairperson + max 8 members.
  4. Which case made the CJI's office a public authority? Subhash Chandra Agarwal.
