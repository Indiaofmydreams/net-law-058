The Charge: Form, Contents, Alteration, Joinder and Discharge

iOverview

What a charge must contain, when it may be altered, separate and joint trial rules, and the standard for discharge/framing of charge (now with 60-day limits).

★Must know

  1. Charge (s.234-237 BNSS; ss.211-214 CrPC): every charge states the offence with which the accused is charged; the law and section; the specific name of the offence if it has one, otherwise the definition; particulars as to time, place, person against whom, or thing in respect of which, the offence was committed (s.235; s.212); in cases of criminal breach of trust or dishonest misappropriation, it is enough to specify gross sum and dates between which the offence was committed (s.235 - as in s.212(2) CrPC) - and the total period must not exceed one year. The manner of commission may be specified where needed (s.236; s.213). The charge is written in the language of the court; read and explained to the accused.
  2. Effect of errors (s.238 BNSS; s.215 CrPC; s.510-511 BNSS (ss.464-465 CrPC)): an error or omission in the charge is not material unless the accused was in fact misled and it occasioned a failure of justice. Willie (William) Slaney v. State of M.P. (1955): the test is prejudice; the court looks at whether the accused knew what he was being tried for. Absence of charge is a curable irregularity unless prejudice (s.510).
  3. Alteration of charge (s.239 BNSS; s.216 CrPC): any court may alter or add to a charge at any time before judgment is pronounced; every alteration is read and explained; if the alteration is not likely to prejudice the accused, the trial proceeds; otherwise the court may direct a new trial or adjourn; sanction is required if the new charge needs sanction. s.240 (s.217): recall of witnesses on alteration. Court cannot convict for an offence for which the charge was not framed unless it is a minor offence (s.245 BNSS; s.222 CrPC) or cognate offence with no prejudice. Dr. Nallapareddy Sridhar Reddy v. State of A.P. (2020); Kantilal Chandulal Mehta v. State of Maharashtra (1969).
  4. Separate charges for distinct offences (s.241; s.218 CrPC): for every distinct offence a separate charge and trial; exceptions: three offences of the same kind within twelve months (s.242; s.219 CrPC); offences committed in the course of the same transaction (s.243; s.220 - one trial); doubt as to which of several offences has been committed - charge in the alternative (s.244; s.221); where the accused is charged with an offence and facts proved reduce it to a minor offence, he may be convicted of the minor (s.245; s.222). Persons who may be charged jointly (s.246; s.223 CrPC): persons accused of the same offence or offences committed in the course of the same transaction, or abetment/attempt, or offences of the same kind committed in conjunction. Withdrawal of remaining charges (s.247; s.224).
  5. Framing of charge/discharge in a Sessions case (s.250-251 BNSS; ss.227-228 CrPC): after committal and hearing both sides, if the Judge considers that there is no sufficient ground for proceeding, he discharges the accused after recording reasons (s.250); the application for discharge is to be made by the accused within sixty days from the date of commitment (new time-limit). If there is ground for presuming that the accused has committed an offence triable by a Magistrate, he may frame a charge and transfer the case to the CJM; if triable exclusively by the Court of Session, he frames a charge in writing (s.251); the charge shall be framed within sixty days from the date of the first hearing on charge (new time-limit); the charge is read and explained and the accused is asked whether he pleads guilty or claims to be tried.
  6. Standard for charge: only a prima facie case; the Judge may sift and weigh the material to the extent of finding if a prima facie case exists, but cannot conduct a mini-trial (Union of India v. Prafulla Kumar Samal, 1979; Sajjan Kumar v. CBI, 2010; Amit Kapoor v. Ramesh Chander, 2012; State of Rajasthan v. Ashok Kumar Kashyap, 2021). Strong suspicion is enough to frame a charge; grave suspicion not rebutted - Onkar Nath Mishra v. State (NCT of Delhi), 2008. The accused has no right to produce defence material at the charge stage (State of Orissa v. Debendra Nath Padhi, 2005 - Constitution Bench - though the Court in Nitya Dharmananda v. Gopal Sheelum Reddy, 2018 allowed sterling-quality documents in rare cases).
  7. Discharge in warrant cases on police report (s.262 BNSS; s.239 CrPC) and on complaint (s.268; s.245): Magistrate discharges where the charge is groundless, recording reasons; if not discharged, charge is framed (s.263; s.240 CrPC).
  8. Joint trial of accused: s.246 BNSS; separate trials may be ordered where joinder causes prejudice. Misjoinder is an irregularity curable under s.511 BNSS unless prejudice is shown.
  9. Charge as the foundation: trial without a charge in Sessions and warrant cases is irregular; in summons-cases the 'substance of the accusation' is stated (s.274; s.251 CrPC).

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
BNSS s.239 (CrPC s.216)Alteration of charge at any time before judgment.
BNSS s.241 (CrPC s.218)Separate charge for every distinct offence.
BNSS s.250 (CrPC s.227)Discharge in sessions case - application within 60 days of committal.
BNSS s.251 (CrPC s.228)Framing of charge - within 60 days from first hearing on charge.

⚖Landmark cases

Willie (William) Slaney v. State of M.P.

1955

Defects in charge are cured if no prejudice; test is whether the accused was misled.

Union of India v. Prafulla Kumar Samal

1979

Judge's power to sift evidence at charge stage is limited to prima facie existence.

State of Orissa v. Debendra Nath Padhi

2005

At the charge stage the accused cannot rely on defence material.

Sajjan Kumar v. CBI

2010

Principles on framing/discharge; strong suspicion suffices.

Kantilal Chandulal Mehta v. State of Maharashtra

1969

Court's power to alter charge subject to prejudice.

State of Rajasthan v. Ashok Kumar Kashyap

2021

Court cannot hold a mini-trial at the charge stage.

✎Memory aids

  • Charge stage: 'Discharge 60 / Charge 60' - application for discharge within 60 days of committal; charge to be framed within 60 days of first hearing.

!Exam traps

  • TrapAlteration can be done any time before judgment, even at the appellate stage in limited cases.
  • TrapDischarge standard is 'prima facie' - not 'beyond reasonable doubt'.
  • TrapThe 60-day timelines are new and have no CrPC equivalent.

?Likely questions

  1. Which BNSS section corresponds to s.216 CrPC? (s.239)
  2. Within how many days is the charge to be framed? (60 from first hearing)
  3. At what stage can a charge be altered? (Any time before judgment)
