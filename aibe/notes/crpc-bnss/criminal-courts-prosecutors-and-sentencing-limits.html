Constitution and Powers of Criminal Courts; Public Prosecutors; Sentencing Limits

iOverview

Hierarchy of courts, which court tries what, how much punishment each court may award, and the role of Public Prosecutors and the Directorate of Prosecution.

★Must know

  1. Classes of criminal courts (s.6 BNSS; s.6 CrPC): (i) Courts of Session; (ii) Judicial Magistrates of the First Class; (iii) Judicial Magistrates of the Second Class; (iv) Executive Magistrates. The BNSS drops the CrPC's Metropolitan Magistrates and metropolitan areas; the Chief Judicial Magistrate heads the Judicial Magistrates in a district.
  2. Court of Session: established by the State Government for every sessions division; Sessions Judge appointed by the High Court; Additional/Assistant Sessions Judges may be appointed by the High Court. HC may appoint judges of one division to exercise jurisdiction in another.
  3. Subordination: Chief Judicial Magistrate is subordinate to the Sessions Judge; every other Judicial Magistrate is subordinate to the CJM; Additional Sessions Judge exercises powers as directed by the Sessions Judge.
  4. Sentencing powers - High Court and Sessions Judge (s.22 BNSS; s.28 CrPC): High Court - any sentence authorised by law; Sessions Judge/Additional Sessions Judge - any sentence authorised by law but a death sentence must be confirmed by the High Court; Assistant Sessions Judge - imprisonment up to ten years and fine to any amount.
  5. Sentencing powers - Magistrates (s.23 BNSS; s.29 CrPC): CJM - any sentence authorised by law except death, life imprisonment or imprisonment exceeding seven years; Judicial Magistrate First Class - imprisonment up to three years, or fine up to Rs 50,000 (CrPC: Rs 10,000), or both, or community service; Judicial Magistrate Second Class - imprisonment up to one year, or fine up to Rs 10,000 (CrPC: Rs 5,000), or both, or community service.
  6. Default imprisonment on non-payment of fine (s.24 BNSS; s.30 CrPC): not exceeding one-fourth of the maximum term of imprisonment for the offence, and not beyond the Magistrate's power. Consecutive sentences (s.25 BNSS; s.31 CrPC): the aggregate must not exceed twice the maximum the court can impose for a single offence, and must not exceed 14 years (CrPC proviso) in the case of a Magistrate.
  7. Court by which offences triable (s.21 BNSS; s.26 CrPC): offences under BNS - High Court, Court of Session, or any other court shown in First Schedule; offences against other laws - the court mentioned in the law or, failing that, per First Schedule Part II. Offences under certain BNS sections (e.g., ss.64-71 rape-related) are ordinarily tried by a court presided over by a woman judge as far as practicable (s.21 proviso; CrPC 26 proviso).
  8. Executive Magistrates: District Magistrate and Sub-Divisional Magistrate - preventive, security, public-order and maintenance-of-peace functions (ss.125-143, 148-167), not trial of offences generally. Special Executive Magistrates may be appointed.
  9. Public Prosecutors (s.18 BNSS; s.24 CrPC): Central/State Government appoints PP for High Court; for districts, the State Government appoints a PP and Additional PPs from a panel of names prepared by the District Magistrate in consultation with the Sessions Judge; the person must have been an advocate for not less than seven years. Assistant Public Prosecutors (s.19; s.25 CrPC) conduct prosecutions in Magistrate courts; a police officer may be appointed APP only if he has not taken part in the investigation and is not below the rank of Inspector. Director of Prosecution and Deputy/Assistant Directors (s.20; s.25A CrPC) head the Directorate of Prosecution in the State.
  10. Role of the Public Prosecutor: officer of the court and independent (Shiv Kumar v. Hukam Chand, 1999; Thakur Ram v. State of Bihar, 1966). A private lawyer may assist the PP and, with the Magistrate's permission, conduct the prosecution (s.339 BNSS; s.302 CrPC), but the PP remains in charge. The victim may engage an advocate to assist the prosecution (proviso to s.338 BNSS; proviso to s.301(2) CrPC).
  11. Additional safeguards in BNSS on courts: audio-video electronic means may be used for appearances (s.530); courts may sit in any place for convenience if necessary; electronic summons and warrants; the procedure of 'trial in absentia' for proclaimed offenders (s.356).

⚖Landmark cases

Shiv Kumar v. Hukam Chand

1999

PP must be independent; private counsel's role is only to assist unless the Court grants permission.

Rekha Murarka v. State of W.B.

2019

Victim can engage counsel but the PP leads prosecution; counsel for victim limited in cross-examination of witnesses.

Thakur Ram v. State of Bihar

1966

Prosecution is the State's function; PP is not an advocate for the complainant.

State of Gujarat v. Hon'ble High Court of Gujarat

1998

Prisoners may be put to work as part of rigorous imprisonment; wages must be paid.

Mohd. Giasuddin v. State of A.P.

1977

Sentencing should aim at reformation; punishment must be individualised.

✎Memory aids

  • Sentence ladder: Magistrate II (1 yr) -> Magistrate I (3 yrs) -> CJM (7 yrs) -> ASJ (10 yrs) -> Sessions Judge (anything; death confirmed by HC) -> High Court (anything).

!Exam traps

  • TrapASJ's limit is ten years (not seven).
  • TrapA Sessions Judge may pass a death sentence but cannot execute it until the High Court confirms (s.407 BNSS).
  • TrapMetropolitan Magistrates no longer exist in BNSS.
  • TrapPP must have seven years' practice; the policeman-APP condition is different.

?Likely questions

  1. What is the maximum sentence a Chief Judicial Magistrate can impose? (Seven years)
  2. A Judicial Magistrate of the First Class can impose a fine of up to? (Rs 50,000)
  3. Which section of BNSS provides for Directorate of Prosecution? (s.20)
  4. Who can appoint a Public Prosecutor in a district? (State Government from a panel)
