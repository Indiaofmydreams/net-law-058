Criminal Procedure Code / Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita

Latest developments

Recent judgments, amendments and institutional changes that are favourite current-affairs questions. Notes cover developments up to 17 Nov 2026; anything tagged Verify should be checked against the official source.

BNSS in force since 1 July 2024: transition and early judicial treatment

1 Jul 2024 onward

The BNSS, BNS and BSA replaced the CrPC, IPC and Evidence Act. Offences before 1 July 2024 are tried under the IPC but proceedings begun afterwards follow BNSS procedure; pending matters continue under CrPC (s.531(2)). Courts have repeatedly applied the principle that procedural law operates prospectively from commencement while Art 20(1) protects accused against retrospective penal provisions.

Why it matters: Conceptual MCQs on s.531 and on 'which code applies' are likely.

Likely question: Which BNSS section deals with repeal and savings?

Answer: s.531

Written grounds of arrest are mandatory in every case

Mihir Rajesh Shah v. State of Maharashtra, 6 Nov 2025 (2025 INSC 1288); earlier Vihaan Kumar v. State of Haryana (Feb 2025)

Grounds of arrest must be conveyed in writing, in a language the arrestee understands, under every statute (IPC/BNS and special laws alike); if oral intimation is made at the time of arrest, the written grounds must follow within a reasonable time and at least two hours before production before the Magistrate for remand; non-compliance renders arrest and remand illegal and entitles the person to release (without prejudice to re-arrest by compliant process). A later Supreme Court order (Sept 2026, Jaskaran Jeet Singh Deol v. State of Punjab) was reported to address the consequences of non-compliance Verify.

Why it matters: Direct link between Art 22(1) and s.47 BNSS; very likely MCQ.

Likely question: Written grounds of arrest must be furnished how long before the remand hearing?

Answer: At least two hours

Section 175(3) and (4) BNSS - Magistrate's power to order investigation

Supreme Court rulings Jan 2026, Apr 2026, Oct 2026 Verify · titles

The Court has examined the new conditions in s.175(3) (affidavit, inquiry, hearing the police) and s.175(4) (report from superior officer and hearing the public servant before a Magistrate directs investigation into complaints against public servants for acts in official duty). Reported holdings: s.175(4) works with s.175(3) and does not stand alone; it widens safeguards for public servants compared with s.156(3) CrPC; and no prior sanction is needed merely to direct FIR registration Verify.

Why it matters: New wording with no CrPC equivalent.

Likely question: Which BNSS section corresponds to s.156(3) CrPC?

Answer: s.175(3)

Bail jurisprudence under BNSS

2025-2026

Narayan v. State of M.P. (2026): conditions under s.480(3) are not engaged for offences up to seven years; Sumit v. State of U.P. (2026 INSC 145): anticipatory bail ordinarily continues without fixed expiry; Salochna Pardi v. State of M.P.: anticipatory bail to be granted with circumspection in serious cases Verify; Shally Mahant v. State of Punjab (9 Feb 2026): an accused who merely does not give the answers the investigating officer desires is not 'non-cooperating' Verify; Bombay High Court (2026) held the s.482(4) bar not to be absolute in all circumstances Verify.

Why it matters: Bail is the most heavily tested area.

Likely question: Is anticipatory bail time-limited?

Answer: No - Sushila Aggarwal

Section 479 BNSS: undertrial release

Aug-Sept 2024 onward

The Supreme Court in the prison conditions matter directed that s.479 BNSS applies to all pending cases irrespective of when the offence was committed and that jail superintendents must move the court on completion of the relevant period; first-time offenders get relief at one-third and others at one-half of the maximum term Verify · order date.

Why it matters: New, beneficial, retrospective in effect.

Likely question: A first-time undertrial offender is entitled to bail after one-third of the maximum sentence under which section?

Answer: s.479

Forensic investigation, e-FIR, audio-video recording: implementation

2024-2026

Mandatory forensic visits for 7+ year offences (s.176(3)), audio-video recording of searches (s.105), e-FIR and electronic summons are being rolled out in phases by the States; the Home Ministry and the National Forensic Sciences University have run training programmes. Courts have begun excluding or discounting evidence where mandatory recordings are absent in some High Court decisions, though the Supreme Court has not laid down a blanket exclusion rule Verify.

Why it matters: 'Which BNSS section requires...' questions.

Likely question: Which section mandates audio-video recording of search?

Answer: s.105

Remission and the 'appropriate Government'

Bilkis Yakub Rasool v. Union of India, 8 Jan 2024

The Supreme Court quashed the Gujarat government's remission of 11 life convicts, holding that the State in which the offender is tried and sentenced (Maharashtra) is the appropriate Government for remission; the earlier Gujarat order was obtained by suppression and fraud.

Why it matters: s.473 BNSS (s.432 CrPC).

Likely question: Which government is the 'appropriate Government' for remission?

Answer: Where the offender was sentenced

Maintenance of divorced Muslim women

Mohd. Abdul Samad v. State of Telangana, 10 Jul 2024

Section 125 CrPC (now s.144 BNSS) applies to all women regardless of religion; the 1986 Act is an additional, not exclusive, remedy.

Why it matters: Maintenance under s.144.

Likely question: Is s.125 CrPC / s.144 BNSS applicable to Muslim women?

Answer: Yes

Sanction for prosecution of public servants

BNSS s.218 and later rulings

BNSS s.218 (s.197 CrPC) keeps the sanction regime and adds a statutory time-limit for the Government (120 days) Verify; the Supreme Court has continued to insist on a 'reasonable connection' with official duty.

Why it matters: Sanction and cognizance.

Likely question: Which section governs sanction to prosecute a public servant?

Answer: s.218 BNSS

Trial in absentia and proclaimed offenders

2024-2026

Section 356 BNSS allows trial of a proclaimed offender in absentia after prescribed notices; High Courts have emphasised notice, representation by State-paid counsel and reasons; the first reported convictions under the provision are being tested in appeals Verify.

Why it matters: New provision; likely a 'which section' question.

Likely question: Trial in absentia - which section?

Answer: s.356

Identification and technology laws interacting with BNSS

Criminal Procedure (Identification) Act, 2022; Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023

The 2022 Act (in force from 4 Aug 2022) permits collection of biometric and biological samples of convicts and arrestees (record retention for 75 years). Interaction with Art 20(3), privacy (Puttaswamy) and s.349 BNSS is likely to attract judicial review; DPDP Act 2023 has exemptions for State investigation.

Why it matters: Related-topic question.

Likely question: Which Act replaced the Identification of Prisoners Act 1920?

Answer: Criminal Procedure (Identification) Act 2022

Cross-over with Constitutional Law: Arts 20-22 and Art 142

Recurring

Art 22(1) (written grounds), Art 21 (speedy trial, bail), Art 20(3) (specimen/voice samples) and Art 142 (complete justice) all feed procedural rulings; Constitution Bench decisions on bail and quashing continue to shape BNSS interpretation.

Why it matters: Integrated questions.

Likely question: Which Article requires production before the Magistrate within 24 hours?

Answer: Art 22(2)
