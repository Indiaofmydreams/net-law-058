Question patterns

  • Section-number recall in both codes: 'Which section of BNSS corresponds to s.438 CrPC?'
  • Time-limit and number questions: 24 hours, 15 days, 60/90 days, 14 days, 3 days, 45 days, 3 months, 6 months, 7 years
  • Cognizable v non-cognizable, bailable v non-bailable, summons v warrant case
  • Who has power: Magistrate class and sentencing limits (s.23 BNSS), Sessions Judge, Executive Magistrate
  • Statement-based items: 'Which of the following is correct about anticipatory bail?'
  • Case-to-principle matching: Lalita Kumari, Arnesh Kumar, Hardeep Singh, Sushila Aggarwal, Bhajan Lal
  • Fact scenarios: arrest without written grounds, default bail on day 61/91, cognizance after limitation, transfer of case
  • New BNSS features: zero FIR, e-FIR, forensic visit, trial in absentia, audio-video recording, witness protection, victim information

High-yield topics

  • FIR - s.173 BNSS (154 CrPC), Lalita Kumari, zero FIR, e-FIR, preliminary enquiry
  • Arrest - s.35 (41), s.35(3) notice (41A), s.35(7) new, s.47-48 rights, s.58 24-hour rule
  • Remand and default bail - s.187 (167), s.479 (436A)
  • Bail - ss.478, 480, 482, 483; Sibbia, Sushila Aggarwal, Arnesh Kumar, Satender Kumar Antil
  • Cognizance - s.210 (190), s.223 (200) with the new 'hear accused' proviso, s.218 (197) sanction
  • Charge/discharge - ss.234-247; s.250 discharge within 60 days; s.251 charge within 60 days
  • Trial types - sessions (248-260), warrant (261-273), summons (274-282), summary (283-288)
  • Section 358 (319 CrPC) additional accused - Hardeep Singh
  • Plea bargaining - ss.289-300
  • Maintenance - s.144 (125)
  • Inherent powers - s.528 (482); Bhajan Lal categories
  • Limitation - s.514 (468); s.337 (300) double jeopardy; s.359 (320) compounding; s.360 (321) withdrawal

Time strategy

  • Most CrPC/BNSS items are recall: 60-90 seconds each. Use the open book only to confirm numbers and periods.
  • When the question quotes an old CrPC section, convert it using the concordance; do not guess.
  • For 'which of the following is NOT' questions, eliminate options that are correct under either code.

Common traps

  • Police custody: maximum 15 days in total (s.187), but BNSS permits it to be taken in parts within the first 40 or 60 days of the 60/90-day detention window (CrPC practice: only the first 15 days - Anupam Kulkarni).
  • Default bail period is 90 days only where the offence is punishable with death, life or imprisonment of not less than 10 years; 60 days for all other offences (Rakesh Kumar Paul, 2017).
  • Summons-case = offence NOT punishable with death, life or imprisonment exceeding 2 years; warrant-case = death, life or more than 2 years.
  • Anticipatory bail is not time-limited by default (Sibbia; Sushila Aggarwal); BNSS s.482(4) bars it only for BNS ss.65 and 70(2) (aggravated sexual offences against minors / gang rape of minors).
  • BNSS does not recognise Metropolitan Magistrates; only Sessions, JMFC, JMSC and Executive Magistrates.
  • FIR is not substantive evidence; s.181 (162 CrPC) statements to police cannot be signed and are used only to contradict.
  • Cognizance is taken of the offence, not the offender (Raghubans Dubey).
  • The Magistrate must examine the complainant under s.223 BNSS, and now must also hear the accused before taking cognizance on a complaint.
  • Section 358 (319 CrPC) needs 'strong and cogent evidence' - more than prima facie (Hardeep Singh).
  • Offence law is applied as at the date of the offence (Art 20(1)); procedure follows the new code for proceedings begun after 1 July 2024 (s.531).
