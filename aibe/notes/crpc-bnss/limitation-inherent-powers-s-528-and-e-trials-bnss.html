iOverview

Custody and disposal of seized property, the effect of errors, limitation for cognizance, the High Court's inherent power (quashing), and the new electronic-mode and repeal provisions.

★Must know

  1. Order for custody and disposal of property pending trial (s.497 BNSS; s.451 CrPC): where any property is produced before a court during an inquiry or trial, the court may make such order as it thinks fit for its proper custody pending conclusion; if the property is subject to speedy and natural decay, it may be sold; BNSS (new, per press): the court must ensure that the property is photographed/videographed and a statement prepared and, after the identification/photography, the property can be released or disposed of within thirty days Verify. Sunderbhai Ambalal Desai v. State of Gujarat (2002) - valuable articles and vehicles should not be allowed to rust in police stations; release on superdari; General Insurance Council v. State of A.P. (2010). Order for disposal at the conclusion of the trial (s.498; s.452): destruction, confiscation or delivery to the person entitled; payment to an innocent purchaser (s.499; s.453); appeal against orders (s.500; s.454); stay of order (s.501; s.455); restoration of possession of immovable property after conviction for criminal force (s.502; s.456); procedure by police upon seizure of property (s.503; s.457) - report to Magistrate, order for disposal; procedure when no claimant (s.504; s.458); power to sell perishable property (s.505; s.459).
  2. Irregular proceedings: irregularities which do not vitiate proceedings (s.506 BNSS; s.460 CrPC) - e.g., a Magistrate not empowered who erroneously takes cognizance of certain offences, issues a search warrant, tries an offender summarily, etc.; irregularities which vitiate (s.507; s.461) - e.g., attachment of property by a non-empowered Magistrate, trial of an offender by a Magistrate not empowered, taking cognizance without empowerment; proceedings in wrong place (s.508; s.462); non-compliance with confession-recording provisions (s.509; s.463) - the court may take evidence to prove that the statement was voluntary; effect of omission to frame, or absence of, or error in, charge (s.510; s.464); finding or sentence when reversible by reason of error, omission or irregularity (s.511; s.465) - not reversed unless failure of justice has in fact been occasioned; objection must be raised at the earliest (Shamnsaheb M. Multtani v. State of Karnataka, 2001); defect or error in a charge (s.512-513; ss.466-467).
  3. Limitation for taking cognizance (s.514 BNSS; s.468 CrPC): six months - offence punishable with fine only; one year - imprisonment not exceeding one year; three years - imprisonment exceeding one year but not three years; the period is computed from the date of the offence or when it came to the knowledge of the person aggrieved or the police (s.515; s.469); exclusion of time spent diligently in another court or the period of stay (s.516; s.470); exclusion of the date on which the court is closed (s.517; s.471); continuing offences - fresh period at every moment (s.518; s.472); extension where the court is satisfied that the delay has been properly explained or that it is necessary to do so in the interests of justice (s.519; s.473). No limitation for offences punishable with more than three years. Sarah Mathew (2014); Japani Sahoo v. Chandra Sekhar Mohanty (2007); State of Punjab v. Sarwan Singh (1981).
  4. Inherent powers of the High Court (s.528 BNSS; s.482 CrPC): nothing in the Sanhita limits or affects the inherent powers of the High Court to make such orders as may be necessary to give effect to any order under the Sanhita, or to prevent abuse of the process of any court or otherwise to secure the ends of justice. Not a source of new power; cannot override express provision; used sparingly, e.g., to quash FIR/complaint/proceedings; Bhajan Lal categories: (1) allegations taken at face value do not constitute any offence; (2) FIR and other materials do not disclose a cognizable offence justifying investigation without a Magistrate's order; (3) uncontroverted allegations and evidence collected do not disclose commission of an offence; (4) allegations disclose only a non-cognizable offence and no investigation is permitted without a Magistrate's order; (5) allegations are so absurd and inherently improbable that no prudent person could reach a conclusion; (6) express legal bar in the Code or other statute; (7) the proceeding is manifestly attended with mala fides or instituted maliciously to wreak vengeance. Cases: R.P. Kapur v. State of Punjab (1960); Neeharika Infrastructure v. State of Maharashtra (2021); Rajiv Thapar v. Madan Lal Kapoor (2013) - 'sterling' defence material; Parbatbhai Aahir (2017); Gian Singh (2012); State of M.P. v. Laxmi Narayan (2019); Mohammad Wajid v. State of U.P. (2023) - the HC should not ignore the allegations of mala fides. Quashing does not apply to heinous offences on compromise; the HC may use s.528 to give interim stay but cannot stop investigation in a routine manner.
  5. Trial and proceedings in electronic mode (s.530 BNSS; new): all trials, inquiries and proceedings under the Sanhita, including issuance, service and execution of summons and warrants; examination of the complainant and witnesses; recording of evidence in inquiries and trials; all appellate proceedings or other proceedings, may be held in electronic mode, by use of electronic communication or audio-video electronic means. Also provides for electronic filing of the chargesheet and submission of documents; use of video-conferencing for production of prisoners; the High Court makes rules. e-Courts mission and e-Sakshya, e-Sign, MedLEaPR, e-Prisons platforms are the implementing tools Verify.
  6. Repeal and savings (s.531 BNSS): the CrPC 1973 is repealed; the Chapters of the CrPC are replaced; s.531(2): pending matters are governed by the CrPC. Section 528: High Court's inherent powers.
  7. Other miscellaneous provisions: Criminal Procedure (Identification) Act, 2022 (replaced the Identification of Prisoners Act 1920): permits taking of measurements - fingerprints, palm-prints, footprints, photographs, iris and retina scans, physical and biological samples and behavioural attributes - of convicts and of persons arrested; records retained for 75 years; [cross-ref Related Topics].

!Exam traps

  • TrapInherent power under s.528 is of the High Court only, not of subordinate courts (a Sessions Court has no such power).
  • TrapLimitation under s.514 applies to cognizance, not to the filing of the FIR.
  • TrapIrregularities are curable only if no failure of justice (s.511).
