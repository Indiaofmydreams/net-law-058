Process to Compel Production of Things; Search and Seizure; Reciprocal Arrangements with Foreign Countries

iOverview

Summons to produce, search warrants, how a search must be conducted (including the new mandatory audio-video recording), seizure by police, attachment of proceeds of crime, and cross-border cooperation.

★Must know

  1. Summons to produce document or other thing (s.94 BNSS; s.91 CrPC): a court, or a police officer in charge of a police station, may summon any person in whose possession a document or thing is believed to be, if necessary or desirable for investigation or trial. BNSS clarifies that 'document or other thing' includes electronic communication and digital devices - so that digital evidence can be summoned. Exception: documents in postal/telegraph custody (s.95; s.92 CrPC). Compliance by producing the thing, not necessarily by attending.
  2. Search warrants (s.96 BNSS; s.93 CrPC): issued where (a) the court has reason to believe a person summoned under s.94 will not produce the document; (b) it is not known who possesses it; or (c) the court considers that a general search or inspection will serve the purposes. The warrant may be directed to specified places; the Magistrate may specify the part of the house.
  3. Search of place suspected to contain stolen property, forged documents (s.97; s.94 CrPC) by a District or Sub-divisional Magistrate; power to declare certain publications forfeited (s.98; s.95 CrPC) - State Government; application to the High Court to set aside forfeiture (s.99; s.96 CrPC) within two months; search for persons wrongfully confined (s.100; s.97 CrPC); power to compel restoration of abducted persons (s.101; s.98 CrPC).
  4. Conduct of a search (ss.102-103 BNSS; ss.99-100 CrPC): the person in charge of a closed place must allow free ingress; the officer may break open doors if refused after demand; search to be made in the presence of two or more independent and respectable inhabitants of the locality (or, if none are available, from another locality); a list of things seized is prepared and signed by the witnesses; a copy given to the occupant; a woman's person is searched only by a woman with strict regard to decency; the occupier is entitled to be present. BNSS s.105 (new): the process of search and seizure, including preparation of the list of seized articles and the signing by witnesses, shall be recorded through audio-video electronic means, preferably by mobile phone, and the recording forwarded forthwith to the District Magistrate/Sub-divisional Magistrate/Chief Judicial Magistrate.
  5. Disposal of things found in search beyond jurisdiction (s.104; s.101 CrPC). Police seizure (s.106 BNSS; s.102 CrPC): any police officer may seize any property alleged or suspected to have been stolen, or found under circumstances which create suspicion of an offence, and must report it forthwith to the Magistrate. The Supreme Court has held that bank accounts can be seized/frozen under s.102 CrPC (Tapas Neogy v. State of W.B., 1999; Teesta Setalvad v. State of Gujarat, 2018) - the provision cannot be used for immovable property (Nevada Properties v. State of Maharashtra, 2019).
  6. Attachment, forfeiture or restoration of property (s.107 BNSS; new): where the police officer investigating has reason to believe that any property is proceeds of crime, he may, with the Magistrate's approval, apply for attachment; the Magistrate may attach it after hearing; if the offence results in conviction the Court may order distribution to those affected; if the accused is acquitted/discharged the property is restored. 'Proceeds of crime' is defined as property derived or obtained directly or indirectly from criminal activity, including its value.
  7. Police search without warrant (s.185 BNSS; s.165 CrPC): an officer in charge of a police station or investigating officer may search or cause search where he has reasonable grounds to believe that thing necessary for investigation cannot otherwise be obtained without undue delay; he must record the grounds in writing; search must be recorded by audio-video means; a copy is sent to the nearest Magistrate. s.186 (s.166): an officer may require another officer to search.
  8. Evidentiary effect of illegal search: illegality in search does not by itself make seized evidence inadmissible; the court examines whether there was prejudice or violation of a safeguard (Pooran Mal v. Director of Inspection, 1974; Radhakishan v. State of U.P., 1963; R.M. Malkani v. State of Maharashtra, 1973). Compare Art 20(3) and s.349 BNSS (s.311A CrPC): a Magistrate may order any person to give specimen signatures or handwriting; Ritesh Sinha v. State of U.P. (2019): voice samples may be ordered; Selvi v. State of Karnataka (2010): narco-analysis, polygraph, brain-mapping without consent are impermissible.
  9. Reciprocal arrangements for assistance in certain matters (Chapter VIII BNSS; Chapter VIIA CrPC, ss.105A-105L): letters of request to courts or authorities in countries with reciprocal arrangements for investigation or evidence; attachment and forfeiture of property in India or abroad derived from crime committed in a contracting State; BNSS expands these provisions and ties them to proceeds of crime and extradition Verify · section-level numbering of ss.111-124. Letters rogatory (earlier CrPC ss.166A-166B) are now dealt with in the BNSS chapter on investigation/ reciprocal arrangements.

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
BNSS s.94 (CrPC s.91)Summons to produce document or thing - now covers electronic devices.
BNSS s.105 (new)Mandatory audio-video recording of search and seizure.
BNSS s.106 (CrPC s.102)Police power to seize property suspected stolen or found in suspicious circumstances.
BNSS s.107 (new)Attachment, forfeiture or restoration of property which is proceeds of crime.
BNSS s.185 (CrPC s.165)Search by police without warrant - reasons recorded; A/V recording.

⚖Landmark cases

Pooran Mal v. Director of Inspection

1974

Illegally obtained evidence is admissible unless excluded by statute or Constitution.

State of Punjab v. Baldev Singh

1999

Search of person under NDPS - option of Magistrate/gazetted officer; non-compliance vitiates recovery.

Tapas Neogy v. State of W.B.

1999

Bank account is 'property' for s.102 CrPC; police can seize.

Teesta Setalvad v. State of Gujarat

2018

Seizure/freezing under s.102 must be reported to Magistrate forthwith.

Nevada Properties Pvt. Ltd. v. State of Maharashtra

2019

s.102 does not cover immovable property.

Ritesh Sinha v. State of U.P.

2019

Magistrate can order voice samples; Art 20(3) not violated.

Selvi v. State of Karnataka

2010

Involuntary narco/polygraph/BEAP tests violate Art 20(3) and Art 21.

✎Memory aids

  • Search checklist: 'W-L-C-R' - Witnesses (2 independent), List, Copy to occupant, Recording (audio-video, new).

!Exam traps

  • Traps.94 is the successor to s.91 and covers digital devices; s.105 is new.
  • Traps.106 (police seizure) cannot be used for immovable property; s.107 (attachment of proceeds of crime) is the route.
  • TrapIllegal search does not automatically exclude evidence (Indian position), unlike US.

?Likely questions

  1. Audio-video recording of search and seizure is mandatory under which BNSS section? (s.105)
  2. Which BNSS section provides for attachment of proceeds of crime? (s.107)
  3. How many independent witnesses are required for a search? (Two)
