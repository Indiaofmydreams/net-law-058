Trial before a Court of Session

iOverview

Stage-by-stage sessions trial: PP, opening, discharge, charge, plea, evidence, s.351 examination, acquittal, defence, arguments, judgment, hearing on sentence.

★Must know

  1. Sequence of a sessions trial: committal (s.232) -> PP conducts prosecution (s.248; s.225) -> opening of the case by the prosecution describing the charge and the evidence (s.249; s.226) -> hearing on discharge (s.250; s.227) -> framing of charge (s.251; s.228) -> plea of guilty or claim to be tried (s.252; s.229) -> date for prosecution evidence and summoning of witnesses (s.253; s.230) -> prosecution evidence (s.254; s.231) -> examination of the accused (s.351 BNSS; s.313 CrPC) -> acquittal if no evidence (s.255; s.232) -> defence entered (s.256; s.233) -> arguments (s.257; s.234) -> judgment of acquittal or conviction, and if convicted, hearing on sentence (s.258; s.235) -> previous conviction (s.259; s.236).
  2. Plea of guilty (s.252; s.229): the Judge may convict on the plea in his discretion; the plea must be voluntary and unambiguous; courts are cautious in capital cases (the Judge should not convict on a plea in a case involving death sentence without recording evidence).
  3. Evidence for prosecution (s.254; s.231): the Judge takes all such evidence as is produced; may permit cross-examination to be deferred; evidence may be recorded in audio-video mode (s.530). s.255 (s.232): after taking evidence and examining the accused, if the Judge considers that there is no evidence that the accused committed the offence, he shall record an order of acquittal.
  4. Entering upon defence (s.256; s.233): the accused is called upon to enter upon his defence and adduce evidence; the accused may file a written statement; the Judge issues process for compelling attendance of defence witnesses unless he considers the application vexatious or for delay.
  5. Arguments (s.257; s.234): the prosecution sums up its case and the defence replies; if the accused adduces evidence, the prosecution may reply; point of law may be argued with permission; oral arguments are heard and written submissions may be filed. BNSS requires judgment to be delivered within forty-five days of the completion of arguments (s.392(1)), with the copy uploaded within seven days.
  6. Judgment and sentence (s.258; s.235): after the arguments the Judge gives a judgment of acquittal or conviction; if convicted, the Judge shall hear the accused on the question of sentence (unless he proceeds under s.401 probation) and pass sentence (Santa Singh v. State of Punjab, 1976 - hearing on sentence is mandatory; Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab, 1980 - death sentence in the 'rarest of rare' case, special reasons; Manoj v. State of M.P., 2022 - the court must call for mitigating material and a psychological/prison report before awarding death).
  7. Procedure in cases instituted under s.199(2) CrPC (defamation of high functionaries) - s.260 BNSS (s.237 CrPC).
  8. Sessions trial: PP leads prosecution; evidence of the accused as a competent witness only on his request (s.353; s.315); witnesses protection (s.398 BNSS); court may call material witnesses (s.348; s.311 CrPC); evidence may be taken on commission (s.319-325; ss.284-290).
  9. Principles of appreciating evidence: five golden principles of circumstantial evidence (Sharad Birdhichand Sarda v. State of Maharashtra, 1984 - Hanumant Govind Nargundkar, 1952); conviction on the sole testimony of an injured witness; interested witnesses - Masalti v. State of U.P. (1965); hostile witness - Bhagwan Singh v. State of Haryana (1976); Zahira Habibullah Sheikh v. State of Gujarat (2004, 2006) - fair trial and the role of the court as an active participant; PW not to be turned away by delay; test identification parade; dying declaration - Laxman v. State of Maharashtra (2002). [Cross-ref: Evidence / BSA.]

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
BNSS s.248 (CrPC s.225)Trial before Court of Session conducted by Public Prosecutor.
BNSS s.255 (CrPC s.232)Acquittal where no evidence that the accused committed the offence.
BNSS s.258 (CrPC s.235)Judgment; hearing on sentence.
BNSS s.351 (CrPC s.313)Power to examine the accused.

⚖Landmark cases

Santa Singh v. State of Punjab

1976

Failure to hear on sentence is a serious irregularity; sentencing hearing is a distinct stage.

Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab

1980

Rarest of rare; special reasons for death.

Sharad Birdhichand Sarda v. State of Maharashtra

1984

Five golden principles on circumstantial evidence.

Zahira Habibullah Sheikh v. State of Gujarat

2004

Best Bakery case; free and fair trial; court's role in witness protection.

Masalti v. State of U.P.

1965

Conviction possible on the evidence of interested witnesses with caution.

Manoj v. State of M.P.

2022

Guidelines on mitigating material before death sentence.
