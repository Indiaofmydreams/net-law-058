Jurisdiction, Cognizance, Complaints to Magistrates, Sanction and Commitment of Cases

iOverview

Place of inquiry and trial, conditions for cognizance, how a complaint is processed (including the new accused-hearing proviso), sanction, bar on cognizance, supply of documents and commitment to the Court of Session.

★Must know

  1. Ordinary place of inquiry and trial (s.197 BNSS; s.177 CrPC): the court within whose local jurisdiction the offence was committed; offences committed in one of several local areas, or continuing offences, may be tried in any (ss.198-199); the offence may be tried where the act was done or where the consequence ensued (s.199; s.179 CrPC); trial at place where the victim was found; offences by travel on journeys; offences committed outside India (s.208; s.188 CrPC) require the Central Government's sanction.
  2. Conditions for initiating proceedings (Chapter XIV BNSS): cognizance of offences by Magistrates (s.210 BNSS; s.190 CrPC): a Magistrate of the first class (or second class if specially empowered) may take cognizance of an offence (a) upon receiving a complaint of facts which constitute such offence (including by electronic means); (b) upon a police report of such facts; (c) upon information received from any person other than a police officer, or upon his own knowledge. Cognizance is taken of the offence and not of the offender (Raghubans Dubey v. State of Bihar, 1967; Pastor P. Raju v. State of Bihar). 'Taking cognizance' means application of judicial mind to the facts with a view to proceeding (R.R. Chari v. State of U.P., 1951; Subramanian Sethuraman v. State of Maharashtra, 2004).
  3. Transfer on application of the accused (s.211; s.191 CrPC), making over to Magistrates (s.212), cognizance by Courts of Session (s.213 BNSS; s.193 CrPC): the Court of Session cannot take cognizance as a court of original jurisdiction unless the case has been committed to it by a Magistrate; Dharam Pal v. State of Haryana (2014, Constitution Bench): after committal, the Sessions Court may under s.193 CrPC (s.213 BNSS) summon additional persons against whom a prima facie case appears.
  4. Bar on cognizance: (i) s.215 BNSS (s.195 CrPC): offences against public servants' lawful authority (contempt-type offences under the BNS chapter on contempts of lawful authority of public servants), offences against public justice (false evidence etc.) and offences relating to documents given in evidence - only on a written complaint by the public servant concerned or by the court concerned; Sachida Nand Singh v. State of Bihar (1998); Iqbal Singh Marwah v. Meenakshi Marwah (2005) - forged documents produced in court: the bar applies only if the forgery occurred after the document was in custodia legis; (ii) s.217 BNSS (s.196 CrPC): offences against the State (including BNS s.152 successor of sedition-type offences) and criminal conspiracy to commit them - only on the Central or State Government's complaint/sanction; (iii) s.218 (s.197 CrPC): prosecution of Judges, Magistrates and public servants for acts in the course of official duty requires previous sanction of the appropriate government - BNSS (new) requires the government to decide within 120 days of receiving the request, failing which sanction is deemed granted Verify; Matajog Dobey v. H.C. Bhari (1956) - 'reasonable connection' test; D. Devaraja v. Owais Sabeer Hussain (2020) - 'colour of duty'; Subramanian Swamy v. Manmohan Singh (2012) - three months for decision plus one month for legal consultation; Vineet Narain v. Union of India (1998); BNS/BNSS and PC Act s.19/17A cross-ref; (iv) s.222 (s.199 CrPC): prosecution for defamation - only on a complaint by the person aggrieved (with special rules for President, Governors, Ministers and public servants, which may be by the Public Prosecutor with sanction); offences relating to marriage (s.219-221 BNSS) - only on complaint by the aggrieved spouse/relative.
  5. Complaints to Magistrates (Chapter XV BNSS; Chapter XV CrPC): examination of complainant (s.223 BNSS; s.200 CrPC): the Magistrate taking cognizance of an offence on complaint shall examine upon oath the complainant and witnesses present and reduce the substance of the examination to writing, signed by the complainant, witnesses and the Magistrate; no examination is necessary when the complaint is in writing made by a public servant in the discharge of official duties or by a court. The BNSS (new proviso) provides that no cognizance of an offence shall be taken by the Magistrate without giving the accused an opportunity of being heard. [Cross-ref courts: the proviso is read to apply to complaint cases, not to police reports.]
  6. Postponement of issue of process (s.225 BNSS; s.202 CrPC): the Magistrate may postpone issue of process and either inquire himself or direct an investigation by a police officer or other person; it is mandatory to do so where the accused resides beyond the area of the Magistrate's jurisdiction. If the offence is triable exclusively by the Court of Session, the Magistrate shall call upon the complainant to produce all witnesses. Dismissal of the complaint (s.226; s.203): if there is no sufficient ground for proceeding, the reasons are recorded in brief. Issue of process (s.227; s.204): if in the opinion of the Magistrate there is sufficient ground for proceeding, a summons (summons-case) or warrant or summons (warrant-case) is issued; no process is issued until the list of witnesses is filed and fees paid. Dispensing with personal attendance (s.228; s.205).
  7. Principles on summoning: Pepsi Foods (1998) - summoning is serious; Kewal Krishan v. Suraj Bhan (1980) - at s.203 stage only prima facie and not detailed merits; Mehmood Ul Rehman v. Khazir Mohammad Tunda (2015) - inquiry must be reasoned; Birla Corporation Ltd v. Adventz Investments (2019) - inquiry or investigation is mandatory for accused beyond jurisdiction; GHCL Employees Stock Option Trust v. India Infoline Ltd (2013); Jagdish Ram v. State of Rajasthan (2004).
  8. Commencement of proceedings (ss.227-228): s.227 issue of process; s.228 dispensing with personal attendance; s.229 (s.206) special summons in cases of petty offences (by post, fine up to Rs 1,000); s.230 BNSS (s.207 CrPC): supply of police report and documents (FIR, police report, statements recorded, confessions, other documents) to the accused free of cost without delay and in any case within fourteen days from the date of production or appearance (new time-limit; earlier 'forthwith'); documents may be furnished electronically; voluminous documents may be supplied in electronic form. s.231 (s.208): supply of copies in cases triable by Court of Session instituted otherwise than on police report. s.232 (s.209): commitment - when it appears that the offence is triable exclusively by the Court of Session, the Magistrate commits the case after complying with s.230 or s.231; notifies the PP; sends record and exhibits. s.233 (s.210): where a complaint case and a police investigation relate to the same offence, the Magistrate stays the complaint proceedings and calls for the police report; if the police report names persons who are also accused in the complaint, both are tried together as if instituted on a police report; otherwise the complaint proceeds independently.
  9. Cognizance in 'complaint v. police report': An accused may be summoned only if the Magistrate is satisfied that material exists. Chargesheet may be accepted though the investigating officer opined no case; the Magistrate has independent discretion (Abhinandan Jha, 1967; Bhagwant Singh, 1985). For offences exclusively triable by sessions, the Magistrate has only the committal function; he cannot discharge the accused at that stage.
  10. Limitation for taking cognizance: s.514 BNSS (s.468 CrPC): six months for offence punishable with fine only; one year for imprisonment up to one year; three years for imprisonment exceeding one year but not exceeding three years; no limit for offences punishable with more than three years; s.515 (s.469) commencement; s.516 (s.470) exclusion of time; s.518 (s.472) continuing offences; s.519 (s.473) court may condone delay in the interest of justice. Sarah Mathew v. Institute of Cardio Vascular Diseases (2014, Constitution Bench): the relevant date is the date of filing the complaint or initiating criminal proceedings, not the date of cognizance. The period is computed per the punishment prescribed for the gravest offence.

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says

BNSS s.218 (CrPC s.197)Sanction to prosecute judges and public servants - 120-day decision limit (new) Verify.

✎Memory aids

  • Limitation s.514: '6 months - fine; 1 year - up to 1 year; 3 years - up to 3 years; none - above 3 years'.

!Exam traps

  • TrapCognizance is by a Magistrate, not by a Court of Session directly (except after committal).
  • TrapSection 223 hearing of accused applies to complaint cases.
  • TrapLimitation under s.514 is not relevant for offences punishable with more than 3 years.
  • TrapCommittal Magistrate cannot discharge the accused.
