Arrest of Persons (ss.35-62 BNSS / ss.41-60A CrPC) and Rights of the Arrested Person

Very high yield2 Qs expected8 min readBNSS ss.35-62 (CrPC ss.41-60A); Arts 20-22 Constitution

iOverview

One of the most heavily tested clusters. Learn the grounds of arrest without warrant, the notice of appearance, the written grounds rule, 24-hour production, medical examination, handcuffs and the safeguard cases.

★Must know

  1. Arrest without warrant by police (s.35(1) BNSS; s.41 CrPC): (a) any person who commits a cognizable offence in the officer's presence; (b) person against whom a reasonable complaint, credible information or reasonable suspicion exists of a cognizable offence punishable with imprisonment up to seven years - only if the officer is satisfied that arrest is necessary to prevent further offence, for proper investigation, to prevent evidence disappearing or tampering, to prevent inducement/threat to witnesses, or to ensure presence in court - and he must record reasons in writing; (c) person against whom credible information exists of a cognizable offence punishable with more than seven years or death; (d) proclaimed offender; (e)-(i) possession of stolen property, obstructing a police officer, escapee, deserter, person liable to be extradited, released convict failing to notify address.
  2. Notice of appearance (s.35(3)-(6) BNSS; s.41A CrPC): where arrest is not required under s.35(1), police shall issue a notice directing the person to appear. The person must comply; if he complies, he shall not be arrested unless the officer records reasons for arrest and obtains approval. If he does not comply, he may be arrested (subject to the Court's order).
  3. s.35(7) BNSS (new): for an offence punishable with imprisonment of less than three years, and where the person is infirm or is above sixty years of age, arrest may be made only with the prior permission of an officer not below the rank of Deputy Superintendent of Police.
  4. Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar (2014): arrest is not automatic for offences punishable up to seven years; police must satisfy the conditions of s.41(1)(b) and Magistrates must not authorise detention mechanically. Satender Kumar Antil v. CBI (2022) re-emphasised compliance.
  5. Procedure on arrest (s.36 BNSS; s.41B CrPC): police officer shall bear accurate, visible and clear identification of name; prepare a memorandum of arrest attested by at least one witness who is a family member or a respectable member of the locality, and countersigned by the arrested person; inform the arrested person of the right to have a relative or friend informed. s.37 BNSS (41C CrPC): State to establish control rooms; a designated police officer of rank not below Assistant Sub-Inspector at every police station and district to maintain particulars of arrested persons, and to display them prominently. s.38 BNSS (41D CrPC): arrested person is entitled to meet an advocate of his choice during interrogation, though not throughout (Poolpandi v. Superintendent, Central Excise, 1992; Nandini Satpathy v. P.L. Dani, 1978 - right to silence and counsel's presence).
  6. Arrest by private person (s.40 BNSS; s.43 CrPC): any private person may arrest, or cause to be arrested, a proclaimed offender or a person who in his presence commits a non-bailable and cognizable offence; he must without unnecessary delay hand him over to a police officer or take him to the nearest police station. Arrest by Magistrate (s.41; s.44 CrPC). Protection of members of the Armed Forces (s.42; s.45 CrPC): no arrest for acts done in official duty without the Central Government's consent.
  7. How arrest is made (s.43 BNSS; s.46 CrPC): by actual touch or confinement unless there is submission to custody; if the person forcibly resists, all means necessary may be used; the right to cause death is limited to persons accused of offences punishable with death or life imprisonment; a woman is not to be arrested after sunset or before sunrise except in exceptional circumstances with a written report and prior permission of the Judicial Magistrate of the First Class; for a woman, arrest is by touch only unless circumstances indicate otherwise and, as far as practicable, by a woman police officer. s.43(3) BNSS (new) allows handcuffs, keeping in view the nature and gravity of the offence, for habitual or repeat offenders, persons who escaped from custody, and those accused of organised crime, terrorist act, drug-related crime, illegal possession of arms, murder, rape, acid attack, counterfeiting, human trafficking, sexual offences against children, or offences against the State. Prem Shankar Shukla v. Delhi Administration (1980) and Sunil Batra (1978) restrict handcuffing as a general rule - treated as the baseline.
  8. Search of the arrested person (s.49; s.51 CrPC): search with decency; women only by women; articles taken to be listed and receipt given. s.46 BNSS (s.49 CrPC): no more restraint than necessary to prevent escape.
  9. Right to know grounds and bail (s.47 BNSS; s.50 CrPC): every arrested person must be forthwith informed of the full particulars of the offence and of the grounds of arrest; for bailable offences he must be informed of the right to be released on bail and to arrange sureties. s.48 BNSS (s.50A CrPC): the arrested person's friend, relative or nominee must be informed of the arrest and place; the arresting officer must make a record; the Magistrate must verify compliance.
  10. Mihir Rajesh Shah v. State of Maharashtra (6 Nov 2025; 2025 INSC 1288): under Art 22(1) and s.47 BNSS the grounds of arrest must be furnished in writing, in a language the arrestee understands, in every case under every statute; where oral intimation is necessary at the spot, the written grounds must follow within a reasonable time and in any event at least two hours before production for remand; failure renders the arrest and remand illegal. Earlier: Pankaj Bansal v. Union of India (2023) (PMLA), Prabir Purkayastha v. State (NCT of Delhi) (2024) (UAPA), Vihaan Kumar v. State of Haryana (Feb 2025).
  11. Medical examination: s.51 BNSS (s.53 CrPC) - at the request of police by a registered medical practitioner; s.52 (s.53A) - examination of an accused of rape; s.53 (s.54) - examination at the arrested person's own request, if he alleges torture, to produce evidence; s.54 (s.54A) - identification; s.56 (s.55A) - duty to take reasonable care of the health and safety of the arrested person.
  12. Production before Magistrate: s.57 BNSS (s.56 CrPC) - the arrested person must be taken before a Magistrate or the officer in charge of the police station without unnecessary delay; s.58 (s.57 CrPC) - no detention beyond 24 hours (excluding journey time) without the Magistrate's order; Art 22(2). Release only on bond, bail or the Magistrate's order (s.59; s.58 CrPC).
  13. Custodial safeguards: D.K. Basu v. State of W.B. (1997) - 11 requirements (identification of police, memo, witness, medical check every 48 hours, intimate relative, diary entry, access to lawyer, display at control room); Joginder Kumar v. State of U.P. (1994) - arrest should not be routine; right to inform someone; Nilabati Behera v. State of Orissa (1993) and Rudul Sah v. State of Bihar (1983) - compensation for custodial death/illegal detention under Arts 32/226; Sheela Barse v. State of Maharashtra (1983) - women in custody; Inder Mohan Goswami v. State of Uttaranchal (2007) - guidelines for warrants.
  14. Escape and re-arrest (s.60-61 BNSS; ss.59-60 CrPC); s.62 BNSS (s.60A CrPC): no arrest except in accordance with the Sanhita or any other law.
  15. Related constitutional safeguards: Art 22(1) (grounds and counsel), Art 22(2) (24 hours), Art 22(3)-(7) (preventive detention not covered by 22(1)-(2)), Art 20(3) (self-incrimination), Art 21 (procedure fair, just, reasonable).

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
BNSS s.35(1) (CrPC s.41)Arrest without warrant by police: cognizable offence, reasonable suspicion, reasons to be recorded.
BNSS s.35(3) (CrPC s.41A)Notice of appearance in lieu of arrest.
BNSS s.35(7)New safeguard: persons over 60 or infirm, offences under 3 years - DSP permission.
BNSS s.47 (CrPC s.50)Grounds of arrest - now to be in writing per Mihir Rajesh Shah (2025).
BNSS s.58 (CrPC s.57)24-hour limit on detention without Magistrate's order.
BNSS s.43(3)Handcuffs - for specified categories of offenders.

⚖Landmark cases

Joginder Kumar v. State of U.P.

1994

Arrest not to be routine; right to inform a friend or relative; police must justify arrest.

D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal

1997

Eleven mandatory guidelines on arrest and detention.

Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar

2014

No automatic arrest in offences up to 7 years; checklist under s.41(1)(b).

Pankaj Bansal v. Union of India

2023

Written grounds of arrest must be furnished (PMLA).

Prabir Purkayastha v. State (NCT of Delhi)

2024

Grounds of arrest in writing, UAPA; arrest and remand vitiated.

Mihir Rajesh Shah v. State of Maharashtra

2025

Written grounds mandatory in every case; at least two hours before remand; arrest/remand illegal otherwise.

Prem Shankar Shukla v. Delhi Administration

1980

Handcuffing is prima facie inhuman; allowed only in exceptional circumstances with reasons.

Nilabati Behera v. State of Orissa

1993

Compensation for custodial death - public law remedy.

Poolpandi v. Superintendent, Central Excise

1992

Right to consult lawyer does not extend to presence throughout interrogation.

Sanjay Chandra v. CBI

2012

Bail not punishment; liberty is the rule.

✎Memory aids

  • Arrest memo: 'I-W-C' - Identification, Witness attested, Countersigned.
  • 24 hours (s.58) / 2 hours (written grounds before remand) / 3 years + 60 age (s.35(7)).

!Exam traps

  • Traps.35(3) notice replaces the old s.41A; s.35(7) is NEW and has no CrPC equivalent.
  • TrapSection 47 BNSS = s.50 CrPC (grounds); s.48 = s.50A CrPC (inform relative).
  • TrapArt 22(1)-(2) do not apply to preventive detention or enemy aliens.
  • TrapRight to meet advocate is during interrogation, not throughout.

?Likely questions

  1. Which section of BNSS corresponds to s.41 CrPC? (s.35)
  2. What is the time limit for producing an arrested person before a Magistrate? (24 hours)
  3. A woman may not be arrested between sunset and sunrise except with whose permission? (JMFC)
  4. Under BNSS, for an infirm person above 60, arrest for an offence below 3 years requires permission of? (DSP)
