iOverview

Highest-yield cluster with the most case law. Know bailable v. non-bailable, anticipatory bail principles, default bail and the new s.479 rule, cancellation and the bond provisions.

★Must know

  1. Bail in bailable offences (s.478 BNSS; s.436 CrPC): when a person other than one accused of a non-bailable offence is arrested or detained without warrant or appears before a court, and is prepared to give bail, he shall be released on bail, as of right; the court or officer may release him on a personal bond without sureties; if he is unable to furnish bail within one week of his arrest, he shall be deemed to be an indigent person and released on a personal bond; the right to bail may be exercised before conviction at any time; if a person fails to comply with conditions of the bond, the court may refuse bail on a subsequent occasion (s.478(3); s.436(2)).
  2. Bail in non-bailable offences (s.480 BNSS; s.437 CrPC): bail may be granted but not if there appear reasonable grounds for believing that the person has been guilty of an offence punishable with death or imprisonment for life; nor if it is a cognizable offence and he has previously been convicted of an offence punishable with death, life or imprisonment for seven years or more, or has been convicted on two or more occasions of cognizable offences punishable with three years or more but less than seven years - except where the person is under sixteen years, a woman, or sick or infirm, who may be released; the court may also release in the other cases if satisfied it is in the interest of justice. Bail if, after the conclusion of the trial and before judgment, the court finds reasonable grounds for believing the accused is not guilty (s.480(6)). Where a person is accused of an offence punishable with imprisonment of seven years or more, or of offences under the chapters on offences against the body, property and public tranquillity... the court shall impose conditions: attendance, no repetition, no inducement/threat to witnesses (s.480(3); s.437(3)). Reasons for granting or refusing bail to be recorded in writing (s.480(4)). Court may direct arrest and committal for violation (s.480(5)). Narayan v. State of M.P. (2026) - the s.480(3) mandatory conditions are not engaged for offences punishable up to seven years Verify.
  3. Principles of bail: Gudikanti Narasimhulu v. Public Prosecutor (1978) - 'bail is the rule, jail the exception'; State of Rajasthan v. Balchand (1977); Sanjay Chandra v. CBI (2012) - the object of bail is to secure presence and bail is not to be refused as a punishment; Prahlad Singh Bhati v. NCT Delhi (2001) - factors (nature of accusation, severity, character of evidence, danger of absconding, likelihood of repetition, danger to witnesses); Satender Kumar Antil v. CBI (2022) - classification of offences into Categories A to D: (A) offences punishable with imprisonment up to seven years - bail as a rule (Arnesh Kumar compliance); (B) death or life imprisonment; (C) special statutes with stringent bail conditions (NDPS s.37, PMLA s.45, UAPA s.43D(5)); (D) economic offences - and directed that bail be considered promptly (Part of Govt. direction to enact a Bail Act). Ash Mohammad v. Shiv Raj Singh (2012); Siddharam Satlingappa Mhetre v. State of Maharashtra (2010). Special statutes: Union of India v. K.A. Najeeb (2021) - prolonged incarceration can override statutory restriction; NIA v. Zahoor Ahmad Shah Watali (2019) - limited scrutiny at UAPA bail; Vijay Madanlal Choudhary v. Union of India (2022) - PMLA s.45 upheld; Manish Sisodia v. CBI/ED (2024) and Prem Prakash v. Union of India (2024) - bail is the rule even under PMLA after prolonged detention; K. Kavitha v. ED (2024).
  4. Anticipatory bail (s.482 BNSS; s.438 CrPC): when any person has reason to believe that he may be arrested on an accusation of having committed a non-bailable offence, he may apply to the High Court or the Court of Session for a direction that in the event of arrest he shall be released on bail; the court may impose conditions: to make himself available for interrogation, not to induce/threaten witnesses, not to leave India without permission, and any other condition as in s.480(3); an interim order may be passed. s.482(4) BNSS: the section does not apply to arrests on accusations of offences under BNS s.65 (rape of a woman under sixteen/twelve years) and s.70(2) (gang rape of a woman under eighteen years) - (CrPC s.438(4) bar covered IPC ss.376(3), 376AB, 376DA, 376DB) Verify · the BNS section mapping. The presence of the applicant at the final hearing (s.438(1B) of the 2005 Amendment) was never brought into force.
  5. Anticipatory bail jurisprudence: Gurbaksh Singh Sibbia v. State of Punjab (1980, Constitution Bench) - anticipatory bail is not an unfettered right, courts must not impose rigid restrictions; not limited to a fixed period; not confined to exceptional cases; Sushila Aggarwal v. State (NCT of Delhi) (2020, Constitution Bench) - protection need not be limited in time and may continue till the end of trial, unless the court finds special reasons; the court may impose appropriate conditions; Siddharam Mhetre (2010) - the factors; Prathvi Raj Chauhan v. Union of India (2020) - SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act s.18/18A bars anticipatory bail unless a prima facie case is not made; Sumit v. State of U.P. (2026 INSC 145) - once granted, ordinarily continues without a fixed expiry Verify · details. Concurrent jurisdiction of the Court of Session and High Court (the HC may relegate to the Sessions Court).
  6. Special powers of the High Court or Court of Session regarding bail (s.483 BNSS; s.439 CrPC): may direct that any person accused of an offence and in custody be released on bail; may impose any condition; may set aside or modify any condition imposed by a Magistrate; notice to the PP is mandatory for offences exclusively triable by the Court of Session or punishable with life imprisonment; the informant/his authorised representative must be present at the hearing of a bail application in cases of serious sexual offences against women/children; s.483(3) (s.439(2)) - the High Court or Court of Session may direct that any person who has been released on bail under this chapter be arrested and committed to custody (cancellation). Cancellation principles: Dolat Ram v. State of Haryana (1995) - cogent and overwhelming circumstances (misuse, interference with witnesses, absconding, new offences); Mahipal v. Rajesh Kumar (2019) - cancelling an order granting bail on the ground of perversity differs from cancelling because of post-bail conduct; Neeru Yadav v. State of U.P. (2016); Abdul Basit v. Mohd. Abdul Kadir Chaudhary (2014).
  7. Bail to require accused to appear before the next appellate court (s.481 BNSS; s.437A CrPC): before conclusion of the trial and before disposal of the appeal, the accused shall be required to execute bail bonds with sureties to appear before the higher court if an appeal or petition is filed against the judgment, for six months.
  8. Default bail (s.187(3) BNSS; s.167(2) CrPC): see Section 2.7: indefeasible right when the investigation is not completed within 60/90 days; the right accrues the moment the period expires and application is made; Rakesh Kumar Paul (2017); M. Ravindran (2021); Bikramjit Singh (2020); Ritu Chhabaria (2023).
  9. Release of undertrial prisoners (s.479 BNSS; s.436A CrPC): where a person has, during the period of investigation, inquiry or trial, undergone detention for a period extending up to one-half of the maximum period of imprisonment specified for that offence, he shall be released on bail by the court on his personal bond with or without sureties; BNSS (new proviso): a first-time offender (a person who has never been convicted of any offence in the past) shall be released on bail after having undergone detention for a period extending up to one-third of the maximum period; the section does not apply to offences punishable with death or life imprisonment and to persons against whom proceedings are pending in more than one offence or case; the time for delay caused by the accused is excluded; the jail Superintendent must apply in writing to the court when the accused completes the period. Bhim Singh v. Union of India (2015) - directions to apply s.436A; In Re: Inhuman Conditions in 1382 Prisons (Aug 2024) - s.479 BNSS applies to all pending cases irrespective of the date of offence/ FIR Verify.
  10. Bonds (ss.484-496 BNSS; ss.440-450 CrPC): the amount of every bond shall be fixed with due regard to the circumstances and shall not be excessive (s.484; s.440 - Moti Ram v. State of M.P., 1978; Hussainara); bond of accused and sureties (s.485; s.441); declaration by sureties (s.486; s.441A); discharge from custody (s.487; s.442); power to order sufficient bail when that first taken is insufficient (s.488; s.443); discharge of sureties (s.489; s.444); deposit instead of recognizance (s.490; s.445); procedure when bond is forfeited (s.491; s.446) - the court records the proof of forfeiture and calls upon the person to pay the penalty; cancellation of bond and bail bond (s.492; s.446A); procedure in case of insolvency/death of surety or when a bond is forfeited (s.493; s.447); bond required from minor (s.494; s.448); appeal from orders under s.491 (s.495; s.449); power to direct levy of amount due on certain recognizances (s.496; s.450).
  11. Practical points: Bail may be denied for risk of absconding or tampering; refusal of bail by a Magistrate can be challenged in the Sessions Court/HC (concurrent), no revision is available against a bail order; bail orders must be reasoned (Brij Nandan Jaiswal v. Munna @ Munna Jaiswal, 2009; Ajwar v. Waseem, 2024 - unreasoned bail orders are set aside); no mechanical cash bail conditions (Moti Ram).

⚖Landmark cases

Gurbaksh Singh Sibbia v. State of Punjab

1980

Constitution Bench: anticipatory bail not time-limited; liberal construction.

Sushila Aggarwal v. State (NCT of Delhi)

2020

Constitution Bench: no fixed time limit on anticipatory bail protection.

Siddharam Satlingappa Mhetre v. State of Maharashtra

2010

Factors for anticipatory bail; personal liberty.

Satender Kumar Antil v. CBI

2022

Guidelines for bail; categories A-D; Arnesh Kumar to be followed.

Sanjay Chandra v. CBI

2012

Bail is not punishment; economic offences not an exception.

State of Rajasthan v. Balchand

1977

Basic rule: bail, not jail.

Dolat Ram v. State of Haryana

1995

Cancellation of bail needs cogent and overwhelming circumstances.

Union of India v. K.A. Najeeb

2021

Prolonged incarceration justifies bail even under UAPA s.43D(5).

Bail conditions must not be excessive; local surety requirement unreasonable.

✎Memory aids

  • Bail ladder: s.478 (bailable - right) -> s.480 (non-bailable - discretion) -> s.482 (anticipatory) -> s.483 (HC/Sessions; cancellation) -> s.479 (undertrial) -> s.187(3) (default).
  • Old numbers: 436-437-437A-438-439-436A.

!Exam traps

  • TrapAnticipatory bail is NOT time-limited per se, and is not a right.
  • Traps.479: 1/2 of maximum term for ordinary undertrials; 1/3 for first-time offenders; no relief for death/life offences.
  • TrapCancellation of bail needs supervening circumstances; setting aside a perverse order is a different power.
  • TrapBail bonds: personal bond for indigent persons after one week in bailable offences.

?Likely questions

  1. Which BNSS section deals with anticipatory bail? (s.482)
  2. First-time offender undertrial may be released after serving up to what fraction of the maximum sentence? (One-third)
  3. Which case held that anticipatory bail is not time-limited? (Sushila Aggarwal; Sibbia)
  4. Who may cancel bail under s.483? (High Court or Court of Session)
