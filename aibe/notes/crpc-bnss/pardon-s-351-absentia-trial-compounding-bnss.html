iOverview

A high-scoring cluster: s.337 double jeopardy, s.343 approver, s.346 adjournment, s.348 summoning witnesses, s.351 accused's examination, s.356 trial in absentia, s.358 additional accused, s.359 compounding, s.360 withdrawal.

★Must know

  1. Evidence in the presence of the accused (s.308 BNSS; s.273 CrPC): all evidence is taken in the presence of the accused or his advocate; evidence by audio-video electronic means is allowed; examination of the witness by video-conferencing is valid (State of Maharashtra v. Dr. Praful B. Desai, 2003). Recording of evidence in summons-, warrant- and sessions cases (ss.309-311; ss.274-276 CrPC): by the Magistrate/Judge in narrative form; language of record; memorandum when the Magistrate cannot record; demeanour remarks (s.315; s.280). Evidence of medical witnesses (s.326; s.291 CrPC); reports of Government scientific experts (s.328; s.293 CrPC) - the Director of Forensic Science Laboratory and others - may be used as evidence without calling the expert, though the court may summon him; no formal proof of certain documents (s.329; s.294 CrPC); evidence on affidavit of a formal character (s.331; s.296 CrPC); evidence of public servants, experts and police officers by electronic means (s.336 - new Verify).
  2. Double jeopardy (s.337 BNSS; s.300 CrPC): a person who has been tried by a competent court for an offence and convicted or acquitted shall not be tried again for the same offence, or for any other offence on the same facts for which a different charge might have been made under s.242(1) (s.221) or for which he might have been convicted under s.243(2) (s.222). Exceptions: acquittal/discharge withdrawn; different offence with consequence not known at the time; offences resulting in a different consequence; the Court was not competent. Art 20(2); State of Bombay v. S.L. Apte (1961); Kolla Veera Raghav Rao v. Gorantla Venkateswara Rao (2011).
  3. Legal aid to accused at State expense (s.341 BNSS; s.304 CrPC): where the accused has no pleader and the court considers him unable to engage one, the court shall assign a pleader at State expense in a trial before the Court of Session (and High Court may extend to other courts); Khatri v. State of Bihar (1981); Suk Das v. Union Territory of Arunachal Pradesh (1986) - failure to provide legal aid vitiates the trial. Right to be defended by a pleader (s.340; s.303 CrPC).
  4. Tender of pardon to an accomplice (s.343 BNSS; s.306 CrPC): a CJM at any stage of investigation or inquiry, and a Magistrate of the First Class at the inquiry or trial, may tender pardon on the condition of making a full and true disclosure of the whole of the circumstances within his knowledge relating to the offence and to every other person concerned; applies to offences triable exclusively by the Court of Session or by the Special Judge, offences punishable with imprisonment of seven years or more; s.344 (s.307): after commitment, the Court of Session may tender pardon; s.345 (s.308): trial of a person who fails to comply with the condition of pardon; Suresh Chandra Bahri v. State of Bihar (1994); Bangaru Laxman v. State (2012) - Court's duty on pardon; the approver is a competent witness (Evidence Act/BSA) but his evidence needs corroboration in material particulars.
  5. Power to postpone or adjourn proceedings (s.346 BNSS; s.309 CrPC): proceedings are to be continued from day to day until all the witnesses in attendance have been examined; adjournments only for special reasons recorded in writing; no adjournment merely to enable a party to show cause against sentence; costs may be imposed; once the evidence has begun, the case should proceed day to day and not be adjourned beyond the next day (Vinod Kumar v. State of Punjab, 2015 - day-to-day trial; Hussain v. Union of India, 2017 - time-bound trials). Where witnesses are present, no adjournment without examining them unless there are special reasons recorded in writing. The remand of accused in custody may not exceed fifteen days at a time.
  6. Local inspection (s.347; s.310); power to summon material witness or examine person present (s.348 BNSS; s.311 CrPC): at any stage of an inquiry, trial or other proceeding, the Court may summon any person as a witness, or examine any person in attendance, or recall and re-examine any person already examined, if his evidence appears essential to the just decision of the case; it is the duty of the court to do so, and the court is not confined by the prosecution's list (Zahira Habibullah Sheikh, 2006; Mohanlal Shamji Soni v. Union of India, 1991; Rajendra Prasad v. Narcotic Cell, 1999). s.349 BNSS (s.311A CrPC): Magistrate may order any person (including an arrested person) to give specimen signature or handwriting.
  7. Power to examine the accused (s.351 BNSS; s.313 CrPC): the Court may at any stage and shall, after the prosecution witnesses have been examined and before the accused is called for his defence, question him generally on the case to enable him to explain any circumstances appearing in the evidence against him; he is not on oath; the court cannot draw adverse inference from refusal to answer; the answers may be taken into consideration in the inquiry or trial and may be put in evidence for or against him in any other inquiry or trial for any other offence which such answers may tend to show he committed. The court must put every material incriminating circumstance to the accused; omission may vitiate the conviction if prejudice is shown (Asraf Ali v. State of Assam, 2008; Reena Hazarika v. State of Assam, 2019; Premchand v. State of Maharashtra, 2023). The accused may now give his statement through audio-video electronic means in specified situations. s.352 (s.314) oral arguments and memoranda; s.353 (s.315): accused is a competent defence witness on his own request, no adverse comment on his not giving evidence; s.354 (s.316): no influence by promise or threat; s.355 (s.317): dispensing with personal attendance of the accused during inquiries and trials where he is represented by pleader.
  8. Trial and judgment in absentia of proclaimed offender (s.356 BNSS; new): where a person declared a proclaimed offender has absconded to evade trial and there is no immediate prospect of arresting him, the trial may proceed in his absence as if he were present and judgment may be pronounced. Conditions (as reported): framing of charge; at least ninety days after the framing of charge; two successive notices by publication in a national or local daily at an interval of thirty days; service on the relative or last-known address; recording of reasons; appointment of a State-paid advocate to represent him; the person is allowed to appear at any stage subject to the court's conditions; no appeal against conviction unless he appears within three years of the judgment Verify · details . Absence trial has been compared with s.299 CrPC (record of evidence in absence of accused).
  9. Procedure where accused does not understand proceedings (s.357; s.318 CrPC); power to proceed against other persons appearing to be guilty (s.358 BNSS; s.319 CrPC): where, in the course of an inquiry or trial, it appears from the evidence that any person not being the accused has committed an offence for which he could be tried together with the accused, the court may proceed against him and he is added; the court may detain him and the proceedings are commenced afresh; the trial of such person starts afresh. Hardeep Singh v. State of Punjab (2014, Constitution Bench): the power can be exercised on the basis of evidence adduced at trial (even examination-in-chief); the standard is higher than prima facie but lower than that for conviction - 'strong and cogent evidence'; to be exercised sparingly; applies after the commencement of the trial; also to the Sessions Court, even in absence of committal of that person; Brijendra Singh v. State of Rajasthan (2017); Sarabjit Singh v. State of Punjab (2009); Labhuji Amratji Thakor v. State of Gujarat (2019).
  10. Compounding of offences (s.359 BNSS; s.320 CrPC): Table 1 - offences compoundable by the persons mentioned without the court's permission (e.g., hurt, wrongful restraint, defamation, adultery-type offences as re-enacted, criminal trespass, mischief of certain kinds); Table 2 - offences compoundable with the court's permission (e.g., voluntarily causing hurt by dangerous weapons, theft, cheating, criminal breach of trust of a limited kind). The composition has the effect of an acquittal. Non-compoundable offences cannot be compounded but may be quashed under s.528 on settlement (Gian Singh v. State of Punjab, 2012; Narinder Singh v. State of Punjab, 2014; Parbatbhai Aahir v. State of Gujarat, 2017; Ram Gopal v. State of M.P., 2021 - heinous and serious offences excluded). Offence under s.138 NI Act is compoundable (s.147 NI Act; Damodar S. Prabhu v. Sayed Babalal H., 2010). [Cross-ref: Negotiable Instruments Act, ADR.]
  11. Withdrawal from prosecution (s.360 BNSS; s.321 CrPC): the Public Prosecutor or Assistant PP in charge of a case may, with the consent of the court, at any time before judgment is pronounced, withdraw from the prosecution of any person; under BNSS the court shall not permit withdrawal without hearing the victim (new). Sheonandan Paswan v. State of Bihar (1987) - consent is a judicial function; Abdul Karim v. State of Karnataka (2000); State of Kerala v. K. Ajith (2021) - withdrawal for political reasons rejected; Bar of withdrawal in cases pending against MPs/MLAs without High Court's permission (Ashwini Upadhyay v. Union of India, 2021 - HC permission required).
  12. Procedure for accused of unsound mind (ss.367-378 BNSS; ss.328-339 CrPC): where the Magistrate/Court has reason to believe that the accused is of unsound mind and incapable of making his defence, he shall inquire into the fact of such unsoundness and cause examination by a psychiatrist or clinical psychologist; trial is postponed; the accused may be released on bail with surety or kept in safe custody in a mental health establishment; resumed when he is fit. If the accused is found to be of unsound mind at the time of the act, the defence of insanity applies (BNS s.22 (IPC s.84)). Mental Healthcare Act 2017 applies.
  13. Offences affecting the administration of justice (ss.379-391 BNSS; ss.340-352 CrPC): procedure for offences under BNS ss.206-229 relating to false evidence, contempt, forged documents given in evidence; the Court may make a complaint after preliminary inquiry; appeals from orders under s.379 are provided; summary procedure for contempt in the presence of the court (s.345 CrPC; corresponding BNSS section in the s.379-391 block).
  14. Open court and in camera trial (s.366 BNSS; s.327 CrPC): the place in which any Criminal Court is held for inquiring into or trying any offence shall be an open court to which the public may have access; for BNS ss.64-71 (rape) trials, in camera trial is mandatory; no publication of the proceedings without the court's permission (BNS s.72 (IPC s.228A) - victim's identity). Nipun Saxena v. Union of India (2019) - identity of victims of sexual offences.

⚖Landmark cases

Hardeep Singh v. State of Punjab

2014

s.319 CrPC power; strong and cogent evidence; applies at any stage after trial begins.

Zahira Habibullah Sheikh v. State of Gujarat

2006

Court's duty under s.311 to ensure fair trial; witness intimidation.

Gian Singh v. State of Punjab

2012

HC may quash non-compoundable offences on settlement with caution.

Narinder Singh v. State of Punjab

2014

Guidelines for quashing on settlement; s.307 IPC not ordinarily.

Sheonandan Paswan v. State of Bihar

1987

Withdrawal from prosecution requires the court's consent on proper grounds.

Asraf Ali v. State of Assam

2008

Duty to put all incriminating circumstances to the accused under s.313.

State of Maharashtra v. Praful B. Desai

2003

Video conferencing recording of evidence is valid.

State of Bombay v. S.L. Apte

1961

s.403 (old) and Art 20(2): same offence test.

Vinod Kumar v. State of Punjab

2015

Day-to-day trial; adjournments only for special reasons.

Ram Gopal v. State of M.P.

2021

Quashing of non-compoundable offences on compromise; guidelines.

Suk Das v. Union Territory of Arunachal Pradesh

1986

Free legal aid is a constitutional right; trial without legal aid is vitiated.

✎Memory aids

  • s.337 = double jeopardy; s.343 = pardon; s.346 = adjournment; s.348 = material witness; s.351 = accused; s.358 = additional accused; s.359 = compounding; s.360 = withdrawal.
  • Old CrPC: 300-306-309-311-313-319-320-321.

!Exam traps

  • Traps.358 (319) 'strong and cogent evidence' > prima facie but < conviction standard.
  • Traps.351 statement is not on oath; accused's silence cannot lead to adverse inference.
  • TrapCompounding = acquittal; withdrawal = acquittal (summons) or discharge (warrant/Sessions before charge).
  • TrapBNSS s.360: victim must be heard before withdrawal is allowed.
