Process to Compel Appearance: Summons, Warrants, Proclamation and Attachment

iOverview

Difference between summons and warrant, bailable and non-bailable warrants, service on corporate bodies, electronic summons, proclamation for absconders, attachment and the BNSS 'proclaimed offender' reforms.

★Must know

  1. Summons (ss.63-71 BNSS; ss.61-69 CrPC): in writing, in duplicate, signed by the presiding officer or by such officer as the High Court may direct, bearing the court's seal; specifies time and place; served by a police officer or an officer of the court or public servant; served personally by delivering/tendering a copy (BNSS adds service by electronic communication: s.64(2) - the summons bears the image of the court's seal and is served by electronic means as per rules). Service on corporate bodies (secretary, local manager, director or by registered post to the principal officer); service when the person cannot be found (leave with an adult family member; if none, affix to the house); service on a Government servant through the head of his office; service outside local jurisdiction.
  2. Summons v. warrant: summons is an order to appear; warrant is an order to arrest and produce. Summons is the general mode in a summons-case; in a warrant-case the Magistrate may issue either (s.227 BNSS; s.204 CrPC). Court may issue a warrant in lieu of or in addition to summons if the person fails to appear or the court believes he will not (s.89 BNSS; s.87 CrPC).
  3. Warrants of arrest (s.72 onwards BNSS; ss.70-81 CrPC): in writing, signed by presiding officer, bearing seal; remains in force until cancelled or executed (s.72); court may direct by endorsement that a person be released if he gives bail with surety - a 'bailable warrant' (s.73; s.71 CrPC); directed to police officers or, if urgent, to any other person (s.75; s.73 CrPC); the officer must notify the substance of the warrant and, on request, show it; a person arrested must be brought without delay before the court; warrants may be executed outside the jurisdiction with the help of the local Magistrate/police.
  4. Non-bailable warrants should be issued only for good reason - Inder Mohan Goswami v. State of Uttaranchal (2007): consider seriousness, possibility of absconding, previous history, whether summons were served; Court must first try summons, then bailable warrant, then non-bailable warrant, then proclamation.
  5. Proclamation for person absconding (s.84 BNSS; s.82 CrPC): if the court has reason to believe that a person against whom a warrant has been issued has absconded or is concealing himself, it may publish a written proclamation requiring him to appear within not less than thirty days. The proclamation is read publicly in the town/village, affixed to the house and to the court-house, and also published in a daily newspaper. BNSS s.84 also lets the court declare a person a 'proclaimed offender' in respect of grave offences (those punishable with imprisonment of ten years or more, life or death) if he fails to appear.
  6. Attachment of property of absconder (s.85 BNSS; s.83 CrPC): after proclamation the court may attach movable or immovable property of the person; claims and objections (s.86; s.84 CrPC) within six months; release, sale and restoration (s.87; s.85 CrPC). The BNSS adds a provision for identification, attachment and forfeiture of property of a proclaimed person, including property abroad through letters of request (s.86 - Verify · section letter).
  7. Bonds for appearance (s.90 BNSS; s.88 CrPC): any court may require a person present before it to execute a bond with or without sureties to appear. s.91 (s.89): arrest on breach of the bond.
  8. Provisions applicable to summons and warrants (s.92 BNSS; s.90 CrPC): the chapter's provisions apply to every summons and warrant issued under the Sanhita.
  9. Lavesh v. State (NCT of Delhi) (2012): 'absconding' under s.82 CrPC means deliberate hiding. Court must satisfy itself on the facts before proclamation; a person declared proclaimed offender cannot ordinarily get anticipatory bail (Lavesh; Prem Shankar Prasad v. State of Bihar, 2021 - noted in 2026 digests).

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
BNSS s.64(2)Summons may be served by electronic communication bearing the image of the court's seal.
BNSS s.72 (CrPC s.70)Form of warrant of arrest - in force until cancelled or executed.
BNSS s.84 (CrPC s.82)Proclamation for absconding person - 30 days to appear.
BNSS s.85 (CrPC s.83)Attachment of property of absconder.
BNSS s.89 (CrPC s.87)Warrant in lieu of or in addition to summons.

⚖Landmark cases

Inder Mohan Goswami v. State of Uttaranchal

2007

Guidelines for issuing non-bailable warrants; liberty to be balanced; reasons required.

Lavesh v. State (NCT of Delhi)

2012

Absconder who has been proclaimed an offender cannot normally claim anticipatory bail.

Pepsi Foods v. Special Judicial Magistrate

1998

Summoning order requires application of mind.

State of Punjab v. Sarwan Singh

1981

Limitation not a bar to summons in certain circumstances; proclaiming process.

Raghubans Dubey v. State of Bihar

1967

Cognizance is of the offence; once taken, the Magistrate may proceed against all those involved.

✎Memory aids

  • Escalation of process: Summons -> Bailable warrant -> Non-bailable warrant -> Proclamation (30 days) -> Attachment -> Proclaimed offender -> Trial in absentia (s.356).

!Exam traps

  • TrapProclamation applies to persons (accused) who abscond; not to witnesses.
  • Trap30 days is the minimum period given in the proclamation.
  • TrapProclaimed offender status under BNSS is limited to serious offences.

?Likely questions

  1. How many days must a proclamation give the absconder to appear? (Not less than 30)
  2. Which BNSS section corresponds to s.82 CrPC? (s.84)
  3. Can summons be served electronically under BNSS? (Yes - s.64(2))
