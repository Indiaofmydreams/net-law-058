Appeals, Reference, Revision and Transfer of Criminal Cases

iOverview

Who may appeal and to which court, victim's right to appeal, appeals against acquittal, powers of appellate court, suspension of sentence, revision and its bars, and transfer powers.

★Must know

  1. No appeal unless expressly provided (s.413 BNSS; s.372 CrPC). Proviso: the victim has the right to prefer an appeal against any order passed by the Court acquitting the accused or convicting for a lesser offence or imposing inadequate compensation, and such appeal lies to the court to which an appeal ordinarily lies against the conviction. Satya Pal Singh v. State of M.P. (2015) and Mallikarjun Kodagali v. State of Karnataka (2018): the victim need not seek leave and may appeal even against acquittal.
  2. Appeal from orders requiring security or refusing to accept or rejecting sureties (s.414; s.373 CrPC). Appeals from convictions (s.415 BNSS; s.374 CrPC): (1) from a conviction by a High Court in its extraordinary original criminal jurisdiction - to the Supreme Court; (2) from a conviction by a Sessions Judge or an Additional Sessions Judge, or in a trial in which a sentence of imprisonment for more than seven years has been passed against him or any other person convicted at the same trial - to the High Court; (3) from any other conviction by a Magistrate - to the Court of Session. No appeal where the accused has pleaded guilty and been convicted on his plea, except as to the extent or legality of the sentence (s.416; s.375 CrPC). Petty cases (s.417; s.376): no appeal in the cases of small punishment Verify · the thresholds. Appeal by the State Government against the sentence for enhancement (s.418; s.377).
  3. Appeal against acquittal (s.419 BNSS; s.378 CrPC): the District Magistrate may, in any case instituted on a police report, direct the Public Prosecutor to present an appeal to the Court of Session against an order of acquittal passed by a Magistrate in respect of a cognizable and non-bailable offence; the State Government may direct the Public Prosecutor to appeal to the High Court from an order of acquittal passed by any court (other than a High Court) or an order of acquittal passed by the Court of Session in revision; in cases instituted otherwise than on a police report (complaint cases), the High Court's special leave is required; the complainant may apply for special leave to appeal against acquittal in a complaint-case (to the High Court); the application must be made within sixty days (six months if the complainant is a public servant) Verify; under BNSS, no appeal on behalf of the State shall be entertained without the leave of the High Court, and the application for leave by the State requires an opportunity to the accused to be heard. Chandrappa v. State of Karnataka (2007) - principles for appeals against acquittal (double presumption of innocence; interference only if view is perverse or impossible); Babu v. State of Kerala (2010).
  4. Powers of the appellate court (ss.427-428 BNSS; ss.386-387 CrPC): may dismiss the appeal, in an appeal from conviction reverse the finding and sentence and acquit or discharge or order re-trial, alter the finding, or reduce the sentence; it may not enhance the sentence in an appeal by the accused alone (an appeal by the State/revision is required, with notice). Summary dismissal (s.425; s.384). Appellate court may take further evidence (s.432; s.391). Judgments of the appellate court (s.429-430). Suspension of sentence pending appeal; release on bail (s.430 BNSS; s.389 CrPC): the appellate court may, for reasons recorded, order that the execution of the sentence or order be suspended and, if he is in confinement, that he be released on bail or on his own bond; for sentences of death or imprisonment for life, or of ten years or more, the PP's opportunity to oppose is mandatory; Bhagwan Rama Shinde Gosavi v. State of Gujarat (1999) and Kishori Lal v. Rupa (2004) - the principles; Atul Tripathi v. State of U.P. (2014); Saudan Singh v. State of U.P. (2021) - a liberal approach; Angana v. State of Rajasthan (2009); Anil Kumar Yadav v. State (NCT of Delhi) (2018) - convicts with sentence 5+ years.
  5. Abatement of appeals (s.435; s.394): an appeal against conviction abates on the death of the appellant (other than an appeal against a sentence of fine); the appeal by the accused may continue by relatives in certain cases. Reference to the High Court (s.436 BNSS; s.395 CrPC): a court may refer to the High Court a question as to the validity of an Act, ordinance or regulation, where the determination is necessary for disposal of a case pending before it and it is of the opinion that the Act is invalid and has not been so declared. The High Court decides the question and the court disposes accordingly.
  6. Revision (ss.438-443 BNSS; ss.397-402 CrPC): the High Court or any Sessions Judge may call for and examine the record of any proceeding before any inferior criminal court within its jurisdiction to satisfy itself as to the correctness, legality or propriety of any finding, sentence or order, and as to the regularity of any proceedings (s.438; s.397). No revision in relation to any interlocutory order (s.438(2); s.397(2)): Amar Nath v. State of Haryana (1977), Madhu Limaye v. State of Maharashtra (1977) - 'interlocutory' is limited to orders of a purely interim or temporary nature and not matters which affect or decide the rights of parties; V.C. Shukla v. State (Delhi Admn.) (1980); K.K. Patel v. State of Gujarat (2000); Girish Kumar Suneja v. CBI (2017) - interlocutory v. intermediate orders. Where an application has been made to either the High Court or Sessions Judge, no further application by the same person is to be entertained by the other (s.438(3); s.397(3)).
  7. Powers of High Court in revision (s.442 BNSS; s.401 CrPC): may exercise any of the powers of an appellate court; may order an inquiry; no order to the prejudice of the accused shall be made unless he has had an opportunity of being heard; no conversion of a finding of acquittal into one of conviction (s.442(3); s.401(3)); no revision where an appeal lies and no appeal has been brought (s.442(4); s.401(4)) - the High Court may treat the revision as an appeal if the party would have been entitled to appeal. Sessions Judge's powers (s.440-441; s.399-400). Power to withdraw/transfer cases within Session division (s.443; s.402). Revision is not a substitute for appeal; revisional power is to be exercised sparingly and for 'perversity', 'palpable error' or 'jurisdictional error' (Pathumma v. Muhammad, 1986; State of Kerala v. Puttumana Illath Jathavedan Namboodiri, 1999; Amit Kapoor v. Ramesh Chander, 2012).
  8. Transfer of criminal cases (ss.447-451 BNSS; ss.406-410 CrPC): the Supreme Court (s.447; s.406) - on application of the Attorney General/ a party, may transfer a case or appeal from one High Court to another, or from a Criminal Court subordinate to one HC to a court subordinate to another HC, whenever it is made to appear that such an order is expedient for the ends of justice; the application is to be supported by affidavit and frivolous applications may attract compensation to the other side; Maneka Sanjay Gandhi v. Rani Jethmalani (1979) - transfer where a reasonable apprehension of unfair trial; Zahira Habibullah Sheikh (2004); Nahar Singh Yadav v. Union of India (2011). The High Court (s.448; s.407) may transfer within its jurisdiction; the Sessions Judge (s.449; s.408) may transfer within the division.
  9. Extra: Appeal to Supreme Court (Art 134, Art 136, Criminal Appeal jurisdiction) and Supreme Court (Enlargement of Criminal Appellate Jurisdiction) Act 1970 - where HC reverses acquittal and sentences to death/life/10 years, appeal lies as of right.

ProvisionWhat it says

✎Memory aids

  • Appeals: Magistrate conviction -> Sessions; Sessions conviction (or >7 years) -> HC; HC original conviction -> SC.
  • Revision bars: interlocutory orders; conversion of acquittal to conviction; where appeal lies and not filed.

!Exam traps

  • TrapAn appeal by the accused cannot lead to enhancement of sentence; the State must appeal or move revision.
  • TrapThe High Court cannot convert an acquittal into conviction in revision.
  • TrapVictim's right to appeal is a proviso to s.413 BNSS and not a separate section.

?Likely questions

  1. Which BNSS section corresponds to s.397 CrPC? (s.438)
  2. Victim's right to appeal appears in which section? (proviso to s.413)
  3. Can the High Court convert an acquittal into conviction in revision? (No)
  4. Who can transfer a case from one High Court to another? (Supreme Court)
