BNSS 2023: Overview, Transition, Definitions and Classification of Offences and Trials

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

BNSS s.531Repeal of CrPC and savings: pending proceedings continue under CrPC.
BNSS s.2(1)Definitions - includes victim, electronic communication, audio-video electronic means.
BNSS s.4 (CrPC s.4)Offences under BNS tried per BNSS; offences under other laws tried per BNSS unless the special law provides otherwise.
BNSS s.5 (CrPC s.5)Savings of special/local law and special jurisdiction; BNSS does not override them.
First ScheduleClassification of BNS offences: cognizable?, bailable?, by which court triable.

Cases in one line

  • Directorate of Enforcement v. Deepak Mahajan 1994 Distinguished inquiry, investigation, trial; Magistrate's remand powers apply to arrests by authorities under special statutes.
  • Pepsi Foods Ltd v. Special Judicial Magistrate 1998 Summoning of an accused is a serious matter; Magistrate must apply mind.
  • Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar 1979 Speedy trial and legal aid are part of Art 21.
  • A.R. Antulay v. R.S. Nayak 1992 Seven-judge bench; right to speedy trial; no fixed time-limit but case-by-case.
  • Khatri v. State of Bihar 1981 Legal aid right arises at first production and remand.

Memory aids

  • Warrant case = more than 2 years; summons case = 2 years or less.
  • New BNSS time-limits: '3-14-60-90-45': e-FIR signature 3 days; preliminary enquiry/ document supply 14 days; discharge and charge 60 days; default bail 60/90; judgment 45 days.

Exam traps

  • TrapThe BNSS is not retrospective in substantive liability: Art 20(1) still applies.
  • TrapA 'complaint' under s.2(1) is made to a Magistrate - a complaint to police is an 'information'/FIR.
  • TrapDo not confuse 'summons' (s.63) with 'summons-case' (a type of trial).

Constitution and Powers of Criminal Courts; Public Prosecutors; Sentencing Limits

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

BNSS s.6Classes of criminal courts: Session, JMFC, JMSC, Executive Magistrates.
BNSS s.22Sentences which High Court and Sessions Judge may pass; ASJ up to 10 years.
BNSS s.23Sentences which Magistrates may pass: CJM up to 7 years; JMFC 3 years/Rs 50,000; JMSC 1 year/Rs 10,000.
BNSS s.18Public Prosecutors - minimum seven years' standing as advocate.
BNSS s.20Directorate of Prosecution.

Cases in one line

  • Shiv Kumar v. Hukam Chand 1999 PP must be independent; private counsel's role is only to assist unless the Court grants permission.
  • Rekha Murarka v. State of W.B. 2019 Victim can engage counsel but the PP leads prosecution; counsel for victim limited in cross-examination of witnesses.
  • Thakur Ram v. State of Bihar 1966 Prosecution is the State's function; PP is not an advocate for the complainant.
  • State of Gujarat v. Hon'ble High Court of Gujarat 1998 Prisoners may be put to work as part of rigorous imprisonment; wages must be paid.
  • Mohd. Giasuddin v. State of A.P. 1977 Sentencing should aim at reformation; punishment must be individualised.

Memory aids

  • Sentence ladder: Magistrate II (1 yr) -> Magistrate I (3 yrs) -> CJM (7 yrs) -> ASJ (10 yrs) -> Sessions Judge (anything; death confirmed by HC) -> High Court (anything).

Exam traps

  • TrapASJ's limit is ten years (not seven).
  • TrapA Sessions Judge may pass a death sentence but cannot execute it until the High Court confirms (s.407 BNSS).
  • TrapMetropolitan Magistrates no longer exist in BNSS.
  • TrapPP must have seven years' practice; the policeman-APP condition is different.

Arrest of Persons (ss.35-62 BNSS / ss.41-60A CrPC) and Rights of the Arrested Person

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

BNSS s.35(1) (CrPC s.41)Arrest without warrant by police: cognizable offence, reasonable suspicion, reasons to be recorded.
BNSS s.35(3) (CrPC s.41A)Notice of appearance in lieu of arrest.
BNSS s.35(7)New safeguard: persons over 60 or infirm, offences under 3 years - DSP permission.
BNSS s.47 (CrPC s.50)Grounds of arrest - now to be in writing per Mihir Rajesh Shah (2025).
BNSS s.58 (CrPC s.57)24-hour limit on detention without Magistrate's order.
BNSS s.43(3)Handcuffs - for specified categories of offenders.

Cases in one line

  • Joginder Kumar v. State of U.P. 1994 Arrest not to be routine; right to inform a friend or relative; police must justify arrest.
  • D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal 1997 Eleven mandatory guidelines on arrest and detention.
  • Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar 2014 No automatic arrest in offences up to 7 years; checklist under s.41(1)(b).
  • Pankaj Bansal v. Union of India 2023 Written grounds of arrest must be furnished (PMLA).
  • Prabir Purkayastha v. State (NCT of Delhi) 2024 Grounds of arrest in writing, UAPA; arrest and remand vitiated.
  • Mihir Rajesh Shah v. State of Maharashtra 2025 Written grounds mandatory in every case; at least two hours before remand; arrest/remand illegal otherwise.
  • Prem Shankar Shukla v. Delhi Administration 1980 Handcuffing is prima facie inhuman; allowed only in exceptional circumstances with reasons.
  • Nilabati Behera v. State of Orissa 1993 Compensation for custodial death - public law remedy.
  • Poolpandi v. Superintendent, Central Excise 1992 Right to consult lawyer does not extend to presence throughout interrogation.
  • Sanjay Chandra v. CBI 2012 Bail not punishment; liberty is the rule.

Memory aids

  • Arrest memo: 'I-W-C' - Identification, Witness attested, Countersigned.
  • 24 hours (s.58) / 2 hours (written grounds before remand) / 3 years + 60 age (s.35(7)).

Exam traps

  • Traps.35(3) notice replaces the old s.41A; s.35(7) is NEW and has no CrPC equivalent.
  • TrapSection 47 BNSS = s.50 CrPC (grounds); s.48 = s.50A CrPC (inform relative).
  • TrapArt 22(1)-(2) do not apply to preventive detention or enemy aliens.
  • TrapRight to meet advocate is during interrogation, not throughout.

Process to Compel Appearance: Summons, Warrants, Proclamation and Attachment

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

BNSS s.64(2)Summons may be served by electronic communication bearing the image of the court's seal.
BNSS s.72 (CrPC s.70)Form of warrant of arrest - in force until cancelled or executed.
BNSS s.84 (CrPC s.82)Proclamation for absconding person - 30 days to appear.
BNSS s.85 (CrPC s.83)Attachment of property of absconder.
BNSS s.89 (CrPC s.87)Warrant in lieu of or in addition to summons.

Cases in one line

  • Inder Mohan Goswami v. State of Uttaranchal 2007 Guidelines for issuing non-bailable warrants; liberty to be balanced; reasons required.
  • Lavesh v. State (NCT of Delhi) 2012 Absconder who has been proclaimed an offender cannot normally claim anticipatory bail.
  • Pepsi Foods v. Special Judicial Magistrate 1998 Summoning order requires application of mind.
  • State of Punjab v. Sarwan Singh 1981 Limitation not a bar to summons in certain circumstances; proclaiming process.
  • Raghubans Dubey v. State of Bihar 1967 Cognizance is of the offence; once taken, the Magistrate may proceed against all those involved.

Memory aids

  • Escalation of process: Summons -> Bailable warrant -> Non-bailable warrant -> Proclamation (30 days) -> Attachment -> Proclaimed offender -> Trial in absentia (s.356).

Exam traps

  • TrapProclamation applies to persons (accused) who abscond; not to witnesses.
  • Trap30 days is the minimum period given in the proclamation.
  • TrapProclaimed offender status under BNSS is limited to serious offences.

Process to Compel Production of Things; Search and Seizure; Reciprocal Arrangements with Foreign Countries

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

BNSS s.94 (CrPC s.91)Summons to produce document or thing - now covers electronic devices.
BNSS s.105 (new)Mandatory audio-video recording of search and seizure.
BNSS s.106 (CrPC s.102)Police power to seize property suspected stolen or found in suspicious circumstances.
BNSS s.107 (new)Attachment, forfeiture or restoration of property which is proceeds of crime.
BNSS s.185 (CrPC s.165)Search by police without warrant - reasons recorded; A/V recording.

Cases in one line

  • Pooran Mal v. Director of Inspection 1974 Illegally obtained evidence is admissible unless excluded by statute or Constitution.
  • State of Punjab v. Baldev Singh 1999 Search of person under NDPS - option of Magistrate/gazetted officer; non-compliance vitiates recovery.
  • Tapas Neogy v. State of W.B. 1999 Bank account is 'property' for s.102 CrPC; police can seize.
  • Teesta Setalvad v. State of Gujarat 2018 Seizure/freezing under s.102 must be reported to Magistrate forthwith.
  • Nevada Properties Pvt. Ltd. v. State of Maharashtra 2019 s.102 does not cover immovable property.
  • Ritesh Sinha v. State of U.P. 2019 Magistrate can order voice samples; Art 20(3) not violated.
  • Selvi v. State of Karnataka 2010 Involuntary narco/polygraph/BEAP tests violate Art 20(3) and Art 21.

Memory aids

  • Search checklist: 'W-L-C-R' - Witnesses (2 independent), List, Copy to occupant, Recording (audio-video, new).

Exam traps

  • Traps.94 is the successor to s.91 and covers digital devices; s.105 is new.
  • Traps.106 (police seizure) cannot be used for immovable property; s.107 (attachment of proceeds of crime) is the route.
  • TrapIllegal search does not automatically exclude evidence (Indian position), unlike US.

Security Proceedings, Maintenance of Wives, Children and Parents, Public Order and Preventive Action by Police

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

BNSS s.144 (CrPC s.125)Maintenance of wife, children, parents; interim maintenance within 60 days.
BNSS s.146 (CrPC s.127)Alteration in allowance.
BNSS s.163 (CrPC s.144)Orders in urgent cases of nuisance or apprehended danger; 2 months, extendable to 6.
BNSS s.126 (CrPC s.107)Security for keeping the peace in other cases.
BNSS s.170 (CrPC s.151)Arrest to prevent commission of cognizable offences.

Cases in one line

  • Mohd. Ahmed Khan v. Shah Bano Begum 1985 s.125 CrPC applies to Muslim divorced wives; secular remedy.
  • Danial Latifi v. Union of India 2001 Upheld the 1986 Act but read it to provide a fair provision for the divorced wife beyond iddat.
  • Mohd. Abdul Samad v. State of Telangana 2024 Divorced Muslim women can seek maintenance under s.125 CrPC; the 1986 Act does not oust it.
  • Rajnesh v. Neha 2021 Guidelines on disclosure affidavit, interim maintenance, date of maintenance.
  • Babulal Parate v. State of Maharashtra 1961 s.144 valid as reasonable restriction under Art 19.
  • Madhu Limaye v. SDM, Monghyr 1970 s.144 is not an arbitrary power; Magistrate must act judicially.
  • Anuradha Bhasin v. Union of India 2020 s.144 orders subject to proportionality, publication and review.
  • Chaturbhuj v. Sita Bai 2008 Wife living separately for sufficient reason can claim maintenance; provision is a measure of social justice.

Memory aids

  • Maintenance beneficiaries: 'W-C-C-P' - Wife, minor Child, disabled adult Child, Parents.
  • s.163 = 2 months / 6 months.

Exam traps

  • TrapMaintenance cannot be ordered for a married daughter (even a minor married daughter qualifies only through the husband); minor married child is covered only if she herself is unable.
  • TrapDivorced wife is covered until she remarries.
  • Traps.163 BNSS is NOT s.144 CrPC maintenance; maintenance is s.144 BNSS (old s.125). Do not mix the numbers.

Information to Police, FIR, Investigation, Remand, Default Bail and Police Report

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

BNSS s.173 (CrPC s.154)FIR - zero FIR, e-FIR (3 days), copy to informant, preliminary enquiry (14 days).
BNSS s.175(3) and (4) (CrPC s.156(3))Magistrate's order for investigation; new safeguards for public servants.
BNSS s.176(3)Mandatory forensic visit for offences punishable with 7 years or more; videography.
BNSS s.183 (CrPC s.164)Confessions and statements before Magistrate.
BNSS s.187 (CrPC s.167)Remand: 15 days police custody max; 60/90 days detention; default bail.
BNSS s.193 (CrPC s.173)Police report; 90-day victim update; 2 months in rape cases.
BNSS s.196 (CrPC s.176)Magistrate's inquiry into death in custody.

Cases in one line

  • Lalita Kumari v. Government of U.P. 2014 FIR registration mandatory when cognizable offence disclosed; limited preliminary inquiry.
  • State of Haryana v. Bhajan Lal 1992 Seven categories for quashing FIR/complaint.
  • T.T. Antony v. State of Kerala 2001 No second FIR for the same cognizable offence; fresh FIR for a different version is permitted subject to sameness.
  • Sakiri Vasu v. State of U.P. 2008 Magistrate's s.156(3) power includes monitoring the investigation.
  • Priyanka Srivastava v. State of U.P. 2015 Affidavit and prior recourse to s.154(3) are required before s.156(3).
  • Tahsildar Singh v. State of U.P. 1959 Meaning of s.162: statements to police cannot be used except to contradict.
  • Bhagwant Singh v. Commissioner of Police 1985 Informant must be given notice before Magistrate accepts a closure report.
  • Vinubhai Haribhai Malaviya v. State of Gujarat 2019 Magistrate can order further investigation up to the stage before trial concludes.
  • Rakesh Kumar Paul v. State of Assam 2017 Default bail - 90 days only for offences with a minimum of 10 years.
  • Sanjay Dutt v. State 1994 Default bail is an indefeasible right.
  • Uday Mohanlal Acharya v. State of Maharashtra 2001 Right to default bail is lost if the chargesheet is filed before the right is availed.
  • CBI v. Anupam J. Kulkarni 1992 Police custody permitted only in the first 15 days of detention.
  • Hasib v. State of Bihar 1972 FIR is not substantive evidence.
  • Satvinder Kaur v. State (Delhi) 1999 Police cannot refuse an FIR on territorial grounds; zero FIR.
  • Mukund Lal v. Union of India 1989 Case diary cannot be used by the accused; court may use it.

Memory aids

  • FIR 'E-Z-P': e-FIR signed in 3 days; Zero FIR anywhere; Preliminary enquiry within 14 days (3-7 years).
  • Remand: '15-60-90': 15 days police custody; 60/90 days overall; default bail after.

Exam traps

  • TrapPreliminary enquiry is available only in the 3-to-7-year category and with DSP-level permission; not for 7+ years.
  • TrapFIR can be lodged at any police station (zero FIR); investigation is done by the territorially competent station.
  • TrapDefault bail right is lost once the chargesheet is filed before the application is made.
  • TrapConfession before Magistrate: no police officer to be present; 24 hours reflection if from police custody.

Jurisdiction, Cognizance, Complaints to Magistrates, Sanction and Commitment of Cases

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

BNSS s.210 (CrPC s.190)Cognizance of offences by Magistrates.
BNSS s.218 (CrPC s.197)Sanction to prosecute judges and public servants - 120-day decision limit (new) Verify.
BNSS s.223 (CrPC s.200)Examination of complainant; accused to be heard before cognizance on complaint (new proviso).
BNSS s.225 (CrPC s.202)Postponement of issue of process - mandatory inquiry/investigation if accused resides outside jurisdiction.
BNSS s.230 (CrPC s.207)Supply of documents to accused within 14 days.
BNSS s.232 (CrPC s.209)Commitment of cases to Court of Session.
BNSS s.514 (CrPC s.468)Bar to cognizance after limitation: 6 months / 1 year / 3 years.

Cases in one line

  • Raghubans Dubey v. State of Bihar 1967 Cognizance is taken of the offence, not the offender.
  • R.R. Chari v. State of U.P. 1951 Definition of 'taking cognizance'.
  • Pepsi Foods v. Special Judicial Magistrate 1998 Summoning must be on application of mind.
  • Dharam Pal v. State of Haryana 2014 Sessions Court can summon additional accused after committal under s.193 CrPC.
  • Sarah Mathew v. Institute of Cardio Vascular Diseases 2014 Limitation is computed from the date of filing the complaint.
  • Matajog Dobey v. H.C. Bhari 1956 Reasonable connection between the act and official duty required for sanction.
  • Subramanian Swamy v. Manmohan Singh 2012 Time-limit for sanction; private citizen can seek sanction.
  • Iqbal Singh Marwah v. Meenakshi Marwah 2005 s.195(1)(b)(ii) does not bar forgery before the document is produced in court.
  • Mehmood Ul Rehman v. Khazir Mohammad Tunda 2015 Summoning order must reflect application of mind.
  • Birla Corporation Ltd v. Adventz Investments 2019 Inquiry under s.202 mandatory for outstation accused; scope.

Memory aids

  • Limitation s.514: '6 months - fine; 1 year - up to 1 year; 3 years - up to 3 years; none - above 3 years'.

Exam traps

  • TrapCognizance is by a Magistrate, not by a Court of Session directly (except after committal).
  • TrapSection 223 hearing of accused applies to complaint cases.
  • TrapLimitation under s.514 is not relevant for offences punishable with more than 3 years.
  • TrapCommittal Magistrate cannot discharge the accused.

The Charge: Form, Contents, Alteration, Joinder and Discharge

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

BNSS s.239 (CrPC s.216)Alteration of charge at any time before judgment.
BNSS s.241 (CrPC s.218)Separate charge for every distinct offence.
BNSS s.250 (CrPC s.227)Discharge in sessions case - application within 60 days of committal.
BNSS s.251 (CrPC s.228)Framing of charge - within 60 days from first hearing on charge.

Cases in one line

  • Willie (William) Slaney v. State of M.P. 1955 Defects in charge are cured if no prejudice; test is whether the accused was misled.
  • Union of India v. Prafulla Kumar Samal 1979 Judge's power to sift evidence at charge stage is limited to prima facie existence.
  • State of Orissa v. Debendra Nath Padhi 2005 At the charge stage the accused cannot rely on defence material.
  • Sajjan Kumar v. CBI 2010 Principles on framing/discharge; strong suspicion suffices.
  • Kantilal Chandulal Mehta v. State of Maharashtra 1969 Court's power to alter charge subject to prejudice.
  • State of Rajasthan v. Ashok Kumar Kashyap 2021 Court cannot hold a mini-trial at the charge stage.

Memory aids

  • Charge stage: 'Discharge 60 / Charge 60' - application for discharge within 60 days of committal; charge to be framed within 60 days of first hearing.

Exam traps

  • TrapAlteration can be done any time before judgment, even at the appellate stage in limited cases.
  • TrapDischarge standard is 'prima facie' - not 'beyond reasonable doubt'.
  • TrapThe 60-day timelines are new and have no CrPC equivalent.

Trial before a Court of Session

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

BNSS s.248 (CrPC s.225)Trial before Court of Session conducted by Public Prosecutor.
BNSS s.255 (CrPC s.232)Acquittal where no evidence that the accused committed the offence.
BNSS s.258 (CrPC s.235)Judgment; hearing on sentence.
BNSS s.351 (CrPC s.313)Power to examine the accused.

Cases in one line

  • Santa Singh v. State of Punjab 1976 Failure to hear on sentence is a serious irregularity; sentencing hearing is a distinct stage.
  • Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab 1980 Rarest of rare; special reasons for death.
  • Sharad Birdhichand Sarda v. State of Maharashtra 1984 Five golden principles on circumstantial evidence.
  • Zahira Habibullah Sheikh v. State of Gujarat 2004 Best Bakery case; free and fair trial; court's role in witness protection.
  • Masalti v. State of U.P. 1965 Conviction possible on the evidence of interested witnesses with caution.
  • Manoj v. State of M.P. 2022 Guidelines on mitigating material before death sentence.

Memory aids

  • Sessions trial: Committal - Opening - Discharge - Charge - Plea - Evidence - s.351 - Acquittal? - Defence - Arguments - Judgment - Sentence hearing.

Exam traps

  • TrapPP (not complainant) conducts prosecution in the Court of Session.
  • TrapAcquittal under s.255 comes after the examination of accused under s.351.
  • TrapSentence hearing under s.258(2) is mandatory.

Trial of Warrant-cases, Summons-cases, Summary Trials and Plea Bargaining

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

BNSS s.274 (CrPC s.251)Summons-case: substance of accusation stated.
BNSS s.282 (CrPC s.259)Conversion of a summons-case into a warrant-case.
BNSS s.283 (CrPC s.260)Power to try summarily - offences up to 3 years (BNSS) - up to 2 years (CrPC).
BNSS ss.289-300 (CrPC ss.265A-265L)Plea bargaining - application within 30 days of framing of charge.

Cases in one line

  • Murlidhar Meghraj Loya v. State of Maharashtra 1976 Plea bargaining unacceptable in socio-economic offences affecting public health.
  • Kasambhai v. State of Gujarat 1980 Plea bargaining discouraged; conviction must rest on proof.
  • State of Gujarat v. Natwar Harchandji Thakor 2005 Plea bargaining as a legal reform measure.
  • Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar 1979 Speedy trial; prolonged undertrial detention.

Memory aids

  • Plea bargaining eligibility: 'NOT death/life/>7 years, NOT socio-economic, NOT women/children <14'; apply within 30 days of charge.

Exam traps

  • TrapPlea bargaining is NOT available for offences punishable with more than 7 years.
  • TrapSummary trial limit rose to 3 years in BNSS (2 years in CrPC); maximum sentence in a summary trial remains 3 months.
  • TrapA formal charge is not framed in summons-cases.

General Provisions on Inquiries and Trials: Evidence, Examination of Accused, Pardon, Adjournment, Additional Accused, Compounding, Withdrawal, Absentia Trial, Unsound Mind

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

BNSS s.337 (CrPC s.300)Person once convicted or acquitted not to be tried for the same offence.
BNSS s.343 (CrPC s.306)Tender of pardon to accomplice.
BNSS s.346 (CrPC s.309)Power to postpone or adjourn proceedings.
BNSS s.348 (CrPC s.311)Power to summon material witness or examine person present.
BNSS s.351 (CrPC s.313)Power to examine the accused.
BNSS s.356Trial in absentia of proclaimed offender (new).
BNSS s.358 (CrPC s.319)Power to proceed against other persons appearing to be guilty.
BNSS s.359 (CrPC s.320)Compounding of offences.
BNSS s.360 (CrPC s.321)Withdrawal from prosecution; victim to be heard.

Cases in one line

  • Hardeep Singh v. State of Punjab 2014 s.319 CrPC power; strong and cogent evidence; applies at any stage after trial begins.
  • Zahira Habibullah Sheikh v. State of Gujarat 2006 Court's duty under s.311 to ensure fair trial; witness intimidation.
  • Gian Singh v. State of Punjab 2012 HC may quash non-compoundable offences on settlement with caution.
  • Narinder Singh v. State of Punjab 2014 Guidelines for quashing on settlement; s.307 IPC not ordinarily.
  • Sheonandan Paswan v. State of Bihar 1987 Withdrawal from prosecution requires the court's consent on proper grounds.
  • Asraf Ali v. State of Assam 2008 Duty to put all incriminating circumstances to the accused under s.313.
  • State of Maharashtra v. Praful B. Desai 2003 Video conferencing recording of evidence is valid.
  • State of Bombay v. S.L. Apte 1961 s.403 (old) and Art 20(2): same offence test.
  • Vinod Kumar v. State of Punjab 2015 Day-to-day trial; adjournments only for special reasons.
  • Ram Gopal v. State of M.P. 2021 Quashing of non-compoundable offences on compromise; guidelines.
  • Suk Das v. Union Territory of Arunachal Pradesh 1986 Free legal aid is a constitutional right; trial without legal aid is vitiated.

Memory aids

  • s.337 = double jeopardy; s.343 = pardon; s.346 = adjournment; s.348 = material witness; s.351 = accused; s.358 = additional accused; s.359 = compounding; s.360 = withdrawal.
  • Old CrPC: 300-306-309-311-313-319-320-321.

Exam traps

  • Traps.358 (319) 'strong and cogent evidence' > prima facie but < conviction standard.
  • Traps.351 statement is not on oath; accused's silence cannot lead to adverse inference.
  • TrapCompounding = acquittal; withdrawal = acquittal (summons) or discharge (warrant/Sessions before charge).
  • TrapBNSS s.360: victim must be heard before withdrawal is allowed.

Judgment, Sentencing, Compensation to Victims, Probation, Witness Protection and Confirmation of Death Sentence

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

BNSS s.392 (CrPC s.353)Judgment - within 45 days of arguments (new); copy in 7 days.
BNSS s.396 (CrPC s.357A)Victim compensation scheme.
BNSS s.398Witness protection scheme (new).
BNSS s.401 (CrPC s.360)Probation of good conduct or admonition.
BNSS s.403 (CrPC s.362)No alteration of judgment except clerical.
BNSS s.407 (CrPC s.366)Death sentence must be confirmed by the High Court.

Cases in one line

  • Mahender Chawla v. Union of India 2018 Witness Protection Scheme approved; binding as law until Parliament legislates.
  • Ankush Shivaji Gaikwad v. State of Maharashtra 2013 Court must consider the question of compensation in every case.
  • Laxmi v. Union of India 2014 Acid attack: compensation, regulation of sale, free treatment.
  • Mohd. Arif @ Ashfaq v. Registrar, Supreme Court of India 2014 Review of death sentence in open court by a three-judge bench.
  • Union of India v. V. Sriharan 2016 Life imprisonment means rest of life; fixed-term life without remission permissible; limits on executive remission.
  • Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab 1980 Rarest of rare; balance sheet of aggravating and mitigating circumstances.
  • Hari Singh Mann v. Harbhajan Singh Bajwa 2001 Court cannot alter a signed judgment except clerical errors.

Memory aids

  • Death sentence: Sessions Court -> s.407 HC confirmation -> two judges -> third judge if difference -> SC.

Exam traps

  • TrapProbation may be refused only for special reasons recorded in writing.
  • TrapA signed judgment cannot be altered except to correct clerical errors.
  • TrapConfirmation proceedings do not take away the convict's own right of appeal.

Appeals, Reference, Revision and Transfer of Criminal Cases

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

BNSS s.413 (CrPC s.372)No appeal unless provided; victim's right to appeal proviso.
BNSS s.415 (CrPC s.374)Appeals from convictions.
BNSS s.419 (CrPC s.378)Appeal in case of acquittal.
BNSS s.430 (CrPC s.389)Suspension of sentence; bail pending appeal.
BNSS s.438 (CrPC s.397)Calling for records - revision; interlocutory bar.
BNSS s.442 (CrPC s.401)High Court's powers of revision.
BNSS s.447 (CrPC s.406)Supreme Court's power to transfer cases.

Cases in one line

  • Satya Pal Singh v. State of M.P. 2015 Victim's right to appeal under the proviso to s.372 CrPC.
  • Mallikarjun Kodagali v. State of Karnataka 2018 Victim can appeal against acquittal without leave; includes complainant.
  • Chandrappa v. State of Karnataka 2007 Principles for appellate review of acquittal.
  • Amar Nath v. State of Haryana 1977 Meaning of interlocutory order; revision bar.
  • Madhu Limaye v. State of Maharashtra 1977 Intermediate orders can be revised.
  • Maneka Sanjay Gandhi v. Rani Jethmalani 1979 Transfer on reasonable apprehension of an unfair trial.
  • K.K. Patel v. State of Gujarat 2000 Revisional power; order of issue of process is not interlocutory.

Memory aids

  • Appeals: Magistrate conviction -> Sessions; Sessions conviction (or >7 years) -> HC; HC original conviction -> SC.
  • Revision bars: interlocutory orders; conversion of acquittal to conviction; where appeal lies and not filed.

Exam traps

  • TrapAn appeal by the accused cannot lead to enhancement of sentence; the State must appeal or move revision.
  • TrapThe High Court cannot convert an acquittal into conviction in revision.
  • TrapVictim's right to appeal is a proviso to s.413 BNSS and not a separate section.

  • Laxman Naskar v. Union of India 2000 Factors relevant for premature release.
  • Union of India v. V. Sriharan 2016 Constitution Bench - life means rest of life; restrictions on s.432 remission.
  • Bilkis Yakub Rasool v. Union of India 2024 Appropriate Government is where the trial was held; remission set aside.
  • Shatrughan Chauhan v. Union of India 2014 Inordinate delay in disposal of mercy petitions is a ground for commutation.
  • Epuru Sudhakar v. Government of A.P. 2006 Pardon power is reviewable on limited grounds.

  • Mercy: 30 days (Governor) then 60 days (President).

Exam traps

  • TrapRemission under s.473 BNSS (executive statutory power) is different from Art 72/161 constitutional power.
  • Traps.475 prevents release before 14 years only where death was a possible punishment.

Bail and Bonds: Regular, Anticipatory, Default and Undertrial Release; Cancellation

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

BNSS s.478 (CrPC s.436)Bail in bailable offences as of right; indigent after 1 week.
BNSS s.480 (CrPC s.437)Bail in non-bailable offences.
BNSS s.481 (CrPC s.437A)Bail bonds for appearance before appellate court.
BNSS s.482 (CrPC s.438)Anticipatory bail.
BNSS s.483 (CrPC s.439)Special powers of HC and Sessions Court; cancellation.
BNSS s.479 (CrPC s.436A)Maximum period of detention for undertrials: one-half; first-time offender one-third.
BNSS s.187(3) (CrPC s.167(2))Default bail after 60/90 days.

Cases in one line

  • Gurbaksh Singh Sibbia v. State of Punjab 1980 Constitution Bench: anticipatory bail not time-limited; liberal construction.
  • Sushila Aggarwal v. State (NCT of Delhi) 2020 Constitution Bench: no fixed time limit on anticipatory bail protection.
  • Siddharam Satlingappa Mhetre v. State of Maharashtra 2010 Factors for anticipatory bail; personal liberty.
  • Satender Kumar Antil v. CBI 2022 Guidelines for bail; categories A-D; Arnesh Kumar to be followed.
  • Sanjay Chandra v. CBI 2012 Bail is not punishment; economic offences not an exception.
  • State of Rajasthan v. Balchand 1977 Basic rule: bail, not jail.
  • Dolat Ram v. State of Haryana 1995 Cancellation of bail needs cogent and overwhelming circumstances.
  • Union of India v. K.A. Najeeb 2021 Prolonged incarceration justifies bail even under UAPA s.43D(5).
  • Moti Ram v. State of M.P. 1978 Bail conditions must not be excessive; local surety requirement unreasonable.
  • Bhim Singh v. Union of India 2015 Direction to implement s.436A for undertrials.
  • Prem Prakash v. Union of India 2024 Bail under PMLA - the stringent conditions yield to Art 21 after prolonged detention.
  • Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar 2014 Safeguards against arbitrary arrest; basis for bail in 7-year offences.

Memory aids

  • Bail ladder: s.478 (bailable - right) -> s.480 (non-bailable - discretion) -> s.482 (anticipatory) -> s.483 (HC/Sessions; cancellation) -> s.479 (undertrial) -> s.187(3) (default).
  • Old numbers: 436-437-437A-438-439-436A.

Exam traps

  • TrapAnticipatory bail is NOT time-limited per se, and is not a right.
  • Traps.479: 1/2 of maximum term for ordinary undertrials; 1/3 for first-time offenders; no relief for death/life offences.
  • TrapCancellation of bail needs supervening circumstances; setting aside a perverse order is a different power.
  • TrapBail bonds: personal bond for indigent persons after one week in bailable offences.
