Execution, Suspension, Remission and Commutation of Sentences; Mercy Petitions

iOverview

How sentences are executed, set-off of pre-trial detention, remission, commutation, the 14-year rule for life convicts and the new mercy-petition time-lines.

★Must know

  1. Execution of death sentence: after confirmation, a warrant is issued; hanging until dead; postponement in the case of a pending appeal/SLP; pregnant woman - the HC may commute to life imprisonment (BNSS provisions replacing ss.413-416 CrPC) Verify · section numbers. Execution of sentences of imprisonment (warrant to the jail); levy of fine (warrant for attachment and sale of movable property; Collector); fine and imprisonment in default.
  2. Set-off of period of detention (s.468 BNSS; s.428 CrPC): the period of detention undergone by the accused during investigation, inquiry or trial is set off against the term of imprisonment imposed on conviction; for a life sentence it is not set off (Bhagirath v. Delhi Administration, 1985 - life imprisonment is for life; State of Maharashtra v. Najakat Alia Mubarak Ali, 2001 - period of detention to be considered in a particular context); BNSS provides that set-off does not apply to imprisonment in default of fine unless so ordered.
  3. Mercy petition (s.472 BNSS; new; formerly governed by prison manuals): a convict sentenced to death may submit a mercy petition to the Governor within thirty days from the date on which the Superintendent of the jail informs him of the dismissal of his appeal/SLP/review, and if the Governor rejects it, to the President within sixty days from the date of the Governor's decision; where there are multiple convicts in the same case, mercy petitions must be filed by all within sixty days, and the President decides on all together; the decision of the President is final and not subject to appeal or judicial review on the merits Verify · the periods. Judicial review of mercy decisions: Epuru Sudhakar v. Government of A.P. (2006); Shatrughan Chauhan v. Union of India (2014) - inordinate delay can commute; Kehar Singh v. Union of India (1989).
  4. Power to suspend or remit sentences (s.473 BNSS; s.432 CrPC): the appropriate Government may suspend the execution of a sentence or remit the whole or any part of the punishment, with or without conditions; the Government may ask the presiding Judge of the convicting/confirming court for his opinion; the person must not be released if conditions are not fulfilled; the Governor's constitutional power under Art 161 is independent (Maru Ram v. Union of India, 1980). BNSS adds that a sentence of death may be commuted only to imprisonment for life; a sentence of life imprisonment only to imprisonment of not less than seven years; and imprisonment of seven years or more to imprisonment of not less than three years; the fine only may be remitted Verify · exact lines.
  5. Power to commute sentence (s.474; s.433 CrPC): the appropriate Government may commute a death sentence to life imprisonment or any other term; life imprisonment to a term not exceeding fourteen years or fine; rigorous to simple imprisonment; fine to imprisonment... Restriction on powers of remission or commutation in certain cases (s.475 BNSS; s.433A CrPC): where a sentence of imprisonment for life has been imposed on conviction of an offence for which death is one of the punishments provided by law, or a death sentence has been commuted to life, the person shall not be released unless he has served at least fourteen years of imprisonment. Concurrent power of Central Government in respect of death sentences (s.476; s.434); State Government to act in consultation with the Central Government in cases investigated by a Central agency (s.477; s.435).
  6. Remission jurisprudence: Laxman Naskar v. Union of India (2000) - factors for premature release; Union of India v. V. Sriharan (2016) - life sentence means life subject to remission by the appropriate Government after 14 years; fixed term by court; Joseph v. State of Kerala (2023) - policy-based remission; Bilkis Yakub Rasool v. Union of India (8 Jan 2024) - the 'appropriate Government' for remission is the State in which the offender was tried and sentenced, i.e., Maharashtra, not Gujarat where the offence occurred; remission orders for the 11 convicts quashed; Rashidul Jafar @ Chotu v. State of U.P. (2022) - remission must be considered even if no application is made, where a policy exists; A.G. Perarivalan v. State of Tamil Nadu (2022) - Art 142 used for release after the Governor's delay.
  7. Suspension of sentences: s.430; Parole and furlough are governed by prison laws, not the BNSS.

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
BNSS s.468 (CrPC s.428)Set-off of detention period.
BNSS s.472Mercy petitions - time limits (new).
BNSS s.473 (CrPC s.432)Suspension and remission of sentences.
BNSS s.475 (CrPC s.433A)14-year minimum for life convicts of offences punishable with death.

⚖Landmark cases

Maru Ram v. Union of India

1980

s.433A CrPC valid; executive remission subject to review.

Laxman Naskar v. Union of India

Factors relevant for premature release.

Union of India v. V. Sriharan

2016

Constitution Bench - life means rest of life; restrictions on s.432 remission.

Bilkis Yakub Rasool v. Union of India

2024

Appropriate Government is where the trial was held; remission set aside.

Inordinate delay in disposal of mercy petitions is a ground for commutation.

2006

Pardon power is reviewable on limited grounds.

✎Memory aids

  • Life convicts of capital offences: minimum 14 years actual imprisonment before remission (s.475).
  • Mercy: 30 days (Governor) then 60 days (President).

!Exam traps

  • TrapRemission under s.473 BNSS (executive statutory power) is different from Art 72/161 constitutional power.
  • Traps.475 prevents release before 14 years only where death was a possible punishment.

?Likely questions

  1. Which BNSS section corresponds to s.432 CrPC? (s.473)
  2. Minimum imprisonment before release of a life convict for a capital offence? (14 years)
  3. Mercy petition to Governor must be filed within? (30 days)
