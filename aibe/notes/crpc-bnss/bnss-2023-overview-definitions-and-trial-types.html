BNSS 2023: Overview, Transition, Definitions and Classification of Offences and Trials

iOverview

Foundation chapter: what changed, when, and the vocabulary the rest of the paper is built on (cognizable, bailable, summons-case, warrant-case, complaint, police report, inquiry, investigation, victim).

★Must know

  1. The Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (Act 46 of 2023) received Presidential assent on 25 December 2023 and came into force on 1 July 2024 together with the BNS and BSA. It repeals the CrPC 1973 (s.531). The CrPC itself replaced the CrPC 1898 and came into force on 1 April 1974.
  2. Size: BNSS has 531 sections (CrPC: 484). Hundreds of provisions were re-worded, a few dozen new provisions and many time-limits were added, and a few CrPC provisions were dropped (for example the Metropolitan Magistrate machinery and the special provision on juveniles in s.27 CrPC).
  3. Savings (s.531(2)): appeals, applications, trials, inquiries or investigations pending on 1 July 2024 continue under the CrPC as if the BNSS had not come into force. Proceedings begun after that date follow BNSS procedure. The offence is judged under the law in force on the date of the act (Art 20(1)): conduct before 1 July 2024 is an IPC offence, conduct on or after it is a BNS offence, even though the procedure in a fresh FIR is BNSS.
  4. Purpose and philosophy: procedural code - it prescribes machinery for investigation, inquiry, trial and punishment; substantive criminal law lies in the BNS. BNSS adds technology (audio-video recording, electronic summons, e-FIR, electronic trial), time-bound steps, victim-centric provisions and a statutory witness-protection scheme.
  5. Definitions (s.2(1) BNSS; s.2 CrPC): 'cognizable offence' - police may arrest without warrant and investigate without Magistrate's order; 'non-cognizable' - police need a warrant and the Magistrate's order to investigate; 'bailable' and 'non-bailable' as per First Schedule (or other law); 'summons-case' - offence not being a warrant-case; 'warrant-case' - offence punishable with death, imprisonment for life or imprisonment exceeding two years; 'complaint' - allegation made orally, in writing or electronically to a Magistrate with a view to action under the Sanhita (a police report is not a complaint; but a police report that discloses only a non-cognizable offence is treated as a complaint, Explanation); 'police report' - report forwarded under s.193(3) (173(2) CrPC); 'inquiry' - every inquiry other than a trial conducted by a Magistrate or Court; 'investigation' - collection of evidence by police or any person authorised by a Magistrate; 'judicial proceeding' - includes any proceeding in which evidence is or may be legally taken on oath; 'victim' - person who has suffered loss or injury by the act or omission of the accused, including guardian or legal heir; new definitions of 'electronic communication' and 'audio-video electronic means'.
  6. Inquiry v. investigation v. trial: investigation is by police (executive), inquiry by Magistrate/Court before trial (judicial), trial is the determination of guilt (Directorate of Enforcement v. Deepak Mahajan, 1994; Adri Dharan Das v. State of W.B., 2005 - 'arrest' meaning).
  7. Classification of offences: (1) cognizable / non-cognizable; (2) bailable / non-bailable; (3) compoundable / non-compoundable (s.359); (4) summons-case / warrant-case; (5) triable by Court of Session / Magistrate (First Schedule, Part-I for BNS offences, Part-II for other laws: if punishable with death/life/imprisonment above 7 years - Court of Session; otherwise Magistrate).
  8. Trial procedures: Sessions trial (ss.248-260), warrant-case trial by Magistrate on police report (ss.261-266) and on complaint (ss.267-273), summons-case (ss.274-282), summary trial (ss.283-288), plea bargaining (ss.289-300).
  9. Basic principles: presumption of innocence; burden on prosecution; fair trial and open court (s.366 BNSS/327 CrPC; Art 21); speedy trial (Hussainara Khatoon, 1979; A.R. Antulay, 1992 - bar of limitation not applicable to criminal trial but delay violates Art 21); right to be defended (Art 22(1), s.340 BNSS/303 CrPC); free legal aid (s.341 BNSS/304 CrPC; Art 39A; Khatri v. State of Bihar, 1981; Suk Das v. UT of Arunachal Pradesh, 1986 - trial vitiated without legal aid); no double jeopardy (s.337 BNSS/300 CrPC; Art 20(2)); inherent powers (s.528 BNSS/482 CrPC).
  10. Headline BNSS innovations at a glance: zero FIR and e-FIR (s.173); preliminary enquiry for 3-7 year offences (s.173(3)); mandatory forensic visit for 7+ year offences (s.176(3)); audio-video recording of search and seizure (s.105) and of statements; handcuff rule (s.43(3)); arrest safeguard for the infirm/over-60 (s.35(7)); police custody in parts (s.187); proclaimed offender and trial in absentia (ss.84, 356); attachment of proceeds of crime (s.107); victim information within 90 days (s.193(3)(ii)); discharge and charge within 60 days (ss.250-251); documents supplied within 14 days (s.230); judgment within 45 days (s.392); witness protection scheme (s.398); mercy-petition time-lines (s.472); undertrial release rules (s.479); trial in electronic mode (s.530); community service as punishment (BNS s.4(f), s.23 BNSS).

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
BNSS s.531Repeal of CrPC and savings: pending proceedings continue under CrPC.
BNSS s.2(1)Definitions - includes victim, electronic communication, audio-video electronic means.
BNSS s.4 (CrPC s.4)Offences under BNS tried per BNSS; offences under other laws tried per BNSS unless the special law provides otherwise.
BNSS s.5 (CrPC s.5)Savings of special/local law and special jurisdiction; BNSS does not override them.
First ScheduleClassification of BNS offences: cognizable?, bailable?, by which court triable.

⚖Landmark cases

Directorate of Enforcement v. Deepak Mahajan

1994

Distinguished inquiry, investigation, trial; Magistrate's remand powers apply to arrests by authorities under special statutes.

Pepsi Foods Ltd v. Special Judicial Magistrate

1998

Summoning of an accused is a serious matter; Magistrate must apply mind.

Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar

1979

Speedy trial and legal aid are part of Art 21.

A.R. Antulay v. R.S. Nayak

1992

Seven-judge bench; right to speedy trial; no fixed time-limit but case-by-case.

Khatri v. State of Bihar

1981

Legal aid right arises at first production and remand.

✎Memory aids

  • Warrant case = more than 2 years; summons case = 2 years or less.
  • New BNSS time-limits: '3-14-60-90-45': e-FIR signature 3 days; preliminary enquiry/ document supply 14 days; discharge and charge 60 days; default bail 60/90; judgment 45 days.

!Exam traps

  • TrapThe BNSS is not retrospective in substantive liability: Art 20(1) still applies.
  • TrapA 'complaint' under s.2(1) is made to a Magistrate - a complaint to police is an 'information'/FIR.
  • TrapDo not confuse 'summons' (s.63) with 'summons-case' (a type of trial).
