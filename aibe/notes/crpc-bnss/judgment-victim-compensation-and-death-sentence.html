Judgment, Sentencing, Compensation to Victims, Probation, Witness Protection and Confirmation of Death Sentence

iOverview

Contents and timing of judgments, victim compensation, new witness protection scheme, probation, no alteration after signing, and High Court confirmation of death sentences.

★Must know

  1. Judgment (s.392 BNSS; s.353 CrPC): pronounced in open court by the presiding officer either immediately after the termination of the trial or at some subsequent time of which notice is given; BNSS requires pronouncement within forty-five days from the date of completion of arguments (reasons to be recorded for any delay in the court record), and the judgment must be made available to the parties electronically within seven days Verify · numbers. Language and contents (s.393; s.354): in the language of the court; points for determination, the decision and reasons; in a conviction, the offence and section of law; in an acquittal, the offence of which the accused is acquitted and a direction that he be set at liberty; where the conviction is for an offence punishable with death or life imprisonment, the judgment should state the reasons for the sentence awarded - for death, 'special reasons' (s.393(3); s.354(3)).
  2. Order for notifying address of previously convicted offender (s.394; s.356 CrPC). Order to pay compensation (s.395 BNSS; s.357 CrPC): when a court imposes a fine, it may order the whole or part to be applied in payment of expenses, compensation to any person for loss or injury, compensation to dependants (fatal accidents), or to the bona fide purchaser of stolen property; when the sentence does not include a fine, the court may order the accused to pay compensation to the victim (s.357(3)); Hari Singh v. Sukhbir Singh (1988) - courts should liberally use compensation; Ankush Shivaji Gaikwad v. State of Maharashtra (2013) - the court has a duty to consider compensation in every case.
  3. Victim compensation scheme (s.396 BNSS; s.357A CrPC): every State Government, in coordination with the Central Government, shall prepare a scheme for compensation to the victim or his dependants who have suffered loss or injury as a result of the crime and who require rehabilitation; the court may recommend compensation; the District/State Legal Services Authority decides the quantum; interim treatment s.397 (s.357C): all hospitals - public or private - shall provide first-aid or medical treatment free of cost to victims of acid attack, rape, and certain sexual offences (BNS ss.64-71 etc.) and shall immediately inform the police. Nipun Saxena v. Union of India (2019); Laxmi v. Union of India (2014) - acid attack victims, compensation of Rs 3 lakh minimum, regulation of acid sales; Delhi Domestic Working Women's Forum v. Union of India (1995) - rape victim compensation; Bodhisattwa Gautam v. Subhra Chakraborty (1996).
  4. Witness protection scheme (s.398 BNSS; new): every State Government shall prepare and notify a Witness Protection Scheme for the State with a view to ensuring the protection of witnesses. Mahender Chawla v. Union of India (2018) - the Supreme Court approved the Witness Protection Scheme, 2018 as law under Art 141 until legislation; Zahira Habibullah (2004); Sakshi v. Union of India (2004) - safeguards for child witnesses.
  5. Compensation to persons groundlessly arrested (s.399; s.358 CrPC) - Magistrate may order compensation up to Rs 1,000 Verify to the person arrested; costs on frivolous complaints (s.400; s.359). Order to release on probation of good conduct or after admonition (s.401 BNSS; s.360 CrPC): in cases of persons not under twenty-one years convicted of an offence punishable with a fine only or with imprisonment for seven years or less; women convicted of offence not punishable with death or life; and persons under twenty-one convicted of offence not punishable with death or life; the court must consider the age, character, antecedents, circumstances; s.402 (s.361): special reasons to be recorded if probation is not applied. The Probation of Offenders Act 1958 takes precedence; Ved Prakash v. State of Haryana (1981); Rattan Lal v. State of Punjab (1964) - the beneficial provision applies even to pre-Act offences; Chhanni v. State of U.P. (2006); Lakhvir Singh v. State of Punjab (2021). Also see Juvenile Justice Act 2015.
  6. No alteration of judgment once signed (s.403 BNSS; s.362 CrPC): save to correct a clerical or arithmetical error (functus officio); Hari Singh Mann v. Harbhajan Singh Bajwa (2001); State of Punjab v. Davinder Pal Singh Bhullar (2011) - inherent powers cannot recall a judgment on merits. Copy of judgment to the accused free of cost (s.404; s.363); judgment in translation (s.405; s.364); copy to be sent to the District Magistrate (s.406; s.365).
  7. Confirmation of death sentence (ss.407-412 BNSS; ss.366-371 CrPC): when the Court of Session passes a sentence of death, the proceedings are submitted to the High Court and the sentence is not executed unless confirmed (s.407; s.366); the HC may direct further inquiry or additional evidence (s.408; s.367); confirm, annul, alter or order a new trial (s.409; s.368); confirmation by at least two Judges (s.410; s.369); procedure if there is a difference of opinion (s.411; s.370: third Judge); order of confirmation (s.412; s.371). The convict has an independent right of appeal to the High Court; the HC must independently scrutinise the evidence. Mohd. Arif @ Ashfaq v. Registrar, Supreme Court (2014): review in death sentence cases is heard in open court by three Judges; Shatrughan Chauhan v. Union of India (2014) - delay in execution; Santosh Kumar Satishbhushan Bariyar v. State of Maharashtra (2009); Swamy Shraddananda (2008) - life imprisonment for the remainder of life; Union of India v. V. Sriharan (2016, Constitution Bench) - fixed-term life imprisonment without remission up to a specified number of years by the High Court/Supreme Court is permissible; death sentence executed by hanging (Deena v. Union of India, 1983 - constitutional).

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
BNSS s.392 (CrPC s.353)Judgment - within 45 days of arguments (new); copy in 7 days.
BNSS s.396 (CrPC s.357A)Victim compensation scheme.
BNSS s.398Witness protection scheme (new).
BNSS s.401 (CrPC s.360)Probation of good conduct or admonition.
BNSS s.403 (CrPC s.362)No alteration of judgment except clerical.
BNSS s.407 (CrPC s.366)Death sentence must be confirmed by the High Court.

⚖Landmark cases

Mahender Chawla v. Union of India

2018

Witness Protection Scheme approved; binding as law until Parliament legislates.

Ankush Shivaji Gaikwad v. State of Maharashtra

2013

Court must consider the question of compensation in every case.

Laxmi v. Union of India

2014

Acid attack: compensation, regulation of sale, free treatment.

Mohd. Arif @ Ashfaq v. Registrar, Supreme Court of India

2014

Review of death sentence in open court by a three-judge bench.

Union of India v. V. Sriharan

2016

Life imprisonment means rest of life; fixed-term life without remission permissible; limits on executive remission.

Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab

1980

Rarest of rare; balance sheet of aggravating and mitigating circumstances.

Hari Singh Mann v. Harbhajan Singh Bajwa

2001

Court cannot alter a signed judgment except clerical errors.

✎Memory aids

  • Death sentence: Sessions Court -> s.407 HC confirmation -> two judges -> third judge if difference -> SC.

!Exam traps

  • TrapProbation may be refused only for special reasons recorded in writing.
  • TrapA signed judgment cannot be altered except to correct clerical errors.
  • TrapConfirmation proceedings do not take away the convict's own right of appeal.
