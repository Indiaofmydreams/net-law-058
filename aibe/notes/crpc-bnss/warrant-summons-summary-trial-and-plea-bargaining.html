Trial of Warrant-cases, Summons-cases, Summary Trials and Plea Bargaining

iOverview

The Magistrate's trial procedures. Know who may try which category, the procedure steps, summary trial limits and the eligibility conditions for plea bargaining.

★Must know

  1. Warrant-cases on police report (ss.261-266 BNSS; ss.238-243 CrPC): compliance with s.230 (supply of documents); discharge if the charge is groundless (s.262); framing of charge (s.263) - in writing and read to the accused; plea of guilty (s.264); evidence for prosecution (s.265) - the Magistrate hears the prosecution and takes all evidence; evidence for defence (s.266). Warrant-cases on complaint (ss.267-273 BNSS; ss.244-250 CrPC): evidence for prosecution (s.267); discharge (s.268; s.245); procedure where accused not discharged (s.269); evidence for defence (s.270); acquittal or conviction (s.271; s.248); absence of complainant (s.272; s.249) - the Magistrate may discharge the accused if offence is compoundable or non-cognizable; compensation for accusation without reasonable cause (s.273; s.250) - the Magistrate may order the complainant or informant to pay compensation to the accused.
  2. Summons-cases (ss.274-282 BNSS; ss.251-259 CrPC): substance of the accusation is stated to the accused (no formal charge) (s.274); conviction on plea of guilty (s.275); conviction on plea of guilty in absence of accused in petty cases (s.276; s.253 - fine not exceeding Rs 1,000 sent by post/through pleader); procedure (s.277); acquittal or conviction (s.278); non-appearance or death of the complainant (s.279; s.256) - the Magistrate may acquit the accused unless he adjourns; withdrawal of complaint (s.280; s.257) - with Magistrate's permission; power to stop proceedings in certain cases (s.281; s.258) - the Magistrate of the first class with the CJM's previous sanction may stop proceedings in a summons-case instituted otherwise than on a complaint and release/acquit; power to convert summons-cases into warrant-cases (s.282; s.259) - where the offence is punishable with imprisonment exceeding six months and the Magistrate thinks it in the interests of justice.
  3. Summary trials (ss.283-288 BNSS; ss.260-265 CrPC): the Chief Judicial Magistrate, a Judicial Magistrate of the First Class specially empowered by the High Court, may try summarily offences not punishable with death, imprisonment for life or imprisonment exceeding three years (CrPC: two years) - along with theft, receiving stolen property, assisting concealment, etc. where the value does not exceed Rs 20,000 Verify · value limit; No appeal in petty cases; no sentence of imprisonment exceeding three months in a summary trial (s.285; s.262 CrPC). The procedure is that of a summons-case; a record of the date, offence, plea, evidence, finding and sentence is kept (s.286; s.263); a judgment is brief (s.287; s.264). BNSS also provides that a Magistrate shall try summarily certain petty cases (those punishable with fine only up to a small limit Verify) and promotes community service as a sentence for petty offences.
  4. Plea bargaining (Chapter XXIII BNSS ss.289-300; Chapter XXIA CrPC ss.265A-265L): applies to offences other than those punishable with death, imprisonment for life or imprisonment exceeding seven years; excluded: offences affecting the socio-economic condition of the country, and offences committed against a woman or a child below fourteen years of age (BNSS s.289; s.265A). The accused must file an application in the court of trial within thirty days from the date of framing of charge (s.289(1)), accompanied by an affidavit stating that he has voluntarily preferred it and has not been previously convicted of the same offence. The court examines the accused in camera to satisfy that the application is voluntary; issues notice to the PP/complainant/victim; a mutually satisfactory disposition is worked out; the victim is entitled to compensation; the sentence may be less than the minimum (half of the minimum where a minimum is provided; one-fourth of the punishment otherwise) and the accused may be released on probation (s.265E CrPC; corresponding BNSS section in the s.289-300 block). The judgment is final and no appeal lies except an SLP under Art 136 or writ under Arts 226/227 (s.265G CrPC; corresponding BNSS section in the s.289-300 block). The statements made by the accused in plea-bargaining cannot be used for any other purpose (s.265K). Set-off of detention period applies (s.468). Plea bargaining is not available to juveniles (separate regime).
  5. Plea bargaining cases: State of Gujarat v. Natwar Harchandji Thakor (2005) - Gujarat HC upheld the constitutionality in the context of the new chapter; Murlidhar Meghraj Loya v. State of Maharashtra (1976) and Kasambhai v. State of Gujarat (1980) - plea bargaining in socio-economic offences was disapproved.
  6. Attendance of persons confined or detained in prisons (Chapter XXII BNSS) - production before court by video-conferencing is permitted under BNSS.
  7. Judgment in summary trials: the Magistrate records: serial number of the case, date of commission, date of complaint, the offender's name, parentage and residence, the offence and value of property, plea and examination, finding, sentence, date of termination.

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
BNSS s.274 (CrPC s.251)Summons-case: substance of accusation stated.
BNSS s.282 (CrPC s.259)Conversion of a summons-case into a warrant-case.
BNSS s.283 (CrPC s.260)Power to try summarily - offences up to 3 years (BNSS) - up to 2 years (CrPC).
BNSS ss.289-300 (CrPC ss.265A-265L)Plea bargaining - application within 30 days of framing of charge.

⚖Landmark cases

Murlidhar Meghraj Loya v. State of Maharashtra

1976

Plea bargaining unacceptable in socio-economic offences affecting public health.

Kasambhai v. State of Gujarat

1980

Plea bargaining discouraged; conviction must rest on proof.

State of Gujarat v. Natwar Harchandji Thakor

2005

Plea bargaining as a legal reform measure.

Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar

1979

Speedy trial; prolonged undertrial detention.

✎Memory aids

  • Plea bargaining eligibility: 'NOT death/life/>7 years, NOT socio-economic, NOT women/children <14'; apply within 30 days of charge.

!Exam traps

  • TrapPlea bargaining is NOT available for offences punishable with more than 7 years.
  • TrapSummary trial limit rose to 3 years in BNSS (2 years in CrPC); maximum sentence in a summary trial remains 3 months.
  • TrapA formal charge is not framed in summons-cases.

?Likely questions

  1. Plea bargaining application must be filed within how many days of framing of charge? (30)
  2. Maximum punishment in a summary trial? (3 months)
  3. A summons-case may be converted into a warrant case if offence is punishable above? (6 months)
