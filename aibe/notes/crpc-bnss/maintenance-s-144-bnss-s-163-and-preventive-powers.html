Security Proceedings, Maintenance of Wives, Children and Parents, Public Order and Preventive Action by Police

iOverview

Maintenance (s.144 BNSS = s.125 CrPC) and s.163 (old s.144) are AIBE favourites. Also know security for peace/good behaviour, dispersal of unlawful assemblies, public nuisance and police preventive arrests.

★Must know

  1. Security for keeping the peace and good behaviour (Executive Magistrates): s.125 BNSS (s.106 CrPC) - on conviction; s.126 (s.107) - security for keeping the peace in other cases (likely breach of peace); s.127 (s.108) - security for good behaviour from persons disseminating certain matters (those promoting disaffection, class hatred or defamatory threats); s.128 (s.109) - security for good behaviour from vagrants and suspected persons; s.129 (s.110) - security from habitual offenders (up to three years). Bonds with or without sureties, for periods up to one year (peace) or three years (habitual offenders). Cases: Madhu Limaye v. State of Maharashtra (1970) - preventive jurisdiction valid.
  2. Maintenance of wives, children and parents (s.144 BNSS; s.125 CrPC): a person having sufficient means who neglects or refuses to maintain (a) his wife (including a divorced wife who has not remarried), unable to maintain herself; (b) his legitimate or illegitimate minor child (married or not); (c) his major child (not a married daughter) who is physically or mentally disabled and unable to maintain himself; or (d) his father or mother unable to maintain himself/herself - may be ordered by a Magistrate of the First Class to pay a monthly allowance (no ceiling since 2001), payable from the date of order or, if so ordered, from the date of application. Interim maintenance and expenses of proceedings may be ordered; the application for interim maintenance is to be disposed of within 60 days from service of notice (proviso).
  3. Disqualifications of the wife (s.144(4) BNSS; s.125(4)): she is living in adultery, refuses without sufficient reason to live with her husband, or they are living separately by mutual consent. Wife is entitled to maintenance even if she is living separately for a justifiable reason such as cruelty or second marriage (Chaturbhuj v. Sita Bai, 2008).
  4. Procedure: proceedings may be taken in any district where the person resides, where the wife resides or where he last resided with the wife; evidence recorded in the presence of the person against whom the order is proposed (or his advocate); alteration/cancellation on change of circumstances (s.146; s.127 CrPC); enforcement through warrants to levy the amount as fines and imprisonment up to one month (s.147; s.128 CrPC). The remedy is secular and applies to persons of all religions (Shah Bano, 1985; Danial Latifi v. Union of India, 2001; Shabana Bano v. Imran Khan, 2009; Mohd. Abdul Samad v. State of Telangana, 2024 - a divorced Muslim woman can apply under s.125 CrPC / s.144 BNSS, with the Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act 1986 as an additional remedy).
  5. Rajnesh v. Neha (2021): guidelines to harmonise maintenance proceedings - mandatory affidavit of disclosure of assets, income and liabilities; maintenance from the date of application; set-off; execution; overlapping jurisdiction (Hindu Adoption and Maintenance Act, DV Act, CrPC/BNSS). Bhuwan Mohan Singh v. Meena (2014): speedy disposal; Badshah v. Urmila Badshah Godse (2014): purposive interpretation including de facto wives. Capt. Ramesh Chander Kaushal v. Veena Kaushal (1978): parents' maintenance is a social obligation. [Cross-ref: Family Law.]
  6. Unlawful assemblies (s.148 BNSS; s.129 CrPC): dispersal by an Executive Magistrate or officer in charge of a police station by civil force/command; use of armed forces (s.149; s.130) - Magistrate of highest rank; duty of officers (s.150; s.131); protection against prosecution for acts done in good faith under these sections (s.151; s.132) - sanction needed.
  7. Public nuisances (s.152 BNSS; s.133 CrPC): conditional order to remove nuisance by DM/SDM/Executive Magistrate; if not obeyed, the person may show cause or the order becomes absolute; Vasant Manga Nikumba v. Baburao Bhikanna (1995): nuisance cannot be removed with delay.
  8. Orders in urgent cases of nuisance or apprehended danger (s.163 BNSS; s.144 CrPC): the District Magistrate, Sub-divisional Magistrate or any Executive Magistrate empowered by the State Government may issue a written order directing a person to abstain from a certain act or to take certain order with respect to property where there is sufficient ground for proceeding and immediate prevention or speedy remedy is desirable; may be ex parte; the order remains in force for two months (extendable by the State Government up to six months, and if necessary to prevent danger to human life, health or safety, or a riot or affray); the Magistrate may rescind or alter on application; the order must be reasoned. Cases: Babulal Parate v. State of Maharashtra (1961) - s.144 valid; Madhu Limaye (1970) - power of Executive Magistrate subject to judicial review; Anuradha Bhasin v. Union of India (2020) - orders must be published, proportionate, reasoned and for a limited period; Ramlila Maidan Incident, In re (2012) - right to assemble and right to sleep; Amit Sahni v. Commissioner of Police (2020) - public roads cannot be occupied indefinitely.
  9. Disputes as to immovable property likely to cause a breach of the peace (s.164 BNSS; s.145 CrPC): Executive Magistrate may declare possession; (s.165; s.146) attachment pending decision; (s.166; s.147) disputes regarding right of use of land or water.
  10. Preventive action by police (ss.168-172 BNSS; ss.149-153 CrPC): police may intervene to prevent commission of cognizable offences (s.168); information of design to commit a cognizable offence (s.169); arrest to prevent commission of cognizable offences (s.170; s.151 CrPC) - the person must not be detained beyond 24 hours unless required by any other provision; prevention of injury to public property (s.171; s.152); inspection of weights and measures (s.172; s.153).

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
BNSS s.144 (CrPC s.125)Maintenance of wife, children, parents; interim maintenance within 60 days.
BNSS s.146 (CrPC s.127)Alteration in allowance.
BNSS s.163 (CrPC s.144)Orders in urgent cases of nuisance or apprehended danger; 2 months, extendable to 6.
BNSS s.126 (CrPC s.107)Security for keeping the peace in other cases.
BNSS s.170 (CrPC s.151)Arrest to prevent commission of cognizable offences.

⚖Landmark cases

Mohd. Ahmed Khan v. Shah Bano Begum

1985

s.125 CrPC applies to Muslim divorced wives; secular remedy.

Danial Latifi v. Union of India

2001

Upheld the 1986 Act but read it to provide a fair provision for the divorced wife beyond iddat.

Mohd. Abdul Samad v. State of Telangana

2024

Divorced Muslim women can seek maintenance under s.125 CrPC; the 1986 Act does not oust it.

Rajnesh v. Neha

2021

Guidelines on disclosure affidavit, interim maintenance, date of maintenance.

Babulal Parate v. State of Maharashtra

1961

s.144 valid as reasonable restriction under Art 19.

Madhu Limaye v. SDM, Monghyr

1970

s.144 is not an arbitrary power; Magistrate must act judicially.

Anuradha Bhasin v. Union of India

2020

s.144 orders subject to proportionality, publication and review.

Chaturbhuj v. Sita Bai

2008

Wife living separately for sufficient reason can claim maintenance; provision is a measure of social justice.

✎Memory aids

  • Maintenance beneficiaries: 'W-C-C-P' - Wife, minor Child, disabled adult Child, Parents.
  • s.163 = 2 months / 6 months.

!Exam traps

  • TrapMaintenance cannot be ordered for a married daughter (even a minor married daughter qualifies only through the husband); minor married child is covered only if she herself is unable.
  • TrapDivorced wife is covered until she remarries.
  • Traps.163 BNSS is NOT s.144 CrPC maintenance; maintenance is s.144 BNSS (old s.125). Do not mix the numbers.

?Likely questions

  1. Which BNSS section deals with maintenance of wife, children and parents? (s.144)
  2. Order under s.163 BNSS remains in force for? (2 months, extendable to 6)
  3. Is there a maximum limit on maintenance? (No)
