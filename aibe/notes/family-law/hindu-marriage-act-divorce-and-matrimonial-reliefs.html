Hindu Marriage Act - Matrimonial Reliefs, Divorce Grounds, Mutual Consent and Irretrievable Breakdown

iOverview

Restitution, judicial separation and divorce, each with grounds, special grounds for wives, the S.13B procedure and the Supreme Court's use of Art 142 for irretrievable breakdown.

★Must know

  1. S.9 restitution of conjugal rights: where a spouse has withdrawn from the society of the other without reasonable excuse, the court may decree restitution; the burden of proving reasonable excuse lies on the withdrawing spouse. Constitutional validity upheld in Saroj Rani v Sudarshan Kumar Chadha (1984), overruling T. Sareetha (AP HC, 1983). A fresh challenge (Ojaswa Pathak, 2019) is pending - verify.
  2. S.10 judicial separation: on any ground in S.13(1) (and wife's grounds in S.13(2)); decree can be rescinded on application.
  3. S.13(1) divorce grounds (either spouse): (i) voluntary sexual intercourse with another person (adultery - a single act suffices); (ia) cruelty; (ib) desertion for a continuous period of at least 2 years immediately before the petition; (ii) conversion to another religion; (iii) incurable unsound mind or mental disorder such that the petitioner cannot reasonably be expected to live with respondent; (v) venereal disease in communicable form; (vi) renunciation of the world by entering a religious order; (vii) not heard of as alive for 7 years. Leprosy (clause (iv)) was omitted by the Personal Laws (Amendment) Act 2019.
  4. S.13(1A): either party may seek divorce if there has been no resumption of cohabitation for 1 year after a judicial separation decree, or no restitution for 1 year after a restitution decree.
  5. S.13(2) grounds only for the wife: (i) husband had another wife before the Act and she is alive; (ii) husband guilty of rape, sodomy or bestiality; (iii) no cohabitation for 1 year after a maintenance decree/order under S.18 HAMA or S.125 CrPC (S.144 BNSS); (iv) option of puberty: married before 15 and repudiated before 18.
  6. S.14 bar: no divorce petition within 1 year of marriage unless exceptional hardship to petitioner or exceptional depravity of respondent (court may allow).
  7. Cruelty: physical or mental conduct of such a kind that cohabitation is harmful or injurious. N.G. Dastane v S. Dastane (1975): standard of proof is preponderance of probabilities. Samar Ghosh v Jaya Ghosh (2007): non-exhaustive illustrations of mental cruelty (persistent unjustifiable conduct, unilateral refusal of intercourse, long separation, false allegations, humiliation). Shobha Rani v Madhukar Reddi (1988): intention is not necessary. K. Srinivas Rao v D.A. Deepa (2013): false criminal complaints (e.g. S.498A) can amount to mental cruelty.
  8. Desertion: factum of separation + animus deserendi + absence of consent or justifying cause + statutory period (Bipinchandra Shah v Prabhavati, 1957; Lachman Utamchand Kirpalani v Meena, 1964). Constructive desertion: the spouse who forces the other to leave is the deserter.
  9. S.13B mutual consent: petition jointly by both; living separately for 1 year or more; unable to live together; mutually agreed to dissolve. The court may grant the decree after 6 months and not later than 18 months from presentation, if the petition is not withdrawn. Consent must continue until the decree (Sureshta Devi v Om Prakash, 1991; Hitesh Bhatnagar v Deepa Bhatnagar, 2011).
  10. Amardeep Singh v Harveen Kaur (2017): the 6-month period in S.13B(2) is directory and may be waived where the marriage has broken down irretrievably and no chance of reconciliation exists.
  11. Shilpa Sailesh v Varun Sreenivasan (Constitution Bench, 1 May 2023): the Supreme Court may use Art 142 to dissolve a marriage on the ground of irretrievable breakdown, waiving the 6-month period and the need for separate proceedings; it is a discretionary power, not a right, and is exercised with attention to the duration of marriage, litigation, attempts at settlement and equities. Irretrievable breakdown is still not a statutory ground (Naveen Kohli v Neelu Kohli, 2006 urged Parliament; Law Commission 71st and 217th Reports).
  12. S.15: remarriage after divorce is allowed once the time for appeal has expired (S.28) or the appeal has been dismissed. S.23: decree conditions (no collusion, no condonation, no taking advantage of own wrong; duty to attempt reconciliation).
  13. S.24 interim maintenance and litigation expenses; S.25 permanent alimony (lump sum or periodic, either spouse may claim); S.26 custody, maintenance and education of children (welfare paramount); S.27 disposal of joint property. Kiran Jyot Maini v Anish Pramod Patel (2024): alimony is for support, not to punish or equalise wealth - verify.
  14. Rajnesh v Neha (2021): guidelines on maintenance - disclosure affidavit of assets and liabilities in all proceedings, maintenance from the date of application, overlapping proceedings, criteria for quantum (status, needs, income, liabilities).

⚖Landmark cases

Saroj Rani v Sudarshan Kumar Chadha

1984

S.9 is constitutional; restitution decree is not a violation of privacy or Art 21; T. Sareetha overruled.

N.G. Dastane v S. Dastane

1975

Civil standard of proof (preponderance) applies to matrimonial offences.

Samar Ghosh v Jaya Ghosh

2007

Illustrative guidelines on mental cruelty; breakdown considerations.

Naveen Kohli v Neelu Kohli

2006

Recommended irretrievable breakdown as a statutory ground.

Amardeep Singh v Harveen Kaur

2017

6-month cooling period under S.13B(2) is directory.

Shilpa Sailesh v Varun Sreenivasan

2023

Art 142 can dissolve marriage on irretrievable breakdown; cooling period may be waived; factors and safeguards laid down.

Sureshta Devi v Om Prakash

1991

Mutual consent must subsist at the time of the decree; either party may withdraw before decree.

Rajnesh v Neha

2021

Uniform guidelines on maintenance, affidavit of disclosure, interim maintenance from date of application.

K. Srinivas Rao v D.A. Deepa

2013

Baseless criminal complaints amount to mental cruelty.

✎Memory aids

  • Divorce grounds: A-C-D-C-I-V-R-N = Adultery, Cruelty, Desertion, Conversion, Insanity, Venereal, Renunciation, Not heard (7 yrs).
  • Wife-only: B-R-M-P = Bigamy (pre-1955), Rape/sodomy/bestiality, Maintenance non-resumption, Puberty option.

!Exam traps

  • TrapLeprosy is no longer a ground (omitted 2019); the question may still list it as an option.
  • TrapDesertion period: 2 years; judicial separation/restitution non-compliance: 1 year; not heard of: 7 years.
  • Trap6-month cooling period is directory, not mandatory (Amardeep Singh); Art 142 is available only to the Supreme Court.
  • TrapIrretrievable breakdown is NOT a statutory ground under HMA.
  • TrapAdultery under S.13(1)(i) needs one voluntary act, not a 'living in adultery' course of conduct.
