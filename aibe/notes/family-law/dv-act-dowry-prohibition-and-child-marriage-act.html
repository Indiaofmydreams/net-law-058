iOverview

Protective statutes for women in the family: definitions, reliefs, penalties and the leading cases.

★Must know

  1. Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act 2005 (in force 26 October 2006): civil remedy with a criminal enforcement component. 'Aggrieved person' (S.2(a)) is a woman who is or has been in a domestic relationship with the respondent (marriage, relationship in the nature of marriage, adoption, joint family) and alleges domestic violence. Respondent (S.2(q)): any adult male; the words 'adult male' were struck down in Hiral P. Harsora v Kusum Narottamdas Harsora (2016) - women can be respondents. Domestic violence (S.3) includes physical, sexual, verbal, emotional and economic abuse and harassment for dowry. Shared household (S.2(s)): where the woman lives or has lived in a domestic relationship, whether or not she has title - Satish Chander Ahuja v Sneha Ahuja (2020) overruled S.R. Batra v Taruna Batra (2007) on narrow reading.
  2. Reliefs: protection orders (S.18), residence orders (S.19), monetary relief (S.20), custody orders (S.21), compensation (S.22), interim and ex parte orders (S.23). Application to the Magistrate (S.12); the court shall fix first hearing within 3 days and endeavour to dispose within 60 days (S.12(5)); Protection Officer (S.8-9), service providers (S.10), counselling and in-camera proceedings; breach of a protection order is an offence (S.31) - imprisonment up to 1 year or fine up to Rs 20,000 or both; appeal to Sessions Court in 30 days (S.29). Remedies under the Act are in addition to other laws (S.26). D. Velusamy v D. Patchaiammal (2010) and Indra Sarma v V.K.V. Sarma (2013): 'relationship in the nature of marriage' needs holding out as spouses, legal age to marry, being qualified to marry, voluntary cohabitation for a significant period; a casual or 'keep' relationship is outside the Act.
  3. Dowry Prohibition Act 1961: 'dowry' means property given or agreed to be given directly or indirectly at, before or after marriage in connection with the marriage (S.2); gifts given without demand are outside the definition, subject to list-keeping rules. S.3: giving, taking or abetting dowry - at least 5 years' imprisonment and fine of not less than Rs 15,000 or the value of the dowry, whichever is more; S.4: demanding dowry - 6 months to 2 years and fine up to Rs 10,000; S.4A banned advertisements; S.6 dowry to be transferred to the woman within 3 months, held in trust; S.8: offences are cognizable, non-bailable and non-compoundable; S.8A burden of proof on the person who takes or demands dowry; S.8B Dowry Prohibition Officers.
  4. Cruelty by husband or relatives: S.85 BNS (S.498A IPC) - up to 3 years and fine; S.86 BNS defines cruelty (wilful conduct likely to drive the woman to suicide or cause grave injury, or harassment to coerce her or her relatives to meet unlawful demands for property or valuable security). Dowry death: S.80 BNS (S.304B IPC) - death of a woman within 7 years of marriage by burns or bodily injury or otherwise than under normal circumstances, with cruelty or harassment soon before death in connection with dowry; punishment 7 years to life; presumption under S.118 BSA (S.113B IEA). Abetment of suicide: S.108 BNS (S.306 IPC); S.117 BSA presumption within 7 years of marriage. S.82 BNS - bigamy.
  5. Arnesh Kumar v State of Bihar (2014): police must not arrest automatically for offences punishable up to 7 years; apply S.41 CrPC (S.35 BNSS) and record reasons. Preeti Gupta v State of Jharkhand (2010) and Dara Lakshmi Narayana v State of Telangana (2024): warn against misuse by exaggerated or omnibus allegations; Rajesh Sharma v State of U.P. (2017) (Family Welfare Committees) was modified by Social Action Forum for Manav Adhikar v Union of India (2018). Rupali Devi v State of U.P. (2019): the court at the wife's refuge place has territorial jurisdiction over mental cruelty offences. Satvir Singh v State of Punjab (2001) and Kans Raj v State of Punjab (2000): 'soon before death' means a proximate and live link, no fixed period.
  6. Prohibition of Child Marriage Act 2006: child = male under 21, female under 18. Child marriage is voidable at the option of the child party, by petition to the district court within 2 years after attaining majority (S.3); void in certain cases (S.12: child taken away from guardian by enticement or force, sold for marriage, or trafficked for immoral purposes). S.4: maintenance and residence to the female contracting party until remarriage; S.5-6: custody, maintenance and legitimacy of children. Punishments: S.9 adult male above 18 marrying a child, S.10 solemnising, S.11 promoting or permitting - rigorous imprisonment up to 2 years and fine up to Rs 1 lakh (no imprisonment for a woman for S.9-10). S.13 injunction power; S.15 offences cognizable and non-bailable; Child Marriage Prohibition Officers (S.16). Independent Thought v Union of India (2017): sexual intercourse with a wife aged 15-18 is rape (Exception 2 read down); BNS S.63 exception 2 now sets the age at 18. Marital rape of an adult wife is still excepted (challenge pending in the Supreme Court - verify).
  7. Other: Surrogacy (Regulation) Act 2021 (altruistic surrogacy only, intending married couple, five years of marriage and infertility certificate; woman 25-50 and man 26-55 years) and the Assisted Reproductive Technology (Regulation) Act 2021 - peripheral; the Supreme Court in 2025 relaxed the age limits for couples who had begun the process before the Act (verify).

⚖Landmark cases

Hiral P. Harsora v Kusum Narottamdas Harsora

2016

'Adult male' in S.2(q) DV Act struck down; women can be respondents.

Satish Chander Ahuja v Sneha Ahuja

2020

Shared household is not limited to property owned by the husband; overrules S.R. Batra.

D. Velusamy v D. Patchaiammal

2010

Conditions for a relationship in the nature of marriage.

Arnesh Kumar v State of Bihar

2014

No routine arrest in S.498A cases; magistrate must verify.

Independent Thought v Union of India

2017

Exception 2 to S.375 IPC read down: sex with wife under 18 is rape.

Satvir Singh v State of Punjab

2001

'Soon before' dowry death means proximate link, no fixed time.

✎Memory aids

  • DV reliefs 18-22: Protection - Residence - Money - Custody - Compensation.
  • 304B = 7 to life; 498A = up to 3 years.

!Exam traps

  • TrapDV Act reliefs are civil, but breach of a protection order is a criminal offence.
  • TrapDV Act: respondents can be women (relatives) after Harsora.
  • TrapDowry Act S.3 minimum is 5 years (not 6 months).
  • TrapDowry death: 7 years minimum, may extend to life.
  • TrapPCMA: marriage is voidable (not void) at the option of the child party, except S.12 cases.
