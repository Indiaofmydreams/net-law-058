iOverview

Art 44, judicial calls for a UCC, and the state UCC laws (Uttarakhand, Gujarat, Assam) are active current-affairs hooks.

★Must know

  1. Art 44 (DPSP): the State shall endeavour to secure a UCC for citizens throughout India. Art 25-26 protect religious freedom; personal laws and Part III: State of Bombay v Narasu Appa Mali (1952) held personal laws are not 'laws in force' under Art 13; contrary views in later cases. Shayara Bano (2017) tested triple talaq under Part III; Sabarimala (2018) and Joseph Shine (2018) show a pro-equality trend.
  2. Judicial calls for UCC: Shah Bano (1985), Sarla Mudgal (1995), John Vallamattom (2003), Jorden Diengdeh (1985). Law Commission 21st Report (2018): UCC neither necessary nor desirable at this stage; the 22nd Law Commission invited views in 2023.
  3. Uttarakhand Uniform Civil Code Act 2024: passed February 2024 and operationalised 27 January 2025 - first state UCC. Covers marriage age 21/18, mandatory registration of marriages and live-in relationships, ban on polygamy and halala, equal intestate succession (Class I heirs), and rights of children of live-in relationships; excludes Scheduled Tribes. (Verify current rules and amendments.)
  4. Gujarat UCC 2026: tabled in the Assembly in March 2026 and passed in early April 2026; the President's assent was reported - verify the commencement date. Follows the Uttarakhand framework with stricter penalties for non-registration (reported).
  5. Assam UCC Bill 2026: passed by the Assembly in the last week of May 2026 (reported as the third state); assent and commencement - verify. Largely similar framework; tribal and constitutional carve-outs.
  6. Common features of state UCCs (as reported): marriage age 21/18; ban on polygamy; mandatory registration of marriage; live-in registration; equal intestate share for sons and daughters; Scheduled Tribes excluded; wills unaffected. Constitutional questions raised: legislative competence (marriage and succession are in the Concurrent List, Entry 5, so states can legislate subject to Art 254 and Presidential assent) and religious freedom (Art 25).
  7. Goa: Portuguese Civil Code of 1867 is the long-standing example of a common civil code.
  8. Triple talaq, Shayara Bano and the 2019 Act, as well as the Waqf Amendment Act 2025, are the key central statutory/judicial developments touching Muslim personal law.

!Exam traps

  • TrapArt 44 is a Directive Principle, not enforceable.
  • TrapMarriage and succession are in the Concurrent List, so states may enact UCCs with Presidential assent where they conflict with central law.
  • TrapUttarakhand was first (in force 27 Jan 2025); Goa has an older common code.
