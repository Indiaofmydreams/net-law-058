Hindu Succession Act 1956 (as amended 2005) and the Joint Family

iOverview

Intestate succession of Hindu males and females, daughters' coparcenary rights (S.6), woman's absolute ownership (S.14), and the character of property inherited under S.8.

★Must know

  1. Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act 2005 (in force 9 September 2005): S.6 makes a daughter a coparcener by birth in the same manner as a son, with the same rights and liabilities; S.23 (restriction on daughter's right to a dwelling house) and S.24 (widow's remarriage disqualification) omitted; S.4(2) (agricultural land exemption) omitted.
  2. Vineeta Sharma v Rakesh Sharma (2020, three judges): the daughter's coparcenary right is by birth; it is not necessary that the father was alive on 9 September 2005; Prakash v Phulavati (2016) overruled to that extent; Danamma @ Suman Surpur v Amar (2018) partly overruled. A partition claimed by oral evidence is not accepted unless supported by public documents or a court decree (partition is a registered deed or a decree; S.6(5) Explanation). Daughters have equal rights in Mitakshara coparcenary property and can claim partition.
  3. Arunachala Gounder v Ponnusamy (2022): self-acquired property of a Hindu male dying intestate (including before 1956) devolves by inheritance; a daughter inherits it, and on her death without issue it goes to her father's heirs (S.15(2)(b)).
  4. S.8 (intestate male): property devolves first on Class I heirs, then Class II, then agnates, then cognates. Class I (16 heirs after 2005): son, daughter, widow, mother, son and daughter of a predeceased son, son and daughter of a predeceased daughter, widow of a predeceased son, widow of a predeceased son of a predeceased son and the four added in 2005 (children of predeceased daughter of predeceased daughter and children of predeceased son of predeceased daughter, etc.). Father is not Class I - he is the first entry in Class II (so mother inherits, father does not, unless no Class I heir exists).
  5. S.10 distribution among Class I: the widow(s) together take one share, each son one, each daughter one, each branch of a predeceased son/daughter one share jointly, mother one share. S.9: Class I heirs take simultaneously and exclude all others.
  6. S.14 woman's property: any property possessed by a female Hindu, acquired before or after the Act, is held by her as full owner and not as a limited owner. S.14(2) exception: property acquired by gift, will, instrument, decree or award where the instrument expressly prescribes a restricted estate. V. Tulasamma v Sesha Reddy (1977): property given to a widow in lieu of maintenance becomes absolute under S.14(1).
  7. S.15 (intestate female): first sons, daughters (and children of predeceased children) and the husband; then heirs of the husband; then mother and father; then heirs of the father; then heirs of the mother. S.15(2): property inherited from her father or mother, in absence of children, goes to the father's heirs; property inherited from her husband or father-in-law, in absence of children, goes to the husband's heirs.
  8. Disqualifications: S.25 murderer; S.26 descendants of a convert (the convert himself is not disqualified); S.27 other disqualifications; disease, defect and unchastity are not grounds (S.28). S.30 allows testamentary disposition of coparcenary interest.
  9. Joint family and coparcenary: HUF = all lineal descendants of a common ancestor, wives and unmarried daughters; coparcenary = the narrower body of the propositus and three generations of male lineal descendants (now daughters included by birth). Mitakshara - rights by birth and survivorship (modified); Dayabhaga - rights by death, no right by birth. Karta: the manager; alienation of coparcenary property only for legal necessity, benefit of the estate or indispensable duties (Hanooman Prasad Pandey v Babooee Munraj Koonwari, 1856 PC); gifts only of a small portion for pious purposes (Kamla Devi v Bachulal Gupta, 1957). A woman can be karta (Sujata Sharma v Manu Gupta, Delhi HC, 2015).
  10. Darubai v Kamalabai (2026 INSC 613, reported 3 June 2026; Karol and Masih JJ): property inherited under S.8 does not acquire coparcenary character; the heirs take as tenants in common with defined shares; karta-ship does not arise from mere descent from a paternal ancestor; one heir cannot sell the shares of the others as karta. It follows Yudhishter v Ashok Kumar (1987) and Uttam v Saubhag Singh (2016). The Court also (2026) reportedly held that the 2005 amendment does not limit daughters' pre-existing inheritance rights - verify.
  11. S.6(4) after 2005: no pious obligation of a son or grandson to pay a father's or grandfather's debts, but debts contracted before 2005 are protected. S.6(3): on a coparcener's death after 2005 the interest devolves by testamentary or intestate succession, not survivorship.

⚖Landmark cases

Vineeta Sharma v Rakesh Sharma

2020

Daughter is coparcener by birth; father's survival on 9 Sept 2005 not required; oral partition not accepted without proof.

Prakash v Phulavati

2016

Earlier held the amendment was prospective and needed father alive in 2005 - overruled on this point.

V. Tulasamma v Sesha Reddy

1977

S.14(1) enlarges a widow's maintenance-estate into absolute ownership.

Arunachala Gounder v Ponnusamy

2022

Daughter inherits the self-acquired property of her father who died intestate before 1956; Mitakshara principles and S.15 reasoning.

Yudhishter v Ashok Kumar

1987

Property inherited by son from father under S.8 is his separate property, not HUF property.

Darubai v Kamalabai

2026

S.8-inherited property is not coparcenary; co-heirs hold shares; no karta power to alienate others' shares.

✎Memory aids

  • Class I: 'Son-Daughter-Widow-Mother' plus the predeceased branches; Father = Class II no.1.
  • V-S-R: Vineeta Sharma = Rights by birth.

!Exam traps

  • TrapFather is NOT a Class I heir; mother is.
  • TrapDaughters' right is by birth; the father need not be alive in 2005 (Vineeta Sharma overrules Prakash).
  • TrapS.14(1) gives absolute ownership; S.14(2) restricts only if the instrument prescribes a restricted estate.
  • TrapThe convert is not disqualified; the convert's descendants are (S.26).
  • TrapA Hindu female's inherited property goes back to the source family (S.15(2)) when she has no issue.

?Likely questions

  1. Which of the following is a Class I heir?
  2. Whether a daughter's coparcenary right requires the father to be alive in 2005.
  3. Devolution of a Hindu female's property (S.15).
  4. Nature of property inherited under S.8 (separate vs coparcenary).
  5. Effect of S.14.
