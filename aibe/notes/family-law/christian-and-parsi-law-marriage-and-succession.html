Christian and Parsi Law - Marriage, Divorce, Succession

iOverview

Indian Christian Marriage Act 1872, Indian Divorce Act 1869, Indian Succession Act 1925 (Christians and Parsis) and the Parsi Marriage and Divorce Act 1936.

★Must know

  1. Indian Christian Marriage Act 1872: applies where at least one party is a Christian; marriage is solemnised by a minister of the church, a clergyman, a Marriage Registrar or a licensed person; marriage age: groom 21 and bride 18; notice and publication requirements; marriage registered. A marriage solemnised in breach is void in certain cases.
  2. Indian Divorce Act 1869 (applies where the petitioner or respondent professes Christianity): S.10 divorce grounds (adultery, cruelty, desertion for 2 years, unsound mind, venereal disease, conversion/failure to comply with restitution decree, wife's grounds including rape, sodomy, bestiality). The 2001 Amendment (Indian Divorce (Amendment) Act 2001) equalised grounds for husband and wife (earlier, the wife needed adultery plus another ground - struck down in Ammini E.J. v Union of India, Kerala HC 1995); it inserted S.10A: divorce by mutual consent after separation for 2 years or more (compare 1 year under HMA and SMA). Leprosy was omitted from the 1869 Act by the 2019 Act - verify.
  3. Alimony under the Divorce Act: S.36 interim alimony and S.37 permanent alimony (up to 1/5th of husband's average income); custody S.41.
  4. Christian intestate succession (ISA 1925, Part V, Chapter II): widow takes 1/3 of the estate and lineal descendants take the remaining 2/3 (S.33(a)); if there are no lineal descendants but there are kindred, widow takes 1/2 and kindred 1/2 (S.33(b)); if there are no descendants or kindred, the widow takes the entire estate (S.33(c)); children take equally; no preference of sons over daughters. Mary Roy v State of Kerala (1986): Travancore Christian Succession Act 1916 was superseded by the ISA 1925; daughters now share equally with sons.
  5. Wills (ISA 1925, Part VI): S.59 competent testator (sound mind, not minor); S.63 execution - signed by testator (or by another in his presence and by his direction) with a mark if illiterate, and attested by two or more witnesses, each of whom has seen the testator sign; wills of a Christian or any non-Hindu are governed by ISA irrespective of religion. S.118 (bequest to religious or charitable uses within a certain time before death) was struck down in John Vallamattom v Union of India (2003) as violating Art 14 and 25.
  6. Parsi Marriage and Divorce Act 1936: S.3 conditions - monogamy, no prohibited relationship, age 21 for male and 18 for female, solemnised by a priest (Dastur) in the presence of two Parsi witnesses (Ashirvad ceremony); S.4 bigamy void and punishable (S.5); a Parsi who ceases to be a Parsi by conversion may be sued for divorce (S.32(j)).
  7. Parsi divorce (S.32): (a) non-consummation within 1 year due to wilful refusal; (b) unsound mind at the time of marriage and continuing; (c) wife pregnant by another at marriage; (d) adultery, fornication, bigamy, rape or unnatural offence by respondent; (dd) cruelty; (e) hurt/venereal disease; (f) husband guilty of rape; (g) imprisonment of 7 years or more; (h) desertion for 2 years; (i) failure to comply with a decree for separate maintenance for 1 year; (j) conversion; S.32B mutual consent after 1 year of marriage. Matrimonial courts consist of a judge with Parsi delegates.
  8. Parsi intestate succession (ISA 1925 S.50-56): widow and children take equal shares (a widow takes a share equal to each child); parents take a half share of a child's share; no distinction between sons and daughters; rules differ if the intestate is a Parsi woman (husband or widower takes a child-equal share).
  9. Jews and others: Indian Succession Act governs; Jewish law is uncodified and applied as custom.

S.118 ISA struck down as discriminatory against Christians.

✎Memory aids

  • Christian widow: 1/3 - 1/2 - all (descendants - kindred - none).

!Exam traps

  • TrapChristian widow gets 1/3 (not 1/4) where there are descendants.
  • TrapChristian mutual consent requires 2 years' separation (not 1).
  • TrapParsi marriage needs a Parsi priest and two Parsi witnesses.
