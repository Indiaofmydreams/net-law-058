Muslim Law III - Gifts (Hiba), Wills (Wasiyat), Waqf, Pre-emption, Inheritance

iOverview

Property law of Muslims: hiba needs delivery of possession; wills are limited to one-third; waqf is a pious endowment now governed by the Waqf Act 1995 as amended in 2025; pre-emption has three demands; inheritance has no representation.

★Must know

  1. Hiba (gift): requisites - (i) declaration by donor, (ii) acceptance by donee (express or implied), (iii) delivery of possession (actual or constructive). Writing and registration are not required for a Muslim gift (S.129 TPA preserves the rule); a registered deed without delivery is not enough. Gift of future property is void; gift of undivided share (musha) in a divisible property is irregular under Hanafi law but valid in indivisible property. Gift by a donor to his minor child may be completed by declaration if the donor is the guardian (Hafeeza Bibi v Shaikh Mohammad Farooq, 2011 - possession/declaration). Gift is revocable by the donor (except in cases such as gift to a relative within prohibited degrees, gift by husband to wife, gift for consideration). Hiba-bil-iwaz (gift for consideration) is a sale in effect; hiba-ba-shart-ul-iwaz is a gift with a condition of return; sadaqah is a gift for pious purposes.
  2. Marz-ul-maut (death-illness): a gift made while apprehending death from the illness that actually causes death is governed by the rules of wills - limited to one-third of the net estate, and a gift to an heir needs the consent of the other heirs; delivery required.
  3. Wills (wasiyat): Sunni - a bequest is valid only up to one-third of the net estate (after funeral expenses and debts) without the consent of heirs; a bequest to an heir needs the consent of the other heirs (given after the testator's death); a will may be oral; consent of heirs not needed for bequest to a stranger up to 1/3. Shia - bequest to an heir up to 1/3 is valid without the consent of other heirs; a bequest in excess of 1/3 needs ratification. The bequest to a person not in existence is void unless the person is born within six months. A bequest in favour of a murderer-legatee is invalid (Sunni).
  4. Waqf: permanent dedication of property by a person professing Islam for a purpose recognised as religious, pious or charitable (S.3(r) Waqf Act 1995). Modes: declaration (oral or written), usage (waqf by user - prospectively removed by the 2025 amendment), endowment by will (within 1/3), mortgage/ immemorial usage. Waqf-alal-aulad (family waqf) is valid under the Mussalman Wakf Validating Act 1913, provided the ultimate benefit is for the poor. The mutawalli is a manager, not an owner (can be removed for breach). Waqf Board and Tribunal supervise. Property once waqf is inalienable.
  5. Waqf (Amendment) Act 2025 (renames the 1995 Act as the Unified Waqf Management, Empowerment, Efficiency and Development Act): in an interim order of 15 September 2025 (2025 INSC 1116, CJI Gavai and Masih J), the Supreme Court did not stay the Act as a whole but stayed: the requirement that a waqf donor must have practised Islam for 5 years (until rules prescribe a determination process); the proviso to S.3C(2) (property under inquiry not treated as waqf) and S.3C(3)-(4) (officer to correct revenue records), pending a Waqf Tribunal decision. It capped non-Muslim members at 4 of 22 in the Central Waqf Council and 3 of 11 in a State Waqf Board. It did not stay the removal of 'waqf by user' (prospective), S.3D (protected monuments), S.3E (Scheduled Tribes land), mandatory deed and registration, and the application of the Limitation Act. Final decision: pending in the sources reviewed - verify.
  6. Pre-emption (shufa): the right of a co-owner or neighbour to purchase property sold to a stranger by substituting himself for the buyer. Classes: shafi-i-sharik (co-sharer), shafi-i-khalit (participator in appendages/immunities such as a right of way), shafi-i-jar (adjoining neighbour). Three demands: talab-i-mowasibat (immediate demand), talab-i-ishhad (demand with witnesses), talab-i-khusumat (filing suit). Pre-emption is a weak right, defeated by any legitimate means (Bishan Singh v Khazan Singh, 1958); Bhau Ram v Baij Nath Singh (1962) upheld pre-emption on ground of vicinage as a reasonable restriction (then Art 19(1)(f)). Hindu and Christian law do not recognise pre-emption by custom except where custom exists.
  7. Inheritance (Sunni/Hanafi): three classes - Sharers (Quranic heirs; 12: husband, wife, daughter, son's daughter, father, mother, true grandfather, true grandmother, full sister, consanguine sister, uterine brother, uterine sister), Residuaries (agnatic, e.g. son, father, brother), Distant kindred (dhawul arham). There is no representation (a grandchild is excluded if a son of the deceased is alive) and no distinction of birth rights. Wife: 1/8 with children, 1/4 without; husband: 1/4 with children, 1/2 without; mother: 1/6 with children or two or more siblings, otherwise 1/3; father: 1/6 with son; daughter alone 1/2, two or more 2/3 jointly; a daughter with a son becomes residuary and takes half of the son's share. Doctrines: awl (proportionate reduction when shares exceed unity) and radd (return of surplus to sharers). Shias do not follow the Asaba (residuary) system; they distribute among three classes of heirs by nearness of relationship. A murderer is disqualified (Sunni); apostasy and conversion no longer disqualify (Caste Disabilities Removal Act 1850).
  8. Women's property: Muslim women own and dispose of property absolutely and independently of the husband; a wife's mahr is a debt.

⚖Landmark cases

Hafeeza Bibi v Shaikh Mohammad Farooq

2011

Gift under Muslim law requires declaration, acceptance and delivery of possession; registration not necessary.

Bishan Singh v Khazan Singh

1958

Pre-emption is a weak right; the pre-emptor must show the right at all stages.

Bhau Ram v Baij Nath Singh

1962

Pre-emption on ground of vicinage is a reasonable restriction.

Waqf (Amendment) Act 2025 interim order

2025

Selective stay: 5-year practising-Muslim clause and part of S.3C stayed; non-Muslim members capped; Act not stayed as a whole.

✎Memory aids

  • Hiba = DAD (Declaration, Acceptance, Delivery).
  • Pre-emption: Immediate - Witness - Suit.

!Exam traps

  • TrapA Muslim gift needs possession; a registered deed alone does not validate it.
  • TrapWill: 1/3 limit; bequest to heirs needs consent of the other heirs (Sunni).
  • TrapPre-emption has three demands - immediate, with witnesses, and suit.
  • TrapNo representation in Muslim inheritance.
  • TrapWaqf is not a trust; mutawalli is manager, not owner.
