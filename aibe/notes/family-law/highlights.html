Hindu Marriage Act 1955 - Validity of Marriage, Void and Voidable Marriages, Children

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

S.5, 7, 8 HMAConditions, ceremonies, registration.
S.11, 12 HMAVoid and voidable marriages.
S.16 HMALegitimacy of children of void/voidable marriages.
S.17 HMA; BNS S.82Bigamy.
S.18 HMAPunishment for contravention of S.5(iii), (iv), (v).

Cases in one line

  • Bhaurao Shankar Lokhande v State of Maharashtra 1965 Second marriage must be performed with the essential ceremonies for bigamy; invocation of saptapadi where it is part of the rites.
  • Priya Bala Ghosh v Suresh Chandra Ghosh 1971 Saptapadi is essential only where the customary rites include it.
  • Dolly Rani v Manish Kumar Chanchal 2024 A Hindu marriage needs valid ceremonies; a certificate of registration does not validate a marriage without them; marriage is a sacrament, not a commercial event.
  • Sarla Mudgal v Union of India 1995 A Hindu husband who converts to Islam to marry again is still guilty of bigamy; the first marriage subsists until dissolved under HMA.
  • Lily Thomas v Union of India 2000 Reaffirmed Sarla Mudgal; conversion does not end a subsisting Hindu marriage.
  • Revanasiddappa v Mallikarjun 2011 / 2023 Children of void or voidable marriages can claim a share in their parents' property.
  • Joseph Shine v Union of India 2018 Adultery (S.497 IPC) struck down as violating Arts 14, 15, 21.

Memory aids

  • V-O-I-D = (i) bigamy, (iv) prohibited degrees, (v) sapinda: 1-4-5 void.
  • Voidable = Impotence, Insanity (S.5(ii)), Intimidation/fraud, Illicit pregnancy.

Exam traps

  • TrapAge breach does not make an HMA marriage void; PCMA makes it voidable at the child's option.
  • TrapS.11 void marriages: only breaches of S.5(i), (iv), (v). S.5(ii) breach is voidable.
  • TrapRegistration is evidentiary, not a validity condition.
  • TrapChildren of void marriages are legitimate but inherit only from their parents.

Hindu Marriage Act - Matrimonial Reliefs, Divorce Grounds, Mutual Consent and Irretrievable Breakdown

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

S.9, 10, 13, 13B, 14, 15 HMARestitution, separation, divorce, mutual consent, bars.
S.24-27 HMAInterim maintenance, permanent alimony, children, property.
Art 142Complete justice power used to dissolve marriages (Shilpa Sailesh).

Cases in one line

  • Saroj Rani v Sudarshan Kumar Chadha 1984 S.9 is constitutional; restitution decree is not a violation of privacy or Art 21; T. Sareetha overruled.
  • N.G. Dastane v S. Dastane 1975 Civil standard of proof (preponderance) applies to matrimonial offences.
  • Samar Ghosh v Jaya Ghosh 2007 Illustrative guidelines on mental cruelty; breakdown considerations.
  • Naveen Kohli v Neelu Kohli 2006 Recommended irretrievable breakdown as a statutory ground.
  • Amardeep Singh v Harveen Kaur 2017 6-month cooling period under S.13B(2) is directory.
  • Shilpa Sailesh v Varun Sreenivasan 2023 Art 142 can dissolve marriage on irretrievable breakdown; cooling period may be waived; factors and safeguards laid down.
  • Sureshta Devi v Om Prakash 1991 Mutual consent must subsist at the time of the decree; either party may withdraw before decree.
  • Rajnesh v Neha 2021 Uniform guidelines on maintenance, affidavit of disclosure, interim maintenance from date of application.
  • K. Srinivas Rao v D.A. Deepa 2013 Baseless criminal complaints amount to mental cruelty.

Memory aids

  • Divorce grounds: A-C-D-C-I-V-R-N = Adultery, Cruelty, Desertion, Conversion, Insanity, Venereal, Renunciation, Not heard (7 yrs).
  • Wife-only: B-R-M-P = Bigamy (pre-1955), Rape/sodomy/bestiality, Maintenance non-resumption, Puberty option.

Exam traps

  • TrapLeprosy is no longer a ground (omitted 2019); the question may still list it as an option.
  • TrapDesertion period: 2 years; judicial separation/restitution non-compliance: 1 year; not heard of: 7 years.
  • Trap6-month cooling period is directory, not mandatory (Amardeep Singh); Art 142 is available only to the Supreme Court.
  • TrapIrretrievable breakdown is NOT a statutory ground under HMA.
  • TrapAdultery under S.13(1)(i) needs one voluntary act, not a 'living in adultery' course of conduct.

Maintenance Across Laws - HAMA, S.144 BNSS (S.125 CrPC), Muslim Women Acts, Senior Citizens Act

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

S.144-147 BNSS (S.125-128 CrPC)Maintenance of wives, children and parents; enforcement.
S.18-23 HAMAWife, widowed daughter-in-law, children, parents, dependants.
S.24-25 HMAInterim and permanent alimony.
S.3, 4, 5 Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act 1986Iddat maintenance; relatives/Waqf Board; option for S.125.

Cases in one line

  • Mohd Ahmed Khan v Shah Bano Begum 1985 S.125 CrPC applies to Muslim wives; mahr is not a substitute for maintenance.
  • Danial Latifi v Union of India 2001 1986 Act valid; 'reasonable and fair provision' covers the woman's entire future.
  • Mohd Abdul Samad v State of Telangana 2024 A divorced Muslim woman can invoke S.125 CrPC (S.144 BNSS) irrespective of the 1986 Act; secular remedy.
  • Rajnesh v Neha 2021 Guidelines on affidavit of disclosure, interim maintenance, quantum.
  • X v Y (2025 INSC 197) 2025 S.25 HMA maintenance available to a spouse in a void marriage; discretionary.
  • Chanmuniya v Virendra Kumar Singh Kushwaha 2010 'Wife' in S.125 given a broad meaning; live-in couples can be treated as married for maintenance; larger bench reference.
  • Chaturbhuj v Sita Bai 2008 Wife with insufficient independent income can claim S.125.

Memory aids

  • Shah Bano - Statute response (1986) - Danial saves it - Samad (2024) settles it.

Exam traps

  • TrapS.125 (S.144 BNSS) has no upper monetary cap.
  • TrapA divorced wife who has remarried loses S.125 rights; one who has not remarried retains them.
  • TrapA wife living in adultery or living separately by mutual consent cannot claim.
  • TrapShah Bano (1985) was legislatively overridden by the 1986 Act, but Danial Latifi read it down and Abdul Samad (2024) confirmed S.125 for all.
  • TrapHAMA S.19 (daughter-in-law) is against the father-in-law, not the husband's relatives generally.

Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act 1956 - Adoption; Adoption under JJ Act 2015

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

S.6-12 HAMAConditions, capacity, effects of adoption.
S.56-73 JJ Act 2015Secular adoption; CARA.

Cases in one line

  • Shabnam Hashmi v Union of India 2014 The JJ Act adoption right is available irrespective of religion; right to adopt is a facet of Art 21 interpretation, not a fundamental right.
  • Lakshmi Kant Pandey v Union of India 1984 Safeguards for inter-country adoption; best interests of the child.
  • Sawan Ram v Kalawati 1967 Adoption requires actual giving and taking; evidence.

Memory aids

  • S.7 male + wife's consent; S.8 female unmarried or ex-married; S.9 giver; S.10 adoptee; S.11 other conditions; S.12 effects.

Exam traps

  • TrapA Hindu mother alone cannot give in adoption when the father is alive and a Hindu of sound mind.
  • TrapDatta homam is not essential for validity.
  • TrapAdoptee age limit is 15 unless custom; adopter 21-year gap applies only to opposite-sex adoption.
  • TrapA valid adoption cannot be cancelled (S.15).

Guardianship and Custody - Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act 1956; Guardians and Wards Act 1890

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

S.6-13 HMGANatural guardians, powers, welfare principle.
S.7, 17, 25 GWA 1890Appointment, welfare, return of ward.

Cases in one line

  • Gita Hariharan v Reserve Bank of India 1999 Mother is natural guardian even in the father's lifetime if he is absent/indifferent; 'after' read as 'in absence of'.
  • ABC v State (NCT of Delhi) 2015 Unwed mother can be the guardian of her child without disclosing or notifying the father.
  • Roxann Sharma v Arun Sharma 2015 Mother has a preferential claim to custody of child below 5.
  • Nil Ratan Kundu v Abhijit Kundu 2008 Welfare of the child is paramount; strict legal rights give way.

Memory aids

  • Father, then mother - but mother for under-5 custody; welfare is king (S.13).

Exam traps

  • TrapWelfare principle prevails over the statutory order of natural guardians.
  • TrapA natural guardian's sale of minor's immovable property without court permission is voidable (not void).
  • TrapCustody under 5 is 'ordinarily' with the mother, not an absolute rule.
  • TrapMarried girl: husband is the natural guardian.

Hindu Succession Act 1956 (as amended 2005) and the Joint Family

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

S.6, 8, 9, 10, 14, 15, 25, 26 HSACoparcenary, intestate succession, woman's property, disqualifications.

Cases in one line

  • Vineeta Sharma v Rakesh Sharma 2020 Daughter is coparcener by birth; father's survival on 9 Sept 2005 not required; oral partition not accepted without proof.
  • Prakash v Phulavati 2016 Earlier held the amendment was prospective and needed father alive in 2005 - overruled on this point.
  • V. Tulasamma v Sesha Reddy 1977 S.14(1) enlarges a widow's maintenance-estate into absolute ownership.
  • Arunachala Gounder v Ponnusamy 2022 Daughter inherits the self-acquired property of her father who died intestate before 1956; Mitakshara principles and S.15 reasoning.
  • Yudhishter v Ashok Kumar 1987 Property inherited by son from father under S.8 is his separate property, not HUF property.
  • Darubai v Kamalabai 2026 S.8-inherited property is not coparcenary; co-heirs hold shares; no karta power to alienate others' shares.

Memory aids

  • Class I: 'Son-Daughter-Widow-Mother' plus the predeceased branches; Father = Class II no.1.
  • V-S-R: Vineeta Sharma = Rights by birth.

Exam traps

  • TrapFather is NOT a Class I heir; mother is.
  • TrapDaughters' right is by birth; the father need not be alive in 2005 (Vineeta Sharma overrules Prakash).
  • TrapS.14(1) gives absolute ownership; S.14(2) restricts only if the instrument prescribes a restricted estate.
  • TrapThe convert is not disqualified; the convert's descendants are (S.26).
  • TrapA Hindu female's inherited property goes back to the source family (S.15(2)) when she has no issue.

Muslim Law I - Sources, Marriage (Nikah), Mahr, Maintenance

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

S.2, 3 Shariat Application Act 1937Application of Muslim personal law; declaration.
Prohibition of Child Marriage Act 2006Applies in principle to all communities.

Cases in one line

  • Abdul Kadir v Salima 1886 Dower is an obligation on the husband, not consideration for marriage.
  • Hamira Bibi v Zubaida Bibi 1916 Widow's right of retention is not a right of ownership; cannot be transferred.
  • Shamim Ara v State of U.P. 2002 Talaq must be pronounced for a reason and communicated; proof required; an averment in a written statement is not talaq.

Memory aids

  • 10-Hanafi, 3-Maliki dirhams.
  • Irregular = curable; Void = incurable.

Exam traps

  • TrapMahr is not consideration; it is an obligation.
  • TrapHanafi minimum is 10 dirhams, Maliki is 3.
  • TrapShia law does not need witnesses for nikah; Sunni law does.
  • TrapMuta marriage is recognised only by Ithna Ashari Shias.
  • TrapWidow's lien is not ownership.

Muslim Law II - Divorce: Talaq, Khula, Mubarat, DMMA 1939, 2019 Act

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

S.2, 4, 5 DMMA 1939Grounds, apostasy, dower rights.
S.3, 4, 7 Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Marriage) Act 2019Triple talaq void, punishment, cognizability and bail.

Cases in one line

  • Shayara Bano v Union of India 2017 Instant triple talaq is unconstitutional/void (3:2).
  • Rukia Khatoon v Abdul Khalique Laskar 1981 Talaq must be for reasonable cause and preceded by attempts at reconciliation.
  • Shamim Ara v State of U.P. 2002 Talaq must be communicated and proved; a plea in written statement is not talaq.
  • Jorden Diengdeh v S.S. Chopra 1985 Urged a UCC; commented on personal law inconsistencies.
  • Sarla Mudgal v Union of India 1995 Conversion to Islam to marry a second wife does not dissolve the first Hindu marriage; urged UCC.

Memory aids

  • DMMA S.2 numbers: 4-2-7-3-2 (missing, maintenance, jail, obligations, insanity).
  • Shayara = 3:2, 2017; Act = 2019; max 3 years.

Exam traps

  • TrapTalaq-ul-biddat is void since 2017; talaq-e-hasan and ahsan are still lawful (hasan under challenge).
  • TrapThe 2019 Act punishes the husband (3 years) but does not make talaq-e-hasan an offence.
  • TrapIddat for a widow is 4 months 10 days, not 3 months.
  • TrapDMMA gives the wife a right to apply; the husband has no DMMA remedy.
  • TrapApostasy does not automatically dissolve the marriage (DMMA S.4).

Muslim Law III - Gifts (Hiba), Wills (Wasiyat), Waqf, Pre-emption, Inheritance

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

S.129 TPASaves Muslim gift rules.
S.3(r) Waqf Act 1995 (amended 2025)Definition of waqf.
Mussalman Wakf Validating Act 1913Validity of family waqf.

Cases in one line

  • Hafeeza Bibi v Shaikh Mohammad Farooq 2011 Gift under Muslim law requires declaration, acceptance and delivery of possession; registration not necessary.
  • Bishan Singh v Khazan Singh 1958 Pre-emption is a weak right; the pre-emptor must show the right at all stages.
  • Bhau Ram v Baij Nath Singh 1962 Pre-emption on ground of vicinage is a reasonable restriction.
  • Waqf (Amendment) Act 2025 interim order 2025 Selective stay: 5-year practising-Muslim clause and part of S.3C stayed; non-Muslim members capped; Act not stayed as a whole.

Memory aids

  • Hiba = DAD (Declaration, Acceptance, Delivery).
  • Pre-emption: Immediate - Witness - Suit.

Exam traps

  • TrapA Muslim gift needs possession; a registered deed alone does not validate it.
  • TrapWill: 1/3 limit; bequest to heirs needs consent of the other heirs (Sunni).
  • TrapPre-emption has three demands - immediate, with witnesses, and suit.
  • TrapNo representation in Muslim inheritance.
  • TrapWaqf is not a trust; mutawalli is manager, not owner.

Christian and Parsi Law - Marriage, Divorce, Succession

Medium yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

S.10, 10A Indian Divorce Act 1869Grounds; mutual consent after 2 years.
S.33 ISA 1925Christian intestate succession: widow 1/3.
S.3, 32 Parsi Marriage and Divorce Act 1936Conditions; divorce.

Cases in one line

  • Mary Roy v State of Kerala 1986 ISA 1925 applies to Syrian Christians of Travancore; equal share to daughters.
  • John Vallamattom v Union of India 2003 S.118 ISA struck down as discriminatory against Christians.
  • Ammini E.J. v Union of India (Kerala HC) 1995 Discriminatory divorce grounds for Christian wives struck down.

Memory aids

  • Christian widow: 1/3 - 1/2 - all (descendants - kindred - none).

Exam traps

  • TrapChristian widow gets 1/3 (not 1/4) where there are descendants.
  • TrapChristian mutual consent requires 2 years' separation (not 1).
  • TrapParsi marriage needs a Parsi priest and two Parsi witnesses.

Special Marriage Act 1954

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

S.4, 5-13, 15, 19, 21, 21A, 27, 28 SMAConditions, procedure, registration, effects, divorce.

Cases in one line

  • Supriyo @ Supriya Chakraborty v Union of India 2023 No fundamental right to marry; SMA not read down to include same-sex marriage; matter for Parliament.
  • Lata Singh v State of U.P. 2006 Adults may marry a person of their choice; inter-caste marriage protected.
  • Shafin Jahan v Asokan K.M. (Hadiya case) 2018 Right to marry a person of choice is part of Art 21.
  • Shakti Vahini v Union of India 2018 Directions against honour killings and khap panchayat interference.

Memory aids

  • 30-30-3: notice 30, objection 30, marry within 3 months.

Exam traps

  • Trap30-day notice and 30-day objection; marry within 3 months of notice.
  • TrapSMA marriage severs a Hindu from his undivided family (S.19).
  • TrapSuccession under SMA marriage is under ISA unless both are Hindus/Buddhists/Sikhs/Jains (S.21A).
  • TrapSupriyo (2023) did not legalise same-sex marriage.

Domestic Violence Act 2005, Dowry Prohibition Act 1961, Cruelty and Dowry Death (BNS), Child Marriage Act 2006

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

S.2(a), 3, 12, 17-23, 31 DV ActAggrieved person, abuse, application, residence, reliefs, penalty.
S.3, 4, 6, 8A Dowry Prohibition ActPenalties, trust, burden of proof.
S.80, 85, 86, 108 BNSDowry death; cruelty; abetment of suicide.
S.3, 9-11, 15 PCMA 2006Voidable; punishments; offences cognizable and non-bailable.

Cases in one line

  • Hiral P. Harsora v Kusum Narottamdas Harsora 2016 'Adult male' in S.2(q) DV Act struck down; women can be respondents.
  • Satish Chander Ahuja v Sneha Ahuja 2020 Shared household is not limited to property owned by the husband; overrules S.R. Batra.
  • D. Velusamy v D. Patchaiammal 2010 Conditions for a relationship in the nature of marriage.
  • Arnesh Kumar v State of Bihar 2014 No routine arrest in S.498A cases; magistrate must verify.
  • Independent Thought v Union of India 2017 Exception 2 to S.375 IPC read down: sex with wife under 18 is rape.
  • Satvir Singh v State of Punjab 2001 'Soon before' dowry death means proximate link, no fixed time.

Memory aids

  • DV reliefs 18-22: Protection - Residence - Money - Custody - Compensation.
  • 304B = 7 to life; 498A = up to 3 years.

Exam traps

  • TrapDV Act reliefs are civil, but breach of a protection order is a criminal offence.
  • TrapDV Act: respondents can be women (relatives) after Harsora.
  • TrapDowry Act S.3 minimum is 5 years (not 6 months).
  • TrapDowry death: 7 years minimum, may extend to life.
  • TrapPCMA: marriage is voidable (not void) at the option of the child party, except S.12 cases.

Uniform Civil Code and Constitutional Dimensions of Personal Law

Medium yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Art 44, 25, 26, 14, 15, 21UCC and fundamental rights.
Entry 5, List IIIMarriage, divorce, succession - Concurrent List; state UCCs need Art 254 assent.

Cases in one line

  • Shayara Bano v Union of India 2017 Triple talaq struck down.
  • State of Bombay v Narasu Appa Mali 1952 Personal laws are not 'laws in force' for Art 13.

Memory aids

  • UCC states: Uttarakhand (2025), Gujarat (2026), Assam (2026) - reported.

Exam traps

  • TrapArt 44 is a Directive Principle, not enforceable.
  • TrapMarriage and succession are in the Concurrent List, so states may enact UCCs with Presidential assent where they conflict with central law.
  • TrapUttarakhand was first (in force 27 Jan 2025); Goa has an older common code.

Family Courts Act 1984, Live-in Relationships and Miscellaneous Family Statutes

Medium yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

S.7, 9, 13, 19 Family Courts Act 1984Jurisdiction, reconciliation, no lawyer as of right, appeal.

Cases in one line

  • Y. Narasimha Rao v Y. Venkata Lakshmi 1991 Conditions for recognising a foreign matrimonial decree.
  • S. Khushboo v Kanniammal 2010 Live-in relationship between adults is not an offence.
  • Pratibha Rani v Suraj Kumar 1985 Stridhan is the wife's absolute property.

Memory aids

  • FCA: Conciliation first (S.9); lawyers only by leave (S.13).

Exam traps

  • TrapFamily Court appeals lie to the High Court within 30 days.
  • TrapA foreign divorce is not recognised if obtained by a court without jurisdiction under the parties' marital law.
