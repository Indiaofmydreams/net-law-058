iOverview

Natural guardians, limits on a guardian's powers over immovable property, and the supremacy of the welfare principle in custody disputes.

★Must know

  1. S.4 HMGA: minor = person who has not completed 18 years. The Act supplements (does not replace) the Guardians and Wards Act 1890.
  2. S.6 natural guardians: for a boy or unmarried girl - the father, and after him the mother; but custody of a minor who has not completed 5 years shall ordinarily be with the mother. For an illegitimate boy or unmarried girl - the mother, and after her the father. For a married girl - the husband. No guardian can be appointed for the minor's undivided interest in joint family property managed by an adult member (S.12).
  3. Gita Hariharan v Reserve Bank of India (1999): 'after' in S.6(a) means 'in the absence of', i.e. the father's absence or disinterest, not after his death; the mother can act as natural guardian even during the father's lifetime if the father is indifferent or incapable. Jijabai Vithalrao Gajre v Pathankhan (1970) took a similar view. ABC v State (NCT of Delhi) (2015): an unwed mother can be the sole guardian and need not name the father.
  4. S.7: adoptive father, and after him adoptive mother, is natural guardian of an adopted son. S.9: testamentary guardian appointed by will (father, or mother where the father is dead/unfit).
  5. S.8 powers of natural guardian: may do all acts necessary or reasonable and proper for the benefit of the minor or the minor's estate, but cannot bind the minor by a personal covenant. Without the previous permission of the court, the guardian cannot (a) mortgage, charge, sell, gift, exchange or transfer immovable property of the minor, or (b) lease it for over 5 years or beyond 1 year after the minor attains majority. A transfer in breach is voidable at the instance of the minor or any person claiming under him (S.8(3)). Court permission only for necessity or evident advantage (S.8(4)).
  6. S.11: a de facto guardian (a person who is not a lawful guardian) cannot deal with the minor's property. S.13: welfare of the minor is the paramount consideration; no right under the Act or any law supersedes it.
  7. Guardians and Wards Act 1890: S.7 (appointment/declaration), S.8 (who may apply), S.17 (court considers the minor's welfare, age, sex, religion, character, preference of an intelligent minor), S.19 (no appointment if the father is fit and alive), S.25 (return of ward who leaves or is removed from the guardian's custody), S.26 (removal of ward from jurisdiction), S.4(1) (minor under 18).
  8. Custody principles: Gaurav Nagpal v Sumedha Nagpal (2009), Nil Ratan Kundu v Abhijit Kundu (2008) - welfare, not parental rights, decides; Roxann Sharma v Arun Sharma (2015) - child under 5 with mother; Vikram Vir Vohra v Shalini Bhalla (2010) - shared parenting; Yashita Sahu v State of Rajasthan (2020) - visitation and custody; Tejaswini Gaud v Shekhar Jagdish Prasad Tewari (2019) - habeas corpus for child custody is a summary remedy and welfare decides; Prateek Gupta v Shilpi Gupta (2017) and V. Ravi Chandran v Union of India (2010) - foreign custody orders are not binding and the child's welfare is examined by the Indian court.
  9. Family Courts Act 1984: family courts decide guardianship and custody matters (S.7); proceedings are confidential and conciliatory (S.9, 10); no right to legal representation as of right (S.13); appeal lies to the High Court within 30 days (S.19).

⚖Landmark cases

Gita Hariharan v Reserve Bank of India

1999

Mother is natural guardian even in the father's lifetime if he is absent/indifferent; 'after' read as 'in absence of'.

ABC v State (NCT of Delhi)

2015

Unwed mother can be the guardian of her child without disclosing or notifying the father.

Roxann Sharma v Arun Sharma

2015

Mother has a preferential claim to custody of child below 5.

Nil Ratan Kundu v Abhijit Kundu

2008

Welfare of the child is paramount; strict legal rights give way.

✎Memory aids

  • Father, then mother - but mother for under-5 custody; welfare is king (S.13).

!Exam traps

  • TrapWelfare principle prevails over the statutory order of natural guardians.
  • TrapA natural guardian's sale of minor's immovable property without court permission is voidable (not void).
  • TrapCustody under 5 is 'ordinarily' with the mother, not an absolute rule.
  • TrapMarried girl: husband is the natural guardian.
