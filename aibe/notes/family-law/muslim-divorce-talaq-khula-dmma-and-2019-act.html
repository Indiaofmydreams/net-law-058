Muslim Law II - Divorce: Talaq, Khula, Mubarat, DMMA 1939, 2019 Act

iOverview

Forms of divorce, the Supreme Court's decision on instant triple talaq (Shayara Bano), the 2019 Act, judicial divorce under DMMA 1939, and the pending talaq-e-hasan challenge.

★Must know

  1. Talaq by husband: talaq-ul-sunnat - (a) ahsan: a single pronouncement in a tuhr (period of purity), followed by abstention from intercourse during the iddat; revocable until iddat ends; (b) hasan: three successive pronouncements in three successive tuhrs, the divorce becoming irrevocable on the third. Talaq-ul-biddat (triple talaq/instant talaq): three pronouncements in one tuhr or a single irrevocable pronouncement.
  2. Shayara Bano v Union of India (22 Aug 2017, 3:2): instant triple talaq (talaq-e-biddat) is void and unconstitutional (Kurian Joseph, Nariman and Lalit JJ held it arbitrary/not part of essential Islamic practice or violative of Art 14; Khehar CJ and Nazeer J dissented). Earlier cases: Rukia Khatoon v Abdul Khalique Laskar (1981) - talaq must be for a reason and preceded by reconciliation attempts; Shamim Ara v State of U.P. (2002) - same, and talaq must be proved.
  3. Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Marriage) Act 2019: S.3 instant triple talaq (spoken, written, electronic) is void and illegal; S.4 punishment up to 3 years' imprisonment and fine; offence is cognizable only on information by the married woman or her blood/marriage relatives; the Magistrate may grant bail only after hearing the woman; compoundable at the woman's instance with the Magistrate's permission; she is entitled to subsistence allowance and custody of minor children.
  4. Talaq-e-hasan: the husband pronounces talaq once a month over three months. Challenged before the Supreme Court (Benazeer Heena v Union of India and connected pleas); in 2026 the Court referred a related dispute to mediation (Justice Kurian Joseph) and on 16 October 2026 a bench of the CJI and Justice Bagchi sought the Centre's response on a plea for uniform procedural safeguards for all forms of talaq and clubbed it with the pending petition (verify the current status).
  5. Delegated talaq (talaq-i-tafweez): the husband delegates the power to the wife (or a third party) to divorce herself on specified conditions. Ila (vow of abstinence for 4 months), zihar (comparing wife to a prohibited relative; wife may seek judicial decree if the husband does not atone) and lian (false accusation of adultery; divorce by court decree) are other forms; DMMA S.2(ix) covers them.
  6. Khula: divorce at the instance of the wife with the husband's consent in return for consideration (often release of mahr); mubarat: mutual desire of both; both are irrevocable divorces (talaq-i-bain) with iddat after. The wife's right to khula without husband's consent is recognised by Kerala High Court (K.C. Moyin v Nafeesa, 1972; Full Bench 2021) - verify.
  7. Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act 1939 S.2 grounds (decree for wife): (i) whereabouts of husband unknown for 4 years; (ii) failure to maintain for 2 years; (iii) husband's imprisonment for 7 years or more (after sentence final); (iv) failure to perform marital obligations for 3 years without reasonable cause; (v) impotency at marriage and continuing; (vi) insanity for 2 years or virulent venereal disease (leprosy omitted 2019); (vii) option of puberty - married before 15, repudiated before 18, not consummated; (viii) cruelty (habitual assault, associating with women of ill repute, forcing immoral life, disposing of her property, obstructing religious practice, unequal treatment of co-wives); (ix) any other ground valid under Muslim law (includes lian, ila, zihar, talaq-i-tafweez).
  8. DMMA S.4: apostasy by a Muslim wife does not itself dissolve marriage; S.5: wife's dower rights are not affected. Conversion of a non-Muslim wife (other than Kitabiya) to Islam and reversion: see S.4 proviso.
  9. Iddat: divorced woman - 3 menstrual courses (or 3 lunar months if not subject to menstruation); widow - 4 months 10 days; pregnant - until delivery. Remarriage prohibited during iddat; a marriage during iddat is irregular (fasid). Halala: a fixed-term marital practice to remarry a thrice-divorced wife; the Uttarakhand UCC 2024 bans it - verify.
  10. Polygamy: up to four wives under personal law (Quranic condition: equal treatment); the Supreme Court has repeatedly urged a UCC (Jorden Diengdeh v S.S. Chopra, 1985; Sarla Mudgal, 1995). State UCCs (Uttarakhand, Gujarat, Assam) prohibit polygamy for their residents (non-tribal) - see 6A-13.

⚖Landmark cases

Shayara Bano v Union of India

2017

Instant triple talaq is unconstitutional/void (3:2).

Rukia Khatoon v Abdul Khalique Laskar

1981

Talaq must be for reasonable cause and preceded by attempts at reconciliation.

Shamim Ara v State of U.P.

2002

Talaq must be communicated and proved; a plea in written statement is not talaq.

Jorden Diengdeh v S.S. Chopra

1985

Urged a UCC; commented on personal law inconsistencies.

Sarla Mudgal v Union of India

1995

Conversion to Islam to marry a second wife does not dissolve the first Hindu marriage; urged UCC.

✎Memory aids

  • DMMA S.2 numbers: 4-2-7-3-2 (missing, maintenance, jail, obligations, insanity).
  • Shayara = 3:2, 2017; Act = 2019; max 3 years.

!Exam traps

  • TrapTalaq-ul-biddat is void since 2017; talaq-e-hasan and ahsan are still lawful (hasan under challenge).
  • TrapThe 2019 Act punishes the husband (3 years) but does not make talaq-e-hasan an offence.
  • TrapIddat for a widow is 4 months 10 days, not 3 months.
  • TrapDMMA gives the wife a right to apply; the husband has no DMMA remedy.
  • TrapApostasy does not automatically dissolve the marriage (DMMA S.4).
