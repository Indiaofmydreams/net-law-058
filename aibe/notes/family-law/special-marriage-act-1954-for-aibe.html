iOverview

Secular civil marriage for any two persons (including inter-faith couples) with a notice procedure, plus registration of existing marriages and divorce provisions.

★Must know

  1. S.4 conditions: (a) neither party has a spouse living; (b) neither is incapable of giving valid consent due to unsoundness of mind, nor suffering from mental disorder making them unfit for marriage and procreation, nor subject to recurrent insanity; (c) groom 21 and bride 18; (d) parties not within prohibited degrees (unless custom permits one party's religion);
  2. Procedure: notice to the Marriage Officer of the district where one party has resided for at least 30 days (S.5); the notice is published for 30 days (S.6-7); objections within 30 days (S.7); inquiry (S.8); marriage solemnised within 3 months of the notice (S.14 - fresh notice if lapsed); solemnisation in the presence of three witnesses and the Marriage Officer (S.11-13); certificate is conclusive evidence (S.13). The Allahabad High Court in Safiya Sultana v State of U.P. (2021) held that publication of notice is optional at the request of the parties; the Supreme Court has recognised the right to marry a person of one's choice as part of Art 21 (Lata Singh v State of U.P., 2006; Shafin Jahan v Asokan K.M., 2018).
  3. S.15-18: registration of marriages solemnised in other forms - conditions: a ceremony was performed; neither party has a spouse; neither is of unsound mind; both completed age; not within prohibited degrees; parties have resided in the district for 30 days; registration makes the marriage a marriage under the Act.
  4. S.19: marriage solemnised under the Act of a member of an undivided family professing Hindu, Buddhist, Sikh or Jain religion is deemed to effect his severance from the family. S.21: succession to property of persons married under the Act and their issue is governed by the Indian Succession Act 1925; S.21A: where both are Hindu, Buddhist, Sikh or Jain, the Hindu Succession Act applies instead.
  5. Matrimonial reliefs: S.22 restitution; S.23 judicial separation; S.24 void marriages (breach of S.4(a), (b), (c), (d)... void conditions); S.25 voidable marriages (non-consummation due to wilful refusal; respondent pregnant by another; consent by force or fraud; mental disorder); S.27 divorce grounds (adultery, desertion for 2 years, imprisonment for 7 years, cruelty, unsound mind, venereal disease; wife's grounds - rape, sodomy, bestiality, non-compliance with a maintenance decree); S.28 mutual consent after 1 year of separation, with the 6-18 month process as under HMA (Amardeep Singh principles apply); S.36 interim alimony and S.37 permanent alimony.
  6. Supriyo @ Supriya Chakraborty v Union of India (17 October 2023, Constitution Bench): no fundamental right to marry; the Court declined to read down or strike down provisions of the SMA to include same-sex marriage; it left the question to Parliament; the Union assured a committee on rights of queer couples; review petitions were dismissed in January 2025 (verify). The Court recognised that queer couples have the right to cohabit and non-discrimination; CARA regulation barring single/queer adoptions stays.
  7. Interfaith couples: the SMA does not require conversion; several states' anti-conversion laws require declarations for conversions, not for SMA marriages; Shakti Vahini v Union of India (2018) and Lata Singh protect adult choice and direct protective measures against honour crimes.

⚖Landmark cases

Supriyo @ Supriya Chakraborty v Union of India

2023

No fundamental right to marry; SMA not read down to include same-sex marriage; matter for Parliament.

Lata Singh v State of U.P.

2006

Adults may marry a person of their choice; inter-caste marriage protected.

Shafin Jahan v Asokan K.M. (Hadiya case)

2018

Right to marry a person of choice is part of Art 21.

Shakti Vahini v Union of India

2018

Directions against honour killings and khap panchayat interference.

  • TrapSupriyo (2023) did not legalise same-sex marriage.
