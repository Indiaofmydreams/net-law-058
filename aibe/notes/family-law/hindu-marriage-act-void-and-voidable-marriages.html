Hindu Marriage Act 1955 - Validity of Marriage, Void and Voidable Marriages, Children

iOverview

Know the five conditions of S.5, which breach makes a marriage void (S.11), voidable (S.12) or only punishable, the role of ceremonies and registration, and S.16 legitimacy of children.

★Must know

  1. Applicability (S.2): Hindus by religion in any form (including Virashaiva, Lingayat, Brahmo, Prarthana, Arya Samaj), Buddhists, Jains, Sikhs, and any domiciled person who is not Muslim, Christian, Parsi or Jew. Does not apply to Scheduled Tribes unless the Centre notifies.
  2. S.5 conditions: (i) monogamy - neither party has a spouse living; (ii) sound mind, capable of valid consent, not suffering from mental disorder unfit for marriage and procreation, no recurrent insanity; (iii) groom 21, bride 18; (iv) not within degrees of prohibited relationship unless custom permits; (v) not sapindas unless custom permits.
  3. Sapinda (S.3(f)): extends to the third generation (inclusive) up the mother's line and the fifth (inclusive) up the father's line. Custom must be ancient, certain, reasonable and not opposed to public policy (S.3(a)).
  4. VOID (S.11): breach of S.5(i), (iv) or (v). Either party may seek a decree of nullity; the marriage is void ab initio even without a decree.
  5. VOIDABLE (S.12): (a) non-consummation due to impotence of the respondent; (b) breach of S.5(ii) (unsound mind etc.); (c) consent obtained by force or fraud (as to nature of ceremony or material fact); (d) respondent pregnant by another person at the time of marriage, petitioner being ignorant. Time limits (S.12(2)): fraud/force petition within one year of discovery/cessation of force and no marital intercourse afterwards; ground (d) within one year of marriage.
  6. Breach of S.5(iii) (age): marriage is neither void nor voidable under HMA; it is punishable (S.18: simple imprisonment up to 15 days or fine up to Rs 1,000). Separately, the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act 2006 makes a child marriage voidable at the option of the party who was a child.
  7. Bigamy: S.17 HMA read with BNS S.82 (IPC 494/495). Second marriage is void and punishable (up to 7 years; 10 years if the earlier marriage is concealed). Essential ceremonies of the second marriage must be proved (Bhaurao Shankar Lokhande v State of Maharashtra, 1965).
  8. Ceremonies (S.7): marriage is solemnised per customary rites of either party; where saptapadi is part of the rite, the marriage is complete and binding on the seventh step (Priya Bala Ghosh v Suresh Chandra Ghosh, 1971). A registration certificate cannot substitute valid ceremonies (Dolly Rani v Manish Kumar Chanchal, 2024).
  9. Registration (S.8): for proof only; failure does not invalidate the marriage. Seema v Ashwani Kumar (2006): registration of marriages of all religions was made compulsory by SC directions.
  10. S.16 children: children of void marriages (S.11) and annulled voidable marriages (S.12) are legitimate. They have rights only in the property of their parents (S.16(3)), not of other relatives. Revanasiddappa v Mallikarjun (2011; larger-bench reference decided 2023): such children share in the parents' self-acquired property and in the parent's share of coparcenary property on partition.
  11. Jurisdiction (S.19): court where marriage was solemnised, respondent resides, parties last resided together, or (since 2003) where the wife petitioner resides. Transfer under S.21A-21B; day-to-day trial under S.21B.
  12. Joseph Shine v Union of India (2018): S.497 IPC (adultery) struck down; adultery stays a civil wrong and a divorce ground. BNS 2023 does not retain the offence.

⚖Landmark cases

Bhaurao Shankar Lokhande v State of Maharashtra

1965

Second marriage must be performed with the essential ceremonies for bigamy; invocation of saptapadi where it is part of the rites.

Priya Bala Ghosh v Suresh Chandra Ghosh

1971

Saptapadi is essential only where the customary rites include it.

Dolly Rani v Manish Kumar Chanchal

2024

A Hindu marriage needs valid ceremonies; a certificate of registration does not validate a marriage without them; marriage is a sacrament, not a commercial event.

Sarla Mudgal v Union of India

1995

A Hindu husband who converts to Islam to marry again is still guilty of bigamy; the first marriage subsists until dissolved under HMA.

Lily Thomas v Union of India

2000

Reaffirmed Sarla Mudgal; conversion does not end a subsisting Hindu marriage.

Revanasiddappa v Mallikarjun

2011 / 2023

Children of void or voidable marriages can claim a share in their parents' property.

Joseph Shine v Union of India

2018

Adultery (S.497 IPC) struck down as violating Arts 14, 15, 21.

✎Memory aids

  • V-O-I-D = (i) bigamy, (iv) prohibited degrees, (v) sapinda: 1-4-5 void.
  • Voidable = Impotence, Insanity (S.5(ii)), Intimidation/fraud, Illicit pregnancy.

!Exam traps

  • TrapAge breach does not make an HMA marriage void; PCMA makes it voidable at the child's option.
  • TrapS.11 void marriages: only breaches of S.5(i), (iv), (v). S.5(ii) breach is voidable.
  • TrapRegistration is evidentiary, not a validity condition.
  • TrapChildren of void marriages are legitimate but inherit only from their parents.

?Likely questions

  1. Which condition breach makes a marriage void under S.11?
  2. Is a marriage in breach of the age condition void?
  3. Rights of children of void marriages (S.16).
  4. Effect of conversion and second marriage (Sarla Mudgal).
  5. Is registration compulsory for validity?
