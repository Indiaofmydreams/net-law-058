iOverview

Procedural machinery and shorter statutes that frequently supply single MCQs.

★Must know

  1. Family Courts Act 1984: establishes Family Courts in cities with population over 10 lakh; S.7 jurisdiction over matrimonial relief, property of spouses, legitimacy, guardianship, maintenance (including S.125 CrPC); S.9 duty to assist in reconciliation; S.10 procedure follows CPC but flexible; S.11 in camera; S.13 no right to legal practitioner without leave of the court; S.19 appeal to High Court within 30 days; no revision.
  2. Live-in relationships: not a statutory status; relief via DV Act (S.2(f)), maintenance (S.125 CrPC), and legitimacy of children (S.16 HMA by analogy). Cases: Lata Singh (2006), Indra Sarma (2013), Velusamy (2010), S. Khushboo v Kanniammal (2010) (live-in is not an offence), Badri Prasad v Dy Director of Consolidation (1978), Tulsa v Durghatiya (2008) (long cohabitation raises presumption of marriage). Uttarakhand UCC requires registration of live-in relationships.
  3. Hindu women's property rights: Stridhan (property given before or after marriage: gifts from parents, husband, relatives) is absolute property of the wife (Pratibha Rani v Suraj Kumar, 1985).
  4. Maintenance of Parents and Senior Citizens Act 2007 and Dowry Prohibition Rules 1985 (list of presents at marriage) are noted in earlier topics.
  5. Foreign decrees: S.13 CPC; Y. Narasimha Rao v Y. Venkata Lakshmi (1991): a foreign divorce decree is recognised only if the court had jurisdiction under the Act the parties were married under, was on merits, and was in accordance with the matrimonial law of the parties.
  6. Transfer of matrimonial petitions: S.24 CPC and Art 142; Anindita Das v Srijit Das (2006) - the Supreme Court transfers petitions under S.25 CPC.

  • TrapA foreign divorce is not recognised if obtained by a court without jurisdiction under the parties' marital law.
