Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act 1956 - Adoption; Adoption under JJ Act 2015

iOverview

The Act is the most 'section-number' heavy part of Hindu law. Learn who can adopt, who can give, who can be adopted, the other conditions, effects and the secular JJ Act route.

★Must know

  1. S.6: valid adoption needs (i) the adopter's capacity and right to adopt, (ii) the giver's capacity to give, (iii) the adoptee's capacity to be adopted, and (iv) compliance with other conditions.
  2. S.7 male: any Hindu male of sound mind who is not a minor may adopt; if married, with the consent of his wife (or all wives), unless she has entirely and finally renounced the world, ceased to be Hindu or been declared of unsound mind. S.8 female: a Hindu female of sound mind and not a minor, if unmarried, or if married, whose marriage has been dissolved, whose husband is dead, has finally renounced the world, ceased to be Hindu or been declared of unsound mind. A married woman cannot adopt in her own right during the subsistence of marriage.
  3. S.9 who may give: father or mother or guardian. The father cannot give without the mother's consent unless she has renounced the world, ceased to be Hindu or is of unsound mind. A guardian can give only with court permission where both parents are dead, have renounced the world, abandoned the child, are of unsound mind or the parentage is unknown. The mother can give if the father is dead or disqualified.
  4. S.10 who may be adopted: a Hindu, not already adopted, not married (unless custom), and not completed the age of 15 (unless custom allows).
  5. S.11 other conditions: for adoption of a son the adopter must not have a Hindu son, son's son or son's son's son (legitimate or adopted) living; for a daughter, no Hindu daughter or son's daughter living; where the adopter is a male and the adoptee a female (or vice versa), the adopter must be at least 21 years older; the same child cannot be adopted by two persons simultaneously; the child must be actually given and taken (datta homam is not essential).
  6. S.12 effects: from the date of adoption the child is deemed the child of the adoptive parents for all purposes and ties with the family of birth are severed; the child cannot marry any person he or she could not marry in the natural family; property vested in the child before adoption continues; the child cannot divest any person of an estate vested before adoption.
  7. S.13 adopters' right to dispose of property is unaffected; S.14 where a male Hindu with more than one wife adopts, the seniormost in marriage is the adoptive mother; S.15 a valid adoption cannot be cancelled by anyone; S.16 presumption of validity of a registered adoption deed; S.17 payments for adoption are prohibited (punishable).
  8. Adoption under the JJ (Care and Protection of Children) Act 2015: secular; Central Adoption Resource Authority (CARA) regulates; Adoption Regulations 2022; the 2021 amendment empowers the District Magistrate to issue adoption orders (instead of courts). Shabnam Hashmi v Union of India (2014): any person may adopt under the JJ Act irrespective of religion; this is an enabling law and does not displace personal laws.
  9. Proof: Sawan Ram v Kalawati (1967) - proof of giving and taking; recitals in a registered deed carry a presumption but are rebuttable. Lakshmi Kant Pandey v Union of India (1984) - guidelines on inter-country adoption.

⚖Landmark cases

Shabnam Hashmi v Union of India

2014

The JJ Act adoption right is available irrespective of religion; right to adopt is a facet of Art 21 interpretation, not a fundamental right.

Lakshmi Kant Pandey v Union of India

1984

Safeguards for inter-country adoption; best interests of the child.

Sawan Ram v Kalawati

1967

Adoption requires actual giving and taking; evidence.

✎Memory aids

  • S.7 male + wife's consent; S.8 female unmarried or ex-married; S.9 giver; S.10 adoptee; S.11 other conditions; S.12 effects.

!Exam traps

  • TrapA Hindu mother alone cannot give in adoption when the father is alive and a Hindu of sound mind.
  • TrapDatta homam is not essential for validity.
  • TrapAdoptee age limit is 15 unless custom; adopter 21-year gap applies only to opposite-sex adoption.
  • TrapA valid adoption cannot be cancelled (S.15).
