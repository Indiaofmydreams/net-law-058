iOverview

Sources and schools; validity of nikah; mahr as an obligation of the husband; and the wife's rights during marriage.

★Must know

  1. Sources: Quran, Sunnah/Hadith, Ijma (consensus), Qiyas (analogical deduction); secondary: Urf (custom), Istihsan, Istislah. Sunni schools: Hanafi (most Indian Muslims), Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali; Shias: Ithna Ashari (Twelvers), Ismaili, Zaidi.
  2. Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act 1937: S.2 applies Muslim law (in cases where parties are Muslims) to intestate succession, special property of females, marriage, dissolution of marriage (talaq, ila, zihar, lian, khula, mubarat), maintenance, dower, guardianship, gifts, trusts and waqfs; customary law is excluded in these matters. It does not cover wills and adoption unless the person makes a declaration under S.3 (as originally framed).
  3. Nikah is a civil contract: proposal (ijab) and acceptance (qubul) at the same meeting, free consent, competence (puberty, sound mind), witnesses (Sunni: two male or one male and two females; Shia: none required). No religious ceremony or writing is essential.
  4. Classification of marriages: valid (sahih), irregular (fasid), void (batil). Irregular: absence of witnesses, marriage during iddat of another, with a woman of a non-Kitabiya religion (for male), fifth wife, unlawful conjunction. An irregular marriage can be regularised by removing the impediment; if consummated it carries limited effects (proper dower or specified dower whichever is less, legitimate children, iddat). Void: marriage within prohibited degrees (consanguinity, affinity, fosterage) - no legal consequences. Muta (temporary) marriage is recognised only by Shias (Ithna Ashari).
  5. Capacity and age: puberty (presumed at 15 years) under classical law; guardianship in marriage (wilayat) of a minor by father or paternal relatives; the minor's option of puberty (khyar-ul-bulugh). The Prohibition of Child Marriage Act 2006 applies to all communities in principle; Society for Enlightenment and Voluntary Action v Union of India (Oct 2024) issued guidelines for enforcing the PCMA - the Court did not resolve the personal-law conflict (verify).
  6. Mahr (dower): an obligation imposed by law on the husband as a mark of respect to the wife (Abdul Kadir v Salima, 1886); not a consideration for marriage; marriage is valid even if mahr is not fixed (proper/customary dower payable). Types: specified, proper (mahr-i-misl), prompt (muajjal), deferred (muwajjal). Hanafi minimum: 10 dirhams (Maliki 3); no maximum. Wife may refuse cohabitation until prompt dower is paid. On dissolution, unpaid dower is a debt.
  7. Widow's right of retention (lien): if the widow is in lawful possession of the husband's property, she may retain it until her dower is paid; the lien is not a right of ownership and cannot be transferred (Hamira Bibi v Zubaida Bibi, 1916).
  8. Maintenance (nafaqah): husband must maintain his wife during marriage (even if she is wealthy) unless she is disobedient (nashiza); also minor children and indigent parents; wife not entitled if she refuses to cohabit without lawful reason. Statutory remedies: S.144 BNSS (S.125 CrPC), Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act 1986.
  9. Guardianship and custody: father is natural guardian of the person and property; mother has the right of hizanat (physical custody) - Hanafi: boy till 7, girl until puberty; Shia: boy till 2, girl till 7; custody can be lost on remarriage to a stranger or on bad character; welfare of the child prevails in practice.

⚖Landmark cases

Abdul Kadir v Salima

1886

Dower is an obligation on the husband, not consideration for marriage.

Hamira Bibi v Zubaida Bibi

1916

Widow's right of retention is not a right of ownership; cannot be transferred.

Shamim Ara v State of U.P.

2002

Talaq must be pronounced for a reason and communicated; proof required; an averment in a written statement is not talaq.

✎Memory aids

  • 10-Hanafi, 3-Maliki dirhams.
  • Irregular = curable; Void = incurable.

!Exam traps

  • TrapMahr is not consideration; it is an obligation.
  • TrapHanafi minimum is 10 dirhams, Maliki is 3.
  • TrapShia law does not need witnesses for nikah; Sunni law does.
  • TrapMuta marriage is recognised only by Ithna Ashari Shias.
  • TrapWidow's lien is not ownership.
