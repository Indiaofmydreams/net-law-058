Opinion of Experts, Character, Judgments, Judicial Notice and Facts Admitted (S.34-55 BSA approx.; IEA S.40-58)

iOverview

Expert opinion is relevant but never binding; judgments are relevant only in special cases; judicially noticeable and admitted facts need no proof.

★Must know

  1. S.39 BSA (IEA 45) expert opinion: relevant when the Court has to form an opinion on foreign law, science or art or (new) 'any other field', identity of handwriting or finger impressions. S.39(2): the Examiner of Electronic Evidence's opinion is relevant.
  2. Expert evidence is weak, advisory, requires reasons, and is a corroborative rule of prudence; the Judge must form his own opinion (Murari Lal v. State of M.P., 1980 - handwriting; State of H.P. v. Jai Lal, 1999; Ramesh Chandra Agrawal v. Regency Hospital, 2009).
  3. Reports of Government scientific experts can be received without calling the expert (BNSS S.329 / CrPC S.293).
  4. Opinion on handwriting (S.47 IEA): of a person acquainted with it; comparison by Court (S.73) - the Court may itself compare, but should be cautious and prefer expert help.
  5. Character evidence: in civil cases irrelevant unless the character is itself a fact in issue (S.52 IEA); in criminal cases good character is relevant (S.53); bad character irrelevant unless in reply or in itself a fact in issue (S.54); previous conviction is relevant as evidence of bad character only where bad character is relevant. Previous sexual experience is irrelevant in sexual-offence consent issues (S.53A IEA).
  6. Judgments (IEA S.40-44): S.40 - previous judgment relevant to bar a second suit or trial (res judicata / autrefois acquit); S.41 - final judgment of a competent court in probate, matrimonial, admiralty or insolvency jurisdiction is conclusive proof of what it declares (judgment in rem); S.42 - other judgments in rem relevant; S.43 - judgments not within S.40-42 are irrelevant unless the existence of the judgment is a fact in issue or relevant; S.44 - any party may show that a judgment was delivered by a court not competent or was obtained by fraud or collusion.
  7. Findings in criminal cases are not binding in civil cases (Karam Chand Ganga Prasad v. Union of India, 1970).
  8. Judicial notice (IEA S.56-57; S.52 BSA): facts of which the Court takes judicial notice need not be proved - all laws in force in India, Parliament and Legislature proceedings, accession and sign manual of the President and Governors, seals of courts, the course of nature, divisions of time, the territories and political situation, the rule of the road at sea and on land. The Court may refer to appropriate books or documents for assistance.
  9. Facts admitted need not be proved (S.53 BSA / IEA 58) - but the Court may in its discretion require proof of facts admitted otherwise than by such admission.
  10. Oral evidence may prove all facts except contents of documents (IEA S.59); oral evidence must be direct - if it refers to a fact which could be seen, heard or perceived, it must be the evidence of a witness who says he saw, heard or perceived it (IEA S.60 / S.55 BSA); hearsay is excluded.

⚖Landmark cases

Murari Lal v. State of Madhya Pradesh

1980

Court is not bound by expert opinion on handwriting; it may itself compare; but caution is advised.

State of Himachal Pradesh v. Jai Lal

1999

Expert must give reasons; the opinion is not conclusive.

Ramesh Chandra Agrawal v. Regency Hospital Ltd.

2009

Expert must be a person with special skill; the report must give data and reasoning.

Karam Chand Ganga Prasad v. Union of India

1970

Criminal court's findings are not binding in civil proceedings.

✎Memory aids

  • Judgments: 40-41-42-43-44 = Bar - Conclusive - In rem - Irrelevant - Challenge (fraud/collusion).

!Exam traps

  • TrapExpert evidence is not binding - the Court may reject it for reasons.
  • TrapS.41 judgments are conclusive (judgment in rem); other judgments are only relevant, not conclusive.
  • TrapJudicial notice vs proof: no proof required for facts judicially noticed.
  • TrapHearsay is inadmissible except where an express exception applies (dying declaration, business entries, etc.).
