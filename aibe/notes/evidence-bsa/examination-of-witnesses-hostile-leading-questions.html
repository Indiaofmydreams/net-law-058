Examination of Witnesses: Leading Questions, Hostile Witness, Cross-examination, Judge's Powers (S.140-169 BSA approx.; IEA S.135-167)

iOverview

Know the order of examination, when leading questions are permitted, what a hostile witness is, how credit is impeached, and the Judge's power to question (S.168 BSA).

★Must know

  1. Order: examination-in-chief -> cross-examination -> re-examination. Cross-examination need not be confined to the facts stated in chief. Re-examination is confined to matters arising out of cross-examination and new matters need the Court's permission (and the adverse party then gets to cross).
  2. Leading questions: a question suggesting the answer. Not allowed in examination-in-chief or re-examination if objected to by the adverse party, except on introductory or undisputed matters or with the Court's permission; allowed in cross-examination (IEA S.141-143).
  3. Hostile witness (IEA S.154): the Court may permit the party calling a witness to put questions that the adverse party might put in cross-examination. The evidence of a hostile witness is not completely effaced - the reliable portion can be accepted after careful scrutiny (Bhagwan Singh, 1976; Khujji @ Surendra Tiwari, 1991; Rabindra Kumar Dey, 1976; Sat Paul v. Delhi Administration, 1976).
  4. Previous statements in writing: cross-examination as to writing without showing it is allowed, but if it is to contradict, the writing must be shown (IEA S.145).
  5. Cross-examination on credit; questions which Court must decide to allow; no question without reasonable grounds (IEA S.148-149); indecent or scandalous questions (S.151); questions intended to insult or annoy (S.152). Proviso to S.146: in a rape trial, no question may be asked about the victim's general immoral character.
  6. Impeaching credit (IEA S.155): evidence of those who believe the witness unworthy of credit; proof that the witness has been bribed; proof of former inconsistent statements.
  7. Corroboration by former statements (IEA S.157): former statements made at or about the time of the fact or before an authority legally competent to investigate.
  8. Refreshing memory (IEA S.159): documents written contemporaneously or soon after - witness may refer; Court may permit use of copies; if the document is used the adverse party can inspect and cross-examine on it.
  9. Judge's power to put questions (IEA S.165 / S.168 BSA): in order to discover or obtain proper proof of relevant facts, the Judge may ask any question in any form at any time and order production of any document; the Judge is an active umpire (Ram Chander v. State of Haryana, 1981; Zahira Habibulla Sheikh v. State of Gujarat - Best Bakery, 2006).
  10. Improper admission/rejection of evidence (IEA S.167 / S.169 BSA): no ground for new trial or reversal unless, after setting aside the improperly admitted evidence, the independent evidence is insufficient to justify the decision.
  11. Rule in Browne v. Dunn: if a party intends to impeach a witness's account, the contradiction should be put to the witness in cross-examination.

⚖Landmark cases

Sat Paul v. Delhi Administration

1976

Hostile witness: there is no need to declare a witness 'hostile' - the Court may permit cross-examination by the calling party.

Bhagwan Singh v. State of Haryana

1976

The evidence of a witness who is declared hostile is not wholly rejected.

Khujji @ Surendra Tiwari v. State of M.P.

1991

Hostile witness's testimony can be relied on to the extent it is corroborated or trustworthy.

Zahira Habibulla H. Sheikh v. State of Gujarat

2006

Court cannot be a mere spectator; duty to ensure a fair trial and to examine witnesses under S.165.

Ram Chander v. State of Haryana

1981

Judge's role under S.165 - to elicit truth, not to merely record.

State of Rajasthan v. Ani @ Hanif

1997

Sole eye-witness testimony and the importance of minor discrepancies being disregarded.

✎Memory aids

  • C-C-R: Chief - Cross - Re-exam; Leading allowed only in the middle one.

!Exam traps

  • TrapLeading questions are permitted in cross, restricted in chief and re-exam.
  • TrapA hostile witness's entire testimony is not automatically discarded.
  • TrapA Judge cannot be accused of partiality merely for putting questions under S.165/168.
  • TrapFailure to cross-examine on a material point may amount to acceptance of that part of the testimony.
