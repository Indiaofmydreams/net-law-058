iOverview

Chapter II is the logical heart of the Act: facts in issue and relevant facts, and the exact categories in which a fact becomes relevant.

★Must know

  1. S.3 BSA (IEA 5): evidence may be given of facts in issue and of relevant facts, and of no others.
  2. S.4 BSA (IEA 6) - res gestae: facts so connected with a fact in issue as to form part of the same transaction are relevant, whether at the same time and place or at different times and places. Test: spontaneity and contemporaneity (Gentela Vijayavardhan Rao v. State of A.P., 1996; Sukhar v. State of U.P., 1999; Ratten v. R).
  3. S.5 (IEA 7): facts that are the occasion, cause or effect of relevant facts.
  4. S.6 BSA (IEA 8): motive, preparation and previous or subsequent conduct. Subsequent conduct is relevant only if it is influenced by the fact in issue (absconding is relevant but not conclusive).
  5. S.7 (IEA 9): facts necessary to explain or introduce a fact in issue or relevant fact - includes identity of things or persons, e.g. Test Identification Parade (not substantive evidence, only corroborative - Matru v. State of U.P., 1971).
  6. S.8 BSA (IEA 10) conspiracy: once there is reasonable ground to believe that two or more persons conspired, anything said, done or written by any one of them in reference to the common intention is relevant against each of them, for proving the conspiracy and also for showing that any such person was a party to it (Mirza Akbar v. King Emperor, 1940; Kehar Singh v. State, 1988; State v. Nalini, 1999).
  7. Facts when relevant to show that a thing was done by the same person or in a given manner (IEA S.11), fact showing right or custom (S.13), state of mind or body or bodily feeling (S.14), course of business (S.16), facts tending to enable the Court to determine the amount of damages (S.12).
  8. Similar facts / 'system' evidence is relevant to show that an act was intentional or done with knowledge, not accidental (IEA S.14 illustrations).
  9. Evidence of an act of a third person as 'opinion' is irrelevant unless covered by the expert/opinion provisions.

⚖Landmark cases

Gentela Vijayavardhan Rao v. State of A.P.

1996

Res gestae requires spontaneity and immediate connection with the transaction.

Kehar Singh v. State (Delhi Administration)

1988

Conspiracy is rarely proved by direct evidence; Section 10 applies once prima facie conspiracy is shown.

State (NCT of Delhi) v. Navjot Sandhu

2005

Conspiracy principles under S.10 applied to the Parliament attack.

Matru v. State of U.P.

1971

TIP is not substantive evidence; it only corroborates court identification.

✎Memory aids

  • Res gestae = same transaction; Section 10 = 'Co-conspirator's words bind co-conspirators'.

!Exam traps

  • TrapS.10 (conspiracy) applies only after reasonable ground to believe in the conspiracy is established.
  • TrapTIP is corroborative; substantive evidence is the identification in court.
  • TrapRes gestae must be contemporaneous - an afterthought narrative is excluded.
