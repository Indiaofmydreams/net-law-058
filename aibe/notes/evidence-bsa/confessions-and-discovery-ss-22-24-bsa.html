Confessions and Discovery (S.22-24 BSA; IEA S.24-30)

iOverview

The law excludes involuntary confessions, confessions to police and confessions in police custody, but allows the portion of an accused's information that leads to discovery of a fact. Confession of a co-accused is only a corroborative aid.

★Must know

  1. 'Confession' is not defined in the Act. Pakala Narayana Swami: it must admit the offence or substantially all facts that constitute the offence; an incriminating admission is not by itself a confession. A confession must be accepted or rejected as a whole (Palvinder Kaur).
  2. S.22 BSA (IEA 24): a confession caused by inducement, threat, COERCION or promise (new word) is irrelevant in a criminal proceeding if it appears to the Court that it was made by a person in authority and gave the accused reasonable grounds to think he would gain an advantage or avoid an evil of a temporal nature in reference to the proceedings.
  3. S.23(1) BSA (IEA 25): no confession made to a police officer shall be proved against a person accused of an offence.
  4. S.23(2) BSA (IEA 26): no confession made in police custody is provable against the accused unless made in the immediate presence of a Magistrate. PROVISO (IEA 27): so much of the information received from an accused in custody, whether it amounts to a confession or not, as relates distinctly to the fact discovered, may be proved.
  5. Discovery rule: Pulukuri Kottaya - what is admissible is the information that distinctly relates to the fact discovered (the place and the accused's knowledge), not the whole statement. Custody includes constructive custody (Udai Bhan).
  6. S.24 BSA (IEA 30): confession of a co-accused, jointly tried for the same offence, proved and affecting the maker and the others, may be taken into consideration against the others. It is NOT substantive evidence - it can only lend assurance to other evidence (Kashmira Singh; Haricharan Kurmi; Bhuboni Sahu). Explanation (new): trial in the absence of an absconding accused / one who fails to comply with a proclamation is deemed a joint trial.
  7. Judicial confession is recorded by a Magistrate under BNSS S.183 (CrPC S.164) after due caution; voluntariness is the test.
  8. Extra-judicial confession is a weak form of evidence; reliability depends on the person to whom it is made and circumstances (Sahadevan v. State of T.N., 2012).
  9. Retracted confession: can found a conviction if voluntary and true, but as a rule of prudence needs general corroboration (Pyare Lal Bhargava).
  10. Statements to NDPS officers under S.67 NDPS are inadmissible as confessions because those officers are 'police officers' for S.25 IEA (Tofan Singh v. State of T.N., 2020). By contrast, statements under S.50 PMLA are admissible and not hit by S.25 (Vijay Madanlal Choudhary, 2022) and statements to Customs officers under S.108 Customs Act are admissible (Illias).

⚖Landmark cases

Pakala Narayana Swami v. Emperor

1939

Definition of confession; admission of incriminating fact is not confession.

Palvinder Kaur v. State of Punjab

1952

Confession must be taken as a whole; exculpatory part cannot be rejected selectively.

Pulukuri Kottaya v. Emperor

1947

Scope of discovery under S.27; only the information distinctly related to the discovered fact is provable.

State of U.P. v. Deoman Upadhyaya

1960

S.27 is a proviso to S.26; applies even if accused was not formally under arrest if in police custody; constitutional validity upheld.

State of Bombay v. Kathi Kalu Oghad

1961

Giving specimen handwriting/fingerprints is not testimonial compulsion under Art 20(3); S.27 information given by accused is not barred by Art 20(3).

Aghnoo Nagesia v. State of Bihar

1966

A confessional FIR lodged by the accused is hit by S.25 IEA and cannot be proved against him.

Kashmira Singh v. State of M.P.

1952

Co-accused confession cannot be the basis of conviction; only lends assurance.

Haricharan Kurmi v. State of Bihar

1964

Co-accused confession is not evidence in the strict sense of S.3; it is a factor to be considered after other evidence is examined.

Selvi v. State of Karnataka

2010

Involuntary narco-analysis, polygraph and BEAP tests violate Art 20(3) and Art 21; but facts discovered pursuant to voluntary tests may be admissible under S.27.

Tofan Singh v. State of Tamil Nadu

2020

NDPS officers are police officers; S.67 NDPS statements are inadmissible as confessions.

✎Memory aids

  • 22 = Pressure out, 23 = Police out (but discovery in), 24 = Partner's confession (co-accused).
  • Pulukuri = Place; Kathi Kalu = Kopy (handwriting/fingerprint, not testimony); Deoman = Detained.

!Exam traps

  • TrapS.27 / S.23(2) proviso is not limited to confessions - 'whether it amounts to a confession or not'.
  • TrapThe information is admissible only if it leads to discovery of a fact; the portion before/after that is excluded.
  • TrapConfession by co-accused requires JOINT trial and same offence; it is not substantive evidence.
  • TrapA statement made to a Magistrate in custody is admissible only if it is a confession recorded in his immediate presence.
  • TrapS.22 requires the inducement to come from a person in authority; 'coercion' is the new word in the BSA.
  • TrapConfession to a police officer remains inadmissible even if the accused is not in custody when making it.

?Likely questions

  1. Which section makes confession to police officer inadmissible? (S.23(1) BSA / S.25 IEA)
  2. What portion of information given by accused in custody is admissible? (relates distinctly to fact discovered)
  3. Evidentiary value of co-accused confession.
  4. Which new word did the BSA add to the inducement section? (coercion)
  5. Statements to NDPS officers - admissible as confession? (No - Tofan Singh)
