Electronic and Digital Evidence (S.2(1)(d)-(e), S.39(2), S.57, S.61-63 BSA; Schedule)

iOverview

The BSA expressly treats electronic or digital records as documents and electronic statements as oral evidence. Secondary electronic evidence is admissible only through S.63, which is the successor to S.65B IEA, but with a tougher certificate (two signatories and a hash value).

★Must know

  1. S.2(1)(d) 'document' includes electronic or digital records (emails, server logs, documents on computers/laptops/smartphones, messages, websites, locational evidence, voice-mail messages stored on digital devices).
  2. S.2(1)(e) 'evidence': oral evidence includes statements made by witnesses through electronic means; documentary evidence includes electronic or digital records produced for the inspection of the Court.
  3. S.57 (primary evidence) has extra Explanations for electronic records: where a record is created or stored in multiple files, or simultaneously in more than one storage space (e.g. a video), each is primary evidence; a record produced from proper custody is primary evidence unless disputed.
  4. S.61: an electronic or digital record cannot be denied admissibility merely because it is electronic; it has the same legal effect, validity and enforceability as any other document.
  5. S.62: the contents of electronic records may be proved in accordance with S.63 (special provision).
  6. S.63(1): information in an electronic record (printed on paper, or stored/recorded/copied in optical or magnetic media or semiconductor memory, produced by a computer or communication device) is deemed a document and admissible without production of the original if the S.63 conditions are met.
  7. S.63(2) conditions (same four as old S.65B(2)): (a) device used regularly to store/process information in the course of regular activities under lawful control; (b) information of that kind regularly fed in the ordinary course; (c) device operating properly throughout the material part of the period (or any failure did not affect the record); (d) the reproduced information derives from information fed in the ordinary course.
  8. S.63(3): where several computers/communication devices (standalone, networked, or through intermediaries) performed the function, they are treated as a single device.
  9. S.63(4): a certificate in the form prescribed by the Schedule must accompany the electronic record when tendered. Part A is signed by the person in charge of the device / in management of the relevant activities; Part B is signed by an expert. The certificate must identify the record, describe how it was produced, give device particulars, address the S.63(2) conditions and disclose the HASH VALUE of the record (e.g. SHA-256).
  10. S.39(2): the opinion of the Examiner of Electronic Evidence under S.79A of the IT Act, 2000 is a relevant fact. (But see Pune Bar Association below on who can sign Part B.)
  11. Trials and examinations can be conducted by electronic means (see BNSS S.530).

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
S.63 BSASuccessor of S.65B IEA. Applies to civil and criminal proceedings.
S.65A IEAOld rule: contents of electronic records may be proved only per S.65B. Now S.62 BSA.
S.22A IEAOral admissions as to contents of electronic records irrelevant unless genuineness of the record is in question - retained in substance in the BSA.
S.81A, 85A, 85B, 85C, 88A, 90A IEAPresumptions: Gazette in electronic form; electronic agreements; secure electronic records and signatures; Electronic Signature Certificates; electronic messages (no presumption as to the sender); electronic records five years old. Continued in the BSA presumptions chapter.

⚖Landmark cases

State (NCT of Delhi) v. Navjot Sandhu (Parliament attack case)

2005

Liberal view: even without a S.65B certificate, secondary evidence of electronic records (call records) could be given under S.63/65 IEA. Overruled on this point by Anvar.

Anvar P.V. v. P.K. Basheer

2014

Three-judge bench: S.65B is a special provision; a certificate under S.65B(4) is mandatory for secondary electronic evidence; oral evidence cannot substitute the certificate; Navjot Sandhu overruled.

Shafhi Mohammad v. State of H.P.

2018

Certificate not needed where the party does not have the device (procedural relaxation). Later declared wrongly decided.

Arjun Panditrao Khotkar v. Kailash Kushanrao Gorantyal

2020

Three-judge bench: Anvar affirmed, Shafhi overruled. Certificate is a mandatory condition for secondary electronic evidence; may be produced at any stage before the trial closes, and the Court can direct its production; no certificate is needed when the original device is itself produced and proved (primary evidence).

Pune Bar Association v. Union of India (W.P.(C) 599 of 2026), decided 22 May 2026

2026

Supreme Court upheld the constitutional validity of S.63(4) BSA and its Schedule - the hash-value disclosure and expert certification have a rational nexus with authenticity and integrity of electronic evidence. The Court held that Part B can be signed by any person with special skill in computer science and cyber forensics and is not confined to an Examiner of Electronic Evidence notified under S.79A IT Act; it did not treat the contrary Madras High Court view as binding precedent, though the question was kept open for fuller examination in a proper case.

R.M. Malkani v. State of Maharashtra

1973

Tape-recorded conversation is admissible if relevant, the voice is identified and accuracy is established; evidence obtained illegally is not excluded merely on that ground.

Ziyauddin Burhanuddin Bukhari v. Brijmohan Mehra

1976

Conditions for admissibility of tape records: voice identification, accuracy, absence of tampering, relevance.

Tomaso Bruno v. State of U.P.

2015

CCTV footage is relevant electronic evidence; failure to produce available CCTV can invite adverse inference.

✎Memory aids

  • A-A-P-P: Anvar (2014) -> Arjun (2020) -> Pune Bar (2026) - the certificate story.
  • S.63 = old 65B; S.62 = old 65A; S.61 = admissibility not denied.
  • Hash = digital fingerprint; Part A = operator, Part B = expert.

!Exam traps

  • TrapS.63 certificate is needed for printouts, CDs, pen drives, screenshots, WhatsApp exports - but NOT when the original device is produced and proved.
  • TrapOral evidence cannot replace the S.63(4) certificate (Anvar, Arjun Panditrao).
  • TrapNew BSA requirement: hash value + TWO signatories (person in charge AND expert). Old S.65B(4) needed only one person in a responsible position.
  • TrapNavjot Sandhu = overruled (by Anvar). Shafhi Mohammad = overruled (by Arjun Panditrao). Do not pick these as 'good law'.
  • TrapElectronic evidence is not 'hearsay' by nature; admissibility and weight are different questions.
  • TrapS.63 applies to civil as well as criminal proceedings.

?Likely questions

  1. Which BSA section replaced S.65B IEA?
  2. What new particular must the S.63(4) certificate carry? (hash value)
  3. Who signs Part B of the certificate? (expert - per Pune Bar Association, any person with special skill in computer science/cyber forensics)
  4. Is a certificate required when the original device is produced?
  5. Which case overruled Navjot Sandhu / Shafhi Mohammad?
