iBackground and transition

Summary
The BSA received Presidential assent on 25 December 2023 and came into force on 1 July 2024, repealing the Indian Evidence Act, 1872. It has 170 sections in 12 chapters (the IEA had 167 sections in 11 chapters) and adds a Schedule containing the electronic-record certificate. The architecture (relevancy -> proof -> production -> examination) is unchanged. Most changes concern electronic and digital records, definitions, some wording (coercion, gender-neutral and BNS/BNSS cross-references) and renumbering.
Transition rule
Proceedings pending before 1 July 2024 continue under the IEA (savings clause - S.170 BSA). BSA governs proceedings commenced after that date. Do not confuse this with BNS, where offences committed before 1 July 2024 are governed by the IPC.
Scope s1
The Act applies to judicial proceedings in or before any Court. It does NOT apply to affidavits presented to a Court or officer, or to proceedings before an arbitrator. (Classic MCQ: affidavits are not evidence under the Act.)

⚡Rapid-fire one-liners

  • BSA in force from 1 July 2024; repeals the Indian Evidence Act, 1872.
  • BSA: 170 sections, 12 chapters, one Schedule (electronic record certificate).
  • S.65B IEA = S.63 BSA.
  • S.63(4) certificate: Part A (person in charge) + Part B (expert) + hash value.
  • Pune Bar Association v. UoI (22 May 2026): S.63(4) valid; Part B can be signed by any person skilled in computer science/cyber forensics.
  • Anvar P.V. (2014): certificate mandatory; Navjot Sandhu overruled.
  • Arjun Panditrao (2020): Shafhi Mohammad overruled; certificate not needed when original device is produced.
  • Affidavits and arbitration proceedings are outside the Act.
  • Confession to police: never provable against the accused.
  • Discovery: only information that distinctly relates to the fact discovered.
  • Co-accused confession: corroborative only, never substantive.
  • Dying declaration need not be recorded by a Magistrate; may be sole basis of conviction.
  • Dowry death presumption: 'shall presume' (S.118 BSA).
  • Abetment of suicide within 7 years: 'may presume' (S.117 BSA).
  • Legitimacy: conclusive proof - birth within 280 days after dissolution.
  • S.109 BSA (106 IEA): special knowledge does not relieve the prosecution.
  • Accused's burden for exceptions: preponderance of probability.
  • Accomplice: competent witness; conviction on uncorroborated testimony not illegal; but corroboration is a rule of prudence.
  • No particular number of witnesses required (Vadivelu Thevar).
  • Leading questions: allowed in cross, not in chief/re-exam without permission.
  • Hostile witness: reliable part of evidence may still be used.
  • Judge's power to ask questions: S.168 BSA (165 IEA).
  • Judgments in rem under probate, matrimonial, admiralty, insolvency jurisdiction: conclusive.
  • TIP is corroborative evidence, not substantive.
  • Tofan Singh (2020): NDPS S.67 statements are inadmissible as confessions.
