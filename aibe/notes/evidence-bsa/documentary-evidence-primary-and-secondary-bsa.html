Documentary Evidence - Primary, Secondary, Proof of Execution and Oral v Documentary (S.56-60, S.64-103 BSA; IEA S.61-100)

iOverview

Contents of a document must be proved by primary evidence; secondary evidence is allowed only in the cases listed in S.60 BSA (S.65 IEA) after a foundation is laid. Know the exclusion of oral by documentary evidence and its provisos.

★Must know

  1. S.56 BSA (IEA 61): contents of documents may be proved by primary or secondary evidence.
  2. S.57 BSA (IEA 62) primary evidence: the document itself produced for the inspection of the Court. Counterparts executed by one party - primary against that party; documents made by one uniform process (printing, lithography, photography) - each is primary evidence of the rest, but copies of a common original are not primary evidence of the original.
  3. S.58 BSA (IEA 63) secondary evidence: certified copies; copies made from the original by mechanical processes which ensure accuracy and copies compared with such copies; copies compared with the original; counterparts against non-executing parties; oral accounts of contents given by a person who has himself seen the document.
  4. S.60 BSA (IEA 65): secondary evidence is admissible when (a) the original is in the possession of the adverse party/a person out of reach/not subject to process who fails to produce it after notice; (b) the existence, condition or contents are admitted in writing by the person against whom it is proved; (c) original destroyed or lost; (d) the original is not easily movable; (e) the original is a public document; (f) the original is a document of which a certified copy is permitted by law; (g) the original consists of numerous accounts that cannot conveniently be examined in Court. For (a), (c), (d) any secondary evidence is admissible; for (b) only the written admission; for (e) and (f) only a certified copy; for (g) the evidence of a skilled person. For electronic records, the S.63 route applies.
  5. Notice to produce (IEA S.66): not required where the document is itself a notice, the adverse party has the original or obtained it by fraud, etc.
  6. Proof of execution (IEA S.67 - signature and handwriting): where a document is alleged to be signed or written by a person, the signature/handwriting must be proved to be his. IEA S.68: if a document is required by law to be attested (will, mortgage, gift), it cannot be used as evidence until at least one attesting witness has been called to prove its execution, if alive, subject to the Court's process and capable of giving evidence. Exception (proviso): documents (other than a will) registered under the Registration Act need not be proved by an attesting witness unless execution is specifically denied.
  7. Proof of Will: propounder must remove suspicious circumstances; at least one attesting witness is mandatory (S.63 Indian Succession Act + S.68 IEA) - H. Venkatachala Iyengar v. B.N. Thimmajamma (1959); Janki Narayan Bhoir (2003); Meena Pradhan v. Kamla Pradhan (2023).
  8. Public documents (IEA S.74) and private documents (S.75); public documents are proved by certified copies (S.76-77); presumption as to genuineness of certified copies (S.79); of records of evidence (S.80); Gazettes and newspapers (S.81); documents thirty years old produced from proper custody (S.90) - Court may presume signature and execution genuine.
  9. Exclusion of oral by documentary evidence (IEA S.91-92): when the terms of a contract, grant or disposition of property have been reduced to writing, no evidence can be given in proof of those terms except the document itself (S.91), and no oral agreement contradicting, varying, adding to or subtracting from its terms is admissible between the parties (S.92).
  10. Provisos to IEA S.92: (1) fraud, intimidation, illegality, want of due execution, want of capacity, absence/failure of consideration, mistake of fact or law may be proved; (2) a separate oral agreement on a matter on which the document is silent and which is not inconsistent with its terms; (3) a separate oral agreement constituting a condition precedent to the attaching of any obligation; (4) a subsequent oral agreement to rescind or modify (except where the contract is required by law to be in writing or registered); (5) usage or custom annexing incidents to contracts; (6) facts showing how the language relates to existing facts.
  11. Ambiguity: patent ambiguity cannot be cured by extrinsic evidence (IEA S.93); latent ambiguity can (IEA S.95-97).

⚖Landmark cases

H. Siddiqui v. A. Ramalingam

2011

Secondary evidence cannot be given without laying the foundation under S.65; non-production of the original must be satisfactorily explained.

Roman Catholic Mission v. State of Madras

1966

Where the original is not produced, secondary evidence can be given only on satisfying the conditions of S.65.

Roop Kumar v. Mohan Thedani

2003

Interpretation of S.91-92: when a document is the sole repository of the terms of a contract, oral evidence is excluded.

H. Venkatachala Iyengar v. B.N. Thimmajamma

1959

Principles on proof of Wills and removal of suspicious circumstances.

Janki Narayan Bhoir v. Narayan Namdeo Kadam

2003

Calling an attesting witness is a mandatory requirement to prove a Will; mere proof of testator's signature is insufficient.

Meena Pradhan v. Kamla Pradhan

2023

Reiterated rules on proof of Will and burden on the propounder to dispel suspicious circumstances.

R.V.E. Venkatachala Gounder v. Arulmigu Viswesaraswami

2003

Objection to the MODE of proof (e.g. unstamped/secondary copy) must be taken when the document is tendered, not later; objection to admissibility on law can be taken later.

✎Memory aids

  • S.65: Always Bring Close Duplicates Probably Fully (a-b-c-d-e-f-g cases).
  • Provisos to S.92: F-Mistake-Silence-Condition-Subsequent-Usage-Language.

!Exam traps

  • TrapPrimary evidence: photocopies of a common original are NOT primary.
  • TrapSection 91 excludes oral evidence about the terms; S.92 excludes oral agreements contradicting them. Proviso (1) - fraud etc. - is the favourite exception.
  • TrapSecondary evidence needs a foundation (notice, non-availability) and cannot be admitted as of right.
  • TrapRegistered documents need no attesting witness, but a will always needs one.
  • TrapUnattested document does not require attesting witness proof.
  • TrapPatent ambiguity cannot be explained; latent ambiguity can.
