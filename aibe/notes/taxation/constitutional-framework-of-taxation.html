iOverview

Who can tax what; limits on taxing power; tax v. fee; interpretation of taxing statutes; tax avoidance v. evasion.

★Must know

  1. Art 265: no tax shall be levied or collected except by authority of law - the foundation; requires a valid statute (not executive order) and a valid collection (2026 Supreme Court cases reaffirm that tax cannot be retained or collected without authority of law). Art 246 with the Seventh Schedule distributes taxing powers; Art 246A (101st Amendment, 2016) - special provision for GST: Parliament and State Legislatures have simultaneous power; Parliament has exclusive power over inter-State supply (Art 269A). Art 279A: GST Council.
  2. Union List (List I) tax entries: Entry 82 - taxes on income other than agricultural income; Entry 83 - duties of customs including export duties; Entry 84 - duties of excise on tobacco and other goods manufactured or produced (post-GST confined to petroleum crude, high-speed diesel, motor spirit, natural gas, aviation turbine fuel and tobacco products); Entry 85 - corporation tax; Entry 86 - taxes on the capital value of assets (excluding agricultural land); Entry 88 - taxes other than stamp duties on... (estate duty omitted/not levied); Entry 89 - terminal taxes on goods or passengers carried by railway, sea or air; Entry 91 - rates of stamp duty in respect of bills of exchange, cheques, promissory notes, bills of lading, letters of credit, policies of insurance, transfer of shares, debentures, proxies and receipts; Entry 97 - residuary (taxes not in lists).
  3. State List (List II) tax entries: Entry 45 - land revenue; Entry 46 - taxes on agricultural income; Entry 47 - duties in respect of succession to agricultural land; Entry 48 - estate duty on agricultural land; Entry 49 - taxes on lands and buildings; Entry 50 - taxes on mineral rights subject to limitations imposed by Parliament by law relating to mineral development; Entry 51 - duties of excise on alcoholic liquors for human consumption and opium, Indian hemp and other narcotic drugs (but not medicinal and toilet preparations); Entry 52 - taxes on the entry of goods into a local area for consumption, use or sale (octroi - subsumed by GST); Entry 54 - taxes on the sale or purchase of goods other than newspapers (now limited to petroleum products and alcohol for human consumption after GST); Entry 55 - taxes on advertisements other than in newspapers; Entry 56 - taxes on goods and passengers carried by road or inland waterways; Entry 57 - taxes on vehicles; Entry 58 - taxes on animals and boats; Entry 59 - tolls; Entry 60 - taxes on professions, trades, callings and employments; Entry 61 - capitation taxes; Entry 62 - taxes on entertainment, amusements, betting and gambling (local); Entry 63 - rates of stamp duty on documents other than those in List I.
  4. Art 276: tax on professions, trades, callings and employments - maximum Rs 2,500 per person per year (by the 60th Amendment 1988). Art 268 - stamp duties and excise on medicinal and toilet preparations levied by Union but collected and appropriated by States; Art 269 - taxes on consignment/ sale of goods in inter-State trade (Union levied, assigned to States); Art 269A - GST on inter-State supply levied and collected by the Government of India and apportioned between Union and States by the GST Council recommendations; Art 270 - taxes levied by the Union and distributed between the Union and States (income tax other than agricultural, and GST (CGST)); Art 271 - Parliament may levy a surcharge for Union purposes only; Art 275 and 282 grants; Art 280 Finance Commission (16th FC - Chairman Arvind Panagariya - award period 2026-31); Art 285 - exemption of Union property from State taxation; Art 289 - State property exempt from Union taxation (except trade or business of the State); Art 286 - restrictions on State taxes on sale or purchase of goods outside the State and in the course of import/export; Art 287-288 (electricity; water).
  5. 101st Amendment (2016) and GST: introduced Art 246A, 269A, 279A, Entry 54 amended; the 'goods and services tax' defined in Art 366(12A) (tax on supply of goods or services or both except alcoholic liquor for human consumption); GST on petroleum crude, HSD, motor spirit, natural gas and ATF applies from the date notified by Council on recommendation (not yet). GST Council: Union Finance Minister (Chairperson), Union Minister of State in charge of Revenue, State Ministers in charge of Finance; quorum one-half of members; decisions by three-fourths of weighted votes (Centre one-third, States two-thirds); recommendations are persuasive, not binding (Union of India v. Mohit Minerals Pvt. Ltd., 2022). [Cross-ref: Constitutional Law.]
  6. Tax, fee and cess: tax - compulsory exaction for public purposes without any specific quid pro quo; fee - payment for specific services rendered with a broad correlation (Commissioner, Hindu Religious Endowments v. Sri Lakshmindra Thirtha Swamiar of Sri Shirur Mutt, 1954); fee must be related to the cost of service in a broad sense (Kewal Krishan Puri v. State of Punjab, 1979); a cess is a tax levied for a specific purpose; surcharges are additional taxes. Retrospective taxation is permissible but subject to Arts 14, 19, 20(1); Vodafone International Holdings B.V. v. Union of India (2012) - indirect transfer of shares of a foreign company not taxable in India under the law then in force; the Finance Act 2012 retrospective amendment (Explanation to s.9(1)(i)) was later withdrawn by the Taxation Laws (Amendment) Act 2021 (no demands for pre-2012 indirect transfers).
  7. Interpretation: charging provisions are strictly construed - 'there is no equity about a tax' (Cape Brandy Syndicate v. IRC, 1921); ambiguity in charging provision is resolved in favour of the assessee, but in exemption notifications, ambiguity is resolved in favour of the Revenue (Commissioner of Customs v. Dilip Kumar & Co., 2018, Constitution Bench); machinery provisions are construed liberally; contemporanea expositio; 'look at' not 'look through' (Vodafone); substance over form in avoidance cases: CIT v. McDowell & Co. (1985) (colourable devices not allowed) but Union of India v. Azadi Bachao Andolan (2003) (tax planning is legitimate within the framework of law); GAAR (Chapter X-A, ss.95-102) effective 1 April 2017; Judicial anti-avoidance rule.
  8. Tax evasion (illegal), tax avoidance (legal but defeating the purpose) and tax planning (within law). Tax and Article 14: classification for taxation allowed with wide latitude (R.K. Garg v. Union of India, 1981; East India Tobacco v. State of A.P., 1962); Art 19(1)(g): tax cannot be confiscatory; Art 20(1): no retrospective penalty; Art 300A: property and tax.
  9. Delegated taxation: essential legislative function (levy) cannot be delegated; rate-fixing may be delegated within guidelines (Hamdard Dawakhana v. Union of India, 1960; Gwalior Rayon Mfg. v. Assistant Commissioner of Sales Tax, 1974); delegated legislation is effective only on publication per statute (Viraj Impex v. Union of India, 2026 Verify); tax statutes and 'law' - Art 265.
  10. Tax tribunals: ITAT (constituted under s.252 of the Income-tax Act 1961); CESTAT; GST Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT, s.109 CGST Act); Authority for Advance Ruling; Settlement Commission replaced by Interim Board for Settlement (2021); Dispute Resolution Committee; Tribunals Reforms Act 2021 and the L. Chandra Kumar principle - tribunals subject to High Court's writ jurisdiction (cross-ref Constitutional Law).

⚖Landmark cases

Commissioner, Hindu Religious Endowments v. Sri Lakshmindra Thirtha Swamiar (Shirur Mutt)

1954

Distinction between tax, fee and special assessment.

CIT v. McDowell & Co. Ltd.

1985

Colourable devices to avoid tax are not permissible; tax planning vs avoidance.

Union of India v. Azadi Bachao Andolan

2003

Treaty shopping/legitimate tax planning is valid; McDowell narrowed.

Vodafone International Holdings B.V. v. Union of India

2012

'Look at' the transaction; indirect transfer outside India not taxable; retrospective amendment followed and later repealed.

Commissioner of Customs v. Dilip Kumar & Co.

2018

Constitution Bench: exemption provisions strictly interpreted; ambiguity benefits the Revenue.

R.K. Garg v. Union of India

1981

Economic and fiscal legislation gets wide latitude under Art 14.

Union of India v. Mohit Minerals Pvt. Ltd.

2022

GST Council recommendations are not binding.

Mineral Area Development Authority v. SAIL

2024

Nine judges (8:1): States can tax mineral rights; royalty is not a tax.

✎Memory aids

  • Union: income (82), customs (83), corporation tax (85). State: agri income (46), land/buildings (49), profession (60).
  • Professional tax cap: Rs 2,500.

!Exam traps

  • TrapAgricultural income tax is a State subject (Entry 46, List II); income tax on all other income is Union (Entry 82, List I).
  • TrapAlcohol for human consumption is outside GST; the State levies excise on it (Entry 51 List II) and VAT (Entry 54).
  • TrapGST Council recommendations are not binding on Parliament or State Legislatures.
  • TrapSurcharge can be levied only by the Union for its own purposes (Art 271).
