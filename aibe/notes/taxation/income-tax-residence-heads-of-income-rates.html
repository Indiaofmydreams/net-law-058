Income-tax: Basic Concepts, Residential Status, Heads of Income, Exemptions, Deductions and Rates

iOverview

Concepts a legal aspirant is expected to know: 'person', 'income', previous year, residence, five heads, important exemptions, deductions, capital gains rates and slabs.

★Must know

  1. Income-tax Act 2025: replaces the Income-tax Act 1961 from 1 April 2026; 'Tax Year' replaces the dual concept of 'previous year' and 'assessment year' (the first Tax Year is 2026-27); fewer sections and chapters than the 1961 Act (the 2025 Act has 536 sections, 23 chapters and 16 schedules Verify); new regime slabs now in s.202 Verify; the substance of tax law is largely retained. Income earned in Financial Year 2025-26 is assessed in AY 2026-27 under the 1961 Act; Tax Year 2026-27 (FY 2026-27) is the first under the 2025 Act. Proceedings for earlier years continue under the 1961 Act by savings. Examination papers in 2026-27 may use either; understand both.
  2. Charge (s.4 of 1961 Act): income-tax is charged on the total income of the previous year of every person at the rates fixed by the annual Finance Act. 'Person' (s.2(31)): an individual, a Hindu undivided family, a company, a firm, an association of persons or body of individuals, a local authority, and every artificial juridical person. 'Assessee' (s.2(7)): person by whom any tax or other sum is payable. 'Income' (s.2(24)): an inclusive definition covering profits and gains, dividend, voluntary contributions, perquisites, capital gains, winnings from lotteries etc. 'Previous year' (s.3): the financial year immediately preceding the assessment year (1 April - 31 March); 'assessment year' (s.2(9)): the 12 months commencing 1 April following the previous year. Agricultural income (s.2(1A)): rent or revenue from land used for agriculture; income from operations on agricultural produce; farm building - exempt from income-tax under s.10(1) (but taken into account for rate purposes under 'partial integration').
  3. Residential status (s.6): an individual is resident if (a) he is in India for 182 days or more in the previous year; or (b) he is in India for 60 days or more in the previous year and 365 days or more in the four preceding years. Exceptions: for an Indian citizen or person of Indian origin leaving India for employment abroad or as crew, the 60-day limit is replaced by 182 days; for a citizen or PIO who visits India with total income (other than foreign sources) exceeding Rs 15 lakh, the limit is 120 days (and 365 days in the four preceding years). Deemed resident (s.6(1A)): an Indian citizen having total income (other than foreign source) exceeding Rs 15 lakh who is not liable to tax in any other country. Resident but not ordinarily resident (RNOR): non-resident in 9 of the 10 preceding years or has been in India for 729 days or less in the 7 preceding years. A Hindu undivided family or firm is resident unless control and management are wholly outside India. A company is resident if it is an Indian company or its place of effective management (POEM) in that year is in India (s.6(3)). Tax incidence: resident and ordinarily resident - global income; RNOR - income received/accruing in India and income from a business controlled in or profession set up in India; non-resident - income received or accruing/deemed to accrue in India.
  4. Scope of total income (s.5) and deemed accrual (s.9): income deemed to accrue or arise in India includes income through a business connection in India, property, asset or source of income in India, transfer of a capital asset situated in India (including indirect transfers - Explanation 5), salary for services rendered in India, interest/royalty/fees for technical services payable by residents; 'significant economic presence' rules (Explanation 2A to s.9(1)(i)) for digital businesses.
  5. Heads of income (s.14): (1) Salaries (ss.15-17) - standard deduction Rs 75,000 under new regime (Rs 50,000 under old); perquisites; leave encashment; gratuity exemption. (2) Income from house property (ss.22-27) - annual value; standard deduction 30% of net annual value; interest on housing loan (s.24(b)) up to Rs 2 lakh for self-occupied property (old regime). (3) Profits and gains of business or profession (ss.28-44) - allowable deductions (s.30-37), depreciation (s.32), presumptive taxation (ss.44AD, 44ADA, 44AE), disallowances (s.40, 40A), tax audit (s.44AB) for turnover over Rs 1 crore (Rs 10 crore if cash transactions under 5%). (4) Capital gains (ss.45-55A): gains on transfer of capital asset; short-term if held 24 months or less (12 months or less for listed securities and equity-oriented funds); long-term otherwise; exemptions in ss.54, 54B, 54EC, 54F; cost of acquisition; indexation withdrawn for transfers after 23 July 2024. (5) Income from other sources (ss.56-59): dividend, interest, gifts exceeding Rs 50,000 from non-relatives (s.56(2)(x)), winnings from lotteries and games, family pension.
  6. Capital gains rates (FY 2025-26, applicable from 23 July 2024): short-term capital gains on listed equity shares and equity-oriented mutual funds where STT is paid - 20% (s.111A); long-term capital gains on listed equity - 12.5% on gains exceeding Rs 1.25 lakh (s.112A); long-term capital gains on other assets - 12.5% without indexation (s.112); STCG on other assets - at slab rates. Securities Transaction Tax (STT) levied under Chapter VII of the Finance (No.2) Act 2004.
  7. Exempt incomes (s.10): agricultural income; allowances for HRA and LTA (subject to rules); gratuity (limits); amount received on maturity of life insurance (subject to s.10(10D) conditions); scholarships; share of profit from a firm; dividend from Indian companies is taxable (post-2020); income of a local authority; political party income (s.13A) subject to conditions; charitable and religious trusts (ss.11-13) - registration under s.12AB; Section 10(1) agricultural; PPF interest.
  8. Deductions from gross total income (Chapter VI-A; available in the old regime and some in new): s.80C (up to Rs 1.5 lakh - life insurance, PPF, ELSS, tuition fees, repayment of home loan principal, NSC); 80CCD(1B) (additional Rs 50,000 NPS); 80D (health insurance premium up to Rs 25,000, Rs 50,000 for senior citizens); 80E (interest on education loan - no limit); 80G (donations); 80GG (rent); 80TTA/80TTB (savings account interest up to Rs 10,000; Rs 50,000 for seniors); 80U (disability); 80CCD(2) (employer NPS contribution - allowed in new regime too). Under the default new regime (s.115BAC), most Chapter VI-A deductions and exemptions are not allowed except 80CCD(2) and a few.
  9. Set-off and carry forward (ss.70-80): intra-head and inter-head set-off; house property loss set-off limited to Rs 2 lakh per year against other heads; carry forward of business loss for 8 years, capital loss for 8 years, speculative loss for 4 years; unabsorbed depreciation indefinitely. Clubbing (ss.60-64): income of spouse (remuneration from concern in which the other spouse has substantial interest), minor child's income (s.64(1A)), transfer of asset to spouse without adequate consideration - clubbed with the transferor's income.
  10. Individual slabs - FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27): New regime (default; s.115BAC): up to Rs 4 lakh - nil; Rs 4-8 lakh - 5%; Rs 8-12 lakh - 10%; Rs 12-16 lakh - 15%; Rs 16-20 lakh - 20%; Rs 20-24 lakh - 25%; above Rs 24 lakh - 30%. Rebate under s.87A (new regime): full rebate for total income up to Rs 12 lakh (maximum Rs 60,000), effectively no tax up to Rs 12.75 lakh of salary income with the standard deduction. Old regime (opt-in): up to Rs 2.5 lakh - nil (Rs 3 lakh for senior citizens, Rs 5 lakh for super seniors); Rs 2.5-5 lakh - 5%; Rs 5-10 lakh - 20%; above Rs 10 lakh - 30%; rebate up to Rs 12,500 for income up to Rs 5 lakh. Health and education cess: 4% on tax plus surcharge. Surcharge for incomes above Rs 50 lakh; capped at 25% in the new regime. Slabs for Tax Year 2026-27 under the 2025 Act continue as above unless changed by the Finance Act 2026 Verify.
  11. Corporate tax: domestic company - 22% (plus surcharge 10% and cess 4% = 25.17%) under s.115BAA if it forgoes exemptions/incentives; 25% for companies with turnover not exceeding Rs 400 crore (earlier regime); 30% otherwise; new manufacturing companies s.115BAB (15%) expired for those commencing production after 31 March 2024; foreign companies 35% plus surcharge; Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT) 15% of book profit under s.115JB (not applicable to s.115BAA companies); partnership firms and LLPs 30%; cooperative societies 22%/25%/30%. Alternate Minimum Tax (AMT) for non-corporate assessees (s.115JC) 18.5%.
  12. Important concepts: 'agricultural income' is exempt; 'casual income' (lotteries, crossword puzzles) is taxed at 30% flat (s.115BB); 'unexplained cash credits' (s.68) taxed at 60% plus surcharge/cess (s.115BBE); 'gifts' (s.56(2)(x)) - exempt from relatives; 'dividend' taxable at slab rates; 'perquisites' as per Rules 3; 'salary' includes basic, DA, bonus, commission, advance; 'income from undisclosed sources'; 'income by deeming' - s.69 etc. Landmark: Navinchandra Mafatlal v. CIT (1954) - 'capital gains' covers transfer of shares; Sumati Dayal v. CIT (1995) - real income and human probabilities; K.P. Varghese v. ITO (1981) - burden under s.52 is on the Revenue; CIT v. Durga Prasad More (1971) - benami/ real nature of transactions.

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
s.4 (1961 Act)Charge of income-tax on total income of the previous year.
s.6Residential status: 182 days or 60 days + 365 days in preceding four years.
s.14Five heads of income: Salaries; House property; PGBP; Capital gains; Other sources.
s.115BACDefault new tax regime; basic exemption Rs 4 lakh; s.87A rebate up to Rs 12 lakh income.
s.80CDeduction up to Rs 1.5 lakh (old regime).

Capital gains - transfer of shares during winding-up; broad concept of 'capital asset' and 'transfer'.

Real income and surrounding circumstances; human probabilities; 'unexplained credits'.

s.52 - burden on Revenue to prove understatement; section read down.

Indirect transfer not taxable; look at the transaction.

✎Memory aids

  • Five heads: 'S-H-B-C-O' - Salaries, House property, Business/Profession, Capital gains, Other sources.
  • Residence: 182 days OR (60 + 365 days in last 4 years).

!Exam traps

  • TrapAgricultural income is exempt but included for rate purposes in some cases.
  • TrapTax Year replaces 'previous year/assessment year' only from FY 2026-27 under the 2025 Act.
  • TrapResidence of a company turns on incorporation in India or POEM.
  • TrapNew regime is the default; the old regime must be opted for.

?Likely questions

  1. Which section defines residential status? (s.6, 1961 Act)
  2. How many heads of income are there? (Five)
  3. Under the new regime, income up to what amount attracts nil tax after rebate for FY 2025-26? (Rs 12 lakh)
  4. The Income-tax Act 2025 comes into force on? (1 April 2026)
