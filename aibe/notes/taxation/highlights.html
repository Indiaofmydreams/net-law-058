1

Constitutional Framework of Taxation and General Principles of Tax Law

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Art 265No tax except by authority of law.
Art 246AConcurrent power on GST (inter-State supply: Parliament only).
Art 276Professional tax cap: Rs 2,500 per annum.
Entry 82, List ITaxes on income other than agricultural income (Union).
Entry 46, List IITaxes on agricultural income (State).
Art 289States' property exempt from Union taxation except for trade.

Cases in one line

  • Commissioner, Hindu Religious Endowments v. Sri Lakshmindra Thirtha Swamiar (Shirur Mutt) 1954 Distinction between tax, fee and special assessment.
  • CIT v. McDowell & Co. Ltd. 1985 Colourable devices to avoid tax are not permissible; tax planning vs avoidance.
  • Union of India v. Azadi Bachao Andolan 2003 Treaty shopping/legitimate tax planning is valid; McDowell narrowed.
  • Vodafone International Holdings B.V. v. Union of India 2012 'Look at' the transaction; indirect transfer outside India not taxable; retrospective amendment followed and later repealed.
  • Commissioner of Customs v. Dilip Kumar & Co. 2018 Constitution Bench: exemption provisions strictly interpreted; ambiguity benefits the Revenue.
  • R.K. Garg v. Union of India 1981 Economic and fiscal legislation gets wide latitude under Art 14.
  • Union of India v. Mohit Minerals Pvt. Ltd. 2022 GST Council recommendations are not binding.
  • Mineral Area Development Authority v. SAIL 2024 Nine judges (8:1): States can tax mineral rights; royalty is not a tax.

Memory aids

  • Union: income (82), customs (83), corporation tax (85). State: agri income (46), land/buildings (49), profession (60).
  • Professional tax cap: Rs 2,500.

Exam traps

  • TrapAgricultural income tax is a State subject (Entry 46, List II); income tax on all other income is Union (Entry 82, List I).
  • TrapAlcohol for human consumption is outside GST; the State levies excise on it (Entry 51 List II) and VAT (Entry 54).
  • TrapGST Council recommendations are not binding on Parliament or State Legislatures.
  • TrapSurcharge can be levied only by the Union for its own purposes (Art 271).
2

Income-tax: Basic Concepts, Residential Status, Heads of Income, Exemptions, Deductions and Rates

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

s.4 (1961 Act)Charge of income-tax on total income of the previous year.
s.6Residential status: 182 days or 60 days + 365 days in preceding four years.
s.14Five heads of income: Salaries; House property; PGBP; Capital gains; Other sources.
s.115BACDefault new tax regime; basic exemption Rs 4 lakh; s.87A rebate up to Rs 12 lakh income.
s.80CDeduction up to Rs 1.5 lakh (old regime).

Cases in one line

  • Navinchandra Mafatlal v. CIT 1954 Capital gains - transfer of shares during winding-up; broad concept of 'capital asset' and 'transfer'.
  • Sumati Dayal v. CIT 1995 Real income and surrounding circumstances; human probabilities; 'unexplained credits'.
  • K.P. Varghese v. ITO 1981 s.52 - burden on Revenue to prove understatement; section read down.
  • CIT v. McDowell & Co. 1985 Tax avoidance through colourable devices not allowed.
  • Union of India v. Azadi Bachao Andolan 2003 Treaty shopping; tax planning allowed.
  • Vodafone International Holdings v. Union of India 2012 Indirect transfer not taxable; look at the transaction.

Memory aids

  • Five heads: 'S-H-B-C-O' - Salaries, House property, Business/Profession, Capital gains, Other sources.
  • Residence: 182 days OR (60 + 365 days in last 4 years).

Exam traps

  • TrapAgricultural income is exempt but included for rate purposes in some cases.
  • TrapTax Year replaces 'previous year/assessment year' only from FY 2026-27 under the 2025 Act.
  • TrapResidence of a company turns on incorporation in India or POEM.
  • TrapNew regime is the default; the old regime must be opted for.
3

Income-tax Procedure: TDS, Advance Tax, Returns, Assessment, Search, Appeals, Penalties and Prosecution

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

s.139(1)Due dates: 31 July (non-audit), 31 October (audit), 30 November (transfer pricing).
ss.207-211Advance tax: 15%, 45%, 75%, 100% (15 Jun / 15 Sep / 15 Dec / 15 Mar).
s.143(2)Scrutiny notice within 3 months from the end of the financial year of filing the return.
ss.246A, 253, 260AAppeals: CIT(A) 30 days; ITAT 60 days; High Court 120 days (substantial question of law).
s.270APenalty: 50% (under-reporting) / 200% (misreporting).
s.276CWilful tax evasion: 6 months-7 years if tax evaded exceeds Rs 25 lakh.

Cases in one line

  • Binoy Viswam v. Union of India 2017 s.139AA (Aadhaar-PAN linking) upheld; privacy argument rejected on facts; not applicable to those who have no Aadhaar.
  • Union of India v. Ashish Agarwal 2022 Treated reassessment notices under the old regime as notices under s.148A; relief for assessees.
  • Union of India v. Rajeev Bansal 2024 Applied TOLA to reassessment; timelines for notices issued during Covid.
  • Pooran Mal v. Director of Inspection 1974 Evidence from a search is admissible even if the search is irregular.
  • Union of India v. Ganpati Dealcom Pvt. Ltd. 2022 Benami Act s.3(2) and s.5 (2016 amendment) unconstitutional / prospective only.
  • ITO v. Lakhmani Mewal Das 1976 'Reason to believe' for reassessment must have a rational connection with the belief.

Memory aids

  • Appeal timeline: 30 (CIT(A)) - 60 (ITAT) - 120 (HC).
  • Advance tax: 15-45-75-100.

Exam traps

  • Traps.143(2) notice is time-barred after 3 months from the end of the FY in which the return was filed.
  • TrapITAT is the last fact-finding authority; HC under s.260A hears only substantial questions of law.
  • TrapProsecution for TDS default (s.276B) has a minimum of three months' imprisonment.
4

GST (I): CGST/IGST Framework, Supply, Levy, Registration, Composition, Input Tax Credit, Returns and Rates

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

CGST s.7Scope of 'supply' - Schedules I, II, III.
CGST s.9Levy and collection; reverse charge.
CGST s.16Eligibility and conditions for ITC; 30 November time limit.
CGST s.17(5)Blocked credits.
CGST s.22Registration: Rs 40 lakh (goods) / Rs 20 lakh (services); special category Rs 20/10 lakh.
CGST s.10Composition scheme up to Rs 1.5 crore turnover.

Cases in one line

  • Union of India v. Mohit Minerals Pvt. Ltd. 2022 GST Council recommendations not binding; cooperative federalism.
  • Safari Retreats Pvt. Ltd. v. Chief Commissioner of CGST 2024 Functionality test for 'plant' in s.17(5)(d); ITC on construction of building used for renting not automatically blocked.
  • Union of India v. VKC Footsteps India Pvt. Ltd. 2021 Inverted duty refund is for input goods only (not input services); Rule 89(5) valid.
  • Union of India v. Filco Trade Centre 2022 Right to rectify GST returns / carry forward credit recognised in the context of technical glitches Verify.

Memory aids

  • GST: 'CGST + SGST (intra-State); IGST (inter-State); UTGST (UT).'
  • Rates after 22 Sept 2025: 5% / 18% / 40% (plus special rates).
  • Registration threshold: Rs 40 lakh goods; Rs 20 lakh services.

Exam traps

  • TrapComposition scheme cannot be used for inter-State supplies or by e-commerce operators.
  • TrapSchedule III items (e.g., sale of land) are neither goods nor services - no GST.
  • TrapAlcohol for human consumption is outside GST.
  • TrapITC time limit: 30 November following the end of the FY or date of annual return, whichever earlier.
5

GST (II): Assessment, Demand and Recovery, Refunds, Appeals (GSTAT), Offences and Penalties

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

CGST s.54Refund: within two years from the relevant date.
CGST s.73 / 74 / 74ADemand and recovery - non-fraud/ fraud; unified s.74A from FY 2024-25.
CGST s.107 / 112Appeal to First Appellate Authority (3 months; 10% pre-deposit) / GSTAT (3 months; further 10%).
CGST s.132Offences and punishment: > Rs 5 crore - up to 5 years; non-bailable.
CGST s.69Power to arrest: only for specified offences.

Cases in one line

  • Radhika Agarwal v. Union of India 2025 Upheld validity of ss.69, 132 and others but subjected arrest powers to Cr.P.C. safeguards, 'reasons to believe'; anticipatory bail available Verify.
  • Union of India v. Mohit Minerals 2022 Council recommendations persuasive.

Memory aids

  • GST appeals: s.107 (3 months, 10% pre-deposit) -> s.112 GSTAT (3 months, +10%) -> s.117 High Court (180 days) -> s.118 Supreme Court.
  • s.132: >Rs 5 crore = up to 5 years + non-bailable.

Exam traps

  • TrapGST arrest power is restricted to offences above Rs 5 crore (and fake invoice cases).
  • TrapGSTAT is the second appeal; the first is before the Appellate Authority.
  • TrapPre-deposit under s.107 is 10% of the disputed tax (max Rs 25 crore each); under s.112 an additional 10% (max Rs 20 crore each).
6

Customs, Stamp Duty and Other Taxes

Medium yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Customs Act s.12Levy of customs duty on imports and exports.
Customs Act s.14Valuation of goods - transaction value.
Customs Act s.128 / 129AAppeals to Commissioner (Appeals) and CESTAT.
Indian Stamp Act s.35Instruments not duly stamped inadmissible in evidence.

Cases in one line

  • In Re: Interplay between Arbitration Agreements and the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 2023 Seven-judge Bench: unstamped agreement inadmissible but not void; curable; overruled N.N. Global.
  • Commissioner of Customs v. Dilip Kumar & Co. 2018 Exemption notifications strictly interpreted.
  • Union of India v. Ganpati Dealcom 2022 Benami Act s.3(2) unconstitutional; prospective operation.
  • Automobile Transport (Rajasthan) Ltd. v. State of Rajasthan 1962 Compensatory taxes do not violate Art 301.

Memory aids

  • Customs appeals: 60 days (Commissioner Appeals) - CESTAT - HC - SC.

Exam traps

  • TrapStamp duty on bills of exchange etc. is a Union subject (Entry 91 List I); stamp duty on other documents is a State subject (Entry 63 List II).
  • TrapUnstamped agreement is inadmissible, not void (2023, seven judges).
