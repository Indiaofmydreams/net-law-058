Income-tax Procedure: TDS, Advance Tax, Returns, Assessment, Search, Appeals, Penalties and Prosecution

iOverview

Collection (TDS, advance tax), returns, scrutiny and reassessment, appellate hierarchy and criminal provisions.

★Must know

  1. PAN (s.139A) is compulsory for specified transactions; linking with Aadhaar (s.139AA) upheld in Binoy Viswam v. Union of India (2017) subject to the limitation that it cannot apply to persons without Aadhaar. TAN for tax deductors.
  2. Tax deduction at source (TDS) (Chapter XVII-B): principle of 'pay as you earn'; s.192 - salary (at average rate); s.193 - interest on securities; s.194 - dividends (10%); s.194A - interest other than on securities (10%, threshold Rs 50,000 for banks and Rs 1 lakh for senior citizens from FY 2025-26 Verify); s.194C - payments to contractors (1% individual/HUF; 2% others); s.194I - rent (2% for machinery and 10% for land/building); s.194J - professional or technical fees (10%; 2% for technical services); s.194Q - purchase of goods exceeding Rs 50 lakh (0.1%); s.195 - payments to non-residents; s.206AA - higher TDS rate of 20% if PAN is not furnished; s.206C - TCS (tax collected at source) on specified goods and transactions. Deductor must deposit TDS by the 7th of the next month, issue TDS certificates (Form 16/16A), and file quarterly returns; failure leads to disallowance (s.40(a)(ia)), interest (s.201(1A)) and penalty; prosecution for failure to deposit TDS (s.276B). Under the 2025 Act, the TDS provisions are consolidated in s.393 Verify.
  3. Advance tax (ss.207-211): payable if estimated tax liability for the year is Rs 10,000 or more; due in instalments: 15% by 15 June; 45% by 15 September; 75% by 15 December; 100% by 15 March; presumptive taxpayers under s.44AD/44ADA pay in a single instalment by 15 March; interest under ss.234B (default in advance tax) and 234C (deferment of instalments) at 1% per month; s.234A interest for delay in filing return.
  4. Returns: s.139(1) - due date for individuals not requiring audit: 31 July; companies and persons requiring audit: 31 October; transfer pricing cases: 30 November; belated return s.139(4) up to 31 December of the assessment year (with fees under s.234F); revised return s.139(5); updated return s.139(8A) - within 24 months from the end of the assessment year (extended to 48 months by the Finance Act 2025 Verify) on payment of additional tax of 25% or 50%; return not required for those below taxable limit except specified conditions. Under the 2025 Act the due dates are by reference to the 'tax year'.
  5. Assessments: processing of return - s.143(1) (intimation; adjustments), done by Centralised Processing Centre; scrutiny assessment - notice under s.143(2) must be served within three months from the end of the financial year in which the return is filed; assessment order under s.143(3); best judgment assessment - s.144 (non-compliance); faceless assessment scheme - s.144B; reassessment - ss.147-151: s.148A mandatory inquiry and show-cause; notice under s.148 within three years from the end of the assessment year, extendable up to 10 years where the income escaping assessment is Rs 50 lakh or more represented in assets or expenditure (s.149); sanction by specified authority; Union of India v. Ashish Agarwal (2022) and Union of India v. Rajeev Bansal (2024) - treatment of reassessment notices issued after 1 April 2021 under the old regime and TOLA relaxation; the Finance Act 2026 reportedly restored a Joint Assessing Officer/ section 147A route with retrospective effect, and the Supreme Court in Sept 2026 stayed a Punjab and Haryana High Court decision striking it down Verify. Limitation for completing assessment: s.153 (9 months from the end of the assessment year in which the income was first assessable - for scrutiny assessments) Verify.
  6. Search, seizure and survey: s.132 - search and seizure authorised by Principal Director/ Director General/ Chief Commissioner on 'reason to believe' based on information; panchnama; seizure; presumption under s.132(4A) that books/documents found belong to the person; s.133A - survey (no seizure; limited power to impound); s.131 - powers of civil court (summons, examination on oath). Courts have held search must follow strict procedure; Pooran Mal v. Director of Inspection (1974) - illegal search does not invalidate seized material; ITO v. Seth Brothers (1969) - 'reason to believe'.
  7. Appeals: first appeal to the Commissioner of Income-tax (Appeals) (now National Faceless Appeal Centre) under s.246A within 30 days of service of the demand/order (s.249); second appeal to the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal under s.253 within 60 days of receipt of the CIT(A)'s order (s.253(3)) - ITAT is the final fact-finding authority; appeal to the High Court under s.260A only on a substantial question of law within 120 days; appeal to the Supreme Court under s.261 (certificate of fitness) or by Special Leave Petition under Art 136. Dispute Resolution Panel (s.144C) for eligible assessees (foreign companies, transfer pricing cases). Revision: s.263 - Principal Commissioner can revise an order that is erroneous and prejudicial to the interest of the Revenue (within two years from end of financial year in which order was passed); s.264 - revision in favour of the assessee (within one year). Rectification of mistake apparent from the record - s.154 (within four years from the end of the financial year). Vivad se Vishwas Scheme 2024; Interim Board for Settlement; Board for Advance Rulings.
  8. Penalties: s.270A - under-reporting of income: 50% of tax payable on under-reported income; misreporting: 200%; s.271AAB (search cases); s.271B (failure to get books audited); s.271F (failure to file return); s.272A and s.272B (default in complying with notices / PAN); s.271C (failure to deduct TDS); penalty orders appealable to CIT(A). Prosecution (Chapter XXII): s.276B - failure to pay TDS to Government: rigorous imprisonment of 3 months to 7 years plus fine; s.276C(1) - wilful attempt to evade tax: if the tax evaded exceeds Rs 25 lakh - 6 months to 7 years; otherwise 3 months to 2 years; s.276CC - wilful failure to furnish return: same thresholds; s.277 - false statement in verification; s.278 - abetment; s.278E - presumption of culpable mental state (accused must prove absence); s.279 - sanction by Principal Commissioner/Commissioner for prosecution; s.279(2) - compounding of offences. Offences are triable by Magistrates (Chapter XXII).
  9. Black Money (Undisclosed Foreign Income and Assets) and Imposition of Tax Act 2015: undisclosed foreign income taxed at 30% plus penalty at 90% (total about 120%); prosecution for wilful failure to disclose foreign assets: imprisonment 6 months to 7 years; Benami Transactions (Prohibition) Act 1988 as amended in 2016: benami transactions prohibited; confiscation; Union of India v. Ganpati Dealcom Pvt. Ltd. (2022) - section 3(2) (criminal provisions) unconstitutional (manifestly arbitrary) and the 2016 amendment operating prospectively; 'tax evasion' under Prevention of Money Laundering Act as a scheduled offence.
  10. Transition to the 2025 Act: tax year; sections renumbered; 'tax year' for all new proceedings after 1 April 2026; ITR forms and rules (Income-tax Rules 2026 Verify); the Act retains scheme of heads, residence, TDS, assessment; old pending cases decided under the 1961 Act. For AIBE, remember: 1961 Act section numbers for case law; use the 'tax year' language for the 2025 Act.

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
s.139(1)Due dates: 31 July (non-audit), 31 October (audit), 30 November (transfer pricing).
ss.207-211Advance tax: 15%, 45%, 75%, 100% (15 Jun / 15 Sep / 15 Dec / 15 Mar).
s.143(2)Scrutiny notice within 3 months from the end of the financial year of filing the return.
ss.246A, 253, 260AAppeals: CIT(A) 30 days; ITAT 60 days; High Court 120 days (substantial question of law).
s.270APenalty: 50% (under-reporting) / 200% (misreporting).
s.276CWilful tax evasion: 6 months-7 years if tax evaded exceeds Rs 25 lakh.

⚖Landmark cases

Binoy Viswam v. Union of India

2017

s.139AA (Aadhaar-PAN linking) upheld; privacy argument rejected on facts; not applicable to those who have no Aadhaar.

Union of India v. Ashish Agarwal

2022

Treated reassessment notices under the old regime as notices under s.148A; relief for assessees.

Union of India v. Rajeev Bansal

2024

Applied TOLA to reassessment; timelines for notices issued during Covid.

Pooran Mal v. Director of Inspection

1974

Evidence from a search is admissible even if the search is irregular.

Union of India v. Ganpati Dealcom Pvt. Ltd.

2022

Benami Act s.3(2) and s.5 (2016 amendment) unconstitutional / prospective only.

ITO v. Lakhmani Mewal Das

1976

'Reason to believe' for reassessment must have a rational connection with the belief.

✎Memory aids

  • Appeal timeline: 30 (CIT(A)) - 60 (ITAT) - 120 (HC).
  • Advance tax: 15-45-75-100.

!Exam traps

  • Traps.143(2) notice is time-barred after 3 months from the end of the FY in which the return was filed.
  • TrapITAT is the last fact-finding authority; HC under s.260A hears only substantial questions of law.
  • TrapProsecution for TDS default (s.276B) has a minimum of three months' imprisonment.

  3. Due date for filing a return by a non-audit individual? (31 July)
  4. Penalty for under-reporting of income? (50% of tax payable)
