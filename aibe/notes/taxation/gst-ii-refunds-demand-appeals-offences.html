GST (II): Assessment, Demand and Recovery, Refunds, Appeals (GSTAT), Offences and Penalties

iOverview

Procedures after registration and return filing: refunds, assessment, audit, demand under s.73/74/74A, the appeal hierarchy ending with GSTAT, and offences with monetary thresholds.

★Must know

  1. Refund (s.54): application within two years from the 'relevant date' (date of export, date of filing return, etc.); refund of tax paid on zero-rated supplies, inverted duty structure, excess payment, unutilised ITC; unjust enrichment bar (refund of incidence passed on to consumers is credited to Consumer Welfare Fund); interest on delayed refund.
  2. Assessment (Chapter XII): self-assessment (s.59); provisional assessment (s.60); scrutiny of returns (s.61); assessment of non-filers (s.62) - best judgment; assessment of unregistered persons (s.63); summary assessment in special cases (s.64) - to protect Revenue. Audit (s.65 - by tax authorities; s.66 - special audit by chartered accountant/cost accountant). Inspection, search and seizure (s.67) - by authorised officer on reasons to believe (Commissioner's authorisation); inspection of goods in movement (s.68; e-way bill); power to arrest (s.69) - Commissioner may authorise arrest where reason to believe that a person has committed an offence specified in s.132(1)(a),(b),(c) or (d) which is punishable under s.132(1)(i) or (ii) (i.e., offences above Rs 5 crore or fake invoice) - such offences are cognizable and non-bailable; offence cognizable and non-bailable where tax evaded exceeds Rs 5 crore. Arrest must follow the Cr.P.C. (now BNSS) procedure (s.69(3)); anticipatory bail is available (Radhika Agarwal v. Union of India, 2025 - the Supreme Court upheld the validity of ss.69 and 132 but held that arrest powers must be exercised on 'reasons to believe' that are recorded and that safeguards of the Cr.P.C. apply).
  3. Demand and recovery: s.73 (non-fraud cases - tax not paid, short paid, erroneous refund or wrongly availed/utilised ITC) - notice within two years and nine months from the due date for annual return; order within three years of that date; payment of tax and interest within 30 days avoids penalty; s.74 (fraud, wilful misstatement or suppression of facts) - notice within four years and six months; order within five years; penalty equal to tax; s.74A (inserted by the Finance Act 2024; effective from FY 2024-25) - unified provision for all cases with a uniform time-limit (notice within 42 months and order within 12 months of notice for the 'non-fraud' type; 54 months for fraud type Verify). s.75 general provisions (opportunity of hearing; adjournment up to three times; no demand beyond show-cause notice); s.76 tax collected but not deposited; s.77-78 (payment of tax; recovery within 3 months of order); s.79 (modes of recovery - attachment and sale, etc.); s.80 (instalments). Interest (s.50) 18% on delayed payment; 24% on undue/excess ITC claim; late fee (s.47).
  4. Penalty (ss.122-129): s.122 - for specified offences (supply without invoice, fake invoice, ITC fraud): penalty of Rs 10,000 or tax evaded whichever is higher; s.125 - general penalty up to Rs 25,000; s.129 - detention, seizure and release of goods in transit (200% of tax for taxable goods; 2% of value or Rs 25,000 for exempt goods); s.130 - confiscation of goods or conveyance; s.126 - no penalty for minor breaches (Rs 5,000 limit); s.127 - general penalty provisions.
  5. Offences and prosecution (s.132): tax evasion of more than Rs 5 crore - imprisonment up to five years plus fine; Rs 2 crore to Rs 5 crore - up to three years; Rs 1 crore to Rs 2 crore - up to one year; fake invoice or ITC fraud (issue of invoices without supply) - up to five years if tax involved exceeds Rs 5 crore; the offence is non-bailable where tax amount exceeds Rs 5 crore; cognizable when exceeding Rs 5 crore; s.132(5) - cognizable and non-bailable; s.132(4); prior sanction by Commissioner; compounding under s.138 (not for offences relating to fake invoices); s.134 - cognizance only with Commissioner's sanction.
  6. Appeals: first appeal to the Appellate Authority (Joint/Additional Commissioner (Appeals)) under s.107 within three months from the date of communication of the order (the Appellate Authority may condone delay up to one month); pre-deposit - full of the admitted tax plus 10% of the disputed tax (maximum Rs 25 crore for CGST and Rs 25 crore for SGST); second appeal to the GST Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT) under s.112 within three months (extendable by 3 months) - pre-deposit additional 10% of the disputed tax (max Rs 20 crore each for CGST and SGST) in addition to the amount paid under s.107; appeal to the High Court under s.117 on a substantial question of law within 180 days; appeal to the Supreme Court under s.118. Revisional authority (s.108) and the Advance Ruling Authority (ss.95-98; Appellate Authority for Advance Ruling).
  7. GSTAT: constituted under s.109 CGST Act as amended by the Finance Act 2023; National Bench (Principal Bench) at New Delhi; State Benches at several cities; operationalised in 2025 - the Government notified the Tribunal in September 2025, appointments of President and Members were made, and the portal for filing appeals went live; the Council extended the period to file legacy appeals in respect of orders passed before the Tribunal began functioning; a relaxed limitation window extends to 31 December 2026 for such cases Verify. GSTAT hears appeals from orders of the Appellate Authority and Revisional Authority; cases involving only issue of place of supply go directly to the National Bench; the Tribunal is subject to the principle in L. Chandra Kumar.
  8. Advance ruling (ss.95-106): binding on the applicant and the concerned officer; for a question of classification, admissibility of ITC, liability to register etc.; Authority for Advance Ruling (AAR) and Appellate Authority (AAAR). Anti-profiteering (s.171): benefit of reduced tax must be passed on; National Anti-Profiteering Authority wound up in 2022; now Competition Commission of India handles cases. Transitional provisions; migration. Tax recovery by banks etc.
  9. Other taxes quick view: Central Excise Act 1944 - now only on tobacco products and petroleum (as residual); Customs Act 1962 (see T6); Compensation Cess phased out; State VAT on petroleum and alcohol; Professional tax; Stamp duty (State laws and Indian Stamp Act 1899); Securities Transaction Tax; Equalisation levy (the 6% on online advertisements was abolished from 1 April 2025 Verify); Gifts and Wealth tax (abolished). Digital Services Tax in international discussions.

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
CGST s.54Refund: within two years from the relevant date.
CGST s.73 / 74 / 74ADemand and recovery - non-fraud/ fraud; unified s.74A from FY 2024-25.
CGST s.107 / 112Appeal to First Appellate Authority (3 months; 10% pre-deposit) / GSTAT (3 months; further 10%).
CGST s.132Offences and punishment: > Rs 5 crore - up to 5 years; non-bailable.
CGST s.69Power to arrest: only for specified offences.

⚖Landmark cases

Radhika Agarwal v. Union of India

2025

Upheld validity of ss.69, 132 and others but subjected arrest powers to Cr.P.C. safeguards, 'reasons to believe'; anticipatory bail available Verify.

Union of India v. Mohit Minerals

2022

Council recommendations persuasive.

✎Memory aids

  • GST appeals: s.107 (3 months, 10% pre-deposit) -> s.112 GSTAT (3 months, +10%) -> s.117 High Court (180 days) -> s.118 Supreme Court.
  • s.132: >Rs 5 crore = up to 5 years + non-bailable.

!Exam traps

  • TrapGST arrest power is restricted to offences above Rs 5 crore (and fake invoice cases).
  • TrapGSTAT is the second appeal; the first is before the Appellate Authority.
  • TrapPre-deposit under s.107 is 10% of the disputed tax (max Rs 25 crore each); under s.112 an additional 10% (max Rs 20 crore each).
