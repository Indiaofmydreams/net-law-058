iOverview

The legal architecture of GST, the concept of 'supply', registration thresholds, composition scheme, ITC conditions and blocked credits, returns, and GST 2.0 rates.

★Must know

  1. GST laws: Central GST Act 2017, Integrated GST Act 2017, Union Territory GST Act 2017, GST (Compensation to States) Act 2017 (compensation cess withdrawn after GST 2.0), and State GST Acts. Effective 1 July 2017. Dual GST: CGST + SGST on intra-State supply; IGST on inter-State supply and imports (IGST on import under Customs Tariff Act s.3(7)). Destination-based consumption tax; tax levied on 'supply'.
  2. Levy (CGST s.9): GST on all intra-State supplies of goods or services or both (except alcoholic liquor for human consumption) at rates notified by the Government on recommendation of the Council, on the transaction value (s.15); not exceeding 20% (CGST) per the Act. Reverse charge: s.9(3) (notified goods/services) and s.9(4) (supplies by unregistered to registered persons, restricted to notified classes); e-commerce operator liable under s.9(5) for notified services.
  3. Supply (CGST s.7): includes all forms of supply of goods or services such as sale, transfer, barter, exchange, licence, rental, lease or disposal made or agreed to be made for a consideration by a person in the course or furtherance of business; import of services for a consideration whether or not in the course of business; and activities specified in Schedule I made without consideration (permanent transfer or disposal of business assets with ITC; supply between related persons or distinct persons in the course of business; supply by principal to agent and vice versa; import of services from a related person outside India). Schedule II classifies activities as supply of goods or services (e.g., transfer of title in goods - goods; lease/ licence - services; works contract - services; renting of immovable property - services; temporary transfer of IPR - services). Schedule III - activities neither a supply of goods nor services (services by an employee to employer in the course of employment; court fees; functions of MPs, MLAs, panchayat members; funeral services; sale of land and (subject to para 5(b) of Schedule II) completed buildings; actionable claims other than lottery, betting and gambling). Composite supply (two or more supplies naturally bundled; tax by principal supply) and mixed supply (tax by the supply attracting the highest rate) - s.8.
  4. Time, value and place of supply: time of supply of goods (s.12) - earlier of date of invoice or last date on which invoice should be issued, or date of payment; services (s.13); value of supply (s.15) - transaction value (price actually paid or payable where parties are not related and price is the sole consideration), plus taxes, incidental expenses, interest; place of supply determines whether supply is intra-State or inter-State (IGST Act ss.10-13); zero-rated supplies - exports and supplies to SEZ (IGST s.16) - refund of ITC or IGST paid.
  5. Taxable person and registration (s.22): every supplier is liable to be registered if aggregate turnover in a financial year exceeds Rs 40 lakh (goods), Rs 20 lakh (services); for special category States Rs 20 lakh (goods) and Rs 10 lakh (services) [thresholds unchanged by GST 2.0 - VERIFY]. Compulsory registration regardless of turnover (s.24): inter-State suppliers of goods, casual taxable persons, persons liable under reverse charge, non-resident taxable persons, e-commerce operators and persons supplying through them, input service distributors, persons deducting TDS/collecting TCS, online information and database access suppliers. Registration within 30 days of becoming liable; PAN-based; GSTIN of 15 digits; cancellation (s.29).
  6. Composition levy (s.10): option for registered persons with aggregate turnover up to Rs 1.5 crore (Rs 75 lakh for special category States) in preceding year to pay tax at a lower rate (1% for manufacturers and traders; 5% for restaurants; 6% for specified service providers up to Rs 50 lakh under s.10(2A)); cannot make inter-State supply, cannot collect tax from customers, cannot claim ITC, not available to e-commerce operators and supplier of notified goods (ice cream, pan masala, tobacco) [verify rates].
  7. Input Tax Credit (ss.16-21): ITC is allowed for tax paid on inward supplies used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of business; conditions (s.16(2)): (a) possession of a tax invoice or debit note or other prescribed documents; (b) receipt of goods or services; (c) tax actually paid to the Government by the supplier (either in cash or through ITC); (d) filing of returns under s.39; and (aa) the details are communicated in GSTR-2B (inserted 2022). Time limit (s.16(4)): invoice-based credit may be availed up to 30 November following the end of the financial year or the date of filing the annual return, whichever is earlier (extended for certain years under amnesty and Finance Act 2024 Verify). Blocked credits (s.17(5)): motor vehicles for transport of persons having seating capacity of not more than thirteen (with exceptions); vessels and aircraft (with exceptions); food and beverages, outdoor catering, beauty treatment, health services, cosmetic and plastic surgery, membership of club/health and fitness centre, rent-a-cab, life insurance and health insurance (except where obligatory/ taxable outward); travel benefits to employees on vacation; works contract services for construction of immovable property (other than plant and machinery) except where input is for further works contract/ construction; goods or services used for personal consumption; goods lost, stolen, destroyed, written off or disposed of by gift or free samples; tax paid under ss.74, 129, 130. Apportionment of credit for exempt supplies (s.17(1), (2) and Rules 42-43). Matching under s.41 (provisional credit - omitted; GSTR-2B invoice-matching now).
  8. Safari Retreats Pvt. Ltd. v. Chief Commissioner of CGST (3 Oct 2024): 'plant or machinery' in s.17(5)(d) must be tested by the functionality test (whether the building is itself the plant used for making outward supply, e.g., hotels, malls); the Court held that a mall's building rented out can be a 'plant' on facts only if it is essential to the activity; the Finance Act 2025 retrospectively replaced 'plant or machinery' with 'plant and machinery' in s.17(5)(d) with effect from 1 July 2017 to neutralise the decision; a review petition was dismissed (2025) and later High Court/ Supreme Court decisions continue to apply the functionality test for 'plant' Verify.
  9. Returns (ss.37-48): GSTR-1 (outward supplies; due 11th of next month or quarterly for QRMP), GSTR-3B (summary return and payment; 20th of next month), GSTR-9 (annual return; 31 December of next year), GSTR-9C reconciliation statement for large taxpayers; GSTR-2A/2B auto-generated statements; TDS (s.51 - 2% by notified government departments on payments exceeding Rs 2.5 lakh); TCS (s.52 - e-commerce operators 0.5% of net value of taxable supplies); electronic ledgers (cash, credit, liability); e-invoicing mandatory for taxpayers with aggregate turnover above Rs 5 crore; e-way bill for consignments exceeding Rs 50,000 in value (Rule 138).
  10. Rates (GST 2.0 - Council's 56th meeting 3 Sept 2025; effective 22 September 2025): the four-slab structure (5%, 12%, 18%, 28%) was replaced by two principal slabs - 5% (merit) and 18% (standard) - and a special 40% rate for sin and luxury goods (tobacco and pan masala, aerated drinks, high-end cars, etc.); special low rates for precious metals and stones (0.25%, 1.5%, 3%) continue; many items moved from 12%/28% to 5%/18%; essentials and certain health/education items exempt or at nil; individual life and health insurance premium exempted; compensation cess was phased out as loans were repaid and the tobacco/pan masala items moved to 40% Verify.
  11. Alcohol for human consumption outside GST (State excise and VAT); petroleum crude, HSD, motor spirit, natural gas, ATF outside GST until notified; electricity outside; transitional - 'GST on tobacco' plus central excise on tobacco products. Remedies for GST compliance: GST Practitioners; GSTN as common portal.
  12. Key case law: Union of India v. Mohit Minerals (2022) - Council recommendations not binding; Union of India v. VKC Footsteps India (2021) - refund of accumulated ITC due to inverted duty structure available only on input goods, not input services (Rule 89(5)) - upheld; Safari Retreats (2024); Mohit; also decisions on retrospective amendments and Rule 86B (restriction on ITC use) Verify · 2026 rulings: Bhandari Scrap Traders (ITC where supplier did not pay tax), Tata Steel (limitation under s.74).

⚖Landmark cases

GST Council recommendations not binding; cooperative federalism.

Safari Retreats Pvt. Ltd. v. Chief Commissioner of CGST

Functionality test for 'plant' in s.17(5)(d); ITC on construction of building used for renting not automatically blocked.

Inverted duty refund is for input goods only (not input services); Rule 89(5) valid.

Right to rectify GST returns / carry forward credit recognised in the context of technical glitches Verify.

✎Memory aids

  • GST: 'CGST + SGST (intra-State); IGST (inter-State); UTGST (UT).'
  • Rates after 22 Sept 2025: 5% / 18% / 40% (plus special rates).
  • Registration threshold: Rs 40 lakh goods; Rs 20 lakh services.

!Exam traps

  • TrapComposition scheme cannot be used for inter-State supplies or by e-commerce operators.
  • TrapSchedule III items (e.g., sale of land) are neither goods nor services - no GST.
  • TrapAlcohol for human consumption is outside GST.
  • TrapITC time limit: 30 November following the end of the FY or date of annual return, whichever earlier.
