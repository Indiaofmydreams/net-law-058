iOverview

Who regulates and who decides: Controller of Certifying Authorities, Adjudicating Officer, TDSAT, CERT-In, NCIIPC, police and BNSS procedure for electronic evidence.

★Must know

  1. Adjudication (S.46): the Adjudicating Officer (an officer not below the rank of Director in the Central Government or equivalent in a State; in practice the Secretary of the IT department of the State) decides civil contraventions of Chapter IX (S.43-45) where the claim for injury or damage does not exceed Rs 5 crore; claims above that go to the competent civil court. S.47: factors for quantum (unfair advantage, loss, repeat). S.45: residuary penalty - compensation up to Rs 25,000 or penalty up to Rs 25,000.
  2. Appeals: the Cyber Appellate Tribunal (S.48) was merged into the Telecom Disputes Settlement and Appellate Tribunal (TDSAT) under the Finance Act 2017 and Tribunal reforms; appeal to the High Court within 60 days on a question of fact or law (S.62). S.61: civil court has no jurisdiction over matters within the Adjudicating Officer's or Tribunal's powers.
  3. Controller of Certifying Authorities (S.17-18): licensing and supervising CAs; Root Certifying Authority of India (RCAI); CAs issue DSCs. CERT-In (S.70B): national agency for cyber security incidents; CERT-In Directions of 28 April 2022 require reporting of specified incidents within 6 hours, maintaining logs for 180 days within India and synchronising system clocks with NIC/NPL servers; VPN providers, data centres and cloud providers retain subscriber information for 5 years. NCIIPC (S.70A): protection of critical information infrastructure; S.70 protected systems.
  4. Investigation: S.78 Inspector-rank police; S.80 search and arrest without warrant in public place by Inspector rank or authorised officer; BNSS 2023 provisions: S.94 (summons to produce documents/things including electronic communication devices), S.105 (audio-video recording of search and seizure), S.173 (e-FIR and Zero FIR), S.530 (trials, inquiries and proceedings by electronic means); evidence by Section 63 BSA certificate (hash value) - see the Evidence / BSA notes. National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in), helpline 1930 and the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) under the Ministry of Home Affairs.
  5. Jurisdiction on the internet: IT Act S.75 (extra-territorial); Casio India v Ashita Tele Systems (Delhi HC, 2003) and Banyan Tree Holding v A. Murali Krishna Reddy (Delhi HC, 2010) - mere accessibility of a website is not enough; 'purposeful availment' and targeting are required for jurisdiction; domain names: Satyam Infoway Ltd v Sifynet Solutions (SC, 2004) - domain names are business identifiers; passing-off applies; Yahoo! Inc v Akash Arora (Delhi HC, 1999).
  6. Cyber-crime and companies: S.85 (offences by companies); Aneeta Hada v Godfather Travels (2012): directors can be prosecuted only if the company is arraigned.
  7. Mutual legal assistance and cross-border data: India is not a party to the Budapest Convention; it has participated in negotiations on the UN Convention against Cybercrime (adopted by the UN General Assembly in December 2024; opened for signature in 2025) - verify India's position.
