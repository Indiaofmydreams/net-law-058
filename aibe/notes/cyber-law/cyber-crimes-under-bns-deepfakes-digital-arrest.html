iOverview

Many cyber-crimes are prosecuted under both the IT Act and the BNS. Current trends: AI deepfakes, the online gaming ban, digital arrest scams and personality-rights litigation.

★Must know

  1. BNS equivalents (verify numbering): S.318 cheating (IPC 420), S.319 cheating by personation, S.336 forgery (includes electronic record), S.303 theft, S.356 defamation, S.351 criminal intimidation, S.75 sexual harassment (IPC 354A), S.77 voyeurism (IPC 354C - includes capture or dissemination of private images), S.78 stalking (IPC 354D - includes monitoring electronic communication), S.79 insulting the modesty of a woman, S.294 obscene material (IPC 292), S.196 promoting enmity through electronic communication, S.152 acts endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity (replaces sedition; electronic communication included), S.111 organised crime (includes cyber-crime) and S.113 terrorist act. 'Document' in BNS includes electronic and digital records. Where both the IT Act and IPC/BNS apply to the same act, Digumarti holds that a special law (IT Act) prevails for the subject it covers.
  2. Deepfakes and AI: the 2026 IT Rules amendment on synthetically generated information (labelling, metadata and 2-3 hour takedowns); S.66C/66D (impersonation), S.66E, S.67/67A (sexual content), BNS defamation and cheating; personality-rights injunctions by the Delhi and Bombay High Courts in 2025 (verify).
  3. Online gaming: the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act 2025 (assent 22 August 2025) prohibits online money games and their advertising and facilitation, establishes an authority for e-sports and social gaming promotion, and prescribes imprisonment and fines (reported up to 3 years and Rs 1 crore for offering; lower for advertising). Challenges are before the Supreme Court (verify). Earlier IT Amendment Rules 2023 created self-regulatory bodies for online gaming; GST rate hikes on online gaming (2023-2025) are a separate issue.
  4. Digital arrest scams: fraudsters impersonate law-enforcement officers (CBI, police, customs) and extract money by 'virtual arrest'; the Supreme Court took suo motu cognisance in 2025 and directed coordination and a central probe (verify); the Government has issued advisories. Prosecuted under S.318/319 BNS, S.66C/66D IT Act and extortion provisions. 'Digital arrest' is not a recognised legal procedure.
  5. Cyber fraud and banking: RBI's customer-liability circular (zero liability for third-party breach reported within 3 working days); UPI frauds; SIM-swap; phishing; vishing; sextortion. The Telecommunications Act 2023 introduces penalties for SIM-fraud and identity-based offences (verify provisions in force).
  6. Cyber stalking, revenge porn and NCII: S.66E, S.67, S.67A, BNS S.77-78; StopNCII; the Delhi High Court's orders for takedown within defined time (Mrs X v Union of India, 2023).
  7. E-commerce and consumer: Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020 and the 2023 Guidelines on Dark Patterns (CCPA) - see the Consumer Protection notes.
  8. Critical infrastructure and national security: S.66F, S.70, S.70A; the National Cyber Security Policy 2013; the Digital India Act is proposed but not enacted (verify).
