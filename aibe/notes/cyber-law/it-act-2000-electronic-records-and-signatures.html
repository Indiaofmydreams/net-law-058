iOverview

The IT Act gives legal recognition to electronic transactions (based on the UNCITRAL Model Law on Electronic Commerce, 1996), creates the digital signature regime and the cyber-offence framework. Amended substantially in 2008 (in force 27 October 2009).

★Must know

  1. Enacted 2000 (in force 17 October 2000). Objects: legal recognition for e-commerce and e-records, facilitate e-governance, amend the IPC, Evidence Act, Bankers' Books Evidence Act and RBI Act. Extends to the whole of India and, under S.1(2) read with S.75, applies to an offence or contravention committed outside India by any person if the act involves a computer, computer system or network located in India.
  2. Exclusions (S.1(4), First Schedule): (1) negotiable instrument (other than a cheque), (2) power of attorney, (3) trust, (4) will or testamentary disposition, (5) contract for sale or conveyance of immovable property or any interest in it. An electronic cheque is not excluded.
  3. Key definitions (S.2): 'computer', 'computer network', 'computer resource', 'data', 'communication device' (S.2(1)(ha)), 'cyber security' (S.2(1)(nb)), 'electronic record' (S.2(1)(t)), 'electronic signature' (S.2(1)(ta)), 'digital signature' (S.2(1)(p)), 'originator', 'addressee', 'subscriber' and 'intermediary' (S.2(1)(w): any person who on behalf of another receives, stores or transmits an electronic record or provides a service relating to it - telecom, network, internet and web-hosting service providers, search engines, online payment, auction and marketplace sites and cyber cafes).
  4. Digital signature (S.3): authentication of an electronic record by a subscriber using an asymmetric crypto system and hash function (public/private key pair). Electronic signature (S.3A, 2008): any technology specified in the Second Schedule (e.g. Aadhaar e-KYC e-sign). Digital Signature Certificate: issued by a Certifying Authority (S.35).
  5. Chapter III e-governance: S.4 legal recognition of electronic records; S.5 of electronic signatures; S.6 use in government and its agencies; S.6A delivery of services by service providers (Common Service Centres); S.7 retention of electronic records; S.7A audit; S.8 publication in the Gazette in electronic form; S.10A (2008): contracts formed through electronic means are not unenforceable merely because electronic form was used.
  6. Chapter IV attribution, acknowledgement and dispatch: S.11 attribution (an electronic record is that of the originator if sent by him, by an authorised person or by an automated system); S.12 acknowledgement of receipt; S.13 time and place of dispatch (when it enters a computer resource outside the originator's control) and receipt (when it enters the designated computer resource); place is the place of business of the originator/addressee.
  7. Chapters V-VI secure electronic records and signatures (S.14-16) and Regulation of Certifying Authorities (S.17-34): Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA) appointed under S.17; licence for Certifying Authorities (S.21); the CCA's functions (S.18) include licensing and supervising CAs and laying down standards; S.35 DSC issue; S.36 representations; S.37 suspension; S.38 revocation; S.39 notice of suspension/revocation; Chapters on penalties and adjudication follow.
  8. Evidence link: S.65B IEA (now S.63 BSA with a certificate in the Schedule and hash value); Anvar P.V. (2014), Arjun Panditrao (2020), Pune Bar Association (2026) - see the Evidence / BSA notes.

⚖Landmark cases

State of Maharashtra v Dr. Praful B. Desai

2003

Evidence by video-conferencing is 'presence' and valid in criminal trials.

Syed Asifuddin v State of Andhra Pradesh (AP HC)

2005

Mobile phone is a 'computer' and the ESN/programming is a computer source code (S.65).

✎Memory aids

  • Five exclusions: N-P-T-W-I = Negotiable instrument, Power of attorney, Trust, Will, Immovable property contract.

!Exam traps

  • TrapNegotiable instruments other than a cheque are excluded; a cheque is not.
  • TrapWills, trusts, powers of attorney and immovable property conveyances are excluded.
  • TrapThe IT Act applies extraterritorially where a computer in India is involved (S.75).
  • TrapS.10A (2008) is about contract formation, not digital signature.
