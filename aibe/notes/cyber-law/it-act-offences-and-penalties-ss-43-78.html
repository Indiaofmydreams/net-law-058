iOverview

The highest-yield Cyber Law content: the civil penalty in S.43, the criminal offences in S.65-74, their punishments, cognizability and compounding.

★Must know

  1. S.43 (civil liability, compensation, no monetary cap after 2008): unauthorised access, downloading/copying, introducing a virus or contaminant, damage, disruption, denial of access, assisting unauthorised access, charging services to another's account, destroying or altering information, stealing/concealing source code (S.43(a)-(j)). S.43A: a body corporate that is negligent in implementing reasonable security practices for sensitive personal data, causing wrongful loss or gain, must compensate; implemented through the 2011 SPDI Rules; S.43A is to be omitted when S.44(2) DPDP Act commences (phased, expected May 2027 - verify).
  2. S.65: tampering with computer source documents - up to 3 years or fine up to Rs 2 lakh or both.
  3. S.66: computer-related offences (dishonest or fraudulent doing of S.43 acts, i.e. hacking) - up to 3 years or fine up to Rs 5 lakh or both.
  4. S.66A (2008): sending offensive, false or annoying messages - up to 3 years and fine; struck down by Shreya Singhal v Union of India (24 March 2015) as vague, over-broad and violative of Art 19(1)(a), not saved by Art 19(2).
  5. S.66B: dishonestly receiving a stolen computer resource - up to 3 years or fine up to Rs 1 lakh or both. S.66C: identity theft (fraudulent use of electronic signature, password or unique identification feature) - up to 3 years and fine up to Rs 1 lakh. S.66D: cheating by personation using a computer resource - up to 3 years and fine up to Rs 1 lakh.
  6. S.66E: violation of privacy - capturing, publishing or transmitting the image of a private area of a person without consent - up to 3 years or fine up to Rs 2 lakh or both. S.66F: cyber terrorism (threatening unity, integrity, security or sovereignty or striking terror by denial of access, unauthorised access or introduction of contaminant, causing death/injury, damage or affecting critical information infrastructure) - imprisonment which may extend to life imprisonment.
  7. S.67: publishing or transmitting obscene material in electronic form - first conviction up to 3 years and fine up to Rs 5 lakh; second or subsequent up to 5 years and fine up to Rs 10 lakh. S.67A: sexually explicit act or conduct - up to 5 years and fine up to Rs 10 lakh (first); up to 7 years and fine up to Rs 10 lakh (subsequent). S.67B: child pornography (publishing, transmitting, creating, browsing, enticing children, recording abuse) - up to 5 years and fine up to Rs 10 lakh (first); up to 7 years and fine up to Rs 10 lakh (subsequent). S.67C: intermediaries must preserve and retain information as prescribed (contravention up to 3 years and fine).
  8. S.68: Controller's directions to certifying authorities (non-compliance up to 2 years or fine up to Rs 1 lakh). S.69: power to intercept, monitor or decrypt any information in the interest of sovereignty, security, public order or investigation of an offence - non-compliance up to 7 years and fine; Information Technology (Procedure and Safeguards for Interception, Monitoring and Decryption of Information) Rules 2009. S.69A: power to direct blocking of public access - non-compliance up to 7 years and fine; Blocking Rules 2009; S.69A and the Rules were upheld in Shreya Singhal. S.69B: monitoring and collecting traffic data for cyber security - non-compliance up to 3 years and fine.
  9. S.70: protected system - unauthorised access punishable up to 10 years and fine; S.70A: National Critical Information Infrastructure Protection Centre (NCIIPC); S.70B: Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In) as the national agency for incident response.
  10. S.71: misrepresentation to the Controller; S.72: breach of confidentiality and privacy by a person who obtained information under the Act's powers - up to 2 years or fine up to Rs 1 lakh or both; S.72A (2008): disclosure of information in breach of lawful contract - up to 3 years or fine up to Rs 5 lakh or both; S.73: publishing a false Digital Signature Certificate - up to 2 years or fine up to Rs 1 lakh; S.74: publication for fraudulent purpose - up to 2 years or fine up to Rs 1 lakh.
  11. S.75: extra-territorial application; S.76: confiscation; S.77: compensation and penalties do not bar other punishments; S.77A: compounding of offences (by a court of competent jurisdiction) - not available for offences with imprisonment of more than 3 years, offences against a woman or child, or where the accused has a previous conviction or enhanced punishment; S.77B: offences punishable with imprisonment of 3 years and above are cognizable; offences punishable with imprisonment of 3 years are bailable; S.78: investigation by a police officer not below the rank of Inspector; S.80: any police officer not below the rank of Inspector (or other officer authorised by the Government) may enter, search and arrest without warrant in a public place.
  12. S.84A (modes or methods for encryption), S.84B (abetment - same punishment as the offence) and S.84C (attempt - up to half of the longest term). S.85: offences by companies (persons in charge liable). S.81: overriding effect; the proviso says nothing restricts exercise of rights under the Copyright Act 1957 or Patents Act 1970.
  13. Special law prevails: Sharat Babu Digumarti v Government of NCT of Delhi (2016) - where S.67 IT Act specifically covers an offence of obscene electronic material, the general S.292 IPC (S.294 BNS) is not attracted and the intermediary protected by S.79 is not prosecutable under IPC; State of Tamil Nadu v Suhas Katti (2004) - first conviction for obscene messages online (S.67 plus IPC S.469, 509). Even after Shreya Singhal, S.66A continued to be invoked; the Supreme Court in PUCL v Union of India (2021 order) directed states to stop prosecutions under it.

⚖Landmark cases

Shreya Singhal v Union of India

2015

S.66A struck down (vague, over-broad, chilling effect); S.69A upheld; S.79(3)(b) read down; discussion, advocacy and incitement distinguished.

State of Tamil Nadu v Suhas Katti

2004

First conviction in India for cyber obscenity and harassment (S.67).

Sharat Babu Digumarti v Government of NCT of Delhi

2016

IT Act (special law) prevails over IPC S.292 for electronic obscenity.

Syed Asifuddin v State of Andhra Pradesh

2005

Tampering with mobile phone ESN is tampering with computer source code under S.65.

✎Memory aids

  • 66: B-C-D-E-F = Stolen resource, identity theft, personation, privacy, terrorism.
  • 67: 3/5 - 67A/B: 5/7; fines Rs 5 lakh (67) vs Rs 10 lakh (67A/B).

!Exam traps

  • TrapS.66A has been struck down; it is not good law but may appear in options.
  • TrapS.66E punishes capture/transmission of images of a private area; S.66C is identity theft; S.66D is cheating by personation.
  • TrapS.67A/67B repeat-offender term is 7 years; S.67 repeat-offender term is 5 years.
  • TrapOffences with 3 years' punishment are cognizable and bailable; offences punishable with imprisonment of 3 years and above are cognizable.
  • TrapS.66F is the only offence with life imprisonment.

?Likely questions

  2. Punishment under S.67 for a first conviction.
  3. Which section was struck down in Shreya Singhal?
  4. Compounding limits (S.77A).
  5. Rank of investigating officer (Inspector) under S.78.
