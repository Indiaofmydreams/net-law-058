iOverview

Privacy is a fundamental right (Puttaswamy, 2017). India's first comprehensive data protection law is the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, operationalised through the DPDP Rules 2025 in phases to about May 2027.

★Must know

  1. K.S. Puttaswamy v Union of India (24 August 2017, 9 judges): right to privacy is a fundamental right under Art 21 and Part III; M.P. Sharma (1954) and Kharak Singh (1962) overruled to the extent inconsistent. Any restriction must satisfy a three-fold test: legality (a law), legitimate aim and proportionality (rational nexus, necessity, minimal impairment). Informational privacy and the right to be left alone recognised. Puttaswamy II (26 September 2018): Aadhaar Act upheld by 4:1 but S.57 (private use) and S.33(2) struck down; Aadhaar cannot be made mandatory for bank accounts and mobile connections. Earlier: PUCL v Union of India (1997) - telephone tapping guidelines; Kharak Singh and R. Rajagopal v State of T.N. (1994) - privacy and press.
  2. Right to be forgotten and personality rights: Justice K.S. Puttaswamy recognised the idea; High Courts (Delhi, Karnataka, Orissa, Kerala) have ordered removal or anonymisation in specific cases (Zulfiqar Ahman Khan v Quintillion Business Media, Delhi HC 2019; Subhranshu Rout v State of Odisha, Orissa HC 2020); in 2025 the Delhi and Bombay High Courts granted personality-rights injunctions against AI-generated misuse of celebrities' voice and likeness (verify).
  3. Pre-DPDP regime: S.43A IT Act and the IT (Reasonable Security Practices and Procedures and Sensitive Personal Data or Information) Rules 2011 (SPDI Rules): sensitive personal data includes passwords, financial information, health, sexual orientation, medical records and biometrics; body corporate must have a privacy policy, obtain written consent, allow review and correction, appoint a grievance officer, and implement ISO 27001 or equivalent security. S.72A penalises disclosure in breach of a lawful contract.
  4. Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 (assent 11 August 2023): applies to processing of digital personal data within India (and of non-digital data that is digitised) and to processing outside India in connection with offering goods or services to Data Principals in India (S.3). It does not apply to personal data processed for personal or domestic purposes, or to data made publicly available by the Data Principal or under a legal obligation. Definitions: Data Principal (the individual; includes parent or guardian of a child and lawful guardian of a person with disability), Data Fiduciary (determines purpose and means), Data Processor, Consent Manager, Significant Data Fiduciary (SDF), Child (below 18).
  5. Grounds for processing: consent or certain 'legitimate uses' (S.4). Consent (S.6) must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous with a clear affirmative action, limited to the specified purpose, and as easy to withdraw as to give; a notice (S.5) in plain language, in English or any Eighth Schedule language, must precede the request. Legitimate uses (S.7): voluntary provision of data, State subsidies/benefits/licences, legal obligation, judicial orders, medical emergency, epidemics, disasters, employment purposes. S.6(9) Consent Managers (registered with the Board).
  6. Obligations of Data Fiduciaries (S.8): accuracy, reasonable security safeguards, breach intimation to the Board and affected Data Principals, erasure when purpose is served or consent withdrawn, publication of contact details of a Data Protection Officer or person answering questions, grievance redressal. Children (S.9): verifiable consent of a parent or lawful guardian; no processing likely to cause detrimental effect; no tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted advertising directed at children. Significant Data Fiduciary (S.10): notified by the Government (volume, sensitivity, risk to rights, security of State); duties - Data Protection Officer in India, independent data auditor, periodic Data Protection Impact Assessment.
  7. Rights of Data Principals (S.11-14): access to a summary of personal data and processing; correction, completion, updating and erasure; grievance redressal; nomination of another person in case of death or incapacity. Duties (S.15): comply with laws, no impersonation, no false or frivolous complaints. No right to compensation is conferred by the Act.
  8. Cross-border transfer (S.16): permitted except to countries or territories restricted by notification of the Central Government (a negative-list model); sectoral laws with stricter localisation prevail. Exemptions (S.17): State instrumentalities for sovereignty, security and public order (by notification); research, archiving and statistics; startups (notified classes); legal rights enforcement; courts; prevention of offences; contracts with foreign persons (processing by Indian outsourcing companies).
  9. Data Protection Board of India (S.18-26): an independent adjudicatory body (a body corporate; chairperson and members appointed by the Central Government, with a head office in the National Capital Region as per the Rules; four members at inception); powers of civil court; inquiry on complaints, references, breach, orders to remedy, penalties; voluntary undertakings (S.31); mediation (S.30); appeals to the Telecom Disputes Settlement and Appellate Tribunal (TDSAT) (S.29); civil courts' jurisdiction barred (S.39).
  10. Penalties (Schedule, S.33): failure to take reasonable security safeguards to prevent a breach - up to Rs 250 crore; failure to give breach notice to the Board and Data Principals - up to Rs 200 crore; breach of obligations regarding children - up to Rs 200 crore; breach of SDF obligations - up to Rs 150 crore; breach of Data Principal's duties - up to Rs 10,000; breach of voluntary undertaking - up to the applicable amount; residuary - up to Rs 50 crore.
  11. DPDP Rules 2025: notified 13 November 2025 with phased commencement - (i) 13 November 2025: Board constitution and administrative provisions (Rules 1-2, 17-21; Act S.1(2), 2, 18-26, 35, 38-43, 44(1) and (3)); (ii) after one year (about 12-13 November 2026): consent manager registration (S.6(9), S.27(1)(d); Rule 4); (iii) after 18 months (about 12 May 2027): all remaining provisions - notice and consent, fiduciary obligations, children's data, rights of Data Principals, cross-border rules and the Board's remaining powers (as reported; verify against the notification). Note that S.44(3) (amendment of S.8(1)(j) of the RTI Act, exempting personal information) is in the first tranche per the commencement list, whereas S.44(2) (omission of S.43A IT Act) falls in the last - verify. Rules also deal with breach reporting (immediately and a detailed report within 72 hours), verifiable parental consent, data retention periods for large platforms and SDF audits (verify details).
  12. Interplay with other laws: S.38 DPDP - the Act is in addition to other laws; in conflict, the DPDP provision prevails to the extent of the inconsistency; sector laws on localisation (RBI payment data rules) continue. Aadhaar Act 2016 (S.8, 29, 33) and Right to Information Act (S.8(1)(j)) interplay noted.

⚖Landmark cases

K.S. Puttaswamy v Union of India (Privacy)

2017

Privacy is a fundamental right; three-fold test of legality, legitimate aim and proportionality.

Guidelines to curb arbitrary telephone tapping.

1994

Right to privacy implicit in Art 21; limits on publication of private life of citizens.

✎Memory aids

  • DPDP rights: A-C-G-N = Access, Correction/erasure, Grievance, Nominate.

!Exam traps

  • TrapMaximum DPDP penalty is Rs 250 crore (security safeguards), not Rs 500 crore.
  • TrapThe child age under DPDP is below 18; behavioural tracking and targeted ads for children are banned.
  • TrapThe Data Protection Board is an adjudicatory body; appeals lie to TDSAT.
  • TrapDPDP provides no right to compensation to individuals; S.43A compensation remains until its omission.
  • TrapOnly consent or legitimate use is a ground; there is no 'legitimate interest' ground as in the GDPR.

?Likely questions

  1. Maximum penalty under DPDP Act.
  2. Age of child under DPDP.
  3. Forum for appeal (TDSAT).
  4. Three-fold test in Puttaswamy.
  5. When were the DPDP Rules notified? (13 Nov 2025).
