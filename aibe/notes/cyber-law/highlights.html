Information Technology Act 2000 - Scope, Definitions, Electronic Records, Digital and Electronic Signatures, E-Governance

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

S.1(4), First Schedule IT ActDocuments to which the Act does not apply.
S.2(1)(t), (ta), (p), (w) IT ActElectronic record, electronic signature, digital signature, intermediary.
S.3, 3A, 4, 5, 10A, 17 IT ActDigital signature, e-signature, recognition, contract formation, Controller.

Cases in one line

  • State of Maharashtra v Dr. Praful B. Desai 2003 Evidence by video-conferencing is 'presence' and valid in criminal trials.
  • Syed Asifuddin v State of Andhra Pradesh (AP HC) 2005 Mobile phone is a 'computer' and the ESN/programming is a computer source code (S.65).

Memory aids

  • Five exclusions: N-P-T-W-I = Negotiable instrument, Power of attorney, Trust, Will, Immovable property contract.

Exam traps

  • TrapNegotiable instruments other than a cheque are excluded; a cheque is not.
  • TrapWills, trusts, powers of attorney and immovable property conveyances are excluded.
  • TrapThe IT Act applies extraterritorially where a computer in India is involved (S.75).
  • TrapS.10A (2008) is about contract formation, not digital signature.
2

IT Act Offences and Penalties (S.43-78)

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

S.43, 43A, 65, 66, 66B-66F, 67, 67A, 67B, 72, 72A IT ActCore penalties and offences.
S.69, 69A, 69B IT ActInterception, blocking, traffic data.
S.77A, 77B, 78, 80 IT ActCompounding, cognizability, investigation, search.

Cases in one line

  • Shreya Singhal v Union of India 2015 S.66A struck down (vague, over-broad, chilling effect); S.69A upheld; S.79(3)(b) read down; discussion, advocacy and incitement distinguished.
  • State of Tamil Nadu v Suhas Katti 2004 First conviction in India for cyber obscenity and harassment (S.67).
  • Sharat Babu Digumarti v Government of NCT of Delhi 2016 IT Act (special law) prevails over IPC S.292 for electronic obscenity.
  • Syed Asifuddin v State of Andhra Pradesh 2005 Tampering with mobile phone ESN is tampering with computer source code under S.65.

Memory aids

  • 66: B-C-D-E-F = Stolen resource, identity theft, personation, privacy, terrorism.
  • 67: 3/5 - 67A/B: 5/7; fines Rs 5 lakh (67) vs Rs 10 lakh (67A/B).

Exam traps

  • TrapS.66A has been struck down; it is not good law but may appear in options.
  • TrapS.66E punishes capture/transmission of images of a private area; S.66C is identity theft; S.66D is cheating by personation.
  • TrapS.67A/67B repeat-offender term is 7 years; S.67 repeat-offender term is 5 years.
  • TrapOffences with 3 years' punishment are cognizable and bailable; offences punishable with imprisonment of 3 years and above are cognizable.
  • TrapS.66F is the only offence with life imprisonment.
3

Intermediary Liability, Safe Harbour (S.79), IT Rules 2021 and the 2026 Synthetic Content Amendment

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

S.79 IT ActSafe harbour and its loss.
IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules 2021; Amendment Rules 2026Due diligence, SSMI duties, SGI labelling, takedown timelines.
S.69A IT Act; Blocking Rules 2009Blocking of public access.

Cases in one line

  • Shreya Singhal v Union of India 2015 S.79(3)(b) read down - actual knowledge means a court order or government notification.
  • MySpace Inc v Super Cassettes Industries 2016 Intermediary with actual knowledge of specific infringing content must take it down; S.79 and Copyright Act harmonised.
  • Christian Louboutin SAS v Nakul Bajaj 2018 Active role (not mere conduit) can forfeit safe harbour.
  • Anuradha Bhasin v Union of India 2020 Internet access and use for speech and trade protected; indefinite shutdown impermissible.

Memory aids

  • 79: Mere conduit + Due diligence = Safe; Aid or Ignore a notice = Sunk.
  • SSMI: 50 lakh users; 3 officers (CCO, Nodal, Grievance).

Exam traps

  • TrapS.79 safe harbour is conditional, not absolute.
  • TrapAfter Shreya Singhal, 'actual knowledge' = court order or government notification, not a private complaint.
  • TrapSSMI threshold is 5 million registered users.
  • TrapUnder the 2026 amendment, takedown on a court order or government notice is 3 hours.
  • TrapPlain AI-generated text is outside the SGI definition.
4

Privacy and Data Protection - Puttaswamy, SPDI Rules 2011, DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Art 21; Part IIIRight to privacy.
S.3-17, 18-29, 33, 39 DPDP Act 2023Applicability, consent, duties, rights, Board, appeal, penalties, bar of civil courts.
S.43A, 72A IT Act; SPDI Rules 2011Earlier compensation and confidentiality regime.

Cases in one line

  • K.S. Puttaswamy v Union of India (Privacy) 2017 Privacy is a fundamental right; three-fold test of legality, legitimate aim and proportionality.
  • K.S. Puttaswamy v Union of India (Aadhaar) 2018 Aadhaar upheld with limits; S.57 struck down; proportionality.
  • PUCL v Union of India 1997 Guidelines to curb arbitrary telephone tapping.
  • R. Rajagopal v State of Tamil Nadu 1994 Right to privacy implicit in Art 21; limits on publication of private life of citizens.

Memory aids

  • DPDP rights: A-C-G-N = Access, Correction/erasure, Grievance, Nominate.

Exam traps

  • TrapMaximum DPDP penalty is Rs 250 crore (security safeguards), not Rs 500 crore.
  • TrapThe child age under DPDP is below 18; behavioural tracking and targeted ads for children are banned.
  • TrapThe Data Protection Board is an adjudicatory body; appeals lie to TDSAT.
  • TrapDPDP provides no right to compensation to individuals; S.43A compensation remains until its omission.
  • TrapOnly consent or legitimate use is a ground; there is no 'legitimate interest' ground as in the GDPR.

Authorities, Adjudication, Jurisdiction and Cyber Investigation Procedure

Medium yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

S.46, 47, 48, 61, 62 IT ActAdjudication, appeal, bar of civil courts.
S.70A, 70B IT ActNCIIPC and CERT-In.
S.94, 105, 173, 530 BNSSElectronic summons, recording, e-FIR, electronic trials.

Cases in one line

  • Banyan Tree Holding v A. Murali Krishna Reddy 2010 Targeting test for jurisdiction over websites.
  • Satyam Infoway v Sifynet Solutions 2004 Domain names get passing-off protection.

Memory aids

  • 46 = Adjudicate up to 5 crore; 62 = HC in 60 days.

Exam traps

  • TrapAdjudicating Officer limit: claims up to Rs 5 crore.
  • TrapAppeal from TDSAT lies to the High Court within 60 days.
  • TrapCERT-In 2022 directions: 6-hour incident reporting.

Cyber Crimes under BNS and Emerging Issues - Deepfakes, Online Gaming, Digital Arrest, Cyber Fraud

Medium yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

S.318, 319, 336, 75-79, 294, 196, 152, 111 BNSBNS provisions often used with the IT Act.
Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act 2025Ban on online money games.

Cases in one line

  • Sharat Babu Digumarti v Government of NCT of Delhi 2016 Special law prevails over general IPC offences.
  • Shreya Singhal v Union of India 2015 S.66A struck down; free speech online.

Memory aids

  • Online fraud = 318/319 BNS + 66C/66D IT Act.

Exam traps

  • Trap'Digital arrest' has no legal basis; it is a fraud.
  • TrapPlain AI text is outside the SGI definition under the 2026 Rules.
  • TrapBNS S.152 replaced sedition but is not a mere renumbering; it is a new formulation.
