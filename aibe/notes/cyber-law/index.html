%Where the marks come from

Cyber Law carries about 2 of 100 questions. About 100 questions, 1 mark each; no negative marking; 3 hours 30 minutes to finish; offline, open book.

What the examiner tests

  • Family Law is a high-volume, low-difficulty zone: most questions are one-line recall of a section number, a time period, a landmark case or 'which is correct' on a personal-law rule. Numbers (ages, years, months, fractions) are the favourite.
  • Expect the 8 Family Law questions to spread roughly as: Hindu law (marriage, divorce, maintenance, adoption, guardianship, succession, joint family) 3-4; Muslim law 2-3; Christian/Parsi/Special Marriage Act 1; statutes such as Domestic Violence Act, Dowry Prohibition Act, Child Marriage Act, maintenance under BNSS 1-2. This split is an estimate.
  • The 2 Cyber Law questions almost always come from the IT Act: offences and punishments (S.65-67B, 66C/66D/66F), S.66A strike-down (Shreya Singhal), S.69A, S.79 intermediary safe harbour, definitions, digital signature, adjudication. Add one current-affairs hook: DPDP Act 2023 / Rules 2025, IT Rules 2021 and the 2026 synthetic-content amendment.
  • Recent-development questions are plausible in both subjects: Shayara Bano and the 2019 Act, Abdul Samad (2024), Shilpa Sailesh (2023), X v Y (2025, S.25 HMA in void marriages), Supriyo (2023), state UCCs (Uttarakhand, Gujarat, Assam), Waqf Amendment Act 2025 interim order, Puttaswamy, DPDP Rules 2025.
  • Because BNS/BNSS replaced IPC/CrPC from 1 July 2024, a stem may quote either numbering (e.g. S.498A IPC or S.85 BNS; S.125 CrPC or S.144 BNSS). Learn both.

Study in this order

  • 1. IT Act offences and penalties (S.43, 65, 66-66F, 67-67B, 69, 69A, 72, 72A)
  • 2. S.79 and intermediary liability; Shreya Singhal; IT Rules 2021 and the 2026 amendment
  • 3. Privacy and data protection: Puttaswamy, SPDI Rules 2011, DPDP Act 2023 and Rules 2025
  • 4. Digital signature, electronic records, authorities and adjudication
  • 5. Emerging: deepfakes, online gaming ban, digital arrest scams, CERT-In

Exam tips

  • Never leave a question blank. In Family Law, eliminate options that mix personal laws (e.g. Hindu-law rules in a Muslim-law question).
  • Tab the bare Acts: HMA S.5, 11-13B, 24-25; HAMA S.6-12, 18-23; HSA S.6, 8, 14-15; DMMA S.2; SMA S.4-5, 27-28; DV Act S.2, 12, 17-23; IT Act S.43, 65-67B, 69A, 79 and the First Schedule.

The latest paper

  • AIBE XXI was held on 7 June 2026 (offline OMR); BCI reported no change in syllabus or pattern. Check the BCI notice for the date of the next AIBE.
