Motor Vehicles Act: Insurance against Third-Party Risks (Chapter XI, Secs 145-164)

Very high yield1–2 Qs expected6 min readSec 146; Sec 147; Sec 149; Sec 157

iOverview

Compulsory insurance and the insurer's liability are heavily tested: Sec 146, 147, 149, 'pay and recover', defences available to insurer.

★Must know

  1. Sec 146 NECESSITY FOR INSURANCE: no person shall use, except as a passenger, or cause or allow any other person to use, a motor vehicle in a public place unless there is in force in relation to the use of the vehicle by that person or that other person, as the case may be, a policy of insurance complying with the requirements of this Chapter. Exception: vehicles owned by the Central/ State Government and used for Government purposes unconnected with commercial enterprise. The insurance requirement is compulsory third-party (Act-only) cover.
  2. Sec 147 REQUIREMENTS OF POLICIES: the policy must be issued by an authorised insurer and must insure the person (owner) or classes of persons specified, against any liability which may be incurred in respect of the death of or bodily injury to any person (including owner of the goods or his authorised representative carried in the vehicle) or damage to any property of a third party caused by or arising out of the use of the vehicle in a public place; and against the death of or bodily injury to any passenger of a public service vehicle caused by or arising out of the use of the vehicle. The policy is not required to cover liability in respect of death or injury arising out of and in the course of employment of the employee (workmen's compensation liability is otherwise covered), the death or bodily injury to the owner's employee other than the driver/ conductor/ ticket examiner; and contractual liability. Limit: unlimited for death or bodily injury; for third-party property damage, the limit is fixed (as per the Central Government rules; under the IRDAI regime the policy covers property damage up to Rs 7.5 lakh as per tariff - verify). Gratuitous passengers: the Supreme Court's decisions (New India Assurance v Asha Rani, 2003 and later cases) address whether the statutory policy covers gratuitous passengers in goods vehicles; verify the current position before answering.
  3. Sec 149 DUTY OF INSURERS TO SATISFY JUDGMENTS AND AWARDS AGAINST PERSONS INSURED IN RESPECT OF THIRD PARTY RISKS: if, after a certificate of insurance has been issued, a judgment or award is obtained in respect of any liability required to be covered by a policy under Sec 147, the insurer shall, notwithstanding that the insurer may be entitled to avoid or cancel the policy, pay to the person entitled to the benefit of the decree any sum not exceeding the sum assured payable thereunder, as if he were the judgment debtor, in respect of the liability, together with any amount payable in respect of costs and any sum payable in respect of interest. Sec 149(2): no sum is payable by an insurer in respect of any judgment unless before the commencement of the proceedings in which the judgment is given the insurer had notice through the Court or the claimant; and the insurer may defend only on the SPECIFIED GROUNDS: (a) that there has been a breach of a specified condition of the policy, being one of the following: (i) a condition excluding the use of the vehicle (A) for hire or reward where the vehicle is on the date of the contract of insurance a vehicle not covered by a permit to ply for hire or reward; (B) for organised racing and speed testing; (C) for a purpose not allowed by the permit under which the vehicle is used where the vehicle is a transport vehicle; (D) without side-car being attached where the vehicle is a motor cycle; or (ii) a condition excluding driving by a named person or persons or by any person who is not duly licensed, or by any person who has been disqualified for holding or obtaining a driving licence during the period of disqualification; or (iii) a condition excluding liability for injury caused or contributed to by conditions of war, civil war, riot or civil commotion; or (b) that the policy is void on the ground that it was obtained by the non-disclosure of a material fact or by a representation of fact which was false in some material particular. The insurer's right is subject to proof that the breach was FUNDAMENTAL and was the cause of the accident, and burden lies on the insurer.
  4. 'PAY AND RECOVER' DOCTRINE: National Insurance Co Ltd v Swaran Singh (2004) (three-Judge Bench): where the driver has a fake or invalid licence, the insurer is liable to pay the third-party claimant but may recover the amount from the owner IF the owner is shown to have been at fault - i.e., the insurer must prove that the owner was aware of the invalidity or failed to verify the licence; mere absence of valid licence is not by itself a ground to deny the third party claim; the Tribunal can direct the insurer to pay first and recover from the insured/ owner (this is the 'pay and recover' principle). In Mukund Dewangan v Oriental Insurance (2017) (three-judge Bench), the Court held that a driver holding a licence to drive a light motor vehicle (LMV) is entitled to drive a transport vehicle of that class whose gross vehicle weight does not exceed 7,500 kg without a separate endorsement; the insurer cannot deny liability on that ground. A November 2024 five-judge Bench decision (Bajaj Allianz General Insurance v Rambha Devi, November 2024) reaffirmed Mukund Dewangan: LMV licence holders can drive light transport vehicles up to 7,500 kg GVW. The principle: an insurer cannot avoid liability to a third party; it may only avoid the insured's indemnity if specific breach is proved.
  5. OTHER INSURANCE POINTS: Sec 148: settlement between insurers and insured persons; Sec 150: rights of third parties against insurers on insolvency of the insured; Sec 151: duty to give information as to insurance; Sec 152: settlement between insurer and insured persons; Sec 156: effect of death on certain causes of action; Sec 157: transfer of certificate of insurance - the certificate of insurance and the policy described therein are deemed to have been transferred in favour of the person to whom the motor vehicle is transferred (deemed transfer, with the transferee to apply within 14 days for the necessary changes). Insurers can also be impleaded (Sec 170). A 'no fault' claim and a 'fault' claim can be pursued (see 2.3).

⚖Landmark cases

National Insurance Co v Swaran Singh

2004

Pay and recover; insurer must prove willful breach by the owner.

Mukund Dewangan v Oriental Insurance

2017

LMV licence covers light transport vehicles up to 7,500 kg GVW.

Bajaj Allianz General Insurance v Rambha Devi (Constitution Bench, Nov 2024)

-

Constitution Bench reaffirmed Mukund Dewangan: LMV licence covers light transport vehicles up to 7,500 kg GVW.

2003

Insurer's liability for owner's breach where owner takes reasonable care.

!Exam traps

  • TrapThe insurer's defences are limited to Sec 149(2) grounds; mere breach is not enough - the breach must be fundamental and causative.
  • TrapInsurer remains liable to the THIRD PARTY even when entitled to avoid the policy as against the insured (pay and recover).
  • TrapSec 146: insurance is compulsory for all vehicles in a public place except Government vehicles used for non-commercial purposes.

?Likely questions

  1. Which section makes insurance compulsory? Sec 146.
  2. Duty of insurers to satisfy judgments: Sec 149.
  3. 'Pay and recover' was laid down in: National Insurance v Swaran Singh.
  4. Defences available to insurer: Sec 149(2).
