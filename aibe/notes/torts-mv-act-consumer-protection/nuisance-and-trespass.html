Nuisance and Trespass

iOverview

Public and private nuisance; essentials, defences, remedies; trespass to person, land and goods.

★Must know

  1. NUISANCE: an unlawful interference with a person's use or enjoyment of land, or of some right over or in connection with it. PRIVATE NUISANCE: unreasonable interference with the use and enjoyment of land (actionable on proof of damage - sensible personal discomfort or physical damage to property; no need for trespass). PUBLIC NUISANCE: an act or omission which materially affects the reasonable comfort and convenience of a class of Her Majesty's subjects; also a crime (Sec 268 IPC, now BNS Sec 270); a private individual can sue only if he suffers 'special damage' over and above the general public (Sec 91 CPC - suit with the Advocate General's leave/ two persons with court's leave).
  2. ESSENTIALS OF PRIVATE NUISANCE: (1) an unreasonable and substantial interference; (2) the interference must be with the use and enjoyment of land; (3) actual damage. Types: physical damage to property (St Helen's Smelting Co v Tipping, 1865 - fumes damaging trees; a higher standard for property damage than for personal discomfort, which is judged by the character of the locality) and interference with comfort (smell, noise, vibration). 'Sticky' factors: locality (Sturges v Bridgman, 1879: 'what would be a nuisance in Belgrave Square would not necessarily be so in Bermondsey'); duration; sensitivity of the plaintiff (Robinson v Kilvert: abnormally sensitive trade not protected); malice (Christie v Davey, 1893: banging walls in response to music lessons; Hollywood Silver Fox Farm v Emmett, 1936: gun shots to disturb foxes); utility is no defence.
  3. DEFENCES: prescription (20 years' continuous enjoyment of the nuisance as of right - Sec 15 Easements Act); statutory authority; consent; coming to the nuisance is NOT a defence (Sturges v Bridgman); act of a stranger; inevitable accident; public benefit/ utility is not a defence. REMEDIES: damages; injunction; abatement (self-help, with notice, without causing unnecessary damage). Indian cases: Ushaben v Bhagyalakshmi Chitra Mandir (1978, Gujarat HC): screening of the film 'Jai Santoshi Maa' hurt religious feelings but was not a nuisance as no interference with enjoyment of land; Radhey Shyam v Gur Prasad (1978 All HC): flour mill's noise/ vibration; Municipal Council, Ratlam v Vardichan (1980): SC compelled the municipality to remove open drains under Sec 133 CrPC (now BNSS Sec 152) - public nuisance and the duty of local bodies cannot be defeated by financial inability; Sec 133 CrPC conditional order of Magistrate for removal of public nuisance (BNSS Sec 152).
  4. TRESPASS: direct interference with the person, goods or land of the plaintiff, actionable per se (without proof of damage). Trespass to the person: assault (apprehension of immediate harmful contact; words alone do not constitute assault if accompanied by no act), battery (direct application of force; hostility not essential - Collins v Wilcock), false imprisonment (total restraint of liberty without lawful justification - Bird v Jones, 1845: partial obstruction is not imprisonment; Meering v Grahame-White Aviation, 1920: restraint without knowledge). Remedy: habeas corpus and damages (Rudul Sah; Bhim Singh). Trespass to land: unauthorised entry/ remaining/ placing anything on the land; the plaintiff needs possession (not ownership); trespass ab initio (Six Carpenters' Case, 1610); remedies: damages, injunction, ejectment, mesne profits (Sec 2(12) CPC), Sec 6 Specific Relief Act (summary possession), self-help re-entry (limited). Defences: licence, necessity, justification by law, private defence. Entick v Carrington (1765): a state officer's entry without legal authority is trespass. Trespass to goods: direct interference with chattels in possession; conversion (dealing with goods inconsistent with the owner's right); detinue (wrongful detention).

!Exam traps

  • TrapTrespass is actionable per se; private nuisance needs proof of damage.
  • TrapComing to the nuisance is no defence; but prescription (20 years) is.
  • TrapTrespass needs DIRECT interference; if consequential, it is nuisance or action on the case.
