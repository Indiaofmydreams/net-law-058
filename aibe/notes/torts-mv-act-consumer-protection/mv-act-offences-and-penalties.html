Motor Vehicles Act: Offences and Penalties (as amended in 2019) and Recent Policy Developments

iOverview

Known penalty figures appear as numerical MCQs. Treat the figures as 'verify against the bare Act'; some States have varied rates under Sec 200.

★Must know

  1. Sec 177: general provision for punishment of offences - fine of Rs 500 (earlier Rs 100); Sec 177A: offences relating to rules of road regulations - Rs 500. Sec 179: disobedience of orders - Rs 2,000; Sec 180: allowing unauthorised persons to drive - Rs 5,000 and/ or imprisonment up to three months; Sec 181: driving without a licence - Rs 5,000; Sec 182: offences relating to licences - Rs 10,000; Sec 183: driving at excessive speed - Rs 1,000 for LMV, Rs 2,000 for medium goods/ passenger vehicles (for a second offence, impounding the licence); Sec 184: DANGEROUS DRIVING - imprisonment up to six months to one year, or fine Rs 1,000-5,000, or both for first offence; subsequent offence within three years: imprisonment up to two years or fine up to Rs 10,000 or both; Sec 185: DRUNKEN DRIVING (blood alcohol content exceeding 30 mg per 100 ml of blood detected through a breath analyser) - first offence: imprisonment up to six months or fine up to Rs 10,000 or both; second or subsequent offence within three years: imprisonment up to two years or fine up to Rs 15,000 or both; Sec 186: driving when mentally or physically unfit - Rs 1,000/ 2,000; Sec 189: racing and trials of speed - imprisonment up to 1 month, fine Rs 5,000, or both; Sec 190: using vehicle in unsafe condition - Rs 1,000; Sec 192: using a vehicle without registration - Rs 5,000 (first), up to Rs 10,000 subsequent; Sec 192A: without permit - up to Rs 10,000; Sec 194: overloading - Rs 20,000 and additional Rs 2,000 per extra tonne; Sec 194B: seat belt - Rs 1,000; Sec 194C: overloading of two-wheelers - Rs 2,000 and disqualification of licence for 3 months; Sec 194D: protective headgear (helmet) violation - Rs 1,000 and disqualification of driving licence for three months; Sec 194E: failure to give way to emergency vehicles - Rs 10,000; Sec 196: driving without insurance - imprisonment up to 3 months or fine Rs 2,000 or both (second offence: 3 months/ Rs 4,000); Sec 199A: OFFENCES BY JUVENILES - where an offence is committed by a juvenile, the guardian or the owner of the motor vehicle shall be deemed to be guilty and shall be liable to be proceeded against and punished accordingly (imprisonment up to three years and a fine of Rs 25,000); the registration of the motor vehicle shall be cancelled for a period of twelve months; the juvenile shall not be eligible for a driving licence until the age of 25 years (the juvenile is to be tried under the Juvenile Justice Act 2015). Sec 200: compounding of certain offences. The Central Government may notify the maximum fines; States can notify different compounding amounts.
  2. Sec 304A IPC (now Sec 106 BNS): causing death by negligence - imprisonment up to 5 years (2 years for doctors under BNS Sec 106(1) proviso) and fine; hit and run: BNS Sec 106(2) - 10 years' imprisonment + fine for a driver who causes death by rash and negligent driving and escapes without reporting to the police/ magistrate (the provision was kept in abeyance by the Government in January 2024 after protests by transporters until consultations with the All India Motor Transport Congress; verify its current status). Sec 281 BNS: rash driving or riding on a public way - up to 6 months or fine Rs 1,000 or both (formerly Sec 279 IPC).
  3. RECENT POLICY/ CASE DEVELOPMENTS: (1) PM-RAHAT cashless treatment scheme (Rs 1.5 lakh; seven days) and Supreme Court monitoring (May and August 2026); (2) Compensation to Victims (Interim Relief) Scheme, 2026 under Sec 164A - applies to insured vehicles; SC asked for wide publicity (August 2026); (3) LMV/ transport vehicle licence ruling (Mukund Dewangan 2017; reaffirmed by a Constitution Bench in November 2024); (4) Good Samaritan law (Sec 134A) and Save Life Foundation direction (May 2026); (5) Supreme Court's treatment of the MV Act as 'beneficial legislation' for interpretation (liberal construction in favour of victims); (6) Digital enforcement: e-challans, ANPR cameras, DigiLocker documents, Vahan/ Sarathi databases; (7) States' rollback/ modification of penalties under Sec 200; (8) National Road Safety Policy and Bharat NCAP; (9) Aggregator guidelines 2020 (Motor Vehicle Aggregator Guidelines) and Sec 93.

!Exam traps

  • TrapDrunken driving threshold is 30 mg alcohol per 100 ml of blood.
  • TrapHelmet violation: Rs 1,000 + 3 months disqualification.
  • TrapJuvenile offences: guardian/ owner deemed guilty; registration cancelled for 12 months.
  • TrapVerify exact figures with the bare Act; States vary.

?Likely questions

  1. BAC limit under Sec 185: 30 mg per 100 ml of blood.
  2. Fine for driving without licence: Rs 5,000.
  3. Sec 199A: guardian/ owner liable when a juvenile commits an offence.
  4. Which section deals with dangerous driving? Sec 184.
