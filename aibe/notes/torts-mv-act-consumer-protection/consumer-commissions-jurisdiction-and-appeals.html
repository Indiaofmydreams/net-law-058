Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions: Composition, Jurisdiction, Procedure and Appeals

iOverview

Pecuniary jurisdiction, limitation, timelines, appeals and pre-deposit are standard numerical questions.

★Must know

  1. THREE-TIER STRUCTURE: District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (District Commission, Sec 28), State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (State Commission, Sec 42), National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (National Commission, Sec 53). The District Commission consists of a President and not less than two other members (one of whom is a woman). State Commission: a President (a person who is or has been a Judge of a High Court) and not less than four members (maximum as prescribed); National Commission: President (a person who is or has been a Judge of the Supreme Court) and not less than four and not more than such number of members as may be prescribed (up to 11). The 'Consumer Protection (Qualification for Appointment, Method of Recruitment, Procedure of Appointment, Term of Office, Resignation and Removal of the President and Members of the State Commission and District Commission) Rules, 2020' prescribe conditions; Selection Committees - the Supreme Court in various orders (including in 2024) directed adherence to the Rules.
  2. PECUNIARY JURISDICTION (Sec 34, 47, 58 as amended by the Consumer Protection (Jurisdiction of the District Commission, the State Commission and the National Commission) Rules, 2021 notified on 30 December 2021): the value of the goods or services PAID AS CONSIDERATION decides jurisdiction: District Commission - up to Rs 50 lakh; State Commission - more than Rs 50 lakh but not more than Rs 2 crore; National Commission - more than Rs 2 crore. (Under the original 2019 Act, the District's limit was Rs 1 crore and the State's Rs 10 crore - these were on the basis of value of goods/ services AND compensation claimed under the 1986 Act; the 2019 Act changed the basis to the consideration paid.) Rutu Mihir Panchal v Union of India (2025 INSC 593, decided 29 April 2025): the Supreme Court upheld the validity of the pecuniary jurisdiction provisions based on the value of consideration paid and the 2021 Rules, rejecting the challenge that the change was arbitrary; it observed that Parliament is free to prescribe jurisdiction and that the 2021 Rules were validly made. In 2026 the Supreme Court has again noted anomalies in the consideration-based approach (for example, cases where the consideration paid is small but the loss/ claim is large, as in hospital or insurance cases) and sought the Centre's response; the legislative/ rule position may change - verify.
  3. TERRITORIAL JURISDICTION (Sec 34(2)): a complaint may be instituted in a District Commission within the local limits of whose jurisdiction (a) the opposite party or each of the opposite parties, where there are more than one, at the time of the institution of the complaint, ordinarily resides or carries on business or has a branch office or personally works for gain; or (b) any of the opposite parties, where there are more than one, at the time of the institution of the complaint, actually and voluntarily resides, or carries on business or has a branch office, or personally works for gain, provided that in such case either the permission of the District Commission is given, or the opposite parties who do not reside, or carry on business or have a branch office, or personally work for gain, as the case may be, acquiesce in such institution; or (c) the cause of action, wholly or in part, arises; or (d) the complainant resides or personally works for gain (NEW in 2019 - convenient forum for the consumer).
  4. WHO MAY FILE (Sec 35(1)): (a) the consumer to whom such goods are sold or delivered or agreed to be sold or delivered, or such service is provided or agreed to be provided; (b) any recognised consumer association, whether the consumer is a member or not; (c) one or more consumers, where there are numerous consumers having the same interest, with the permission of the District Commission, on behalf of or for the benefit of all consumers so interested (class action/ representative complaint); (d) the Central Government, the Central Authority or the State Government; (e) in case of death of a consumer, his legal heir or representative; or (f) in case of a consumer being a minor, his parent or legal guardian. Complaints can be filed ELECTRONICALLY (e-Daakhil/ e-Jagriti portal), by post or in person. Fee: nil for complaints up to Rs 5 lakh; graded fee beyond (Consumer Protection (Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions) Rules 2020).
  5. LIMITATION (Sec 69): the District Commission, the State Commission or the National Commission shall not admit a complaint unless it is filed within TWO YEARS from the date on which the cause of action has arisen; a complaint may be entertained after the period specified if the complainant satisfies the Commission that he had sufficient cause for not filing the complaint within such period (the Commission must record reasons for condoning delay). Continuing wrong: fresh cause of action.
  6. PROCEDURE (Secs 35-38): admissibility of complaint to be decided within 21 days from the date of filing (Sec 36(3)); if the question of admissibility is not decided within 21 days, the complaint shall be deemed to have been admitted; the opposite party must be given a notice and shall give his version within 30 days, extendable by not more than 15 days (Sec 38(2)(a)) - the Supreme Court in New India Assurance v Hilli Multipurpose Cold Storage (2020, Constitution Bench) held that the 45-day limit is mandatory and the Commission has no power to extend beyond 45 days; where the opposite party denies or disputes the allegations that need not be tested by laboratory, the Commission decides on the basis of evidence/ affidavits (documents); Sec 38(7): the complaint shall be decided as expeditiously as possible and endeavour shall be made to decide the complaint within a period of THREE MONTHS from the date of receipt of notice by opposite party where the complaint does not require analysis or testing of commodities and within FIVE MONTHS if it requires analysis or testing. The Commission has the powers of a civil court under the CPC (summoning, discovery, affidavits, commissions) - Sec 38(9); the proceedings are judicial proceedings (Sec 38(10)). Sec 37: MEDIATION - at the first hearing, if it appears to the District Commission that there exists elements of a settlement which may be acceptable to the parties, it may, at the option of the parties, direct them to give their consent in writing to refer the matter for settlement by mediation (Mediation cells attached to Commissions - Secs 74-81).
  7. RELIEFS (Sec 39): remove the defect pointed out by the appropriate laboratory; replace the goods with new goods of similar description which shall be free from any defect; return to the complainant the price or the charges paid; pay such amount as may be awarded as compensation for any loss or injury suffered by the consumer due to the negligence of the opposite party (and PUNITIVE DAMAGES in appropriate circumstances); provide for adequate costs to parties; direct the opposite party to discontinue the unfair trade practice or restrictive trade practice or not to repeat them; not to offer the hazardous or unsafe goods for sale; withdraw the hazardous goods from being offered for sale; cease manufacture of hazardous goods; issue corrective advertisement to neutralise the effect of misleading advertisement; to pay such sum as determined if it is of the opinion that loss or injury has been suffered by a large number of consumers who are not identifiable conveniently (not less than 5% of the value of such defective goods sold or service provided, to be credited to the Consumer Welfare Fund). Sec 40: POWER OF REVIEW - the District Commission shall have the power to review any of the order passed by it if there is an error apparent on the face of the record, either of its own motion or on an application made by any of the parties within thirty days of such order.
  8. APPEALS: Sec 41 - from the District Commission to the State Commission within 45 days (may be entertained after 45 days for sufficient cause); no appeal shall be entertained by the State Commission unless the appellant has deposited FIFTY PER CENT of the amount ordered by the District Commission. Sec 51 - from the State Commission (in its original jurisdiction) to the National Commission within 30 days; with the deposit of 50% of the amount. Sec 67 - from the National Commission (in its original jurisdiction) to the Supreme Court within 30 days (the Supreme Court may entertain after 30 days for sufficient cause). Also: appeals from the CCPA to the National Commission (Sec 24). Second appeal: revision petitions before the National Commission against State Commission's appellate orders (Sec 58(1)(b)): Revision lies where the State Commission appears to have exercised a jurisdiction not vested in it, or failed to exercise a jurisdiction vested in it, or acted illegally or with material irregularity. No second appeal lies against the State Commission's appellate order; revision lies to the National Commission under Sec 58(1)(b), and the Supreme Court may be approached under Art 136.
  9. ENFORCEMENT (Secs 71-72): Sec 71: every order made by a District Commission, State Commission or the National Commission shall be enforced by it in the same manner as if it were a decree made by a Court in a suit before it and the provisions of Order XXI of the CPC shall apply, as far as may be, subject to the modification that every reference therein to the decree shall be construed as reference to the order made under this Act. Sec 72: PENALTY FOR NON-COMPLIANCE of the order - imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than one month but which may extend to three years, or a fine which shall not be less than twenty-five thousand rupees but which may extend to one lakh rupees, or both; the Commission has the powers of the Judicial Magistrate of the First Class for the purpose of trial of such offences (Sec 72(2)); the offences may be tried summarily (Sec 72(3)).
  10. INTERIM ORDERS and special powers: The Commission may issue interim orders; National Commission has power to hear cases against unfair contracts (Sec 59(2)); State Commission - Sec 49(2): declare any terms of contract which is unfair to any consumer to be null and void. Hearings may be conducted by video conferencing under the Rules. Review powers: State/ National Commissions (Sec 50, Sec 60).

⚖Landmark cases

Rutu Mihir Panchal v Union of India

2025 INSC 593

Pecuniary jurisdiction based on consideration paid is constitutionally valid.

New India Assurance Co v Hilli Multipurpose Cold Storage

2020

45-day outer limit for written version is mandatory.

Pioneer Urban Land & Infrastructure v Govindan Raghavan

2019

One-sided terms in builder-buyer agreements are unfair trade practice; consumer not bound by them.

Imperia Structures v Anil Patni

2020

Consumer remedies and RERA remedies are concurrent.

Bar of Indian Lawyers v D.K. Gandhi PS

2024

Advocates' services held outside the Act; judgment was stayed/ referred to a larger Bench - check status.

!Exam traps

  • TrapPecuniary jurisdiction: Rs 50 lakh / Rs 2 crore - NOT the original Rs 1 crore / Rs 10 crore.
  • TrapBasis: value of CONSIDERATION PAID, not the claim.
  • TrapAppeal to the State Commission needs a 50% deposit.
  • TrapComplaint: 2 years; version of the opposite party: 30 + 15 days (strictly 45).
  • TrapSec 72 imprisonment 1 month to 3 years; fine Rs 25,000 to Rs 1 lakh.

?Likely questions

  1. Limitation period for filing a consumer complaint: two years.
  2. District Commission's pecuniary jurisdiction: up to Rs 50 lakh.
  3. Appeal from the District Commission lies to State Commission within 45 days.
  4. Section 72: punishment for non-compliance.
