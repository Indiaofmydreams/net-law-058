Consumer Protection Act: Product Liability, Unfair Contracts and Key Case Law

iOverview

Product liability is a new, examiner-friendly chapter of the 2019 Act. Combine with medical negligence and builder-buyer case law.

★Must know

  1. Chapter VI (Secs 82-87) PRODUCT LIABILITY: Sec 82: a product liability action may be brought by a complainant for any harm caused by a defective product manufactured by a product manufacturer or serviced by a product service provider or sold by a product seller. 'Harm' includes damage to any property other than the product itself, personal injury, illness or death, mental agony or emotional distress attendant to personal injury or illness or damage to property, and any loss of consortium or services or other loss resulting from a harm; it does not include any harm caused to a product itself or any damage to property on account of breach of warranty conditions or any commercial or economic loss, including any direct or consequential loss relating thereto. Sec 84: LIABILITY OF PRODUCT MANUFACTURERS - a product manufacturer shall be liable in a product liability action, if (a) the product contains a manufacturing defect; or (b) the product is defective in design; or (c) there is a deviation from manufacturing specifications; or (d) the product does not conform to the express warranty; or (e) the product fails to contain adequate instructions of correct usage to prevent any harm or any warning regarding improper or incorrect usage. Sec 85: product service provider liable where the service was faulty, imperfect, deficient or inadequate in quality, nature or manner of performance; or there was an act of omission, commission, negligence or conscious withholding of information which caused harm; or the service provider did not issue adequate cautions or instructions; or the service did not conform to express warranty or the terms and conditions of the contract. Sec 86: product seller (who is not a product manufacturer) liable if he has exercised substantial control over designing, testing, manufacturing, packaging or labelling; or altered or modified the product; or made an express warranty independent of the manufacturer; or the product has been sold and the identity of the product manufacturer is not known or the manufacturer is not subject to Indian law / cannot be enforced; or failed to exercise reasonable care in assembling, inspecting or maintaining; or failed to pass on a warning or instruction. Sec 87: EXCEPTIONS - a product manufacturer shall not be liable if at the time of harm the product was misused, altered or modified; sale by a seller is covered under conditions; a warning was given; the harm was caused to a user who was under the influence of alcohol or drugs not prescribed; the product is sold to an experienced user/ in bulk to another manufacturer where the buyer is a sophisticated user with knowledge; etc.
  2. UNFAIR CONTRACTS: State Commission (Sec 49(2)) and National Commission (Sec 59(2)) have the power to declare any terms of contract which is unfair to any consumer to be null and void (if complaints are within their jurisdiction). The Supreme Court in Pioneer Urban Land v Govindan Raghavan (2019) and related decisions held that one-sided builder-buyer agreements (e.g., clauses allowing forfeiture of heavy sums for buyer's default while paying negligible compensation for the builder's delay) are unfair trade practices and not binding; buyers entitled to refund with interest when possession is inordinately delayed (Wg Cdr Arifur Rahman Khan v DLF Southern Homes, 2020; Experion Developers v Sushma Ashok Shiroor, 2022).
  3. MEDIATION (Secs 74-81): Consumer Mediation Cells attached to the District, State and National Commissions; mediation by empanelled mediators; settlement agreement signed by the parties; order of the Commission to give effect; no appeal against orders passed with consent. ARBITRATION CLAUSE: a consumer complaint is not barred by an arbitration agreement - the CPA remedy is an additional statutory remedy (Emaar MGF Land v Aftab Singh, 2018, relying on the 1986 Act and Sec 100 of the CPA 2019; the decision has been reaffirmed). RERA and the CPA are concurrent remedies (Imperia Structures v Anil Patni, 2020). Insurance/ banking, telecom and similar services also under the Act, subject to other statutory forums where the law says so (e.g., Telecom: Sec 7B of the Telegraph Act arbitration was held not to bar consumer forum in the Supreme Court's decision in General Manager Telecom v M. Krishnan, 2009 - the Court held that Sec 7B Telegraph Act made the arbitration route the exclusive remedy for telephone billing disputes; verify current position after the 2019 Act and the Telecommunications Act 2023).
  4. MEDICAL AND PROFESSIONAL SERVICES: Medical negligence cases - Bolam standard and Jacob Mathew; V. Kishan Rao v Nikhil Super Speciality Hospital (2010) held that expert evidence isn't mandatory in each case. Advocates: the Supreme Court in Bar of Indian Lawyers v D.K. Gandhi PS National Institute of Communicable Diseases (14 May 2024) held that advocates' services under a contract of personal service/ professional services are not 'service' under the CPA 2019; the decision was later stayed/ taken up in a review and referred to a larger Bench (verify the current status); earlier, Dr. Jacob Mathew. Banking and insurance services: 'service' under Sec 2(42); Insurance: the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority; the Ombudsman scheme; claims repudiation without valid ground is deficiency.
  5. SUPREME COURT CASE SNAPSHOT: Lucknow Development Authority v M.K. Gupta (1994); Laxmi Engineering Works v P.S.G. (1995); IMA v V.P. Shantha (1995); Spring Meadows Hospital v Harjol Ahluwalia (1998); State of Karnataka v Vishwabharathi House Building Co-op Society (2003) (validity of the Act); Pioneer Urban Land v Govindan Raghavan (2019); Imperia Structures v Anil Patni (2020); New India Assurance v Hilli Multipurpose Cold Storage (2020); Rutu Mihir Panchal v UoI (2025).

!Exam traps

  • TrapProduct liability covers manufacturers, service providers and sellers (Secs 84-86); 'harm' excludes damage to the product itself and purely commercial loss.
  • TrapArbitration clause doesn't oust the consumer forum.
  • TrapUnfair contract declarations are by State/ National Commissions.

  2. Sec 84 liability of: product manufacturer.
  3. Does an arbitration clause bar a consumer complaint? No.
