Motor Vehicles Act: Motor Accident Claims Tribunals and Computation of Compensation (Secs 165-176)

iOverview

Procedure (Secs 165-176) and the Supreme Court's compensation framework (Sarla Verma, Pranay Sethi) are the top-yield areas.

★Must know

  1. Sec 165: constitution of Claims Tribunals by the State Government for the area, adjudicating upon claims for compensation arising out of accidents involving the death of, or bodily injury to, persons arising out of the use of motor vehicles, or damages to any property of a third party so arising, or both. Tribunal: members - a sitting/ retired High Court judge, a District Judge or an Additional District Judge. Sec 175: BAR OF JURISDICTION OF CIVIL COURTS - where any Claims Tribunal has been constituted for any area, no Civil Court shall have jurisdiction to entertain any question relating to any claim for compensation which may be adjudicated upon by the Claims Tribunal for that area.
  2. Sec 166 APPLICATION FOR COMPENSATION: made (a) by the person who has sustained the injury; or (b) by the owner of the property; or (c) where death has resulted from the accident, by all or any of the legal representatives of the deceased; or (d) by any agent duly authorised by the person injured or all or any of the legal representatives of the deceased. Where all the legal representatives have not joined, the application shall be made on behalf of or for the benefit of all and the legal representatives who have not joined shall be impleaded as respondents. Sec 166(2): the application is made to the Claims Tribunal having jurisdiction over the area in which the accident occurred, or to the Claims Tribunal within the local limits of whose jurisdiction the claimant resides or carries on business or within the local limits of whose jurisdiction the defendant resides (as amended in 2019). Sec 166(3) (the six-month limit) was omitted by the 2019 Amendment, with effect from 1 April 2022. Sec 166(4): the Claims Tribunal shall treat any report of accidents forwarded to it under Sec 158(6) as an application for compensation under this Act (2019 amendment). Sec 158(6): the police officer must forward the Detailed Accident Report (DAR) within 30 days to the Claims Tribunal and the insurer; Sec 159: duty of police officers to prepare a DAR within 48 hours (Form of report by the police: Central Motor Vehicles Rules). The Supreme Court in Gohar Mohammed v UPSRTC (2022) gave directions on DAR/ Form-based claims.
  3. Sec 167: OPTION regarding claims for compensation in certain cases - where the death of or bodily injury to any person gives rise to a claim for compensation under this Act and also under the Workmen's Compensation Act 1923, the person entitled to compensation may, without prejudice to the provisions of Chapter X (no-fault liability), claim such compensation under either of those Acts but not under both. Sec 168: AWARD OF THE CLAIMS TRIBUNAL - on receipt of an application for compensation made under Sec 166, the Claims Tribunal shall, after giving notice of the application to the insurer and after giving the parties (including the insurer) an opportunity of being heard, hold an inquiry into the claim or, as the case may be, each of the claims and, subject to the provisions of Sec 162 may make an award determining the amount of compensation which appears to it to be JUST and specifying the person or persons to whom compensation shall be paid; the Tribunal shall specify the amount which shall be paid by the insurer or owner or driver of the vehicle involved in the accident or by all or any of them, as the case may be. Sec 169: PROCEDURE AND POWERS - the Tribunal may follow such summary procedure as it thinks fit; it has the powers of a Civil Court for summoning witnesses, enforcing attendance, discovery and production of documents (the Tribunal is not bound by the strict rules of procedure and evidence - proof on preponderance of probability; the standard of proof in claims is preponderance of probabilities, not beyond reasonable doubt - Bimla Devi v Himachal Road Transport Corporation, 2009). Sec 170: IMPLEADING INSURERS in claims for compensation - where in the course of any inquiry the insurer is given notice of the application, he may, with the previous permission in writing of the Tribunal, contest the claim on any of the grounds in Sec 149(2) (or on the grounds that there's collusion between the person making the claim and the person against whom the claim is made, or the person against whom the claim is made has failed to contest the claim). Sec 171: AWARD OF INTEREST where any claim is allowed - the Tribunal may direct that in addition to the amount of compensation simple interest shall also be paid at such rate and from such date not earlier than the date of making the claim as it may specify (courts commonly award 7.5% to 9% p.a.). Sec 172: AWARD OF COMPENSATORY COSTS in certain cases (false or vexatious claims or defences). Sec 173: APPEAL - any person aggrieved by an award of a Claims Tribunal may, within ninety days from the date of the award, prefer an appeal to the High Court (the High Court may entertain after 90 days if sufficient cause); no appeal by a person who is required to pay any amount in terms of such award shall be entertained by the High Court unless he has deposited with it twenty-five thousand rupees or fifty per cent of the amount so awarded, whichever is less, in the manner directed by the High Court. Sec 174: RECOVERY of money from insurer as arrears of land revenue (the Claims Tribunal issues a certificate to the Collector). Sec 176: power of the State Government to make rules.
  4. COMPUTATION OF COMPENSATION - MULTIPLIER METHOD (Sec 168 'just compensation'): General Manager, Kerala SRTC v Susamma Thomas (1994) adopted the multiplier method; Sarla Verma v Delhi Transport Corporation (2009): standardised the multiplier (age-wise table: 18 for 15-20 years, 17 for 21-25, 18 for 26-30, 17 for 31-35, 16 for 36-40, 15 for 41-45, 13 for 46-50, 11 for 51-55, 9 for 56-60, 7 for 61-65, 5 for 66-70), and the deduction for personal expenses of the deceased: 1/3 where the dependants are 2-3, 1/4 where 4-6, 1/5 where more than 6; for a bachelor, 50% deducted (parents dependent: personal expense 50%); in the case of a married deceased with a dependent spouse, 1/3. National Insurance Co v Pranay Sethi (2017) (five-Judge Bench): FUTURE PROSPECTS must be added to the established income of the deceased - for those with a permanent job: 50% if the deceased was below 40 years, 30% if 40-50 years, 15% if 50-60 years; for self-employed or fixed-wage persons: 40% (below 40), 25% (40-50), 10% (50-60) (no addition above 60). Conventional heads: loss of estate - Rs 15,000; funeral expenses - Rs 15,000; loss of consortium (spousal) - Rs 40,000, with a 10% enhancement every three years. Magma General Insurance v Nanu Ram (2018): loss of consortium extends to parental and filial consortium (children and parents): Rs 40,000 each. Raj Kumar v Ajay Kumar (2011): injury cases - functional disability and loss of earning capacity: the Tribunal must assess the percentage of permanent disability as regards the specific injured person's profession; heads include pain and suffering, medical expenses, attendant charges, loss of amenities, future medical expenses. Notional income for non-earning housewife/ children: courts adopt a notional income (e.g., Lata Wadhwa v State of Bihar - Rs 3,000 per month; Arun Kumar Agrawal v National Insurance (2010) value of services of a homemaker; Kirti v Oriental Insurance (2020)). Interest: simple, 9% in many cases (Supreme Court guidance varies).
  5. NEGLIGENCE/ BURDEN: the claimant must prove negligence of the driver (not required in Sec 140/ 163A claims) by preponderance of probabilities; res ipsa loquitur is applicable in some cases (e.g., a bus falling from a bridge); police charge-sheet is not conclusive (a FIR/ charge sheet can be used as evidence of negligence); acquittal in a criminal case does not bar a claim. Contributory negligence reduces compensation; composite negligence: the claimant can sue any of the joint tortfeasors for the entire amount and the one who pays can recover contribution (Khenyei). Owner's liability: vicarious (the driver is the owner's servant) - also if a vehicle has been sold but registration not transferred, the registered owner remains liable (Pushpa v Shakuntala, 2011: the SC held that 'owner' includes the person in possession after sale - verify).

!Exam traps

  • TrapAppeal against Tribunal's award: High Court within 90 days; insurer/ owner must deposit Rs 25,000 or 50% of the award, whichever is less (Sec 173).
  • TrapCivil Court's jurisdiction is barred by Sec 175.
  • TrapStandard of proof: preponderance of probability.
  • TrapClaims Tribunal can award interest from the date of the claim (Sec 171).
  • TrapPranay Sethi: 40% / 25% / 10% for self-employed; 50% / 30% / 15% for permanent-job holders (reading carefully!).

?Likely questions

  1. Appeal from the Claims Tribunal lies to: High Court within 90 days.
  2. Who may file a claim under Sec 166? Injured person, property owner, legal representatives, authorised agent.
  3. Future prospects for a permanent employee below 40: 50%.
  4. Sec 175: bar of civil courts.
