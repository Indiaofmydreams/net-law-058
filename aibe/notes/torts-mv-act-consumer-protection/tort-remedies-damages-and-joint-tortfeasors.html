Remedies, Damages, Joint Tortfeasors and Fatal Accidents

iOverview

Heads of damages, exemplary damages, Fatal Accidents Act and apportionment.

★Must know

  1. KINDS OF DAMAGES: nominal (right violated, no loss), contemptuous (technical win, worthless claim), general (presumed loss: pain and suffering), special (proved pecuniary loss), aggravated (injury to feelings due to malice/ insult), EXEMPLARY/ PUNITIVE (to punish and deter): Rookes v Devlin (1964) restricted exemplary damages to (i) oppressive, arbitrary or unconstitutional action by government servants, (ii) conduct calculated to make profit exceeding compensation, and (iii) cases authorised by statute. Indian courts award exemplary damages for constitutional torts (Rudul Sah; Nilabati) and in environmental disasters (M.C. Mehta).
  2. PERSONAL INJURY HEADS: pecuniary - medical expenses, loss of earnings, loss of earning capacity, cost of future care; non-pecuniary - pain and suffering, loss of amenities, loss of expectation of life. Death: Fatal Accidents Act 1855 - the dependants (wife, husband, parent, child) can sue for the pecuniary loss from the death caused by wrongful act, neglect or default of another; the action is for the benefit of the dependants and is brought by the personal representative or by dependants (Sec 1A - the dependants can sue where no representative sues within six months). Under the Motor Vehicles Act the Claims Tribunals follow tort principles (see MV module). Doctrine: actio personalis moritur cum persona saved by Sec 306 Succession Act. Survival of action vs dependency claim: the estate's claim (loss to the estate) vs dependants' claim (loss of dependency).
  3. JOINT TORTFEASORS: persons who commit the same tort together (concerted action, common design, or vicarious relation) - each is liable for the whole damage; the plaintiff may sue all or any; release of one does not release others; contribution among tortfeasors (English Civil Liability (Contribution) Act 1978; in India principles of equity). INDEPENDENT/ SEVERAL TORTFEASORS causing the same damage: each liable for the whole damage; apportionment by the court.
  4. REMEDIES BY ACT OF PARTIES: self-defence; recaption; re-entry; abatement of nuisance; distress damage feasant (seizing trespassing animals); expulsion of trespasser. JUDICIAL REMEDIES: damages; injunction (Specific Relief Act Secs 36-42; Order XXXIX CPC); restitution (Specific Relief Act Secs 5-8).
  5. LIMITATION: Limitation Act 1963 - one year (libel, slander, false imprisonment, malicious prosecution); three years residuary (Art 113 - negligence, nuisance, trespass where no specific article, injunction); continuing wrongs (Sec 22); Sec 17 fraud; Sec 5 condonation. Death/ injury claims under MV Act: no limitation after 1 April 2022 for claim applications (Sec 166(3) was omitted by the 2019 Amendment, brought into force 1 April 2022). Consumer claims: two years (Sec 69 CPA 2019).

⚖Landmark cases

Rookes v Devlin

1964

Three categories of exemplary damages.

Rudul Sah v State of Bihar

1983

Exemplary compensation for constitutional tort.

!Exam traps

  • TrapExemplary damages: only the Rookes v Devlin categories at common law; Indian courts are more liberal in public law claims.
  • TrapFatal Accidents Act claims are for the dependants' loss; the estate's survival claim is different.

?Likely questions

  1. Fatal Accidents Act 1855 enables: dependants to claim compensation for death caused by wrongful act.
  2. Joint tortfeasors: liability is joint and several.
