Motor Vehicles Act: Overview, Definitions, Licensing and Registration

Medium-high yield1–2 Qs expected7 min readSec 2(28), 2(34), 2(30), 2(21); Sec 3-4; Sec 39; Sec 129; Sec 134, 134A

  2. Must know (6 points)
  3. Exam traps
  4. Likely questions

iOverview

The Motor Vehicles Act 1988 as amended by the Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act 2019 (effective in phases from 1 September 2019). Know the definitions, age limits and the major registration/licensing rules.

★Must know

  1. The MV Act 1988 replaced the 1939 Act. It regulates licensing of drivers and conductors, registration, permits, control of traffic, insurance against third-party risks, liability without fault, Motor Accident Claims Tribunals, offences and penalties. The 2019 Amendment was a major overhaul: higher penalties, Good Samaritan protection (Sec 134A), Motor Vehicle Accident Fund (Sec 164B), cashless treatment in the golden hour (Sec 162), interim relief scheme (Sec 164A), hit-and-run compensation, removal of the 6-month limit on claims (Sec 166(3) omitted), liability of juveniles' guardians (Sec 199A), recall of defective vehicles (Sec 110A), aggregators (Sec 93), National Transportation Policy and electronic monitoring.
  2. KEY DEFINITIONS (Sec 2): 'motor vehicle' = any mechanically propelled vehicle adapted for use upon roads whether power is transmitted from an external or internal source (Sec 2(28)); excludes vehicles running on fixed rails or used solely in a factory/ enclosed premises, and vehicles with less than 4 wheels and engine capacity not exceeding 25 cc (Sec 2(28) as amended); 'public place' (Sec 2(34)) = a road, street, way or other place, whether a thoroughfare or not, to which the public have a right of access, and includes any place or stand at which passengers are picked up; 'owner' (Sec 2(30)) = the person in whose name the vehicle is registered and includes, where the person is a minor, the guardian; in relation to hire purchase/ agreement of lease/ hypothecation, the person in possession of the vehicle under that agreement; 'light motor vehicle' (Sec 2(21)) = transport vehicle or omnibus the gross vehicle weight of either of which or a motor car or tractor or road roller the unladen weight of any of which does not exceed 7,500 kg; 'transport vehicle' (Sec 2(47)) = public service vehicle, goods carriage, educational institution bus or private service vehicle; 'driver', 'driving licence', 'learner's licence', 'goods carriage', 'stage carriage', 'contract carriage', 'taxi cab', 'aggregator' (a digital intermediary or market place for a passenger to connect with a driver for transportation), 'trailer', 'permit'.
  3. LICENSING (Chapter II, Secs 3-28): Sec 3: no person shall drive a motor vehicle in any public place unless he holds an effective driving licence authorising him to drive the vehicle; transport vehicle - licence must specifically authorise (Sec 3(1)). Sec 4: AGE LIMIT - a person under 18 cannot drive a motor vehicle in a public place; exception: a person above 16 can drive a motor cycle with engine capacity not exceeding 50 cc; a person under 20 cannot drive a transport vehicle (verify the exceptions for transport vehicles in the bare Act). Sec 7: learner's licence; Sec 8: grant of learner's licence; Sec 9: grant of driving licence - applicant must pass a test of competence; Sec 14: currency - non-transport vehicle: 20 years or until the holder attains 40 years, whichever is earlier; Sec 15: renewal; Sec 19: power of licensing authority to disqualify; Sec 20: power of court to disqualify; Sec 21: suspension; Sec 22: suspension/ cancellation on being found not fit. Licence can be digital (DigiLocker). Sec 14(2)(b) transport vehicle: 3 years.
  4. REGISTRATION (Chapter IV, Secs 39-65): Sec 39: no person shall drive a motor vehicle (and no owner shall cause or permit it to be driven) in any public place or any other place unless the vehicle is registered in accordance with the Chapter and the certificate of registration has not been suspended or cancelled (except temporary registration, Sec 43); Sec 41: registration process; certificate of registration; Sec 47: assignment of new registration mark when a vehicle is removed to another State - within 12 months; Sec 50: transfer of ownership - the transferor must report within 14 days; transferee within 30 days; Sec 51: hypothecation; Sec 52: alteration of vehicle needs the registering authority's approval; Sec 56: certificate of fitness for transport vehicles. PERMITS (Chapter V): Sec 66: necessity for permits for transport vehicles; Sec 67: State control; Sec 72-74: stage carriage, contract carriage; goods carriage permits (Sec 79-82); national permit (Sec 88(9)); Sec 93: licensing of aggregators (2019).
  5. CONTROL OF TRAFFIC (Chapter VII-VIII): Sec 112: speed limits fixed by the Central/ State Government; Sec 113: limits of weight and limitations on use; Sec 115: power to restrict conveyance of goods; Sec 119: duty to obey traffic signs; Sec 128: safety measures for drivers and pillion riders of two-wheelers - no more than one pillion rider; Sec 129: wearing of protective headgear (helmet) for drivers and pillion riders of two-wheelers (exempt: Sikhs wearing turban); Sec 138(3): safety belts for car occupants; Sec 134: DUTY OF DRIVER IN CASE OF ACCIDENT - where bodily injury to a person or damage to property of a third party, or damage to any animal, is caused due to an accident in which a motor vehicle is involved, the driver or other person in charge of the vehicle shall (a) unless it is not practicable to do so on account of mob fury or any other reason beyond his control, take all reasonable steps to secure medical attention for the injured person, by conveying him to the nearest medical practitioner or hospital, and it shall be the duty of every registered medical practitioner or the doctor on duty in the hospital immediately to attend to the injured person and render medical aid or treatment without waiting for any procedural formalities, unless the injured person or his guardian, in case he is a minor, desires otherwise; (b) give on demand by a police officer any information required by him, or, if no police officer is present, report the circumstances of the occurrence, including the circumstances, if any, which prevented him from complying with the requirement of clause (a), at the nearest police station as soon as possible, and in any case within 24 hours of the occurrence; (c) give the following information in writing to the insurer, who has issued the certificate of insurance, about the occurrence of the accident, namely: insurance policy number and period of its validity; date, time and place of accident; particulars of persons injured or killed; name of the driver and the particulars of his driving licence.
  6. GOOD SAMARITANS - Sec 134A (inserted 2019): a Good Samaritan shall not be liable for any civil or criminal action for any injury to or death of the victim of an accident involving a motor vehicle where such injury or death resulted from the Good Samaritan's negligence in acting or failing to act while rendering emergency medical or non-medical care or assistance. 'Good Samaritan' = a person who in good faith, voluntarily and without expectation of payment or reward renders emergency medical or non-medical care or assistance at the scene of an accident to the victim or conveys such victim to the hospital. The Sec 134A is backed by the Supreme Court's Save Life Foundation v Union of India (2016), whose guidelines (no compulsion to disclose identity, no detention at hospital, no demand for payment) were codified. In May 2026 the Supreme Court (SaveLife Foundation v Union of India, order dated 26 May 2026) issued detailed directions on emergency response, golden hour care and operationalisation of the cashless scheme within three months.

!Exam traps

  • TrapAge: 18 years for driving; 16 for a gearless motor cycle up to 50 cc; commercial/ transport vehicle: 20 years (verify the 2019 text).
  • TrapHelmet: Sec 129; Good Samaritan: Sec 134A; driver's duty after accident: Sec 134 (report within 24 hours).
  • TrapRegistration is compulsory (Sec 39); transfer reporting - 14 days.

?Likely questions

  1. Good Samaritan protection is under: Sec 134A.
  2. Sec 134 requires the driver to: secure medical attention and report the accident.
  3. Minimum age to drive a non-gear motor cycle up to 50 cc: 16.
