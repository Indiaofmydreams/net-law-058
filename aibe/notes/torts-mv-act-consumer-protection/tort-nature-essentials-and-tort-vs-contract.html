Torts, Motor Vehicles Act and Consumer Protection · Chapter 1 of 17

Nature, Definition and Essentials of Tort; Tort vs Contract and Crime

iOverview

Foundation questions: definition, damnum sine injuria and injuria sine damno, mental element, maxims. Usually 1 question.

★Must know

  1. Indian tort law is largely UNCODIFIED judge-made law, built on English common law as adapted to Indian conditions (Art 372 continues pre-constitutional law; Indian courts apply 'justice, equity and good conscience'). Statutory supplements: Fatal Accidents Act 1855, Motor Vehicles Act 1988, Consumer Protection Act 2019, Public Liability Insurance Act 1991, National Green Tribunal Act 2010, Workmen's/ Employees' Compensation Act 1923.
  2. Definition (Winfield): tortious liability arises from the breach of a duty primarily fixed by law; this duty is towards persons generally and its breach is redressible by an action for unliquidated damages. (Salmond: a civil wrong for which the remedy is a common law action for unliquidated damages and which is not exclusively the breach of a contract or breach of trust or other merely equitable obligation.) Tort = Latin 'tortum' (twisted/wrong).
  3. Essentials: (1) a wrongful act or omission by the defendant; (2) the act must give rise to a legal duty (duty fixed by law, owed to the world at large); (3) the act must cause legal damage (violation of a legal right) - or be actionable per se; (4) the remedy is unliquidated damages (plus injunction etc.).
  4. INJURIA SINE DAMNO: violation of a legal right without actual loss - actionable per se (trespass, libel, assault, battery, false imprisonment). Ashby v White (1703): a returning officer wrongly refused to register a qualified voter; the voter's right was violated though his candidate won - he was entitled to damages. DAMNUM SINE INJURIA: actual loss without violation of a legal right - not actionable. Gloucester Grammar School Case (1410): a schoolmaster could not sue for loss caused by a rival school; Mogul Steamship v McGregor Gow (1892): lawful competition; Bradford Corporation v Pickles (1895): an act lawful in itself is not made unlawful by a bad motive (but motive can matter in nuisance - Christie v Davey; Hollywood Silver Fox Farm v Emmett).
  5. Tort vs CRIME: tort - private wrong, plaintiff sues for compensation, no fixed penalty; crime - wrong against society, prosecuted by State, punishment. Same act may be both (assault, defamation, negligence causing death, nuisance). TORT vs CONTRACT: duty fixed by law vs by parties' consent; privity not required in tort; damages unliquidated in tort; ordinarily no pre-existing relationship needed. Concurrent liability is possible (Donoghue v Stevenson - tort even though contracts existed). TORT vs BREACH OF TRUST: equitable.
  6. Mental element: tort ordinarily needs intention or negligence; some torts are of strict liability (Rylands v Fletcher; absolute liability); motive generally irrelevant (except malicious prosecution, conspiracy, nuisance in some cases). Malice in law = wrongful act done intentionally without just cause; malice in fact = ill will/ spite.
  7. Important maxims: Ubi jus ibi remedium (where there is a right there is a remedy); Volenti non fit injuria; Sic utere tuo ut alienum non laedas (use your own property so as not to harm another); Res ipsa loquitur; Actio personalis moritur cum persona (personal action dies with the person - Sec 306 Indian Succession Act 1925 saves causes of action except defamation, assault and other personal injuries not causing loss to the estate); Respondeat superior; Qui facit per alium facit per se; Damnum sine injuria; Injuria sine damno; Nemo dat; Novus actus interveniens.
  8. Remedies in tort: damages (nominal, contemptuous, general, special, aggravated, exemplary/ punitive), injunction (temporary, perpetual), specific restitution of property, and extra-judicial remedies (self-defence, re-entry, recaption, abatement of nuisance, distress damage feasant).

⚖Landmark cases

Ashby v White

1703

Injuria sine damno: violation of a legal right is actionable even without loss.

Gloucester Grammar School Case

1410

Damnum sine injuria: loss through lawful competition not actionable.

Bradford Corporation v Pickles

1895

A lawful act is not rendered unlawful by a bad motive.

Donoghue v Stevenson

1932

Tort duty independent of contract; neighbour principle.

!Exam traps

  • TrapInjuria sine damno = right violated, no loss, ACTIONABLE; damnum sine injuria = loss, no right violated, NOT actionable.
  • TrapTort remedy is UNLIQUIDATED damages; contract remedy may be liquidated.
  • TrapActio personalis moritur cum persona has statutory exceptions in India (Sec 306 Succession Act).

?Likely questions

  1. Ashby v White illustrates: injuria sine damno.
  2. Which maxim means 'where there is a right there is a remedy'? Ubi jus ibi remedium.
  3. Tort is a civil wrong for which the remedy is: unliquidated damages.
