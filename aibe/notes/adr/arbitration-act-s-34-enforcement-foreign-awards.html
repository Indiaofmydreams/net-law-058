iOverview

The grounds and limits of challenge, the 2015 narrowing of public policy, Gayatri Balasamy (2025) on limited modification, how awards are enforced, and recognition of foreign awards.

★Must know

  1. Setting aside (s.34): recourse to a court against an arbitral award is only by an application for setting aside. Grounds (s.34(2)(a)) - to be proved by the applicant: (i) a party was under some incapacity; (ii) the arbitration agreement is not valid under the law to which the parties have subjected it; (iii) the applicant was not given proper notice of the appointment of an arbitrator or of the arbitral proceedings or was otherwise unable to present his case; (iv) the award deals with a dispute not contemplated by or not falling within the terms of the submission, or contains decisions on matters beyond the scope (the offending part can be severed if separable); (v) the composition of the tribunal or the procedure was not in accordance with the agreement. Grounds under s.34(2)(b) - found by the court: the subject-matter is not capable of settlement by arbitration under the law for the time being in force; or the award is in conflict with the public policy of India. Explanation 1 (2015): an award is in conflict with public policy only if (i) its making was induced or affected by fraud or corruption or was in violation of s.75 (confidentiality of conciliation) or s.81; (ii) it is in contravention with the fundamental policy of Indian law; or (iii) it is in conflict with the most basic notions of morality or justice; Explanation 2: the test as to whether there is contravention of the fundamental policy of Indian law shall not entail a review on the merits of the dispute. s.34(2A) (2015): in a domestic arbitration (not an international commercial arbitration), an award may also be set aside if the court finds that it is vitiated by patent illegality appearing on the face of the award; but an award shall not be set aside merely on the ground of an erroneous application of the law or by reappreciation of evidence.
  2. Procedure and limitation: s.34(3) - the application must be made within three months from the date on which the party making it received the award (or the disposal of a request under s.33); the court may entertain it within a further period of 30 days if sufficiently satisfied that the applicant was prevented by sufficient cause, but not thereafter (Union of India v. Popular Construction Co., 2001 - s.5 Limitation Act does not apply; Simplex Infrastructures Ltd. v. Union of India, 2019 - the 30 days are in addition and not extendable). s.34(4) - on request, the court may adjourn the proceedings for a period to allow the tribunal to resume proceedings or take such other action as will eliminate the grounds for setting aside; s.34(5)-(6) (2015) - the application must be accompanied by an affidavit of prior notice to the other party and shall be disposed of expeditiously within one year from the date of service of notice.
  3. Public policy jurisprudence: Renusagar Power Co. v. General Electric Co. (1994) - narrow meaning of public policy for foreign awards (fundamental policy of Indian law, interests of India, justice or morality); ONGC Ltd. v. Saw Pipes Ltd. (2003) - widened public policy for domestic awards to include 'patent illegality'; ONGC Ltd. v. Western Geco International Ltd. (2014) - added the 'judicial approach', natural justice and 'Wednesbury' unreasonableness; Associate Builders v. Delhi Development Authority (2015) - contours of public policy: fundamental policy, interest of India, justice or morality, patent illegality; the court must not act as an appellate forum; the tribunal is the final judge of facts; Ssangyong Engineering & Construction Co. Ltd. v. NHAI (2019) - after the 2015 Amendment the Saw Pipes/Western Geco expansions are no longer good law; the grounds are only those in the Explanations to s.34(2)(b) and the patent illegality ground (domestic only); NHAI v. Progressive-MVR (2018); Delhi Airport Metro Express Pvt. Ltd. v. DMRC (2021 and 2022) - patent illegality and the curative petition (the court set aside an award on a patent-illegality basis and later criticised its own approach to interference in the curative petition judgment); Patel Engineering Ltd. v. North Eastern Electric Power Corp. (2020) - the limits of interference with awards.
  4. Court's power to modify awards - Gayatri Balasamy v. ISG Novasoft Technologies Ltd. (30 April 2025, Constitution Bench, 4:1): courts exercising jurisdiction under ss.34 and 37 have a limited power to modify an arbitral award: (a) to sever the invalid portion from the valid portion if they are separable (partial setting aside); (b) to correct clerical, computational or typographical errors apparent on the face of the record; (c) in appropriate circumstances to modify post-award interest; and the Supreme Court may, in exercise of its Art 142 powers, modify an award in rare cases with great care and caution, consistent with the Act's limited scope; courts cannot rewrite the award on its merits or reappreciate evidence; Justice K.V. Viswanathan dissented (courts can only set aside). Remand (s.34(4)) remains a separate tool.
  5. Finality and enforcement (ss.35-36): the award is final and binding on the parties and persons claiming under them (s.35); it is enforced under the CPC in the same manner as if it were a decree of the court (s.36(1)) once the time for making an application under s.34 has expired, or such application has been refused. s.36(2) (2015) - the mere filing of a s.34 application does not by itself render the award unenforceable unless the court grants a stay on a separate application; s.36(3) - the court may grant a stay subject to such conditions as it deems fit, and for a money decree shall have due regard to the provisions for grant of stay of a money decree under the CPC (Order XLI Rule 5); proviso (2021) - where the court is satisfied that a prima facie case is made out that the arbitration agreement or contract which is the basis of the award, or the making of the award, was induced or affected by fraud or corruption, it shall stay the award unconditionally pending disposal of the s.34 application (this proviso applies to all court cases arising out of arbitral proceedings irrespective of when commenced). Bank guarantees, security and deposit conditions on stay follow. Enforcement of domestic awards is before the court as defined in s.2(1)(e) at the place where the assets are (or seat); Sundaram Finance Ltd. v. NEPC India Ltd. (1999) - Part I applies to enforcement proceedings even before the 1996 Act formally; s.42 limited.
  6. Appeals (s.37): an appeal lies from (1)(a) an order refusing to refer the parties to arbitration under s.8; (b) an order granting or refusing to grant any measure under s.9; (c) an order setting aside or refusing to set aside an arbitral award under s.34; and (2) from an order of the arbitral tribunal (a) accepting the plea referred to in s.16(2) or (3) (no jurisdiction / excess of authority); (b) granting or refusing interim measure under s.17; to the court authorised by law to hear appeals from original decrees of the court passing the order (s.37(1)); no second appeal, but nothing takes away the right to appeal to the Supreme Court (s.37(3)) - i.e., SLP under Art 136. Section 37 jurisdiction is narrower than appellate jurisdiction over decrees and the appellate court does not re-examine the merits (MMTC Ltd. v. Vedanta Ltd., 2019; Konkan Railway Corp. v. Chenab Bridge Project, 2023). orders under s.11 are not appealable under s.37 (only SLP under Art 136).
  7. International commercial arbitration and domestic awards: an award in an ICA held in India is a 'domestic award' (s.2(7)) and is enforceable under s.36; patent illegality not available in an ICA (s.34(2A) proviso). Enforcement of awards made in India but against foreign parties, and enforcement of foreign awards in India under Part II. Institutional arbitration: ICA (Indian Council of Arbitration, 1965); ICADR (1995) - since taken over by NDIAC; New Delhi International Arbitration Centre Act 2019 (NDIAC) - institution of national importance; MCIA (Mumbai Centre for International Arbitration, 2016); Delhi International Arbitration Centre (DIAC; Delhi High Court); International Arbitration and Mediation Centre (IAMC), Hyderabad (2021); Singapore International Arbitration Centre (SIAC; 2025 Rules); International Chamber of Commerce (ICC); London Court of International Arbitration (LCIA); UNCITRAL Arbitration Rules. The Arbitration Council of India (Part IA) has not been constituted Verify.
  8. Foreign awards - Part II, Chapter I (New York Convention, 1958; India acceded 1960; reciprocity reservation and commercial reservation): s.44 - 'foreign award' - an arbitral award on differences between persons arising out of legal relationships, contractual or not, considered commercial under Indian law, made on or after 11 October 1960 in pursuance of an agreement in writing to which the New York Convention applies and in one of such territories as the Central Government, by notification in the Official Gazette, declares to be territories to which the Convention applies. s.45 - power of a judicial authority to refer the parties to arbitration (at the request of a party, unless it finds that the agreement is null and void, inoperative or incapable of being performed). s.46 - foreign awards are binding on the persons as between whom they were made. s.47 - evidence: the party applying for enforcement shall produce the original award or a duly authenticated copy, the original agreement or certified copy, and evidence to prove that it is a foreign award (translation if not in English). s.48 - conditions for enforcement: enforcement may be refused at the request of the party against whom it is invoked only if that party furnishes proof that (a) the parties were under some incapacity or the agreement is not valid; (b) he was not given proper notice or was unable to present his case; (c) the award deals with a difference not contemplated by or beyond the submission (severable); (d) the composition of the tribunal or procedure was not in accordance with the agreement or the law of the country where the arbitration took place; (e) the award has not yet become binding or has been set aside or suspended by a competent authority of the country in which, or under the law of which, it was made; or the court finds that (a) the subject-matter is not capable of settlement by arbitration under Indian law, or (b) enforcement would be contrary to the public policy of India (Explanation 1: fraud or corruption; violation of s.75/s.81; contravention of the fundamental policy of Indian law; conflict with the most basic notions of morality or justice; Explanation 2: no review on merits). s.49 - once the court is satisfied that the award is enforceable it is deemed to be a decree of that court. s.50 - appeal: lies from an order refusing to refer under s.45 and refusing to enforce under s.48 (not from an order enforcing); no second appeal but Art 136 (the 2015 Amendment reiterated). s.51-52 - saving; Chapter II - Geneva Convention awards (ss.53-60).
  9. Foreign awards jurisprudence: Renusagar (1994); Shri Lal Mahal Ltd. v. Progetto Grano S.p.A. (2014) - public policy under s.48 is narrower than under s.34; the court does not review merits; Shin-Etsu Chemical Co. Ltd. v. Aksh Optifibre Ltd. (2005) - the court at the s.45 stage applies a prima facie test (later modified by the 2015 amendment); Vijay Karia v. Prysmian Cavi E Sistemi SRL (2020) - the grounds under s.48 are exhaustive; the enforcing court shall not go into the merits; the expression 'public policy' is to be given a narrow meaning; the discretion to refuse enforcement is limited; Government of India v. Vedanta Ltd. (2020) - the seat of arbitration is not necessarily the place where the hearing is held; the law governing the arbitration agreement; Bhatia International (2002) overruled prospectively by BALCO; Union of India v. Reliance Industries (2018). Enforcement: foreign award enforceable as a decree once found enforceable; limitation for enforcement is governed by the Limitation Act (Art 136 - 12 years).
  10. Practical road-map for enforcement: domestic award - wait for s.34 period; apply for enforcement under s.36 in the proper court (Order XXI CPC applies); foreign award - file application under s.47-49 in the High Court (original side) having jurisdiction over the assets; no further proceedings to challenge (s.34 is unavailable to foreign awards, but s.48 objections can be raised); appeals per s.50.
  11. Latest developments in arbitration law (2023-2026): Cox and Kings (Dec 2023); Interplay (Dec 2023); CORE (Nov 2024) on unilateral appointment; Gayatri Balasamy (Apr 2025) on modification; Mediation Council of India (Aug 2026); the Expert Committee on Arbitration (Feb 2024) and the Draft Arbitration and Conciliation (Amendment) Bill 2024 - proposals include statutory recognition of emergency arbitrators, an appellate arbitral tribunal, a clearer definition of seat/venue, limiting court intervention, and mandatory institutional timelines; the Supreme Court and the Chief Justice in 2026 have urged making India a hub for institutional arbitration and have criticised delay at the enforcement stage (e.g., enforcement petitions pending for years) Verify.

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
A&C Act s.34Application to set aside; limitation 3 months + 30 days; grounds; public policy narrowed; patent illegality (domestic only).
A&C Act s.36Enforcement as a decree; no automatic stay; unconditional stay if fraud/corruption prima facie (2021).
A&C Act s.37Appeals: s.8 refusal; s.9; s.34; tribunal's s.16(2)/(3) and s.17 orders; no second appeal.
A&C Act s.43Limitation Act applies; s.43(4) exclusion of time if award set aside.
A&C Act s.44Definition of foreign award (New York Convention; notified reciprocating territories).
A&C Act s.48Grounds for refusal to enforce foreign award.
A&C Act s.50Appeals against orders refusing to refer or refusing to enforce.

✎Memory aids

  • s.34: '3 + 30' (months + days). s.36: 'No automatic stay'. s.37: 'Refer, Interim, Set-aside' (s.8, s.9, s.34).
  • Part II: s.44 (definition) - s.45 (reference) - s.48 (grounds) - s.49 (decree) - s.50 (appeal).

!Exam traps

  • Traps.34(3) 30 days is in addition to the 3 months and cannot be extended; s.5 Limitation Act is excluded.
  • TrapPatent illegality is not available against awards in an international commercial arbitration.
  • TrapAn appeal lies against an order refusing to enforce a foreign award (s.50) but not against an order enforcing it.
  • TrapNo second appeal under s.37(3), but SLP under Art 136 remains.
  • TrapGayatri Balasamy permits limited modification - severance and clerical corrections - not a re-write on merits.

?Likely questions

  1. Limitation for filing an application under s.34? (3 months, extendable by max 30 days)
  2. Which case held that courts can modify arbitral awards in limited circumstances? (Gayatri Balasamy v. ISG Novasoft)
  3. Which section lists grounds for refusing enforcement of a foreign award? (s.48)
  4. Is a second appeal available under s.37? (No)
