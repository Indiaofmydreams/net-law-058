iOverview

How a tribunal is kept independent, how it rules on its own jurisdiction, procedure and the strict time-limits, and what a valid award must contain.

★Must know

  1. Disclosure and grounds of challenge (s.12): a person approached for appointment shall disclose in writing any circumstances (financial, business, professional, personal) likely to give rise to justifiable doubts as to independence or impartiality and likely to affect his ability to devote sufficient time (s.12(1)); the Sixth Schedule gives the disclosure form; the Fifth Schedule lists grounds giving rise to justifiable doubts (for example, relationship with a party or counsel; previous involvement; direct or indirect interest in the dispute; being a lawyer in the same law firm as the counsel); s.12(3) - an arbitrator may be challenged only if circumstances give rise to justifiable doubts as to his independence or impartiality or he does not possess the qualifications agreed by the parties; s.12(5) - notwithstanding any prior agreement, a person whose relationship with the parties, counsel or the subject-matter falls under any category of the Seventh Schedule is ineligible to be appointed (examples: employee, consultant or advisor of a party; manager/director/part of the management of a party; a person who regularly advises a party; person with a significant financial relationship); this ineligibility can be waived only by an express written agreement made after disputes have arisen (proviso to s.12(5)).
  2. Case law on independence: TRF Ltd. v. Energo Engineering Projects Ltd. (2017) - a person who is ineligible under the Seventh Schedule cannot nominate another arbitrator (de jure inability); Voestalpine Schienen GmbH v. Delhi Metro Rail Corporation (2017) - independence and impartiality of arbitrators in public contracts; Perkins Eastman Architects DPC v. HSCC (India) Ltd. (2019) - unilateral appointment of a sole arbitrator by one party is invalid; Bharat Broadband Network Ltd. v. United Telecoms Ltd. (2019) - waiver under proviso to s.12(5) only by express agreement in writing; Central Organisation for Railway Electrification v. ECI-SPIC-SMO-MCML (JV) (Nov 2024, Constitution Bench) - the principle of equal treatment of parties applies to the appointment stage; clauses allowing one party to unilaterally appoint, or to nominate from a panel it controls, violate equal treatment (applied prospectively) Verify · details; HRD Corporation (Marcus Oil and Chemical Division) v. Gail (India) Ltd. (2017) - arbitrator's bias test.
  3. Challenge procedure (s.13): parties are free to agree; failing agreement, a party who intends to challenge sends a written statement of reasons to the tribunal within 15 days after becoming aware of the constitution of the tribunal or of the circumstances; if the challenged arbitrator does not withdraw and the other party does not agree, the tribunal decides; if the challenge is unsuccessful the tribunal continues and makes the award; the challenging party may apply to set aside the award under s.34. Termination of mandate (ss.14-15): when an arbitrator becomes de jure or de facto unable to perform his functions or fails to act without undue delay, his mandate terminates if he withdraws or the parties agree; otherwise a party may apply to the court; mandate also terminates on withdrawal, by agreement of the parties, or on delay (s.29A). Substitute arbitrator is appointed under the same rules (s.15(2)); the tribunal decides whether previous hearings are to be repeated (s.15(3)).
  4. Competence-competence (s.16): the arbitral tribunal may rule on its own jurisdiction, including ruling on any objection with respect to the existence or validity of the arbitration agreement; the arbitration clause is treated as an agreement independent of the other terms of the contract; a decision that the contract is null and void does not entail ipso jure invalidity of the arbitration clause. A plea that the tribunal does not have jurisdiction shall be raised not later than the submission of the statement of defence; a plea that the tribunal is exceeding the scope of its authority shall be raised as soon as the matter alleged to be beyond the scope is raised during the proceedings; the tribunal may admit a later plea if it considers the delay justified (s.16(2)-(4)). If the tribunal rules that it has jurisdiction, it continues with the arbitration and makes an award; a party aggrieved can challenge the award under s.34 (s.16(5)-(6)); if the tribunal accepts the plea of no jurisdiction an appeal lies under s.37(2)(a). The doctrine was reaffirmed in SBP & Co. v. Patel Engineering (2005) (later overruled in part by the 2015 Amendment as to the nature of the court's role at s.11) and Vidya Drolia (2020).
  5. Interim measures by the tribunal (s.17): during the arbitral proceedings (or after the award but before enforcement - 2015) a party may apply for orders such as preservation of goods, security for amount in dispute, interim injunction, appointment of receiver; the tribunal's order is deemed an order of the court and is enforceable as if it were an order of the court under the CPC (s.17(2)). s.18 - equal treatment: parties shall be treated with equality and each party shall be given a full opportunity to present his case. s.19 - the tribunal is not bound by the CPC or the Indian Evidence Act (BSA); parties may agree on the procedure; failing agreement the tribunal conducts proceedings as it considers appropriate; determines admissibility, relevance, materiality and weight of evidence. s.21 - commencement of arbitral proceedings: on the date on which a request for the dispute to be referred to arbitration is received by the respondent; s.22 - language; s.23 - statements of claim and defence (to be completed within six months from the date the arbitrator(s) receive notice of appointment - s.23(4), 2019); counter-claim and set-off (s.23(2A), 2015); s.24 - hearings: oral hearings as the tribunal decides; the tribunal shall, as far as possible, hold oral hearings for presentation of evidence or oral argument on day-to-day basis and shall not grant any adjournments unless sufficient cause is made out; may impose costs for adjournments; s.25 - default of a party (claimant's default: terminate proceedings; respondent's default: continue; not treated as admission); s.26 - expert appointed by tribunal; s.27 - court assistance in taking evidence (the tribunal or a party with the approval of the tribunal may apply to the court; court may order a person to give evidence; the person failing is subject to penalties and punishments by the court).
  6. Applicable law and award on the merits (s.28): in domestic arbitration (place in India) the tribunal decides in accordance with the substantive law in force in India; in international commercial arbitration, in accordance with the rules of law designated by the parties (any designation of the law of a country refers to the substantive law and not the conflict-of-law rules); failing designation, the rules of law the tribunal considers appropriate; tribunal decides ex aequo et bono or as amiable compositeur only if the parties expressly authorise; in all cases the tribunal decides in accordance with the terms of the contract and takes into account the usages of the trade (s.28(3)); a tribunal shall not decide against the terms of the contract (2015; Associate Builders). s.29 - decision by majority; the presiding arbitrator may decide procedural questions if authorised by the parties or all members; s.30 - settlement: the tribunal may use mediation, conciliation or other procedures to encourage settlement (with the parties' agreement); if the parties settle, the tribunal terminates the proceedings and, if requested, records the settlement as an arbitral award on agreed terms, which has the same status as any other award (s.30(4)).
  7. Time-limits for completing arbitration - s.29A (2015, substantially revised in 2019): in matters other than international commercial arbitration the award must be made within 12 months from the date of completion of pleadings under s.23(4) (the pleadings must themselves be completed within six months); if the award is made within six months from the date the tribunal enters upon the reference the tribunal is entitled to additional fee as agreed; the parties may by consent extend the period by up to six months; if the award is not made within the period (including the extended period) the mandate terminates unless the court, on application of any party and for sufficient cause, extends the period (before or after expiry); on extension the court may reduce the arbitrators' fees by up to five per cent for each month of delay attributable to the tribunal and may substitute one or all of the arbitrators; proceedings continue from the stage already reached; the court may impose actual or exemplary costs on a party; the application under s.29A is to be disposed of within 60 days from service of notice on the opposite party (s.29A(9)). The ICA is not covered by the 12-month rule (the tribunal 'may endeavour' to complete within 12 months). s.29B (2015) - fast-track procedure: parties may agree in writing to resolve disputes by a sole arbitrator on the basis of written pleadings, documents and submissions, with no oral hearing unless requested or considered necessary, and with the award within six months of the tribunal entering upon the reference.
  8. Form and contents of award (s.31): in writing and signed by the members of the tribunal (signatures of the majority suffice if the reason for any omitted signature is stated); shall state reasons upon which it is based unless the parties have agreed that no reasons are to be given or it is an award on agreed terms under s.30; shall state its date and the place of arbitration; a signed copy shall be delivered to each party. Interest (s.31(7)): unless otherwise agreed, where the award is for payment of money the tribunal may include interest on the sum for the whole or any part of the period between the date on which the cause of action arose and the date of the award; a sum directed to be paid by the award carries interest at 2% higher than the current rate of interest prevailing on the date of the award from the date of the award to the date of payment (unless the award directs otherwise) (s.31(7)(b)); 'current rate of interest' as defined in s.2(b) of the Interest Act 1978. Costs (s.31A, 2015): the court or tribunal has discretion to determine whether costs are payable, the amount and when; the general rule is that costs follow the event (the unsuccessful party pays), with deviation for reasons recorded; relevant circumstances include conduct of the parties, whether a party has succeeded partly, any admissible offer to settle. Partial and interim awards (s.31(6)). s.32 - termination of proceedings by the final award or by order of the tribunal (claimant withdraws; parties agree; continuation becomes unnecessary or impossible); mandate terminates with the termination of proceedings subject to ss.33 and 34(4). s.33 - correction and interpretation: within 30 days of receipt of the award (unless another period agreed) a party with notice to the other may request correction of computational, clerical or typographical errors or errors of similar nature, or interpretation of a specific point; additional award on claims presented but omitted may be requested within 30 days; the tribunal makes corrections within 30 days and additional award within 60 days.
  9. Fees and deposits (ss.38-39): the Fourth Schedule gives a model fee scale (maximum fee Rs 30 lakh for a sole arbitrator plus); ONGC Ltd. v. Afcons Gunanusa JV (2022) - fees of arbitrators in public sector contracts; s.38 - deposits as advance for costs; s.39 - lien of the arbitral tribunal on the award for unpaid costs (the court may order the delivery of the award on payment into court). s.40 - arbitration agreement is not discharged by the death of a party; s.41 - provisions as to insolvency.
  10. Confidentiality and immunity (2019): s.42A - the arbitrator, the arbitral institution and the parties shall maintain confidentiality of all arbitral proceedings except the award where its disclosure is necessary for implementation and enforcement; s.42B - no suit or legal proceedings against an arbitrator for anything done or intended to be done in good faith under the Act. Section 43 - Limitation Act 1963 applies to arbitrations as it applies to proceedings in court; arbitration is deemed to commence on the date referred to in s.21; where an award is set aside the time between the commencement of arbitration and the date of the order of the court is excluded for the purposes of limitation for fresh arbitration (s.43(4)).
  11. Statutory arbitration: s.2(4) - Part I applies to every arbitration under any other enactment in force, except in so far as inconsistent; s.2(5) - the Act applies to arbitration agreed under special enactments with exceptions; e.g., arbitration under the Industrial Disputes Act (voluntary arbitration), Cooperative Societies Acts, MSMED Act 2006 (ss.15-24 - Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council; the Supreme Court in Gujarat State Civil Supplies Corporation v. Mahakali Foods (2022) held that MSMED Act arbitration is statutory and that s.18 prevails over s.8 A&C Act; the facilitation council may itself act as arbitrator or refer the dispute to an institution).

✎Memory aids

  • Challenge: 15 days (s.13). Correction: 30 days; additional award: 60 days (s.33). Award: 12 + 6 months (s.29A).
  • Interest: 2% over current rate from date of award (s.31(7)(b)).

!Exam traps

  • TrapA s.12(5) ineligibility can be waived only after disputes have arisen and only in writing.
  • Traps.29A (12 months) does not apply to international commercial arbitration.
  • TrapThe tribunal decides its own jurisdiction, but the decision can be challenged only through s.34 (or s.37(2) if jurisdiction is rejected).
  • TrapCosts follow the event under s.31A.

?Likely questions

  1. Within what time must the plea of lack of jurisdiction be raised? (Not later than the statement of defence)
  2. Time for challenge of an arbitrator under s.13? (15 days)
  3. An arbitral award must be made within how many months from completion of pleadings? (12)
  4. Which Schedule gives the model fee? (Fourth)
