Mediation and Conciliation: Mediation Act 2023, Mediation Council of India, Singapore Convention and Part III of the A&C Act 1996

iOverview

India now has a standalone mediation statute and a statutory regulator. Conciliation under Part III of the 1996 Act remains in place. Questions test definitions, enforceability and who may challenge a settlement.

★Must know

  1. Mediation Act 2023: received Presidential assent on 14 September 2023; some provisions (including those on the Mediation Council) were brought into force on 9 October 2023 Verify · the status of the remaining provisions. Eight Chapters and two Schedules (about 62 sections). Purpose: to promote, encourage and facilitate mediation (especially institutional mediation) for commercial and other disputes; enforce mediated settlement agreements; provide for community mediation; make online mediation legal; provide for a body for registration of mediators; and give effect to the Singapore Convention on Mediation.
  2. Definition: 'mediation' - a process, whether referred to as mediation, pre-litigation mediation, online mediation, community mediation, conciliation or an expression of similar import, whereby parties to a dispute, with the assistance of a third person (the mediator), attempt to reach an amicable settlement. The mediator facilitates; he does not impose a settlement.
  3. Applicability: (a) mediation conducted in India where all or both parties habitually reside in or are incorporated or have their place of business in India; (b) international mediation (commercial; at least one party is a foreign national, foreign resident or foreign-incorporated entity; mediation conducted in India); (c) mediation agreements providing for mediation under the Act; (d) disputes to which the Central or State Government is party, only commercial disputes. Disputes unfit for mediation are listed in the First Schedule - for example, disputes involving criminal prosecution or non-compoundable offences, claims affecting rights of minors or persons of unsound mind, matters affecting the rights of third parties, and tax matters Verify · list.
  4. Pre-litigation mediation: before filing any civil or commercial suit or proceeding, parties may, by mutual consent (the Act makes this voluntary rather than compulsory Verify), refer the dispute to mediation; a party may withdraw after two mediation sessions; a court may refer the parties to mediation at any stage (cf. s.89 CPC). Mediation is to be completed within 120 days from the first appearance before the mediator, extendable by 60 days with consent Verify. Mediators are subject to disclosure and impartiality duties; confidentiality of mediation communications is protected; communications and proposals made in mediation are privileged and not admissible in other proceedings.
  5. Mediated Settlement Agreement (MSA): written, signed by parties and authenticated by the mediator; final and binding on the parties and enforceable as if it were a judgment or decree of the court; may be registered with the Legal Services Authority (State/District) within 90 days; MSA arising from pre-litigation mediation has the status of a decree; an MSA can be challenged only on grounds of fraud, corruption, impersonation or because the dispute was not fit for mediation, within 90 days of receipt of the MSA (extendable by a further 90 days) Verify · periods.
  6. Mediation Council of India: a statutory body to promote and regulate mediation - registration of mediators; recognition of mediation service providers and institutes; maintaining a repository; setting standards; the Central Government notified the establishment of the Council (August 2026 - reported 27 August 2026 Verify), after a Parliamentary panel's urging. Community mediation: for disputes likely to affect peace and harmony among residents of a locality; Community Mediation Panel of three mediators. Online mediation allowed; international mediation covered; Singapore Convention on Mediation (United Nations Convention on International Settlement Agreements Resulting from Mediation): adopted December 2018; signing ceremony 7 August 2019 (India among the signatories); in force from 12 September 2020; ratification by India is awaited Verify.
  7. Court-annexed mediation: Supreme Court's Mediation and Conciliation Project Committee (MCPC); Civil Procedure ADR and Mediation Rules 2003 (Salem Advocate Bar Association); mediation in matrimonial and commercial disputes; mediators trained; confidential; settlement is recorded as a decree under Order XXIII Rule 3 CPC.
  8. Conciliation under Part III of the A&C Act 1996 (ss.61-81), based on the UNCITRAL Conciliation Rules 1980: s.61 - applies to conciliation of disputes arising out of a legal relationship, contractual or not, and to proceedings relating thereto (not to disputes that by law cannot be submitted to conciliation). s.62 - commencement: by one party sending a written invitation to the other; conciliation begins when the other accepts in writing; if the invitation is not accepted within 30 days it is treated as rejected. s.63 - one conciliator unless parties agree on two or three; s.64 - appointment (each party appoints one when two; third as presiding conciliator). s.65 - statements to conciliator; s.66 - conciliator is not bound by the CPC or the Evidence Act; s.67 - role: assist parties in an independent and impartial manner; guided by principles of objectivity, fairness and justice, trade usage and the circumstances; may conduct proceedings as he considers appropriate and may make proposals for settlement at any stage. s.69 - communication; s.70 - disclosure of information; s.72 - suggestions by parties for settlement; s.73 - settlement agreement: when it appears to the conciliator that there exist elements of a settlement, he formulates the terms and submits them to the parties; if parties agree they draw up and sign a settlement agreement; the conciliator may authenticate it; s.74 - status and effect: the settlement agreement has the same status and effect as if it were an arbitral award on agreed terms under s.30 (so it is enforceable as a decree - s.36); s.75 - confidentiality; s.76 - termination of conciliation (by settlement agreement; by written declaration of conciliator that further efforts are no longer justified; by written declaration of parties; by written declaration of a party); s.77 - no arbitral or judicial proceedings in respect of the dispute while conciliation is on, except to preserve rights; s.80 - the conciliator shall not act as an arbitrator or as a representative or counsel of a party in any arbitral or judicial proceeding in respect of the same dispute; s.81 - admissions, views, suggestions, proposals made in conciliation are not admissible in other proceedings.
  9. Conciliation v. mediation v. arbitration: conciliator may propose terms and the settlement has the status of an award (s.74); mediator facilitates (Mediation Act); arbitrator decides and binds. Statutory conciliation also exists in labour law (Industrial Disputes Act / IR Code) and in the Family Courts Act (s.9).
  10. Relationship with arbitration: parties may agree to 'med-arb' - mediation followed by arbitration; the arbitral tribunal may encourage settlement (s.30 A&C Act) - the tribunal may use mediation, conciliation or other procedures at any time; settlement terms may be recorded as an arbitral award on agreed terms (s.30(4)), having the same status as any other award.

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
A&C Act s.62Conciliation begins on written invitation and acceptance; deemed rejection if not accepted within 30 days.
A&C Act s.73-74Settlement agreement; status of an arbitral award on agreed terms.
A&C Act s.77No arbitral/judicial proceedings during conciliation, except to preserve rights.
A&C Act s.80-81Conciliator cannot act as arbitrator or counsel; conciliation communications inadmissible elsewhere.
Mediation ActStatutory framework; MSA enforceable as a decree; Mediation Council of India.

✎Memory aids

  • Conciliation termination 'S-C-P-P': Settlement; Conciliator's declaration; Parties' joint declaration; one Party's declaration.
  • s.74: settlement agreement = award on agreed terms.

!Exam traps

  • TrapConciliator may propose terms; a mediator does not impose.
  • TrapConciliation settlement has the status of an award on agreed terms (s.74), not a decree directly - but is enforceable as a decree (s.36).
  • TrapA conciliator cannot later act as arbitrator in the same dispute (s.80).
  • TrapThe Mediation Council of India has been set up only recently (2026).

?Likely questions

  1. Which Part of the A&C Act deals with conciliation? (Part III)
  2. A conciliation settlement agreement has the status of? (An arbitral award on agreed terms - s.74)
  3. Mediation Act received assent in which year? (2023)
  4. Which Convention governs international mediated settlement agreements? (Singapore Convention)
