1

ADR Framework: Negotiation, Mediation, Conciliation, Arbitration, Lok Adalat; Section 89 CPC; Legal Services Authorities Act; Gram Nyayalayas; Family Courts; Commercial Courts s.12A

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

CPC s.89 / Order X Rules 1A-1CCourt-referred settlement: arbitration, conciliation, judicial settlement (Lok Adalat), mediation.
LSA Act s.21Lok Adalat award: deemed decree; final and binding; no appeal.
LSA Act s.22CPermanent Lok Adalat may decide on merits if conciliation fails.
Commercial Courts Act s.12AMandatory pre-institution mediation unless urgent interim relief is contemplated.
Family Courts Act s.9 / s.13Settlement efforts; no right of legal representation.

Cases in one line

  • Salem Advocate Bar Association v. Union of India 2005 Constitutional validity of s.89 CPC upheld; model rules for ADR and mediation.
  • Afcons Infrastructure Ltd. v. Cherian Varkey Construction Co. 2010 Reference to arbitration under s.89 requires consent of both parties; categories of cases suited and unsuited for ADR.
  • State of Punjab v. Jalour Singh 2008 Lok Adalat can only facilitate consent-based settlements; it cannot decide on merits.
  • Bhargavi Constructions v. Kothakapu Muthyam Reddy 2018 Lok Adalat award is final; challenge only by writ (Arts 226/227).
  • Patil Automation Pvt. Ltd. v. Rakheja Engineers Pvt. Ltd. 2022 Pre-institution mediation under s.12A Commercial Courts Act is mandatory.
  • K. Srinivas Rao v. D.A. Deepa 2013 Mediation in matrimonial disputes; guidelines.
  • United India Insurance Co. Ltd. v. Ajay Sinha 2008 Permanent Lok Adalat has adjudicatory power in pre-litigation public utility disputes.

Memory aids

  • Lok Adalat: 'FBD' - Final, Binding, Deemed decree; no appeal.
  • Permanent Lok Adalat: public utility + pre-litigation + up to Rs 1 crore + can decide on merits.

Exam traps

  • TrapA Lok Adalat cannot decide on merits; a Permanent Lok Adalat can.
  • TrapAn award of a Lok Adalat is not appealable; the remedy is a writ.
  • TrapCourt-referral to arbitration under s.89 needs the consent of both parties.
  • TrapCommercial suits contemplating urgent interim relief can skip pre-institution mediation.
2

Mediation and Conciliation: Mediation Act 2023, Mediation Council of India, Singapore Convention and Part III of the A&C Act 1996

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

A&C Act s.62Conciliation begins on written invitation and acceptance; deemed rejection if not accepted within 30 days.
A&C Act s.73-74Settlement agreement; status of an arbitral award on agreed terms.
A&C Act s.77No arbitral/judicial proceedings during conciliation, except to preserve rights.
A&C Act s.80-81Conciliator cannot act as arbitrator or counsel; conciliation communications inadmissible elsewhere.
Mediation ActStatutory framework; MSA enforceable as a decree; Mediation Council of India.

Cases in one line

  • Salem Advocate Bar Association v. Union of India 2005 Mediation rules; court-annexed ADR.
  • Afcons Infrastructure v. Cherian Varkey Construction 2010 Cases suitable for mediation/conciliation.
  • K. Srinivas Rao v. D.A. Deepa 2013 Matrimonial mediation.
  • Patil Automation v. Rakheja Engineers 2022 s.12A mediation mandatory for commercial suits.

Memory aids

  • Conciliation termination 'S-C-P-P': Settlement; Conciliator's declaration; Parties' joint declaration; one Party's declaration.
  • s.74: settlement agreement = award on agreed terms.

Exam traps

  • TrapConciliator may propose terms; a mediator does not impose.
  • TrapConciliation settlement has the status of an award on agreed terms (s.74), not a decree directly - but is enforceable as a decree (s.36).
  • TrapA conciliator cannot later act as arbitrator in the same dispute (s.80).
  • TrapThe Mediation Council of India has been set up only recently (2026).
3

Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996 (I): Scheme, Definitions, Arbitration Agreement, Judicial Intervention, Reference (s.8), Interim Relief (s.9), Appointment (s.11), Seat and Venue

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

A&C Act s.2(1)(f)International commercial arbitration - at least one party foreign; central management and control test.
A&C Act s.5Judicial intervention limited to what Part I provides.
A&C Act s.7Arbitration agreement in writing; electronic exchanges count.
A&C Act s.8Reference to arbitration: application before first statement on substance; prima facie no valid agreement ground.
A&C Act s.9Interim relief by court; proceedings must start within 90 days.
A&C Act s.11Appointment: 30 days; disposal endeavour 60 days; SC/HC or designated institution.
A&C Act s.42First court approached has exclusive jurisdiction.

Cases in one line

  • Bharat Aluminium Co. v. Kaiser Aluminium Technical Services (BALCO) 2012 Constitution Bench: Part I applies only to arbitrations seated in India; prospective operation.
  • Indus Mobile Distribution v. Datawind Innovations 2017 Seat designation confers exclusive jurisdiction on the courts of the seat.
  • BGS SGS SOMA JV v. NHPC Ltd. 2019 Venue treated as seat absent a contrary indication.
  • Vidya Drolia v. Durga Trading Corporation 2020 Four-fold test of arbitrability; eyes wide shut; landlord-tenant disputes under TPA arbitrable.
  • Cox and Kings Ltd. v. SAP India Pvt. Ltd. 2023 Group of companies doctrine recognised; non-signatories may be bound.
  • In re: Interplay between Arbitration Agreements and the Indian Stamp Act 2023 Unstamped arbitration agreement is inadmissible but not void; curable; tribunal decides.
  • Amazon.com NV Investment Holdings v. Future Retail Ltd. 2021 Emergency arbitrator's order enforceable under s.17(2).
  • Mayavati Trading v. Pradyuat Deb Burman 2019 At s.11 the court looks only to the existence of an arbitration agreement.
  • Booz Allen & Hamilton v. SBI Home Finance 2011 Disputes relating to rights in rem are non-arbitrable.
  • BCCI v. Kochi Cricket Pvt. Ltd. 2018 2015 Amendment operates prospectively as clarified.

Memory aids

  • s.8 'before first statement on substance'; s.9 '90 days'; s.11 '30 days / 60 days'.
  • Seat chooses supervisory court; venue = seat unless contrary indication.

Exam traps

  • TrapPart I applies if the seat is in India; Part II applies to foreign awards.
  • TrapUnder s.7 the agreement need not be signed if there is a record by exchange of communications.
  • TrapUnder s.9(2) arbitration must begin within 90 days of a pre-arbitral court order.
  • TrapAt s.11 the court only examines existence of an agreement, not merits or stamping.
4

Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996 (II): Independence of Arbitrators, Tribunal's Jurisdiction, Interim Measures, Procedure, Time-limits, Award, Costs and Interest

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

A&C Act s.12(5) and Seventh ScheduleIneligibility of arbitrators; waiver only by written agreement after disputes arise.
A&C Act s.16Competence-competence; separability; plea of no jurisdiction not later than statement of defence.
A&C Act s.17Interim measures by tribunal; enforceable as court order.
A&C Act s.23(4)Pleadings to be completed within six months.
A&C Act s.29AAward within 12 months from completion of pleadings; +6 months by consent; thereafter by court.
A&C Act s.31(7)(b)Post-award interest at 2% above the current rate.
A&C Act s.31ACosts: loser pays.
A&C Act s.33Correction/ interpretation within 30 days; additional award within 60 days.

Cases in one line

  • TRF Ltd. v. Energo Engineering Projects Ltd. 2017 An arbitrator ineligible under s.12(5) cannot nominate another arbitrator.
  • Perkins Eastman Architects DPC v. HSCC (India) Ltd. 2019 Unilateral appointment of sole arbitrator by one party is invalid.
  • Voestalpine Schienen GmbH v. Delhi Metro Rail Corporation 2017 Independence and impartiality; role of Schedules.
  • Central Organisation for Railway Electrification v. ECI-SPIC-SMO-MCML (JV) 2024 Constitution Bench: unilateral appointment/one-sided panels offend equal treatment (prospective).
  • Enercon (India) Ltd. v. Enercon GmbH 2014 Separability; arbitration clause survives contract.
  • Amazon.com NV Investment Holdings v. Future Retail Ltd. 2021 Emergency arbitrator's order is enforceable.
  • ONGC Ltd. v. Afcons Gunanusa JV 2022 Arbitrator fee in public sector arbitrations; Fourth Schedule.
  • Gujarat State Civil Supplies Corporation Ltd. v. Mahakali Foods Pvt. Ltd. 2022 MSMED Act s.18 prevails; statutory arbitration.

Memory aids

  • Challenge: 15 days (s.13). Correction: 30 days; additional award: 60 days (s.33). Award: 12 + 6 months (s.29A).
  • Interest: 2% over current rate from date of award (s.31(7)(b)).

Exam traps

  • TrapA s.12(5) ineligibility can be waived only after disputes have arisen and only in writing.
  • Traps.29A (12 months) does not apply to international commercial arbitration.
  • TrapThe tribunal decides its own jurisdiction, but the decision can be challenged only through s.34 (or s.37(2) if jurisdiction is rejected).
  • TrapCosts follow the event under s.31A.
5

Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996 (III): Setting Aside, Enforcement, Appeals, Public Policy, Modification of Awards; Foreign Awards (Part II); Institutional Arbitration

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

A&C Act s.34Application to set aside; limitation 3 months + 30 days; grounds; public policy narrowed; patent illegality (domestic only).
A&C Act s.36Enforcement as a decree; no automatic stay; unconditional stay if fraud/corruption prima facie (2021).
A&C Act s.37Appeals: s.8 refusal; s.9; s.34; tribunal's s.16(2)/(3) and s.17 orders; no second appeal.
A&C Act s.43Limitation Act applies; s.43(4) exclusion of time if award set aside.
A&C Act s.44Definition of foreign award (New York Convention; notified reciprocating territories).
A&C Act s.48Grounds for refusal to enforce foreign award.
A&C Act s.50Appeals against orders refusing to refer or refusing to enforce.

Cases in one line

  • Renusagar Power Co. v. General Electric Co. 1994 Narrow public policy ground for foreign awards.
  • ONGC Ltd. v. Saw Pipes Ltd. 2003 Patent illegality as a public-policy ground for domestic awards (pre-2015).
  • ONGC Ltd. v. Western Geco International Ltd. 2014 Judicial approach, natural justice and perversity as public policy.
  • Associate Builders v. Delhi Development Authority 2015 Contours of public policy; limited scope of interference.
  • Ssangyong Engineering & Construction Co. v. NHAI 2019 Post-2015: public policy narrowed; patent illegality applies to domestic awards only.
  • Gayatri Balasamy v. ISG Novasoft Technologies Ltd. 2025 Constitution Bench (4:1): limited power to modify awards - severance, correction of clerical errors, post-award interest; Art 142 in rare cases.
  • Union of India v. Popular Construction Co. 2001 s.34(3) limitation is absolute; s.5 of the Limitation Act does not apply.
  • Vijay Karia v. Prysmian Cavi E Sistemi SRL 2020 Enforcement of foreign awards: narrow grounds in s.48; no merit review.
  • Shri Lal Mahal Ltd. v. Progetto Grano S.p.A. 2014 Public policy under s.48 narrower than under s.34.
  • Delhi Airport Metro Express Pvt. Ltd. v. DMRC 2021 Patent illegality and perversity; later curative petition decision.

Memory aids

  • s.34: '3 + 30' (months + days). s.36: 'No automatic stay'. s.37: 'Refer, Interim, Set-aside' (s.8, s.9, s.34).
  • Part II: s.44 (definition) - s.45 (reference) - s.48 (grounds) - s.49 (decree) - s.50 (appeal).

Exam traps

  • Traps.34(3) 30 days is in addition to the 3 months and cannot be extended; s.5 Limitation Act is excluded.
  • TrapPatent illegality is not available against awards in an international commercial arbitration.
  • TrapAn appeal lies against an order refusing to enforce a foreign award (s.50) but not against an order enforcing it.
  • TrapNo second appeal under s.37(3), but SLP under Art 136 remains.
  • TrapGayatri Balasamy permits limited modification - severance and clerical corrections - not a re-write on merits.
