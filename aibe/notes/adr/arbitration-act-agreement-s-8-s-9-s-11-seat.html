iOverview

The architecture of the Act, what makes an arbitration agreement, when a court must send parties to arbitration, how arbitrators are appointed, arbitrability and the seat-venue distinction.

★Must know

  1. Scheme: Part I (ss.2-43) - domestic arbitration and international commercial arbitration held in India (the 'seat' in India); Part IA (ss.43A-43M) - Arbitration Council of India (inserted 2019; never operationalised) Verify · current status; Part II (ss.44-60) - enforcement of certain foreign awards: Chapter I - New York Convention awards (ss.44-52), Chapter II - Geneva Convention awards (ss.53-60); Part III (ss.61-81) - conciliation; Part IV (ss.82-86) - supplementary provisions. Schedules: First (New York Convention), Second (Geneva Protocol 1923), Third (Geneva Convention 1927), Fourth (model fee schedule; 2015), Fifth (grounds giving rise to justifiable doubts as to independence or impartiality; 2015), Sixth (disclosure form), Seventh (persons ineligible to be arbitrators); the Eighth Schedule (qualifications of arbitrators, 2019) was omitted by the 2021 Amendment. Based on the UNCITRAL Model Law on International Commercial Arbitration 1985 and the UNCITRAL Conciliation Rules 1980; the Act replaced the Arbitration Act 1940, the Arbitration (Protocol and Convention) Act 1937 and the Foreign Awards (Recognition and Enforcement) Act 1961.
  2. Amendments: Arbitration and Conciliation (Amendment) Act 2015 (effective 23 October 2015) - 'public policy' narrowed; patent illegality (s.34(2A)); time-limits (s.29A, s.23(4)); s.9 interim relief safeguards; s.11 reforms; Schedules; costs (s.31A); BCCI v. Kochi Cricket Pvt. Ltd. (2018) - the 2015 Amendment applies prospectively to arbitral proceedings commenced on or after 23 Oct 2015 and to court proceedings in relation to them (not to s.34 applications pending on that date). 2019 Amendment - arbitral institutions designation (s.11(3A)), s.23(4), s.29A revisions, Part IA (ACI), confidentiality (s.42A), immunity (s.42B). 2021 Amendment (Act 3 of 2021) - unconditional stay on enforcement of an award if the underlying agreement or award was induced by fraud or corruption (proviso to s.36(3)); omission of the Eighth Schedule. Draft Arbitration and Conciliation (Amendment) Bill 2024 (based on the Expert Committee headed by Dr T.K. Viswanathan, Feb 2024) was circulated for comments but had not been enacted as of November 2026 Verify.
  3. Definitions (s.2(1)): 'arbitration' - any arbitration whether or not administered by a permanent arbitral institution; 'arbitral award' includes an interim award; 'Court' (s.2(1)(e)) - for domestic arbitration the principal civil court of original jurisdiction in a district (including the High Court in the exercise of ordinary original civil jurisdiction) having jurisdiction to decide the subject-matter if it were the subject of a suit, but not a Small Cause Court; for international commercial arbitration the High Court (original jurisdiction or having appellate jurisdiction). 'International commercial arbitration' (s.2(1)(f)) - arbitration relating to disputes arising out of legal relationships, contractual or not, considered commercial under Indian law, where at least one party is (i) an individual who is a national of, or habitually resident in, any country other than India; (ii) a body corporate incorporated outside India; (iii) a company, association or body of individuals whose central management and control is exercised in any country other than India; or (iv) the Government of a foreign country.
  4. Scope (s.2(2)): Part I applies where the place (seat) of arbitration is in India; ss.9, 27, 37(1)(a) and 37(3) apply to international commercial arbitration even if the seat is outside India (unless parties agree otherwise), provided the award is enforceable under Part II. Bharat Aluminium Co. v. Kaiser Aluminium Technical Services (BALCO, 2012, Constitution Bench): Part I applies only to arbitrations seated in India; it overruled Bhatia International v. Bulk Trading S.A. (2002) and Venture Global Engineering v. Satyam Computers (2008); the decision applies prospectively to arbitration agreements executed after 6 September 2012. s.5 - extent of judicial intervention: no judicial authority shall intervene except where so provided in Part I; s.6 - administrative assistance.
  5. Arbitration agreement (s.7): an agreement by the parties to submit to arbitration all or certain disputes which have arisen or may arise in respect of a defined legal relationship, contractual or not; may be a clause in a contract or a separate agreement; must be in writing; written if contained in (a) a document signed by the parties; (b) an exchange of letters, telex, telegrams or other means of telecommunication (including electronic communication) providing a record of the agreement; or (c) an exchange of statements of claim and defence in which the existence of the agreement is alleged by one party and not denied by the other; a reference in a contract to a document containing an arbitration clause constitutes an arbitration agreement if the reference makes that clause part of the contract (s.7(5)). Essentials (K.K. Modi v. K.N. Modi, 1998; Jagdish Chander v. Ramesh Chander, 2007): agreement to refer disputes; binding decision; tribunal's jurisdiction from consent; no mere right to consult; words like 'may' are not conclusive. Separability (s.16(1)(b)): an arbitration clause is independent of the contract; termination or invalidity of the contract does not invalidate the clause (Enercon (India) Ltd. v. Enercon GmbH, 2014). Incorporation by reference: M.R. Engineers v. Som Datt Builders (2009); Inox Wind Ltd. v. Thermocables Ltd. (2018); Duro Felguera S.A. v. Gangavaram Port Ltd. (2017) - s.11 court looks only at existence of the agreement.
  6. Reference to arbitration (s.8): a judicial authority before which an action is brought in a matter which is the subject of an arbitration agreement shall, if a party applies not later than the date of submitting his first statement on the substance of the dispute, refer the parties to arbitration unless it finds that prima facie no valid arbitration agreement exists (2015); the application must be accompanied by the original agreement or a certified copy; arbitration may commence and an award may be made while the application is pending. Sukanya Holdings Pvt. Ltd. v. Jayesh H. Pandya (2003): s.8 does not apply where the suit includes parties or subject-matter not covered by the agreement; Booz Allen & Hamilton v. SBI Home Finance (2011): disputes relating to rights in rem (e.g., mortgage enforcement) are not arbitrable; A. Ayyasamy v. A. Paramasivam (2016) and Rashid Raza v. Sadaf Akhtar (2019) on fraud; Avitel Post Studioz Ltd. v. HSBC PI Holdings (2020): allegations of fraud do not by themselves bar arbitration unless they are of a nature affecting the public or relate to rights in rem.
  7. Arbitrability and the scope of court scrutiny: Vidya Drolia v. Durga Trading Corporation (2020, three judges): a four-fold test for non-arbitrability - disputes (i) relating to actions in rem that do not pertain to subordinate rights in personam; (ii) affecting third-party rights, requiring centralised adjudication; (iii) relating to inalienable sovereign and public interest functions of the State; (iv) expressly or by necessary implication non-arbitrable under a statute; landlord-tenant disputes governed by the Transfer of Property Act are arbitrable, but eviction under special rent statutes is not; courts at the referral stage apply a 'prima facie' test and refuse only where the claim is 'ex facie' non-arbitrable ('eyes wide shut' approach); the arbitral tribunal decides arbitrability (competence-competence). In re: Interplay between Arbitration Agreements under the A&C Act 1996 and the Indian Stamp Act 1899 (13 Dec 2023, seven judges): an unstamped arbitration agreement is not void but inadmissible; the defect is curable; the court at the s.11 stage does not examine stamping; the tribunal does. Cox and Kings Ltd. v. SAP India Pvt. Ltd. (6 Dec 2023, Constitution Bench): the 'group of companies' doctrine is part of Indian arbitration law - a non-signatory may be bound by an arbitration agreement on the basis of the parties' mutual intention, conduct, composite transactions and the commercial reality; the tribunal examines whether a non-signatory is a 'veritable party'; the court's s.11 inquiry is prima facie.
  8. Interim measures by court (s.9): a party may apply to a court before, during or after arbitral proceedings (but before enforcement under s.36) for interim measures such as preservation of goods, injunction, appointment of receiver, security for the amount in dispute, interim custody; if the court grants interim measure before the commencement of arbitral proceedings, the proceedings must be commenced within 90 days (or such further time as the court allows) (s.9(2)); once the tribunal is constituted, the court shall not entertain a s.9 application unless it finds that the remedy under s.17 is not efficacious (s.9(3)); Arcelor Mittal Nippon Steel India Ltd. v. Essar Bulk Terminal Ltd. (2021) - Order XXXVIII Rule 5 CPC principles are not strictly applicable; the court must consider the broad principles of interim relief; Amazon.com NV Investment Holdings v. Future Retail Ltd. (2021) - an emergency arbitrator's interim award under institutional rules is an 'order' of the tribunal under s.17(1) enforceable under s.17(2). Applies in ICA with a foreign seat (s.2(2) proviso).
  9. Appointment of arbitrators (ss.10-11): number must not be even (s.10); default is a sole arbitrator; for three arbitrators each party appoints one and the two appoint the presiding arbitrator (s.11(3)); if a party fails to appoint within 30 days of receipt of a request, or the two appointed arbitrators fail to agree on the third within 30 days (s.11(4)), the appointment is made on request by the Supreme Court or High Court or by any person or institution designated by it (s.11(6) etc.); s.11(5) - sole arbitrator default; s.11(6) - failure of appointment procedure; s.11(6A) (2015) - the Court shall confine itself to the examination of the existence of an arbitration agreement (the 2019 Amendment omitted s.11(6A), but the Supreme Court continues to apply a prima facie, minimal-scrutiny approach: Mayavati Trading Pvt. Ltd. v. Pradyuat Deb Burman, 2019; Vidya Drolia; In re Interplay) Verify; s.11(12) - in international commercial arbitration the Supreme Court (or its designate) appoints; in domestic arbitration the High Court; s.11(13) - the application shall be disposed of expeditiously and endeavour made to dispose of within 60 days from service of notice on the opposite party; s.11(14) - fee fixed per the Fourth Schedule. s.11(3A)-(3B) (2019): the Supreme Court and High Courts shall designate arbitral institutions graded by the Arbitration Council of India; the ACI has not been constituted, so appointments continue to be made by courts.
  10. Seat and venue: place of arbitration (s.20) - parties are free to agree on the place; failing agreement the tribunal determines it having regard to convenience; the tribunal may meet at any place for consultation, hearing witnesses or inspection. Seat = the legal place determining the lex arbitri and the supervisory court; Indus Mobile Distribution Pvt. Ltd. v. Datawind Innovations (2017): once the seat is designated, the courts of the seat have exclusive jurisdiction notwithstanding contrary clauses; Union of India v. Hardy Exploration and Production (India) Inc. (2018) and BGS SGS SOMA JV v. NHPC Ltd. (2019): where the agreement designates a 'venue' without any contrary indicium, the venue is the seat; Mankastu Impex Pvt. Ltd. v. Airvisual Ltd. (2020). Jurisdiction (s.42): where any application has been made in a court under Part I with respect to an arbitration agreement, that court alone has jurisdiction over the arbitral proceedings and all subsequent applications (State of West Bengal v. Associated Contractors, 2015).

✎Memory aids

  • s.8 'before first statement on substance'; s.9 '90 days'; s.11 '30 days / 60 days'.
  • Seat chooses supervisory court; venue = seat unless contrary indication.

!Exam traps

  • TrapPart I applies if the seat is in India; Part II applies to foreign awards.
  • TrapUnder s.7 the agreement need not be signed if there is a record by exchange of communications.
  • TrapUnder s.9(2) arbitration must begin within 90 days of a pre-arbitral court order.
  • TrapAt s.11 the court only examines existence of an agreement, not merits or stamping.

?Likely questions

  3. BALCO decided that Part I of the Act applies only to arbitrations seated where? (In India)
