How to use these notes

  • The 12 marks (4 + 4 + 4) divide evenly across ADR/Arbitration, PIL and Professional Ethics. All three are heavily recall-based and fast to score once the key sections, cases and rules are memorised.
  • Arbitration: learn the architecture of the A&C Act (Part I, Part II, Part III), then ss.7-9, 11-12, 16-17, 23, 29A, 31, 34, 36-37, 44-48. Time-limits and the 2015/2019/2021 amendments are the favourite targets.
  • PIL: learn the evolution (Hussainara -> S.P. Gupta -> Bandhua -> Vishaka), the Supreme Court's 1988 categories, the Balwant Singh Chaufal abuse guidelines and the 2026 remarks of the CJI Surya Kant Bench.
  • Professional Ethics: know the Advocates Act sections (ss.16, 23, 24, 29-35, 36B, 37-38, 49) and the BCI Rules by theme (duty to court, client, opponent, colleagues, other employment). Learn the leading cases on misconduct.
  • Overlaps: Constitutional Law (Arts 32, 226, 39A, 21, 129; PIL jurisdiction), CrPC/BNSS (compounding, plea bargaining, privileges), CPC (s.89, Order X Rules 1A-1C, Order XXIII, Order XXXIIA), Contract (arbitration agreement as contract; stamp duty), Consumer Protection (mediation) and Administrative Law, Labour Law (industrial conciliation; stamp duty).

Question patterns

  • Section recall: 'Section 34 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act deals with ...'
  • Time-limits: 30 days (appointment), 60 days (s.11 disposal), 12 months + 6 months (s.29A), 3 months + 30 days (s.34), 90 days (s.9), 60 days (appeals under the Advocates Act)
  • Case-to-principle: BALCO (seat), Vidya Drolia (arbitrability), Ssangyong (public policy), Cox and Kings (group of companies), Hussainara (first PIL), Harish Uppal (strikes)
  • Fact scenarios: advocate takes contingent fee; advocate appears before a relative judge; PIL by a stranger on a service matter; Lok Adalat award appealed

High-yield topics

  • A&C Act s.7, s.8, s.9, s.11, s.12 and Schedules, s.16, s.17, s.29A, s.31(7), s.34, s.36, s.37, s.43, ss.44-49 and Part III
  • Seat v. venue (BALCO, Indus Mobile, BGS SGS Soma)
  • Public policy and patent illegality (Renusagar, Saw Pipes, Associate Builders, Ssangyong)
  • Court's power to modify an award (Gayatri Balasamy, 2025)
  • Lok Adalat (LSA Act ss.19-22) and Permanent Lok Adalat (ss.22A-22E)
  • Mediation Act 2023 and Mediation Council of India (established 2026)
  • s.89 CPC and Commercial Courts Act s.12A (Patil Automation)

Time strategy

  • Most items are recall; 60 seconds each. Use the open book only to confirm numbers.
  • For arbitration questions, check whether the subject is Part I (seat in India) or Part II (foreign award).
  • For ethics, the BCI Rules are often framed as dos and don'ts; if the conduct benefits the client at the cost of honesty to the court, it is almost always misconduct.

Common traps

  • Section 34 limitation is 3 months, extendable by a maximum of 30 days - not 90 days and not under the Limitation Act s.5.
  • Patent illegality (s.34(2A)) is available only for domestic awards, not for awards in international commercial arbitration.
  • An arbitration agreement need not be signed if exchange of communications provides a record (s.7(4)(b)); electronic communication counts.
  • Lok Adalat awards are decrees but are NOT appealable; challenge is by writ (Bhargavi Constructions).
  • Permanent Lok Adalats can decide on merits if conciliation fails; ordinary Lok Adalats cannot.
  • Conciliation settlement has the status of an arbitral award on agreed terms (s.74).
