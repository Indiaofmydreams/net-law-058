Defamation, Criminal Intimidation, Insult, Obscenity, Public Nuisance, False Evidence, Public Health and Safety

iOverview

Defamation (s.356; IPC 499): making or publishing any imputation concerning any person intending to harm, or knowing or having reason to believe that such imputation will harm, the reputation of such person, by words spoken or intended to be read, signs, visible representations or electronic means.

★Must know

  1. Defamation (s.356; IPC 499): making or publishing any imputation concerning any person intending to harm, or knowing or having reason to believe that such imputation will harm, the reputation of such person, by words spoken or intended to be read, signs, visible representations or electronic means. Punishment (s.356(2)): simple imprisonment up to 2 years, or fine, or both, or community service. Ten exceptions: truth for public good; public conduct of public servants; conduct of any person touching public question; reports of court proceedings; merits of a case decided by court or witnesses and conduct; merits of public performance; censure by authority; accusation in good faith to authorised person; imputation by a person on his own character for protection of interest; caution intended for the good of the person or for public good.
  2. Criminal defamation constitutional: Subramanian Swamy v UoI (2016) upheld IPC 499-500 as a reasonable restriction under Article 19(2) protecting reputation as part of Article 21. Defamation of the deceased, of a company or collection of persons is possible if the imputation would harm the reputation if living.
  3. Printing or engraving defamatory matter; sale of printed defamatory matter (s.357): up to 2 years.
  4. Criminal intimidation (s.351; IPC 503): threatening a person with injury to person, reputation or property, or to any person in whom he is interested, with intent to cause alarm or to cause him to do any act he is not legally bound to do. Punishment s.351(2): up to 2 years or fine or both; threat of death or grievous hurt, imputation of unchastity to a woman, etc. (s.351(3)): up to 7 years; anonymous communication (s.351(4)): up to 2 years additional. Criminal intimidation by anonymous communication is aggravated.
  5. Intentional insult with intent to provoke breach of the peace (s.352; IPC 504): up to 2 years or fine or both. Statements conducing to public mischief (s.353; IPC 505): up to 3 years; rumour or false information spreading alarm (including by electronic means), or inciting groups: up to 3 years; in a place of worship: up to 5 years.
  6. Misconduct in public by a drunken person (s.355; IPC 510): up to 24 hours simple imprisonment or fine up to Rs 1,000 or community service.
  7. Obscenity (s.294; IPC 292): obscene if lascivious or appeals to prurient interest or tends to deprave and corrupt persons likely to read, see or hear it. Test: Hicklin test (adopted in Ranjit Udeshi v State of Maharashtra, 1965) replaced by the community standards test in Aveek Sarkar v State of West Bengal (2014) and Bobby Art International v Om Pal Singh Hoon (1996, Bandit Queen). Punishment: first conviction up to 2 years and fine up to Rs 5,000; subsequent conviction up to 5 years and fine up to Rs 10,000. Sale of obscene objects to a child (s.295): 3 years first, 7 years later. Obscene acts and songs in public (s.296): up to 3 months or fine Rs 1,000. Overlap with IT Act 2000 ss.67-67B.
  8. Public nuisance (s.270; IPC 268): an act or illegal omission causing common injury, danger or annoyance to the public or to people in general in the vicinity. Punishment (s.292): fine up to Rs 1,000; community service possible. Negligent act likely to spread infection of disease dangerous to life (s.271; IPC 269): up to 6 months or fine; malignant act (s.272; IPC 270): up to 2 years.
  9. Adulteration of food or drink intended for sale (s.274; IPC 272): up to 6 months and fine up to Rs 5,000; sale of noxious food or drink (s.275; IPC 273): same; adulteration or sale of drugs (ss.276-278): 6 months to 1 year etc. Rash driving or riding on a public way (s.281; IPC 279): up to 6 months or fine up to Rs 1,000. Rash navigation of a vessel (s.282). Negligent conduct with respect to poisonous substances, fire, explosive substances, machinery, building collapse, animals (ss.286-291).
  10. False evidence (s.227; IPC 191): being legally bound by oath or express provision of law to state the truth, or bound by law to make a declaration, makes a statement which is false and which he either knows or believes to be false or does not believe to be true. Punishment for false evidence (s.229(1); IPC 193): up to 7 years and fine in judicial proceedings; up to 3 years in other cases. Fabricating false evidence (s.228, 230): up to 7 years, or life if used to procure conviction for a capital offence. Giving or fabricating false evidence with intent to procure conviction of a capital offence: life or rigorous up to 10 years; if an innocent person is executed: death or the above.
  11. Section 195 CrPC (BNSS 215) bars courts from taking cognizance of ss.172-188 (contempt of lawful authority) and offences against public justice (ss.193-229) except on a written complaint by the court or public servant concerned. Perjury offences require a complaint by the court; Iqbal Singh Marwah v Meenakshi Marwah (2005) on forged documents and s.195 CrPC.
  12. Causing disappearance of evidence of an offence or giving false information to screen the offender (s.238; IPC 201): punishment depends on the gravity of the offence screened: 7 years if offence punishable with death; 3 years if punishable with life or up to 10 years; one-fourth in other cases.
  13. False charge of offence made with intent to injure (s.248; IPC 211): up to 2 years or fine or both; if the charge is of an offence punishable with death, life or 7 years or more: up to 7 years and fine. Harbouring offender: s.249-252.
  14. Contempt of lawful authority (Chapter XIV): non-attendance in obedience to summons (s.209) and community service; obstruction (s.221); threatening a public servant (s.224); refusing to be sworn (s.229-like); omission to assist public servant (s.222 etc.) Verify · section numbers.

⚖Landmark cases

Subramanian Swamy v Union of India

2016

Criminal defamation under IPC 499 and 500 is constitutional; reputation is part of Article 21.

Aveek Sarkar v State of West Bengal

2014

Hicklin test replaced by the community standards test; Boris Becker photograph not obscene.

Ranjit Udeshi v State of Maharashtra

1965

Lady Chatterley's Lover; Hicklin test applied; obscenity assessed by tendency to deprave and corrupt.

Bobby Art International v Om Pal Singh Hoon

1996

Context and message of the film: nudity in the context of a social message is not obscene.

Shreya Singhal v UoI

2015

Section 66A (offensive messages) unconstitutional; incitement as against advocacy.

Iqbal Singh Marwah v Meenakshi Marwah

2005

Bar under s.195(1)(b)(ii) CrPC applies where the forgery occurs after the document is produced in court.

!Exam traps

  • TrapDefamation: truth is a defence ONLY if published for the public good (Exception 1).
  • TrapIntimidation requires a threat with intent to cause alarm; mere abusive words are insult under s.352 only if intended to provoke breach of the peace.
  • TrapObscenity today: community standards test, not the Hicklin test.
  • TrapFalse evidence is punishable only if the person is legally bound to state the truth.
