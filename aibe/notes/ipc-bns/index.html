%Where the marks come from

Indian Penal Code / Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita carries about 8 of 100 questions. AIBE is a 100-question MCQ test; IPC / BNS carries about 8 questions and Company Law about 2. Pass mark is a percentage of total marks (currently understood as 45 percent for General/OBC and 40 percent for SC/ST/PwD) Verify · against the latest BCI notification. No negative marking has been the practice, so never leave a question blank.

What the examiner tests

  • Test whether a fresh advocate can identify the correct offence on given facts (practice-readiness), not whether they can recite whole sections.
  • Test knowledge of the new criminal codes: since 1 July 2024 trials for new offences run under BNS, so the exam now mixes IPC and BNS references.
  • Test fundamentals that never change: mens rea, general exceptions, murder versus culpable homicide, common intention versus common object, abetment, attempt, theft-extortion-robbery-dacoity ladder, cheating versus criminal breach of trust.
  • Test landmark cases that shaped criminal law (Bachan Singh, Virsa Singh, Nanavati, Barendra Kumar Ghosh, Gian Kaur, Kedar Nath, Tukaram, Jacob Mathew).
  • Test awareness of current legal developments (new BNS offences, sedition replacement, mob lynching, organised crime, community service, marital rape debate).

Question types seen

  • Section recall. Example: Which BNS section punishes murder? (103) / Which IPC section defines culpable homicide? (299) Tip: Memorise the mapping_table at the end; it is the single highest-yield sheet.
  • Fact-scenario (illustration based). Example: A, intending to kill B, shoots at B but kills C. Which offence/doctrine applies? Tip: Identify intention first, then act, then result. Transferred malice (BNS 101 illustration) makes A liable for C's murder.
  • Statement evaluation. Example: Consider statements I-III on common intention; which are correct? Tip: Watch for absolute words (always, only, never). Common intention needs prior meeting of minds; common object does not need a prior concert but needs five or more persons.
  • Case-law matching. Example: Rarest of rare doctrine arises from which case? Tip: Link each case to one-line principle (see case tables).
  • Punishment/number recall. Example: Minimum punishment for dowry death? (7 years) Tip: Make a number sheet: 7 years (dowry death), 10 years (rape minimum), 20 years (rape of girl under 16 minimum), 3 years (cruelty/stalking first conviction) etc.
  • Which is NOT / exception based. Example: Which of the following is not an exception to murder? Tip: Five exceptions to murder: grave and sudden provocation, exceeding private defence, public servant acting in good faith, sudden fight, consent.

Hot zones, 2024 to 2026

  • BNS new offences: mob lynching (103(2)), organised crime (111), petty organised crime (112), terrorist act (113), snatching (304), hit and run (106(2)), sexual intercourse by deceitful means (69), acts endangering sovereignty (152).
  • Community service as a new punishment (BNS 4(f)).
  • Gender-neutral drafting and the definition of gender including transgender persons (BNS s.2).
  • Age of consent for marriage-related exception to rape remains 18 years for the wife (Independent Thought, 2017); constitutional challenge to the marital rape exception is pending Verify.
  • Misuse of cruelty/dowry provisions: Supreme Court cautions on vague, omnibus allegations against in-laws (Arnesh Kumar; Achin Gupta v State of Haryana, 2024) Verify · latest.
  • Free speech versus BNS 196/197/299/152 prosecutions: courts insist on the reasonable-person and proximity tests (Imran Pratapgadhi v State of Gujarat, 2025) Verify.
  • Company law: continuing decriminalisation (Companies (Amendment) Act 2020, Jan Vishwas Act 2023, further Jan Vishwas Bill 2025 Verify · status), Cyrus Investments v Tata Sons (2021), director liability and corporate criminal liability.

How to use these notes

  • AIBE is open-book: you can carry bare acts, but you cannot read them during the exam. Use these notes to know WHERE to look and WHAT is tested.
  • Learn every offence as a triple: IPC section, BNS section, punishment. AIBE questions regularly ask for the BNS number or the changed punishment.
  • Priority labels: HIGH = tested almost every cycle, MEDIUM = tested often, LOW = occasional. Spend roughly 60 percent of time on HIGH sections.
  • Finish each section by solving its practice MCQs, then revise the last_minute_revision block the night before.
