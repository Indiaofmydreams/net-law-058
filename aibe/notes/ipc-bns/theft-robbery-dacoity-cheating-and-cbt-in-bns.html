Offences against Property: Theft, Snatching, Extortion, Robbery, Dacoity, Criminal Misappropriation, Criminal Breach of Trust, Cheating, Stolen Property, Mischief, Trespass

iOverview

Theft (s.303; IPC 378): dishonestly taking any movable property out of the possession of any person without his consent, moving the property in order to such taking.

★Must know

  1. Theft (s.303; IPC 378): dishonestly taking any movable property out of the possession of any person without his consent, moving the property in order to such taking. Ingredients: dishonest intention; movable property; out of possession; without consent; moving. Pyare Lal Bhargava v State of Rajasthan (1963): temporary removal with dishonest intention is theft. K.N. Mehra v State of Rajasthan (1957): taking an aircraft; intention to take dishonestly is satisfied even if the property is to be returned later. Earth attached to the earth becomes movable once severed.
  2. Punishment for theft (s.303(2)): up to 3 years or fine or both; repeat conviction: 1 to 5 years and fine; community service in the case of a first-time offender where the stolen property value is below Rs 5,000 and it is returned or its value restored.
  3. Snatching (s.304, NEW): theft by suddenly or quickly or forcibly seizing or securing or grabbing or taking away from any person or from his possession any movable property, in order to commit theft: up to 3 years and fine.
  4. Theft in a dwelling house, transport, place of worship (s.305): up to 7 years and fine. Theft by clerk or servant (s.306): up to 7 years and fine. Theft after preparation for causing death, hurt or restraint (s.307): up to 10 years rigorous imprisonment.
  5. Extortion (s.308; IPC 383): intentionally putting any person in fear of injury and thereby dishonestly inducing him to deliver property or valuable security. Punishment: up to 7 years or fine or both; putting person in fear of death or grievous hurt: up to 10 years; accusing of an offence punishable with death or life: up to 10 years.
  6. Robbery (s.309; IPC 390): theft or extortion becomes robbery when, in order to committing theft or in committing or carrying away property, the offender voluntarily causes or attempts to cause death, hurt or wrongful restraint, or fear of instant death, instant hurt or instant wrongful restraint. Punishment s.309(4): rigorous imprisonment up to 10 years and fine; highway robbery between sunset and sunrise: up to 14 years.
  7. Dacoity (s.310; IPC 391): five or more persons conjointly commit or attempt to commit robbery, or where the whole number of persons conjointly committing, attempting and aiding amounts to five or more. Punishment: life or rigorous imprisonment up to 10 years and fine. Dacoity with murder: death, life or rigorous imprisonment not less than 10 years (s.310(3)). Robbery or dacoity with attempt to cause death or grievous hurt (s.311): not less than 7 years. Attempt to commit robbery or dacoity when armed (s.312): not less than 7 years. Belonging to a gang of dacoits: life or rigorous imprisonment up to 10 years (BNS 310(4), 310(5)).
  8. Theft vs extortion vs robbery: in theft, property is taken without consent; in extortion, consent is obtained by fear; in robbery, there is theft or extortion plus instant violence or the fear of it. Robbery is the common denominator of both.
  9. Dishonest misappropriation of property (s.314; IPC 403): dishonestly misappropriating or converting to own use any movable property; 6 months to 2 years and fine. Misappropriation of property of a deceased person (s.315; IPC 404): 6 months to 3 years; clerk or servant: up to 7 years. Distinction from theft: in misappropriation the property comes into the accused's possession innocently (finding lost property) and the dishonest intention arises later.
  10. Criminal breach of trust (CBT) (s.316; IPC 405): entrustment with property or dominion over it and dishonest misappropriation or conversion, use or disposal of property in violation of the law or contract. Punishment: s.316(2): up to 5 years or fine or both; s.316(3) carrier, wharfinger or warehouse-keeper: up to 7 years and fine; s.316(4) clerk or servant: up to 7 years and fine; s.316(5) public servant, banker, merchant, agent, broker, attorney: life or up to 10 years and fine. Key cases: R.K. Dalmia v Delhi Administration (1962): 'property' includes funds of a company; Velji Raghavji Patel v State of Maharashtra (1965): a partner is not liable for CBT of partnership property unless a specific entrustment is shown. Entrustment is the key to CBT.
  11. Receiving stolen property (s.317; IPC 410-414): 'stolen property' includes property transferred by theft, extortion, robbery, criminal misappropriation or CBT; dishonestly receiving: up to 3 years (s.317(2)); habitually dealing: life or up to 10 years; assisting in concealment: up to 3 years.
  12. Cheating (s.318; IPC 415): by deceiving a person, fraudulently or dishonestly inducing him to deliver property to any person or consent that any person shall retain any property, or intentionally inducing him to do or omit to do anything which he would not do if not deceived and which causes or is likely to cause damage or harm to body, mind, reputation or property. Punishment: s.318(2) up to 3 years or fine or both; s.318(3) cheating with knowledge that wrongful loss may be caused to a person whose interest the offender is bound to protect: up to 5 years; s.318(4) cheating and dishonestly inducing delivery of property or making, altering or destroying valuable security: up to 7 years and fine.
  13. Cheating vs CBT vs breach of contract: Hridaya Ranjan Prasad Verma v State of Bihar (2000): to constitute cheating, intention to deceive must exist at the time of making the promise; mere breach of contract does not become cheating. Iridium India Telecom v Motorola (2011): a company may be prosecuted for offences requiring mens rea; corporate criminal liability established. Sunil Bharti Mittal v CBI (2015): directors are not vicariously liable merely because of their post; alter ego doctrine.
  14. Cheating by personation (s.319; IPC 416): up to 5 years or fine; Fraudulent removal or concealment of property (s.320-322 etc.).
  15. Mischief (s.324; IPC 425): intent or knowledge of causing wrongful loss or damage to the public or to any person, and causing the destruction of any property or any change in it or its situation which destroys or diminishes its value or utility. Punishment is graded by the amount of damage and the means used (for example, mischief by fire or explosive substance is punished far more severely); check the thresholds in s.324 of the bare act Verify.
  16. Criminal trespass (s.329(1); IPC 441): entry into or upon property in possession of another with intent to commit an offence or to intimidate, insult or annoy; or unlawfully remaining. Punishment: up to 3 months or fine up to Rs 5,000; house trespass (s.329(3)): up to 1 year or fine up to Rs 5,000; lurking house trespass and house-breaking (ss.330-331): housebreaking: up to 2 years; house-breaking by night: up to 3 years; lurking house-trespass and house-breaking in order to commit an offence punishable with death/imprisonment: 10 years etc. Verify · exact numbering of 330-334.
  17. Forgery and documents: Forgery (s.336; IPC 463): making a false document or electronic record with intent to cause damage or injury to the public or any person, or to support any claim or title, or to cause any person to part with property, or to enter into an express or implied contract, or with intent to commit fraud or that fraud may be committed. Punishment (s.336(2)): up to 2 years or fine or both; forgery for cheating (s.336(3)): up to 7 years and fine; forgery to harm reputation (s.336(4)): up to 3 years and fine. Making a false document is defined in s.335; using as genuine a forged document: s.340. Mohd Ibrahim v State of Bihar (2009): a person who sells a property he does not own does not commit forgery by executing a sale deed in his own name; false document requires that the document purports to be someone else's.
  18. Falsification of accounts (s.344; IPC 477A): up to 7 years or fine or both. Counterfeiting coins and currency notes: ss.178-188 (IPC 231-263A) Verify.
  19. Hot topics: cyber and digital cheating via electronic records and the new definition of 'document'; organised crime provisions covering economic offences; theft of vehicles linked to organised crime.

⚖Landmark cases

Pyare Lal Bhargava v State of Rajasthan

1963

Temporary removal of a file to copy it is theft if dishonest intention exists.

K.N. Mehra v State of Rajasthan

1957

Taking a plane with dishonest intention is theft even if the property is to be returned later.

R.K. Dalmia v Delhi Administration

1962

CBT by a person with dominion over the property of a company; 'property' needs no specific identification.

Velji Raghavji Patel v State of Maharashtra

1965

A partner cannot be liable for CBT of partnership property unless a specific entrustment is shown; partners are co-owners.

Hridaya Ranjan Prasad Verma v State of Bihar

2000

Fraudulent or dishonest intention at the inception is necessary for cheating; breach of contract is a civil wrong.

Iridium India Telecom Ltd v Motorola

2011

A company can be prosecuted for offences requiring mens rea through its directing mind.

Sunil Bharti Mittal v CBI

2015

Directors cannot be vicariously liable unless alter ego or active role is established.

Dr Vimla v Delhi Administration

1963

Distinction between 'dishonestly' and 'fraudulently'; fraud does not require wrongful gain or loss.

Mohd Ibrahim v State of Bihar

2009

Execution of a document by a person claiming title that he does not hold is not forgery; false documents must claim to be executed by another.

!Exam traps

  • TrapRobbery is an aggravated form of theft or extortion; dacoity is robbery by five or more persons.
  • TrapIn CBT the property is ENTRUSTED; in criminal misappropriation the property comes into possession innocently; in theft it is taken out of possession.
  • TrapCheating requires deception at the inception; later failure to perform a promise is not cheating.
  • TrapSnatching is a new offence: up to 3 years and fine.
  • TrapDacoity with murder: death, life or rigorous imprisonment 10 years or more.
  • TrapTheft of immovable property is not theft; but severed things become movable.
