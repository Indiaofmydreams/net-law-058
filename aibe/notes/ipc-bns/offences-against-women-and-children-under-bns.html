Indian Penal Code / Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita · Chapter 7 of 19

Offences against Women and Children (Chapter V): Rape, Sexual Harassment, Stalking, Voyeurism, Cruelty, Kidnapping, Trafficking

High yield2 Qs expected8 min readBNS Ss. 63-99, 137-146 (IPC Ss. 354-354D, 366, 370, 375-376E, 498A, 509)

iOverview

Rape (s.63; IPC 375): sexual intercourse (penetration of the vagina, mouth, urethra or anus in any manner

★Must know

  1. Rape (s.63; IPC 375): sexual intercourse (penetration of the vagina, mouth, urethra or anus in any manner; application of mouth) by a man with a woman under seven descriptions: against her will; without her consent; consent obtained by putting her or any person in whom she is interested in fear of death or hurt; consent given believing the man to be her husband; consent given by a person of unsound mind or intoxicated or administered stupefying substance; with or without consent when under 18 years; when unable to communicate consent.
  2. Consent: unequivocal voluntary agreement by words, gestures or any form of non-verbal communication; mere absence of resistance is not consent. Exceptions: (1) medical procedure; (2) sexual intercourse by a man with his own wife, wife not being under 18 years, is not rape (Exception 2; Independent Thought v UoI, 2017 read down the age to 18). The marital rape exception is under constitutional challenge before the Supreme Court Verify · current status.
  3. Punishment (s.64): rigorous imprisonment not less than 10 years, may extend to life, and fine. Aggravated rape (s.64(2)): by police officer, public servant, armed forces member, jail/remand home staff, hospital staff, relative/guardian/teacher, communal or sectarian violence, pregnant woman, woman incapable of giving consent, one in control or dominance, disabled woman, repeated rape of same woman, grievous bodily harm/maim/disfigure/endanger life: not less than 10 years, may extend to imprisonment for life meaning remainder of natural life, and fine.
  4. Rape of a woman under 16 (s.65(1)): not less than 20 years, may extend to life (remainder of natural life) and fine; under 12 (s.65(2)): not less than 20 years, life (remainder of natural life) or death.
  5. Rape causing death or persistent vegetative state (s.66): not less than 20 years, may extend to life (natural life) or death. Repeat offenders (s.71): life (natural life) or death.
  6. Gang rape (s.70): 20 years to life (natural life) and fine payable to the victim; gang rape of a woman under 18: life (natural life) or death.
  7. Sexual intercourse by husband upon wife during judicial separation (s.67): 2 to 7 years and fine. Sexual intercourse by person in authority (s.68): 5 to 10 years and fine.
  8. Sexual intercourse by deceitful means or false promise of marriage (s.69, NEW): up to 10 years and fine; 'deceitful means' includes inducement for employment/promotion, false identity and false promise to marry without intention to fulfil. Supreme Court earlier distinguished false promise from breach of promise: Uday v State of Karnataka (2003); Pramod Suryabhan Pawar v State of Maharashtra (2019); Anurag Soni v State of Chhattisgarh (2019).
  9. Identity of victim (s.72; IPC 228A): disclosure punishable with up to 2 years and fine; s.73: printing or publishing court proceedings without permission, up to 2 years and fine. Nipun Saxena v UoI (2018) on non-disclosure of identity; the victim's name may not be disclosed even in judgments.
  10. Assault or criminal force to a woman with intent to outrage her modesty (s.74; IPC 354): 1 to 5 years and fine. Test of modesty: Rupan Deol Bajaj v KPS Gill (1995); State of Punjab v Major Singh (1967): the act must offend the sense of decency of a woman.
  11. Sexual harassment (s.75; IPC 354A): physical contact and advances involving unwelcome and explicit sexual overtures; demand or request for sexual favours; showing pornography against will; sexually coloured remarks. Punishment: up to 3 years (first three kinds) or up to 1 year (remarks) and fine.
  12. Assault with intent to disrobe (s.76; IPC 354B): 3 to 7 years and fine. Voyeurism (s.77; IPC 354C): watching or capturing image of a woman in a private act; 1 to 3 years on first conviction, 3 to 7 years on repeat.
  13. Stalking (s.78; IPC 354D): following or contacting a woman despite clear indication of disinterest; monitoring internet, email or electronic communication; first conviction up to 3 years and fine, second 5 years. Exceptions: for preventing or detecting crime authorised by the State; under law; reasonable and justified.
  14. Word, gesture or act intended to insult the modesty of a woman (s.79; IPC 509): up to 3 years and fine.
  15. Cruelty by husband or relatives (s.85; IPC 498A): up to 3 years and fine. 'Cruelty' (s.86): wilful conduct likely to drive the woman to suicide or cause grave injury/danger to life, limb or health (mental or physical); harassment to coerce her or her relatives to meet unlawful demand for property or valuable security. Supreme Court cautions against implicating the entire family without specific allegations: Arnesh Kumar v State of Bihar (2014) (no automatic arrest); Preeti Gupta v State of Jharkhand (2010); Kahkashan Kausar v State of Bihar (2022); Achin Gupta v State of Haryana (2024) Verify. Rajesh Sharma (2017) guidelines on Family Welfare Committees were modified in Social Action Forum for Manav Adhikar v UoI (2018).
  16. Bigamy (s.82; IPC 494): marrying again during the life of spouse: up to 7 years and fine; concealment of former marriage: up to 10 years. Sarla Mudgal v UoI (1995): conversion to Islam for second marriage does not dissolve the first; bigamy punishable.
  17. Adultery (IPC 497) was struck down in Joseph Shine v UoI (2018) and is not in BNS; no corresponding provision. IPC 377 (unnatural offences) is not in BNS, but non-consensual carnal intercourse persists in discussions Verify.
  18. Kidnapping (s.137; IPC 359-361): kidnapping from India, or from lawful guardianship (under the IPC: a boy under 16 or a girl under 18; BNS s.137 uses 'child', that is, below 18, for both sexes Verify; or a person of unsound mind). Abduction (s.138; IPC 362): by force or deceitful means to compel a person to go from any place. Kidnapping from lawful guardianship is complete when the minor is taken or enticed away (it is not a continuing offence, whereas abduction is); S. Varadarajan v State of Madras (1965): the minor must be taken or enticed, not merely voluntarily accompany the accused.
  19. Punishment for kidnapping (s.137(2)): up to 7 years and fine. Kidnapping for ransom (s.140(2); IPC 364A): death or life and fine. Kidnapping in order to murder (s.140(1)): life or rigorous imprisonment up to 10 years. Kidnapping to compel marriage (s.87; IPC 366): not less than 10 years, may extend to life Verify · numbers.
  20. Trafficking of persons (s.143; IPC 370): recruiting, transporting, harbouring or receiving persons by threats, force, coercion, abduction, fraud, deception, abuse of power or inducement for exploitation. Punishment: 7 to 10 years; more than one person 10 years to life; minor: 10 years to life; more than one minor: 14 years to life; repeat offender: life (natural life). Exploitation of trafficked person (s.144; IPC 370A): 3 to 5 years (5-7 where a minor). Child-related: procuring (s.96), selling a minor for prostitution (s.98): 10 years to life; buying a minor (s.99): 10 years to life.
  21. Concealment of birth (s.94), causing miscarriage (s.88: up to 3 years; woman: up to 7 years), without consent (s.89: life or up to 10), death by act to cause miscarriage (s.90), act to prevent a child being born alive (s.91), death of quick unborn child (s.92). The MTP Act 1971 (as amended 2021) permits termination up to 24 weeks for specified categories.
  22. Related statutes frequently tested alongside: POCSO Act 2012 (penetrative sexual assault on a child; age of consent 18; mandatory reporting); Criminal Law (Amendment) Act 2013 (Nirbhaya); Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace Act 2013; Juvenile Justice Act 2015 (heinous offences by 16-18-year-olds may be tried as adults after preliminary assessment, Shilpa Mittal v State of NCT Delhi, 2020).

⚖Landmark cases

Tukaram v State of Maharashtra (Mathura rape case)

1979

Acquittal on 'consent' reasoning led to 1983 amendments: presumption of no consent on proof of sexual intercourse where victim says she did not consent (BSA s.120).

State of Punjab v Gurmit Singh

1996

Conviction in rape may rest on sole testimony of the prosecutrix; courts must be sensitive; in-camera trials.

Independent Thought v Union of India

2017

Exception 2 to s.375 read down: sexual intercourse with a wife under 18 is rape.

Mukesh v State (NCT of Delhi)

2017

Death sentence confirmed for Nirbhaya convicts.

Vishaka v State of Rajasthan

1997

Guidelines against sexual harassment at workplace; led to the 2013 Act.

Rupan Deol Bajaj v KPS Gill

1995

Modesty of a woman: the ultimate test is whether the action offends the sense of decency of a woman.

Sarla Mudgal v Union of India

1995

Second marriage after conversion remains bigamy.

Joseph Shine v Union of India

2018

Adultery (IPC 497) unconstitutional; women not chattel.

Arnesh Kumar v State of Bihar

2014

498A arrests must follow checklist under s.41 CrPC (now BNSS 35); no mechanical arrest.

Laxmi v Union of India

2014

Acid sale regulation and compensation for acid-attack victims.

Pramod Suryabhan Pawar v State of Maharashtra

2019

Consent obtained on a false promise of marriage vitiates consent only if the promise was false from inception.

S. Varadarajan v State of Madras

1965

Taking is not the same as allowing a minor to accompany; kidnapping requires active inducement.

!Exam traps

  • TrapAge of consent for sexual intercourse is 18 years; exception for wife is also 18 (Independent Thought).
  • TrapFalse promise of marriage: BNS 69 is now a standalone offence (up to 10 years), distinct from rape.
  • TrapStalking, voyeurism and harassment: remember repeat-offender enhancements.
  • TrapCruelty under s.85 has two limbs (health/suicide-driving and dowry harassment).
  • TrapGang rape of a minor under 18: life (natural life) or death; for adult woman: 20 years to life.
  • TrapDisclosure of victim's identity is an offence (up to 2 years and fine), including through publication of court records without leave.
