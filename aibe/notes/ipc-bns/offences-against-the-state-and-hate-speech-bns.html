iOverview

Waging war against the Government of India (s.147

★Must know

  1. Waging war against the Government of India (s.147; IPC 121): death or life imprisonment and fine. Conspiracy to commit offences against the State (s.148; IPC 121A): life or up to 10 years. Collecting arms to wage war (s.149; IPC 122); concealing a design to wage war (s.150; IPC 123); assaulting the President or Governor with intent to compel or restrain exercise of lawful power (s.151; IPC 124).
  2. Acts endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity of India (s.152; replaces IPC 124A sedition): whoever, purposely or knowingly, by words (spoken or written), signs, visible representation, electronic communication or use of financial means, or otherwise, excites or attempts to excite secession, armed rebellion, subversive activities, encourages feelings of separatist activities, or endangers sovereignty or unity and integrity of India, shall be punished with life imprisonment or imprisonment up to 7 years and fine. Explanation: comments expressing disapprobation of measures of government with a view to obtain their alteration by lawful means do not constitute the offence.
  3. Sedition background: Kedar Nath Singh v State of Bihar (1962) limited s.124A to acts with a tendency to incite violence or public disorder; Balwant Singh v State of Punjab (1995) (slogans); S.G. Vombatkere v UoI (2022): operation of s.124A kept in abeyance; BNS 152 is challenged as retaining sedition in substance Verify · pending challenges.
  4. Unlawful assembly (s.189), common object (s.190), rioting (s.191): see 4.3. Punishment for rioting (s.191(2)): up to 2 years or fine or both; rioting armed with a deadly weapon (s.191(3)): up to 5 years; unlawful assembly (s.189(2)): up to 6 months; unlawful assembly armed: up to 2 years.
  5. Affray (s.194; IPC 159-160): two or more persons fighting in a public place disturbing the public peace: up to 1 month or fine Rs 1,000.
  6. Promoting enmity between different groups on grounds of religion, race, place of birth, residence, language, etc. (s.196; IPC 153A): up to 3 years or fine or both; in a place of worship or at religious gatherings, up to 5 years and fine. Section 197 (IPC 153B): imputations and assertions prejudicial to national integration: up to 3 years.
  7. Hate speech jurisprudence: Pravasi Bhalai Sangathan v UoI (2014): Law Commission to examine; Amish Devgan v UoI (2020): speech is judged by the reasonable, strong-minded, firm and courageous person standard, not weak and vacillating minds, and context and intent matter; Arup Bhuyan v State of Assam (2011): mere membership of a banned organisation is not an offence unless the accused incites violence; Shreya Singhal v UoI (2015): discussion and advocacy are protected, incitement is not (Section 66A IT Act struck down); Imran Pratapgadhi v State of Gujarat (2025): speech must be judged by the standards of reasonable, strong-minded persons; creative expression and a poem do not by themselves attract ss.196/197/299 Verify.
  8. Offences relating to religion: injuring or defiling a place of worship (s.298; IPC 295): up to 2 years or fine or both; deliberate and malicious acts intended to outrage religious feelings (s.299; IPC 295A): up to 3 years or fine or both (Ramji Lal Modi v State of UP, 1957 upheld 295A as a reasonable restriction); disturbing religious assembly (s.300); trespassing on burial places (s.301); uttering words with deliberate intent to wound religious feelings (s.302; IPC 298): up to 1 year.
  9. Obstructing public servants and public duty: assaulting or obstructing a public servant when suppressing riot (s.195); obstructing a public servant in discharge of public functions (s.221; IPC 186): up to 3 months or Rs 2,500 fine; disobedience to order duly promulgated by a public servant (s.223; IPC 188): up to 6 months if it causes obstruction; up to 1 year if danger to life, health or safety or riot. Note that s.223 is now a cognizable-complaint structure tied to BNSS s.215 (complaint by the public servant).
  10. Absconding to avoid service of summons is BNS s.206 (IPC 172); non-attendance in obedience to an order from a public servant is s.209 (IPC 174), where community service is an alternative punishment. Giving false information to a public servant (s.217; IPC 182): up to 6 months or fine up to Rs 5,000.
  11. Public servants: taking gratification other than legal remuneration, bribery and other corruption offences: now under the Prevention of Corruption Act 1988 (amended 2018); IPC 161-165A were repealed earlier, and BNS retains only limited provisions (e.g., s.198 public servant disobeying direction under law).

⚖Landmark cases

Kedar Nath Singh v State of Bihar

1962

Sedition limited to acts having a tendency to create public disorder or incitement to violence.

Shreya Singhal v Union of India

2015

Incitement vs discussion and advocacy; s.66A IT Act struck down as vague.

Amish Devgan v Union of India

2020

Hate speech test: effect on the targeted group, context, intention; reasonable person standard.

Ramji Lal Modi v State of UP

1957

s.295A upheld: only deliberate and malicious insult to religion is punishable.

Arup Bhuyan v State of Assam

2011

Mere membership of a prohibited organisation is not an offence without active incitement or violence.

Masalti v State of UP

1965

Unlawful assembly and the five-member rule.

!Exam traps

  • TrapBNS has no section titled 'sedition'; s.152 is the corresponding offence with a wider textual reach (secession, armed rebellion, subversive activities, electronic communication) and life imprisonment or up to 7 years.
  • TrapSection 153A requires promotion of enmity between GROUPS on specified grounds; 295A requires deliberate and malicious intent to outrage RELIGIOUS feelings of a class.
