Hurt, Grievous Hurt, Acid Attack, Wrongful Restraint/Confinement, Force, Assault

Medium yield1 Qs expected3 min readBNS Ss. 114-136 (IPC Ss. 319-358)

In this chapter

  2. Must know (12 points)
  3. Exam traps

Chapter highlights →My highlights →

Tip: select any text to highlight it or ask a doubt.

iOverview

Hurt (s.114; IPC 319): causing bodily pain, disease or infirmity.

★Must know

  1. Hurt (s.114; IPC 319): causing bodily pain, disease or infirmity. Voluntarily causing hurt (s.115(2); IPC 323): up to 1 year or fine up to Rs 10,000 or both.
  2. Grievous hurt (s.116; IPC 320) eight kinds: emasculation; permanent privation of sight of either eye; permanent privation of hearing of either ear; privation of any member or joint; destruction or permanent impairing of powers of any member or joint; permanent disfiguration of the head or face; fracture or dislocation of a bone or tooth; any hurt which endangers life or causes severe bodily pain for 20 days or renders the sufferer unable to follow ordinary pursuits for 20 days.
  3. Voluntarily causing grievous hurt (s.117(2); IPC 325): up to 7 years and fine. BNS 117(3): where hurt results in permanent disability or persistent vegetative state: rigorous imprisonment not less than 10 years, which may extend to life Verify. BNS 117(4): group of five or more on grounds of race, caste, etc.: up to 7 years and fine (mob-lynching-type for grievous hurt).
  4. Hurt by dangerous weapons (s.118; IPC 324, 326): hurt by weapon or means likely to cause death: up to 3 years; grievous hurt by such means: life or up to 10 years.
  5. Acid attack (s.124; IPC 326A, 326B): causing permanent or partial damage or deformity by acid: not less than 10 years extendable to life, with fine payable to victim to meet medical expenses; throwing or attempting to throw acid: 5 to 7 years and fine. Laxmi v Union of India (2014): regulation of acid sales and compensation.
  6. Hurt to extort property or to constrain an illegal act: ss.119; to extort confession: s.120; to deter public servant: s.121; by poison to commit an offence: s.123.
  7. Act endangering life or personal safety of others (s.125; IPC 336-338).
  8. Wrongful restraint (s.126; IPC 339): obstructing a person from proceeding in a direction in which he has a right to proceed; up to 1 month or fine Rs 5,000 or both. Wrongful confinement (s.127; IPC 340): restraint so as to prevent proceeding beyond limits; 1 year or Rs 5,000 fine; confinement for 3 days or more: up to 2 years; confinement in secret: more.
  9. Force (s.128) and criminal force (s.129; IPC 350): intentional use of force without consent to commit an offence or cause injury, fear or annoyance. Assault (s.130; IPC 351): gesture or preparation causing apprehension of criminal force; mere words do not amount to assault unless accompanied by gestures. Punishment s.131: up to 3 months or fine Rs 1,000 or both.
  10. Assault or criminal force to deter a public servant from duty (s.132; IPC 353): up to 2 years or fine or both. Assault with intent to dishonour (s.133; IPC 355): up to 2 years. Assault in attempt to commit theft (s.134); attempt wrongfully to confine (s.135); grave provocation (s.136) Verify · numbering of ss.132-136 against the bare act.
  11. Grievous hurt differs from hurt by degree and by the closed list in s.116; the tests are 'permanent' and '20 days'.
  12. Related: kidnapping and abduction (below, 4.7).

!Exam traps

  • Trap'Grievous hurt' list is exhaustive (eight kinds); fracture or dislocation of a bone or tooth counts even if it heals.
  • Trap20-day rule: severe bodily pain or inability to follow ordinary pursuits for 20 days.
  • TrapMere words never amount to assault; a gesture plus apprehension is required.
  • TrapAcid attack minimum: 10 years; throwing/attempt: 5 to 7 years.
