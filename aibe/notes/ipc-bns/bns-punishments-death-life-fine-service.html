Punishments in Depth: Death, Life Imprisonment, Remission, Fine, Forfeiture and Community Service

iOverview

BNS s.4 lists six punishments: (a) death

★Must know

  1. BNS s.4 lists six punishments: (a) death; (b) imprisonment for life; (c) imprisonment, which is either rigorous (with hard labour) or simple; (d) forfeiture of property; (e) fine; (f) community service (new).
  2. Community service (BNS s.23 explanation, s.4(f) read with the Explanation): work which the court may direct a convict to perform for the benefit of the community, for which he shall not be entitled to any remuneration. It is intended as a non-custodial reformative alternative for petty and first-time offences. It is provided only in six provisions (see 4.1). The mode and manner of performance is left to the court; the details are to be framed through BNSS and rules Verify.
  3. Commutation (s.5): the appropriate Government may, without the consent of the offender, commute a death sentence to imprisonment for life, and imprisonment for life to imprisonment for a term not less than seven years; BNS removes the IPC 54 and 55 'any other punishment' language. Remission, suspension and commutation are also under BNSS ss.473-477 (CrPC 432-435) and Arts. 72 and 161 of the Constitution (Presidential and Governor's powers).
  4. Imprisonment for life means imprisonment for the remainder of the convict's natural life, unless remitted by the appropriate Government under the statutory scheme (Gopal Vinayak Godse v State of Maharashtra, 1961; Mohd. Munna v Union of India, 2005). It is not equal to 20 years; the 20-year equivalence in s.6 is only for calculating fractions of terms. A convict sentenced to life cannot claim release after 14 years as of right: s.433A CrPC (BNSS 475) bars remission before 14 years' actual imprisonment in offences with a death punishment option.
  5. Special category sentences: Swamy Shraddananda (2) v State of Karnataka (2008) allowed life imprisonment without remission for the entire life or a fixed term (e.g., 30 years) as an alternative to death; Union of India v V. Sriharan (2015): a five-judge Bench confirmed that the High Court and Supreme Court (not the trial court) may impose a modified punishment of life imprisonment for a specific period beyond 14 years or for the full natural life, without remission. The BNS adds explicit 'imprisonment for life for remainder of natural life' in the aggravated rape provisions.
  6. Rigorous vs simple imprisonment (s.7): the court may direct that imprisonment shall be wholly rigorous, wholly simple, or partly each. Imprisonment in default of fine (s.8): where the fine is not paid, the court may direct imprisonment in default; the term shall not exceed one-fourth of the maximum term of imprisonment for the offence when the offence is punishable with both imprisonment and fine; the default imprisonment may be of any description for which the offender might have been sentenced for the offence.
  7. Fine (s.8): where no sum is expressed, the amount of fine is unlimited but shall not be excessive. Where the offence is punishable with fine only, imprisonment in default may be of either description; where it is punishable with imprisonment as well, the default term cannot exceed one-fourth of the maximum term of imprisonment fixed for the offence. The IPC scale linking the amount of fine to the length of default imprisonment has been revised in the BNS; check the bare act for the present limits Verify. Death of the offender does not discharge his property from liability for the fine.
  8. Limit of punishment for offences made up of several offences (s.9; IPC 71): where an offence is made up of parts, each of which is an offence, the offender shall not be punished for more than one of the offences unless expressly provided; the court should not award more than the maximum for the graver offence; this embodies the rule against double punishment for the same act.
  9. Punishment of a person guilty of one of several offences (s.10; IPC 72): where it is doubtful of which offence the accused is guilty, he may be punished for the offence for which the lesser punishment is provided.
  10. Solitary confinement (ss.11-12; IPC 73-74): the court may order that a convict sentenced to rigorous imprisonment for an offence for which solitary confinement is permitted shall be kept in solitary confinement for a portion of the term, not exceeding 3 months in the whole where the term is more than one year; not exceeding one month where the term does not exceed 6 months; not exceeding 14 days at a time with intervals; subject to the Supreme Court's decisions in Sunil Batra v Delhi Administration (1978) (solitary confinement on a convict under death sentence unconstitutional pre-execution) and Shatrughan Chauhan (2014).
  11. Enhanced punishment after previous conviction (s.13; IPC 75): a person previously convicted of an offence under Chapter XII or XVII of the IPC (coins and stamps; property) punishable with imprisonment of three years or more is liable on a subsequent conviction to imprisonment for life or up to ten years. The BNS also builds repeat-offender enhancements into specific offences (for example theft, snatching, stalking and voyeurism) Verify · the exact text of s.13.
  12. Forfeiture of property and attachment: s.4(d) retains forfeiture as a punishment; however, in practice it is used in organised crime (s.111) and terror provisions, and BNSS ss.107 (attachment, forfeiture or restoration of property: proceeds of crime) provides the procedure. BNSS s.107 is a major new power allowing the court, on the police officer's application with the Superintendent of Police's approval, to attach proceeds of crime Verify.
  13. Probation and alternatives: Probation of Offenders Act, 1958 (ss.3, 4, 6) allows release on admonition or on probation for good conduct for first offenders; s.360 CrPC (BNSS s.401) similar; rule: probation is not available where the offence is punishable with death or life; for persons below 21 and women the court shall record reasons for refusing probation (s.6 of the 1958 Act; s.360(3)). Compounding of offences under BNSS s.359 (CrPC 320) lists compoundable offences; BNSS expanded the list modestly Verify.
  14. Victim compensation and fine: BNSS s.395 (CrPC 357) allows the court to order that the fine be applied for compensation; s.396 (CrPC 357A) victim compensation scheme; the Supreme Court in Ankush Shivaji Gaikwad v State of Maharashtra (2013) held that courts should consider compensation in every case; and Laxmi (2014) for acid victims.
  15. Death penalty procedure: BNSS 393(3) (CrPC 354(3)): when the conviction is for an offence punishable with death or alternatively with life imprisonment, the judgment shall state special reasons for the sentence of death. Confirmation by High Court: BNSS ss.407-409 (CrPC 366-368). Hanging by neck vs other methods: Deena v Union of India (1983) upheld hanging as a method; Attorney General v Lachma Devi (1986) public hanging unconstitutional. Execution of the pregnant woman or insane person: not permissible by law; Shatrughan Chauhan (2014) held that supervening insanity and inordinate delay in disposal of mercy petition are grounds for commutation.
  16. Capital punishment under BNS: murder (103(1)), murder by a life convict (104), mob lynching (103(2)), rape causing death or persistent vegetative state (66), rape of a girl under 12 (65(2)), gang rape of a girl under 18 (70(2)), repeat rape offenders (71), organised crime resulting in death (111(2)(a)), terrorist act resulting in death (113(2)(a)), kidnapping for ransom (140(2)), dacoity with murder (310(3)), abetment of suicide of a child (107), waging war (147), abetment of mutiny, giving false evidence leading to execution of an innocent person (s.230) etc. Death is the exclusive punishment in only a few provisions and BNS has not retained a mandatory death sentence except where the Supreme Court has not struck it down (e.g., the IPC 303 position was struck down in Mithu; BNS 104 is framed in the alternative) Verify.
  17. Juveniles: capital punishment and life imprisonment without release cannot be imposed on a child in conflict with law (Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015, s.21); the maximum institutional period is three years for heinous offences tried as an adult in a children's court; see Shilpa Mittal v State of NCT of Delhi (2020): an offence for which maximum is 7 years but no minimum is a 'serious' offence, not 'heinous'.

⚖Landmark cases

Gopal Vinayak Godse v State of Maharashtra

1961

Life imprisonment means imprisonment for the whole of the remaining life unless remitted by the Government.

Mohd. Munna v Union of India

2005

Life imprisonment is not 14 or 20 years; remission is at the Government's discretion.

Swamy Shraddananda (2) v State of Karnataka

2008

Courts may order life imprisonment without remission as a middle path between death and ordinary life.

Union of India v V. Sriharan

2015

Constitution Bench: modified sentences (fixed term or no remission) valid only if imposed by High Court or Supreme Court.

Sunil Batra v Delhi Administration

1978

Prison reforms; solitary confinement on death-sentenced prisoners unconstitutional; Art. 21 survives incarceration.

Shatrughan Chauhan v Union of India

2014

Inordinate delay in executing the death sentence and supervening mental illness are grounds for commutation.

Santosh Kumar Satishbhushan Bariyar v State of Maharashtra

2009

Death penalty requires a full assessment of the offender; sentencing hearing; cannot follow only the crime test.

Ankush Shivaji Gaikwad v State of Maharashtra

2013

Courts should consider victim compensation in every criminal case.

Deena v Union of India

1983

Hanging as a mode of execution is constitutional; Lachma Devi (1986) prohibits public hanging.

!Exam traps

  • TrapLife imprisonment = whole life (subject to remission), not 20 years; 20 years is only for fractions of terms.
  • TrapCommunity service is not a free-standing punishment for all offences; only six provisions.
  • TrapCommutation under s.5 can be done by the appropriate Government without the offender's consent; Presidential and Governor's powers under Arts. 72/161 are constitutional and independent.
