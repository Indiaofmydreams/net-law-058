Indian Penal Code / Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita

Quick MCQs

139 recall questions to close each study session. Tap an option to see the answer and the reason. There is no timer and no negative marking.

0/ 139 correct

1Under BNS, community service as punishment is provided for which offence?

Answer B. BNS 356(2) lists community service for defamation; IPC 500 lacked it.

2For purposes of calculating fractions of terms of punishment, life imprisonment is reckoned as:

Answer B. BNS s.6 (IPC s.57) treats life imprisonment as equivalent to 20 years.

3Which stage of crime is, as a rule, not punishable?

Answer B. Intention alone and, usually, preparation are not punishable; attempt and accomplishment are.

4A child aged 9 who has not attained sufficient maturity commits an act. Under BNS, which provision protects him?

Answer B. s.21 (IPC 83) covers a child between 7 and 12 years of insufficient maturity.

5The right of private defence of body extends to causing death in case of:

Answer C. BNS 38 lists apprehension of death or grievous hurt among the cases where death may be caused.

6Which of the following is NOT a defence to a charge of murder?

Answer B. Dudley & Stephens: necessity is not a defence to murder.

7Burden of proving the plea of insanity lies on:

Answer C. The accused must prove it by preponderance of probabilities.

8Common intention under s.34 IPC (BNS 3(5)) does NOT require:

Answer C. Two or more persons suffice for common intention; five or more is the requirement for an unlawful assembly.

9'They also serve who only stand and wait' is associated with which case?

Answer B. Barendra Kumar Ghosh (1925) on participation under s.34.

10An unlawful assembly under BNS requires a minimum of:

Answer C. BNS 189 (IPC 141) requires five or more persons.

11Punishment for abetment of suicide under BNS s.108 is:

Answer C. BNS 108 (IPC 306): imprisonment up to ten years and fine.

12Criminal conspiracy is dealt with in BNS under:

Answer B. BNS 61 (IPC 120A-120B). s.62 is attempt.

13Which case held that the right to life does not include the right to die?

Answer B. Gian Kaur (1996) overruled Rathinam.

14BNS section that punishes murder is:

Answer C. s.101 defines murder, s.103 prescribes punishment, s.100 defines culpable homicide, s.105 punishes culpable homicide not amounting to murder.

15The minimum punishment for dowry death is:

Answer B. BNS 80(2) (IPC 304B): not less than 7 years, which may extend to life.

16Mob lynching under BNS requires a group of at least:

Answer C. BNS 103(2): a group of five or more persons.

17The 'rarest of rare' doctrine was laid down in:

Answer B. Bachan Singh (1980); Machhi Singh gave categories.

18Which of these is NOT an exception to murder?

Answer C. Necessity is a general exception (s.19), not an exception to murder in s.101.

19Fracture of a bone or tooth is:

Answer B. Fracture or dislocation of a bone or tooth is the seventh kind of grievous hurt (BNS 116).

20Minimum imprisonment for causing hurt by acid under BNS 124(1) is:

Answer C. Not less than 10 years, may extend to life, with fine.

21Mere words, without gesture, amount to:

Answer C. Explanation to assault: mere words do not amount to assault.

22BNS section which defines rape is:

Answer A. s.63 defines rape (IPC 375); s.64 punishes it.

23Sexual intercourse by deceitful means, including false promise of marriage, is punishable under BNS:

Answer C. BNS 69 is a new offence; up to 10 years and fine.

24BNS section corresponding to IPC 498A is:

Answer B. s.85 punishes cruelty; s.86 defines cruelty.

25Dowry death is covered under BNS:

Answer A. BNS 80 (IPC 304B).

26Which judgment struck down adultery as an offence?

Answer B. Joseph Shine v UoI (2018).

27IPC 124A (sedition) is replaced in BNS by:

Answer C. BNS 152 punishes acts endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity of India.

28Punishment for BNS 152 is:

Answer B. Life imprisonment or imprisonment up to seven years, and fine.

29Kedar Nath Singh case concerns:

Answer B. Kedar Nath Singh (1962) restricted sedition to incitement of violence.

30BNS section which defines theft is:

Answer A. s.303 theft (IPC 378-379); s.304 snatching; s.308 extortion; s.309 robbery.

31Dacoity requires a minimum of how many persons?

Answer C. Five or more persons (BNS 310(2); IPC 391).

32Cheating is punished under BNS:

Answer C. s.318 (IPC 415-420); s.316 CBT; s.317 stolen property; s.319 cheating by personation.

33The key ingredient that distinguishes CBT from theft is:

Answer B. CBT requires entrustment of property or dominion over it.

34Maximum punishment for CBT by a public servant or banker is:

Answer D. BNS 316(5) (IPC 409): imprisonment for life or up to 10 years and fine.

35Punishment for defamation under BNS 356(2) includes:

Answer B. Simple imprisonment up to 2 years, or fine, or both, or community service.

36The test applied now for obscenity is:

Answer B. Aveek Sarkar (2014) adopted the community standards test.

37Constitutionality of criminal defamation was upheld in:

Answer B. Subramanian Swamy v UoI (2016).

38The BNS came into force on:

Answer C. BNS, BNSS and BSA came into force on 1 July 2024 (assent 25 December 2023).

39An offence committed on 15 May 2024, FIR registered on 10 August 2024. Substantive offence is under:

Answer B. The date of the offence decides the substantive law (Art. 20(1)).

40Article 20(1) protects against:

Answer C. Art. 20(1) prohibits conviction under ex post facto law and greater penalty than that prescribed at the time of the offence; Art. 20(2) is double jeopardy; Art. 20(3) is self-incrimination.

41Which court may impose life imprisonment without remission for a fixed term beyond 14 years?

Answer C. V. Sriharan (2015): only the High Court and Supreme Court.

42Public hanging was held unconstitutional in:

Answer B. Attorney General of India v Lachma Devi (1986).

43BNS s.4 lists how many types of punishment?

Answer C. Death, life, imprisonment, forfeiture of property, fine, community service.

44Which case recognised the identification doctrine for corporate criminal liability?

Answer B. Iridium (2011) approved the 'directing mind' approach; Standard Chartered (2005) held a company can be prosecuted.

45Rashness is best described as:

Answer B. Rashness = conscious risk-taking hoping no harm will follow; negligence = want of care.

46A guardian who deliberately starves a child to death is liable because of:

Answer B. Illegal omission arises from legal duty to provide food.

47Which case struck down the mandatory death sentence under IPC 303?

Answer B. Mithu v State of Punjab (1983).

48Review petitions against death sentence are heard by a Bench of at least:

Answer C. Mohd. Arif (2014).

49Special reasons for awarding death sentence are required under:

Answer A. BNSS 393(3) (CrPC 354(3)).

50The test 'sufficient in the ordinary course of nature to cause death' is found in:

Answer B. BNS 101 third clause (IPC 300 thirdly); Virsa Singh.

51Exception 5 to murder requires the deceased to be above:

Answer C. Consent exception: person above 18 years.

52Passive euthanasia was recognised in India in:

Answer B. Aruna Shanbaug (2011), refined in Common Cause (2018).

53Minimum imprisonment for giving or taking dowry under the Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961 is:

Answer C. Section 3: not less than five years and fine not less than Rs 15,000 or the value of dowry, whichever is more.

54The presumption as to dowry death is found in:

Answer B. BSA s.118 (Evidence Act 113B); BSA s.117 (113A) deals with abetment of suicide by a married woman.

55Which of the following is NOT an offence under the BNS?

Answer B. Adultery (IPC 497) was struck down in Joseph Shine and omitted.

56Stridhan belongs to:

Answer C. Pratibha Rani v Suraj Kumar (1985).

57A 'child' under POCSO means a person below:

Answer C. POCSO s.2(1)(d): below eighteen years.

58The two-finger test on a rape victim was held unconstitutional in:

Answer A. Lillu @ Rajesh (2013) violated dignity and privacy.

59Is skin-to-skin contact necessary for sexual assault under POCSO s.7?

Answer B. Attorney General v Satish (2021) reversed the Bombay High Court.

60Failure to report an offence under POCSO is punishable with up to:

Answer C. Section 21(1): up to six months or fine or both.

61A servant who has been entrusted with money by his master and misappropriates it commits:

Answer B. Entrustment with property or dominion over it is the CBT element (s.316).

62Taking away a laptop from the possession of its owner dishonestly commits:

Answer B. Dishonestly moving movable property out of possession without consent is theft.

63Section 335 of BNS defines:

Answer B. BNS 335 (IPC 464) making a false document; s.336 forgery; s.344 falsification of accounts.

64A prosecution for false evidence in a court proceeding is initiated on a complaint by:

Answer C. BNSS s.215(1)(b) (CrPC 195(1)(b)): complaint in writing by that court or a superior court.

65Lily Thomas v Union of India (2013) concerned:

Answer B. Lily Thomas (2013) on s.8(4) RPA; Lily Thomas (2000) was on bigamy.

66Giving a bribe to a voter is punishable under:

Answer A. Bribery (IPC 171B) corresponds to BNS 170 Verify · numbering.

67The BNS section that prescribes the punishment for murder is:

Answer C. BNS 101 defines murder; BNS 103 prescribes punishment (IPC 300 and 302).

68IPC 376 (punishment for rape) corresponds to BNS:

Answer B. s.63 defines rape; s.64 punishes it.

69IPC 420 (cheating and dishonestly inducing delivery of property) corresponds to BNS:

Answer C. BNS 318(4): cheating and dishonestly inducing delivery of property; up to 7 years and fine.

70IPC 406 (punishment for criminal breach of trust) corresponds to BNS:

Answer C. BNS 316(2): up to 5 years or fine or both.

71IPC 307 (attempt to murder) corresponds to BNS:

Answer B. BNS 109; s.110 is attempt to commit culpable homicide; s.111 organised crime.

72Stalking is punished under BNS:

Answer C. BNS 78 (IPC 354D). s.75 sexual harassment; s.77 voyeurism; s.79 insulting modesty.

73Common intention under BNS is found in:

Answer A. IPC 34 is BNS 3(5); BNS 34 is a private defence section.

74Liability of every member of an unlawful assembly for an offence committed in prosecution of the common object is under BNS:

Answer B. BNS 190 (IPC 149); s.189 is unlawful assembly; s.191 rioting.

75Criminal conspiracy under BNS is:

Answer C. BNS 61 (IPC 120A and 120B).

76Defamation is punishable under BNS:

Answer C. BNS 356 (IPC 499, 500).

77Criminal intimidation is dealt with in BNS:

Answer A. BNS 351 (IPC 503 and 506).

78Which BNS section covers 'snatching'?

Answer C. BNS 304 (new); s.303 theft.

79Acts endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity of India are punishable under BNS:

Answer C. BNS 152 replaced IPC 124A in substance.

80Cruelty by husband or his relatives (IPC 498A) is BNS:

Answer C. BNS 85 punishes; s.86 defines cruelty.

81Abetment of suicide is punishable under BNS:

Answer C. BNS 108 (IPC 306); s.107 is abetment of suicide of a child or person of unsound mind.

82A child below which age enjoys absolute immunity from criminal liability under BNS?

Answer B. BNS 20 (IPC 82): below 7 years.

83A child between 7 and 12 years is protected if he:

Answer B. BNS 21 (IPC 83).

84Attempt to commit an offence punishable with imprisonment (no specific attempt provision) is punishable with:

Answer B. BNS 62 (IPC 511): up to one-half of the longest term, or the prescribed fine, or both.

85Abetment of an offence punishable with imprisonment, if the offence is not committed and no express provision exists, is punishable with up to:

Answer B. BNS 56 (IPC 116): one-fourth of the longest term.

86A, intending to kill B, fires at B but the bullet kills C. A is guilty of:

Answer B. BNS 102 (IPC 301): culpable homicide by causing death of a person other than the one intended; the same character as if B had been killed.

87A mob of six persons kills a person on the ground of his religion. The applicable provision is:

Answer B. Mob lynching: five or more persons, on listed grounds (BNS 103(2)).

88Which is NOT an exception to murder under BNS 101?

Answer C. Accident is a general exception (s.18), not an exception to murder.

89'Sufficient in the ordinary course of nature to cause death' was explained in:

Answer A. Virsa Singh v State of Punjab (1958).

90The 'rarest of rare' doctrine is associated with:

Answer B. Bachan Singh v State of Punjab (1980).

91The test of gross negligence for criminal liability of doctors was laid down in:

Answer A. Jacob Mathew (2005).

92Who bears the burden of proving insanity as a defence?

Answer B. The accused, on a preponderance of probabilities (Dahyabhai Thakkar).

93Voluntary intoxication is:

Answer B. BNS 24 (IPC 86): knowledge imputed; intention may be considered (Basdev).

94Right of private defence does NOT extend to:

Answer C. BNS 37 (IPC 99): no right against acts of a public servant in good faith, with exceptions.

95A, compelled by a threat of instant death, joins a robbery. He may claim protection under:

Answer B. BNS 32 (IPC 94): compulsion, except murder and offences against the State punishable with death.

96Which of the following requires 'entrustment'?

Answer C. CBT requires entrustment of property or dominion over it.

97Theft of electricity is governed principally by:

Answer B. Electricity Act s.135, a special law (Avtar Singh).

98Robbery is:

Answer C. BNS 309 (IPC 390).

99Dacoity requires at least:

Answer C. Five or more conjointly committing or attempting robbery.

100Maximum punishment for theft under BNS 303(2) is:

Answer B. Up to 3 years or fine or both (with community service possible for petty first offence).

101Cheating requires that the dishonest intention exist:

Answer A. Hridaya Ranjan Prasad Verma (2000).

102A finds a purse, later decides to keep it and spends the money. The offence is:

Answer B. Innocent possession turned dishonest: BNS 314 (IPC 403).

103A pickpocket's attempt where the pocket is empty is:

Answer B. Impossibility is no defence to attempt (illustration to IPC 511 / BNS 62).

104Criminal trespass is an offence against:

Answer B. Entry on property in the possession of another with the requisite intent.

105House-breaking is:

Answer B. BNS 330-331 region (IPC 445).

106The number of exceptions to defamation under BNS 356 (IPC 499) is:

Answer C. Ten exceptions.

107Maximum simple imprisonment for defamation under BNS 356(2) is:

Answer C. Two years, or fine, or both, or community service.

108In which case was the constitutionality of criminal defamation upheld?

Answer B. Subramanian Swamy v UoI (2016).

109The 'community standards' test for obscenity was adopted in:

Answer B. Aveek Sarkar v State of WB (2014).

110Grievous hurt includes any hurt that causes severe bodily pain or inability to follow ordinary pursuits for:

Answer C. The twenty-day test in the eighth kind.

111Fracture or dislocation of a bone or tooth is:

Answer B. Seventh kind of grievous hurt.

112The minimum sentence for acid attack under BNS 124(1) is:

Answer C. 10 years to life with fine.

113Mere words, without any gesture or preparation, amount to:

Answer C. Explanation to the definition of assault.

114Dowry death requires death within how many years of marriage?

Answer C. Seven years.

115Stalking: punishment on second or subsequent conviction under BNS 78 may extend to:

Answer B. First conviction up to 3 years; subsequent up to 5 years.

116Minimum punishment for gang rape of an adult woman under BNS 70(1) is:

Answer C. 20 years, extendable to life (remainder of natural life), with fine to the victim.

117BNS 69 punishes:

Answer B. New offence: up to 10 years and fine.

118Which case struck down the 'two-finger test'?

Answer A. Lillu @ Rajesh v State of Haryana (2013).

119Adultery as an offence was struck down in:

Answer B. Joseph Shine v UoI (2018).

120Consensual same-sex acts between adults were decriminalised in:

Answer C. Navtej Singh Johar v UoI (2018).

121Which Act defines a 'child' as a person below 18 and is gender neutral for sexual offences against children?

Answer A. POCSO Act, 2012.

122'Skin-to-skin contact' is NOT required for sexual assault under POCSO as held in:

Answer A. Attorney General for India v Satish (2021).

123Trafficking of a minor (single) under BNS 143 is punishable with:

Answer D. Trafficking of a minor: not less than 10 years, may extend to life.

124Which of the following was NOT retained as a standalone offence in BNS?

Answer B. Adultery (IPC 497) omitted.

125Attempt to commit suicide (IPC 309) under BNS:

Answer B. BNS 226: attempt to commit suicide to compel or restrain exercise of lawful power by a public servant.

126Community service as a punishment appears in how many BNS provisions?

Answer C. ss.202, 209, 226, 303(2), 355 and 356(2).

127Life imprisonment, for the purpose of fractions of terms, is reckoned as:

Answer B. BNS 6 (IPC 57).

128Imprisonment for life means:

Answer C. Gopal Vinayak Godse; Mohd. Munna.

129Mandatory death sentence under IPC 303 was struck down in:

Answer A. Mithu (1983).

130Article 20(1) of the Constitution prohibits:

Answer C. Art. 20(1).

131A murder committed on 10 June 2024 will be tried under:

Answer B. The date of the offence governs (Art. 20(1)).

132Sedition under IPC 124A was kept in abeyance by the Supreme Court in:

Answer B. S.G. Vombatkere (2022).

133'They also serve who only stand and wait' is linked to:

Answer B. Barendra Kumar Ghosh (1925) on common intention.

134Gian Kaur v State of Punjab (1996) held that:

Answer B. Overruled P. Rathinam.

135Passive euthanasia was permitted by the Supreme Court in:

Answer B. Aruna Shanbaug (2011); Common Cause (2018).

136Hit-and-run (failure to report) under BNS 106(2) carries up to:

Answer D. Up to 10 years and fine.

137Rash driving or riding on a public way is punishable under BNS:

Answer B. BNS 281 (IPC 279).

138A statement made in good faith by a person to the authorities accusing another of an offence is covered by which exception to defamation?

Answer B. Eighth exception: accusation preferred in good faith to an authorised person.

139Which of the following is generally treated as a continuing offence?

Answer B. Abduction continues as long as the person is detained; kidnapping from lawful guardianship is complete once the person is taken or enticed away.
