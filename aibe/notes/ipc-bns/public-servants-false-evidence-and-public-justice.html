iOverview

Chapter structure in BNS: offences by or relating to public servants (ss.198-205

★Must know

  1. Chapter structure in BNS: offences by or relating to public servants (ss.198-205: including public servant unlawfully engaging in trade s.202 and unlawfully buying or bidding for property s.203, personating a public servant s.204, wearing garb or carrying a token used by a public servant with fraudulent intent s.205); contempt of lawful authority of public servants (ss.206-226); false evidence and offences against public justice (ss.227-269) Verify · section ranges.
  2. Absconding to avoid service of summons or other proceeding (s.206; IPC 172): up to 1 month simple imprisonment or fine up to Rs 5,000 or both (if the summons relates to attendance in person or by agent: up to 6 months or fine up to Rs 10,000) Verify. Non-attendance in obedience to an order from a public servant (s.209; IPC 174): up to 1 month or fine up to Rs 1,000 or both, or community service; if the order requires attendance in person or by agent in a court: up to 6 months or fine up to Rs 5,000. Omission to produce a document (s.210), omission to give notice or information (s.211), furnishing false information (s.212), refusing oath (s.213), refusing to answer questions (s.214), refusing to sign statements (s.215), false statement on oath to a public servant (s.216), false information to a public servant (s.217), resistance to the taking of property by lawful authority (s.218), obstructing sale of property offered by lawful authority (s.219), illegal purchase or bid (s.220), obstructing a public servant in discharge of public functions (s.221), omission to assist a public servant (s.222), disobedience to an order duly promulgated by a public servant (s.223), threat of injury to a public servant (s.224), threat of injury to induce a person to refrain from applying for protection to a public servant (s.225), attempt to commit suicide to compel or restrain exercise of lawful power (s.226) Verify · numbering.
  3. Disobedience to order duly promulgated (s.223; IPC 188): the offence requires knowledge of the order and that the disobedience causes or tends to cause obstruction, annoyance or injury to persons lawfully employed, danger to human life, health or safety, or a riot or affray. Prosecution requires a complaint in writing by the public servant concerned (BNSS s.215(1)(a); CrPC 195(1)(a)). Used frequently for Section 144-type orders (BNSS s.163): Anuradha Bhasin v Union of India (2020): orders under s.144 must be proportionate, reasoned and subject to judicial review; indefinite internet shutdown violates Arts. 19(1)(a) and 19(1)(g); Ramlila Maidan Incident, In Re (2012): the police power under s.144 must be exercised in good faith and the right to assemble is subject to reasonable restrictions.
  4. False evidence: ingredients - (i) a person is legally bound by an oath or by an express provision of law to state the truth, or is bound by law to make a declaration upon any subject; (ii) he makes a statement; (iii) the statement is false; (iv) he knows or believes it to be false or does not believe it to be true. The statement may be oral or in writing, and may be made to a court, a public servant or any other authority. Perjury: false evidence in judicial proceedings: up to 7 years and fine (s.229(1)); in other cases up to 3 years and fine. Fabricating false evidence: causing any circumstance to exist or making any false entry in a book, record or electronic record or making any document or electronic record containing a false statement, intending that it may appear in evidence in a judicial proceeding, to lead to a wrong opinion: s.228; punishment s.230 (same scale). Giving or fabricating false evidence with intent to procure a conviction of a capital offence: life or rigorous imprisonment up to 10 years and fine (s.230(1)); if an innocent person is executed in consequence: death or the same (s.230(2)); intent to procure conviction for an offence punishable with life or 7 years: up to 7 years and fine.
  5. Perjury procedure: A court can take cognizance of s.229 only on the written complaint of that court (BNSS s.215(1)(b)(i); CrPC 195(1)(b)(i)); the court must record a finding that it is expedient in the interest of justice to initiate the prosecution (BNSS s.379; CrPC 340), and the inquiry is preliminary: Pritish v State of Maharashtra (2002): the court must form a prima facie opinion; Iqbal Singh Marwah v Meenakshi Marwah (2005): s.195(1)(b)(ii) applies only to documents forged after they are produced in court; Sachida Nand Singh v State of Bihar (1998): the bar does not apply to forgery before the document is filed.
  6. Using evidence known to be false as true (s.231), issuing or signing a false certificate (s.232), using a certificate known to be false (s.233-234), false statement in any declaration which is by law receivable as evidence (s.236), causing disappearance of evidence of offence, or giving false information to screen offender (s.238), intentional omission to give information of offence by a person legally bound to inform (s.239), giving false information respecting an offence committed (s.240), destruction of a document or electronic record to prevent its production as evidence (s.241), false personation for the purpose of an act or proceeding in a suit (s.242), fraudulent removal or concealment of property to prevent its seizure (s.243), fraudulent claim to property (s.244) Verify.
  7. Harbouring an offender (ss.249-250; IPC 212-216A): harbouring an offender is punishable, with an exception for a spouse harbouring the other spouse; punishment depends on the gravity of the offence for which the offender is harboured (up to 5 years where the offence is punishable with death; up to 3 years where punishable with life imprisonment or imprisonment up to 10 years; one-fourth of the longest term in other cases). Taking a gift to screen an offender from punishment is a separate offence (IPC 213) Verify · BNS section numbers.
  8. Offences relating to elections (BNS ss.169-177; IPC 171A-171I) Verify: bribery (s.170; IPC 171B): giving or accepting a gratification to induce a person to exercise or refrain from exercising an electoral right: up to 1 year or fine or both; treating is bribery by food, drink or entertainment; undue influence (s.171; IPC 171C): voluntary interference with the free exercise of any electoral right: up to 1 year or fine or both; personation at elections (s.172; IPC 171D): up to 1 year; false statement in connection with election (s.174; IPC 171G): fine only; illegal payments in connection with an election (s.175; IPC 171H): fine up to Rs 500; failure to keep election accounts (s.176; IPC 171I): fine up to Rs 500. The Representation of the People Act, 1951 (ss.123, 125-136) contains corrupt practices and electoral offences, with electoral disqualification under ss.8-8A. The Supreme Court in Abhiram Singh v C.D. Commachen (2017) (seven-judge Bench) interpreted the corrupt practice of seeking votes in the name of religion, race, caste, community or language under s.123(3) RPA to include the religion of the candidate, the voter and the agent; in Union of India v Association for Democratic Reforms (2002) and PUCL v UoI (2003), the voters' right to know the candidates' criminal and financial antecedents is part of Art. 19(1)(a); Lily Thomas v UoI (2013) struck down s.8(4) of the RPA (immediate disqualification on conviction with 2 years or more); Public Interest Foundation v UoI (2019): directions on publishing criminal antecedents; and the Electoral Bonds Scheme was struck down in Association for Democratic Reforms v UoI (2024) Verify.

⚖Landmark cases

Anuradha Bhasin v Union of India

2020

Restrictions under s.144 must be proportional, reasoned and reviewable; internet access linked to Arts. 19(1)(a) and 19(1)(g).

Iqbal Singh Marwah v Meenakshi Marwah

2005

Bar on cognizance under s.195(1)(b)(ii) CrPC applies only to documents forged after production in court.

Pritish v State of Maharashtra

2002

Court must form a prima facie opinion that it is expedient in the interest of justice to initiate perjury proceedings.

Lily Thomas v Union of India

2013

Section 8(4) RPA struck down; a legislator convicted with two years or more is disqualified immediately.

Abhiram Singh v C.D. Commachen

2017

Appeal for votes on the ground of religion, race, caste, community or language of anyone is a corrupt practice.

Union of India v Association for Democratic Reforms

2002

Voters have the right to know about candidates' antecedents.

!Exam traps

  • TrapPerjury prosecution generally requires the court's complaint; private complaint is barred under the Code of Criminal Procedure/BNSS.
  • TrapGiving false evidence requires that the person be legally bound to state the truth.
  • TrapHarbouring by a spouse is excepted.
