iOverview

Three core statutes. EPA 1986 is an umbrella legislation made under Art 253 after the Stockholm Conference and the Bhopal gas tragedy; the Water and Air Acts create Pollution Control Boards.

★Must know

  1. EPA 1986: Preamble refers to the UN Conference on the Human Environment, Stockholm 1972. Defines 'environment' (water, air and land and their inter-relationship with humans, other living creatures, plants, micro-organism and property), 'environmental pollutant' and 'hazardous substance'.
  2. EPA S.3: Central Government has power to take all measures necessary for protecting and improving the quality of the environment and preventing, controlling and abating pollution; S.5: direction (including closure, prohibition or regulation of any industry, stoppage of electricity or water); S.6: rules to regulate environmental pollution; S.7: no person carrying on an industry shall emit pollutants in excess of prescribed standards; S.8: handling of hazardous substances only per procedural safeguards; S.9: duty to report accidents; S.10: entry and inspection powers.
  3. EPA penalty (S.15): Following the Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2023, imprisonment for contraventions was replaced by monetary penalties adjudicated by an adjudicating officer (penalty of not less than Rs 10,000 which may extend to Rs 15 lakh) - verify against the current text. Cognizance of offences requires a complaint by the Central Government or authorised officer, or a person who has given 60 days' notice (S.19).
  4. Water Act 1974 (enacted under Art 252 on resolutions of States): Central and State Pollution Control Boards (CPCB/SPCBs). 'Pollution' defined; 'stream' includes river, water course, inland water, subterranean waters, sea or tidal waters. S.24: prohibition on the use of stream or well for disposal of polluting matter; S.25: consent of the SPCB required to establish an industry/treatment system that discharges sewage or trade effluent; S.33A: power of Boards to issue directions including closure; S.17: functions of SPCB. The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Amendment Act, 2024 decriminalised many offences (penalties instead of imprisonment, adjudicating officer) and empowered the Centre to prescribe guidelines for the nomination of SPCB Chairpersons and exempt specified categories of industrial plants from consent.
  5. Water Cess Act 1977 levies cess on water consumed by industries for funding of Boards.
  6. Air Act 1981: Enacted after the Stockholm Conference; 'air pollutant' includes noise (1987 amendment). S.19: State Government may declare air pollution control areas; S.20: standards for vehicle emissions; S.21: consent of the SPCB required to establish any industrial plant in an air pollution control area; S.22: no emission beyond standards; S.22A: Board's power to approach the court for restraint; S.31A: directions including closure. The Act was also amended through Jan Vishwas 2023 to decriminalise.
  7. EIA Notification 2006: projects categorised A (Central, MoEFCC on the advice of Expert Appraisal Committee) and B (State, SEIAA with SEAC); steps - screening, scoping, public consultation (public hearing and written responses), appraisal and grant/rejection of Environmental Clearance (EC). EIA is made under EPA S.3 and Rule 5(3) of the EP Rules.
  8. CRZ Notification 2019: regulates activities in Coastal Regulation Zones; CRZ I (ecologically sensitive), CRZ II (urban developed), CRZ III (rural), CRZ IV (water area).
  9. Rules made under EPA: Hazardous and Other Wastes Rules 2016; Bio-medical Waste Management Rules 2016; Plastic Waste Management Rules 2016 (amended 2021 - identified single-use plastics banned from 1 July 2022); E-Waste (Management) Rules 2022; Battery Waste Management Rules 2022; Solid Waste Management Rules 2016; Construction and Demolition Waste Rules 2016; Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules 2000; Ozone Depleting Substances Rules 2000.

⚖Landmark cases

Bhopal Gas Leak Disaster (Process) Act, 1985; Union Carbide Corporation v. Union of India

1989

Settlement for USD 470 million; Act enacted to enable the Government to represent victims.

M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Delhi vehicular pollution)

1998 onwards

Directions to convert public transport to CNG; continuing mandamus on air quality.

Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action v. Union of India

1996

Polluting industries to bear remediation costs; polluter pays.

✎Memory aids

  • Water: 24 Prohibits, 25 Permits (consent). Air: 19 Declares area, 21 Consent.

!Exam traps

  • TrapEPA is made under Art 253; the Water Act under Art 252 (resolutions by States) - Air Act also under Art 253 post Stockholm.
  • Trap'Air pollutant' includes noise by the 1987 amendment to the Air Act.
  • TrapConsent of the SPCB (S.25 Water Act, S.21 Air Act) is required to establish or operate; not mere intimation.
  • TrapEIA notification is a delegated legislation under EPA - not a statute by itself.

?Likely questions

  1. Which Article empowers Parliament to enact EPA 1986?
  2. Which Act includes noise in 'air pollutant'?
  3. Section giving SPCB consent power under the Water Act (S.25).
  4. Who grants EC for Category B projects? (SEIAA)
