1

Constitutional Foundations and Landmark Cases

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Key provisions

Art 21Right to clean environment; right against adverse effects of climate change (with Art 14).
Art 48ADPSP - protect and improve the environment; safeguard forests and wildlife.
Art 51A(g)Fundamental duty - protect and improve the natural environment, have compassion for living creatures.
Art 253Legislation for implementing international agreements; basis for EPA 1986.
Entries 17A, 17B, Concurrent ListForests; protection of wild animals and birds - moved from State List by 42nd Amendment.
Art 243G, 11th Schedule; 12th SchedulePanchayats and municipalities - local environmental duties (soil conservation, water management, forestry, sanitation, solid waste).

Memory aids

  • VOKB: Vellore (principles), Oleum (absolute liability), Kamal Nath (public trust), Bichhri (polluter pays).
  • 42nd Amendment = 48A + 51A(g) + forests to the Concurrent List.

Exam traps

  • TrapAbsolute liability (Oleum Gas, 1987) is not subject to the Rylands v. Fletcher exceptions; strict liability (Rylands v. Fletcher) is.
  • TrapPublic trust doctrine -> Kamal Nath; Precautionary + polluter pays + sustainable development -> Vellore.
  • Trap42nd Amendment inserted Arts 48A and 51A(g) - not the 44th or 86th.
  • TrapDoon Valley (1985) is the first major ecological case.
2

Principles of Environmental Law

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Memory aids

  • P-P-P: Precaution (before harm), Polluter pays (after harm), Public trust (State as trustee).

Exam traps

  • TrapPrecautionary principle is NOT the same as polluter pays - one is about preventing harm, the other about cost allocation.
  • TrapPublic trust doctrine is not about liability, it is about State ownership/trusteeship of resources.
  • TrapIntergenerational equity vs sustainable development - the former is the ethical basis, the latter is the policy outcome.
3

National Green Tribunal Act, 2010

Very high yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai v. Ankita Sinha 2021 NGT possesses suo motu powers to take cognizance of environmental matters.
  • Vellore Citizens' Welfare Forum 1996 Principles that S.20 NGT Act now statutorily enjoins NGT to apply.

Memory aids

  • 7 Acts in Schedule I: W-W-F-A-E-P-B (Water, Water Cess, Forest, Air, EPA, PLI, Biodiversity).

Exam traps

  • TrapThe Wildlife (Protection) Act is NOT in Schedule I - NGT has no original jurisdiction under it.
  • TrapAppeal from NGT lies to the Supreme Court within 90 days - not to the High Court.
  • TrapLimitation is six months (+ 60 days), not three years.
  • TrapNGT is not bound by the CPC and the Evidence Act but follows natural justice.
4

Environment (Protection) Act, 1986; Water Act, 1974; Air Act, 1981

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • Bhopal Gas Leak Disaster (Process) Act, 1985; Union Carbide Corporation v. Union of India 1989 Settlement for USD 470 million; Act enacted to enable the Government to represent victims.
  • M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Delhi vehicular pollution) 1998 onwards Directions to convert public transport to CNG; continuing mandamus on air quality.
  • Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action v. Union of India 1996 Polluting industries to bear remediation costs; polluter pays.

Memory aids

  • Water: 24 Prohibits, 25 Permits (consent). Air: 19 Declares area, 21 Consent.

Exam traps

  • TrapEPA is made under Art 253; the Water Act under Art 252 (resolutions by States) - Air Act also under Art 253 post Stockholm.
  • Trap'Air pollutant' includes noise by the 1987 amendment to the Air Act.
  • TrapConsent of the SPCB (S.25 Water Act, S.21 Air Act) is required to establish or operate; not mere intimation.
  • TrapEIA notification is a delegated legislation under EPA - not a statute by itself.
5

Forests, Wildlife, Biodiversity and Tribal Rights

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Cases in one line

  • T.N. Godavarman Thirumulpad v. Union of India 1996 onwards Broad dictionary meaning of 'forest'; continuing mandamus; all felling banned without Central approval.
  • Orissa Mining Corporation v. Ministry of Environment and Forests (Niyamgiri) 2013 Gram Sabhas must decide on religious/cultural claims affected by mining diversion.
  • Centre for Environmental Law, WWF-I v. Union of India 2013 Protection of Asiatic lions; translocation (Kuno) approved.
  • M.K. Ranjitsinh v. Union of India 2024 Right against adverse effects of climate change; balance of conservation of Great Indian Bustard with renewable energy.

Memory aids

  • WPA 2022: 6 -> 4 schedules; vermin out; CITES in.

Exam traps

  • TrapWildlife Act, Forest Rights Act and Indian Forest Act 1927 are outside NGT's Schedule I.
  • TrapForest (Conservation) Act was renamed (2023) as Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam.
  • TrapAfter the 2022 amendment the Wildlife Act has four schedules, not six.
  • TrapNBA is headquartered at Chennai.
6

International Environmental Law and Climate Framework

High yieldRead the full chapter →

Memory aids

  • 72-92-97-15: Stockholm, Rio, Kyoto, Paris.
  • BRMS: Basel (wastes), Rotterdam (PIC), Montreal (ozone), Stockholm 2001 (POPs).

Exam traps

  • TrapKyoto = binding targets on developed countries; Paris = nationally determined contributions for all.
  • TrapMontreal Protocol is about ozone-depleting substances; Kigali Amendment is about HFCs.
  • TrapBasel = hazardous wastes; Rotterdam = hazardous chemicals trade (PIC); Stockholm Convention = POPs; Minamata = mercury.
  • TrapStockholm 1972 (human environment) is different from the Stockholm Convention 2001 (POPs).
