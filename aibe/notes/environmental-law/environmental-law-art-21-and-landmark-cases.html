iOverview

The Supreme Court has read environmental protection into Art 21 and developed a body of principles by judicial creativity.

★Must know

  1. Rural Litigation and Entitlement Kendra v. State of U.P. (Doon Valley, 1985): first case where the Court ordered closure of mining operations because of ecological damage; balance between development and environment.
  2. Subhash Kumar v. State of Bihar (1991): right to life includes the right to pollution-free water and air; PIL is not for personal vendetta.
  3. M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Oleum Gas Leak, 1987): ABSOLUTE LIABILITY - enterprise engaged in hazardous or inherently dangerous activity is strictly and absolutely liable, with no exceptions of the Rylands v. Fletcher rule; deep-pocket theory (compensation correlated to the magnitude and capacity of the enterprise).
  4. Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action v. Union of India (Bichhri, 1996): polluter pays - the polluter must bear the cost of remedial action and compensation; burden of proof on the polluter.
  5. Vellore Citizens' Welfare Forum v. Union of India (1996): sustainable development, precautionary principle and polluter pays are part of the environmental law of the land through Arts 21, 47, 48A, 51A(g) and customary international law; burden of proof on the developer/industry (Rio Principle 15).
  6. A.P. Pollution Control Board v. M.V. Nayudu (1999): precautionary principle and the reversal of the burden of proof; need for expert technical bodies; Court's reliance on scientific uncertainty.
  7. M.C. Mehta v. Kamal Nath (1997): PUBLIC TRUST doctrine - State is trustee of natural resources (air, sea, water, forests) for the public; cannot transfer to private ownership for commercial use (Span Motel case).
  8. T.N. Godavarman Thirumulpad v. Union of India (1996-continuing): 'forest' given the dictionary meaning irrespective of classification/ownership; continuing mandamus; CAMPA and compensatory afforestation orders.
  9. M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Taj Trapezium case, 1996-97): Taj protection; industrial shifting; use of CNG; the Court appointed expert bodies.
  10. M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Ganga pollution/tanneries, 1987): ordered tanneries to set up effluent treatment plants; Municipal bodies' duty to treat sewage.
  11. Narmada Bachao Andolan v. Union of India (2000): development projects - rehabilitation; balance of ecological costs with development.
  12. Re: Noise Pollution (2005): right to silence/peace is part of Art 21; time restrictions on loudspeakers (10 p.m. to 6 a.m.) under the Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000.
  13. Animal Welfare Board of India v. A. Nagaraja (2014): Art 21 'life' includes the dignity and well-being of animals; Jallikattu ban. In 2023 a Constitution Bench upheld the Tamil Nadu amendment allowing Jallikattu (Animal Welfare Board v. Union of India).
  14. Orissa Mining Corporation v. MoEF (Niyamgiri, 2013): Gram Sabhas have the final say on religious/cultural rights under the Forest Rights Act in mining diversion.
  15. Mantri Techzone v. Forum for Peoples' Collective Efforts (2019): 'no-development' zones; precautionary principle; polluter pays in construction.
  16. M.K. Ranjitsinh v. Union of India (21 March 2024): Great Indian Bustard case - right to a healthy environment and the right against the adverse effects of climate change are recognised under Arts 14 and 21; balance of renewable energy transition with protection of endangered species.
  17. Bhopal: Union Carbide Corporation v. Union of India (1989) settlement of USD 470 million; the Court dismissed the Centre's curative petition for additional compensation in March 2023.

§Key provisions

ProvisionWhat it says
Art 21Right to clean environment; right against adverse effects of climate change (with Art 14).
Art 48ADPSP - protect and improve the environment; safeguard forests and wildlife.
Art 51A(g)Fundamental duty - protect and improve the natural environment, have compassion for living creatures.
Art 253Legislation for implementing international agreements; basis for EPA 1986.
Entries 17A, 17B, Concurrent ListForests; protection of wild animals and birds - moved from State List by 42nd Amendment.
Art 243G, 11th Schedule; 12th SchedulePanchayats and municipalities - local environmental duties (soil conservation, water management, forestry, sanitation, solid waste).

✎Memory aids

  • VOKB: Vellore (principles), Oleum (absolute liability), Kamal Nath (public trust), Bichhri (polluter pays).
  • 42nd Amendment = 48A + 51A(g) + forests to the Concurrent List.

!Exam traps

  • TrapAbsolute liability (Oleum Gas, 1987) is not subject to the Rylands v. Fletcher exceptions; strict liability (Rylands v. Fletcher) is.
  • TrapPublic trust doctrine -> Kamal Nath; Precautionary + polluter pays + sustainable development -> Vellore.
  • Trap42nd Amendment inserted Arts 48A and 51A(g) - not the 44th or 86th.
  • TrapDoon Valley (1985) is the first major ecological case.

?Likely questions

  2. Public trust doctrine - which case?
  3. Under which Article is the right to a clean environment protected?
  4. Which amendment inserted Art 48A and Art 51A(g)?
  5. Which case recognises a right against the adverse effects of climate change?
