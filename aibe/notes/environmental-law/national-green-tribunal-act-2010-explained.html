National Green Tribunal Act, 2010

iOverview

NGT is a specialised body (replacing the National Environment Tribunal Act 1995 and National Environment Appellate Authority Act 1997) with technical and judicial members and jurisdiction over 'substantial questions relating to environment' arising under seven laws.

★Must know

  1. Established under Art 21 spirit and Law Commission recommendation; Act received assent in 2010; NGT is the third country (after Australia and New Zealand) to set up such a body.
  2. Principal Bench at New Delhi; regional benches at Bhopal, Pune, Kolkata and Chennai. Chairperson must be a sitting/retired Supreme Court Judge or Chief Justice of a High Court; Judicial Members and Expert Members (minimum 10, maximum 20 full-time members each).
  3. Original jurisdiction (S.14): all civil cases involving a substantial question relating to environment (including enforcement of any legal right relating to environment) arising out of the implementation of the enactments in Schedule I. Limitation: six months from the date the cause of action first arose, extendable by up to 60 days (S.14(3)).
  4. Schedule I laws: Water Act 1974; Water Cess Act 1977; Forest (Conservation) Act 1980; Air Act 1981; Environment (Protection) Act 1986; Public Liability Insurance Act 1991; Biological Diversity Act 2002. NOT included: Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972, Indian Forest Act 1927, Forest Rights Act 2006.
  5. S.15: relief, compensation and restitution for victims of pollution and environmental damage, including restoration of property and environment.
  6. S.16: appeals against orders under Water Act, Air Act, EPA, EIA clearances etc. (e.g. EC orders) lie to the NGT.
  7. S.17: no-fault liability for death, injury or property damage from an accident involving hazardous substances (S.17(3) read with the Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991). Strict liability on the 'person responsible'.
  8. S.18(3): application or appeal shall be dealt with as expeditiously as possible and disposed of within six months of filing.
  9. S.19: NGT is not bound by the CPC or the Evidence Act, but is guided by principles of natural justice; has the powers of a civil court for specified matters; may review its own decision.
  10. S.20: NGT must apply the principles of sustainable development, the precautionary principle and the polluter pays principle.
  11. S.22: appeal to the Supreme Court within 90 days from the communication of award/decision/order.
  12. Suo motu powers: NGT may take suo motu cognizance (Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai v. Ankita Sinha, 2021). High Courts retain writ jurisdiction under Arts 226/227 (L. Chandra Kumar principle; NGT orders are subject to HC judicial review under Art 226 in appropriate cases, though the statutory appeal lies to the Supreme Court).
  13. NGT can award compensation as 'environmental compensation' on the basis of the polluter pays principle and it can direct restoration.

⚖Landmark cases

Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai v. Ankita Sinha

2021

NGT possesses suo motu powers to take cognizance of environmental matters.

Vellore Citizens' Welfare Forum

1996

Principles that S.20 NGT Act now statutorily enjoins NGT to apply.

✎Memory aids

  • 7 Acts in Schedule I: W-W-F-A-E-P-B (Water, Water Cess, Forest, Air, EPA, PLI, Biodiversity).

!Exam traps

  • TrapThe Wildlife (Protection) Act is NOT in Schedule I - NGT has no original jurisdiction under it.
  • TrapAppeal from NGT lies to the Supreme Court within 90 days - not to the High Court.
  • TrapLimitation is six months (+ 60 days), not three years.
  • TrapNGT is not bound by the CPC and the Evidence Act but follows natural justice.
