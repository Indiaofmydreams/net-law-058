Principles of Environmental Law

iOverview

These principles are recited in virtually every environmental judgment and NGT order. Learn the definition, the source and the leading case.

★Must know

  1. Sustainable development: development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs (Brundtland Report, 1987; Rio Principles 3-4). Vellore and Narmada Bachao Andolan applied it in India. The Supreme Court speaks of a 'golden balance' of environment and development.
  2. Precautionary principle: lack of full scientific certainty is not a reason to postpone measures to prevent environmental degradation (Rio Principle 15). In India it also shifts the burden of proof to the developer (Vellore; Nayudu).
  3. Polluter pays: the polluter bears the cost of pollution and restoration (Rio Principle 16) - includes costs of restoration and compensation (Bichhri; Vellore). NGT Act S.20 requires NGT to apply sustainable development, precautionary and polluter pays.
  4. Public trust doctrine: the State holds natural resources as a trustee for the public (Kamal Nath; M.I. Builders v. Radhey Shyam Sahu, 1999; Fomento Resorts v. Minguel Martins, 2009).
  5. Intergenerational equity: the present generation holds the planet in trust for the future (State of H.P. v. Ganesh Wood Products, 1995).
  6. Absolute liability: Oleum Gas case, 1987.
  7. Sic utere tuo ut alienum non laedas: use your property so as not to injure another's - nuisance and transboundary harm (Trail Smelter Arbitration, 1941).
  8. Common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities (CBDR-RC): Rio Principle 7; UNFCCC Art 3; central to India's climate position.
  9. No-fault liability: Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991 and NGT Act S.17(3).
  10. Environmental rule of law: Right of citizens to approach courts - wide locus standi in PIL and Citizens' suit provisions (S.19 EPA - 60-day notice).

✎Memory aids

  • P-P-P: Precaution (before harm), Polluter pays (after harm), Public trust (State as trustee).

!Exam traps

  • TrapPrecautionary principle is NOT the same as polluter pays - one is about preventing harm, the other about cost allocation.
  • TrapPublic trust doctrine is not about liability, it is about State ownership/trusteeship of resources.
  • TrapIntergenerational equity vs sustainable development - the former is the ethical basis, the latter is the policy outcome.

?Likely questions

  1. Match principle with case.
  2. Which Rio principle contains the precautionary approach? (Principle 15)
  3. Burden of proof shifts to the developer under which principle?
