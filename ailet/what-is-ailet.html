THE EXAM EXPLAINED

What is AILET?

The All India Law Entrance Test is the admission test of National Law University Delhi. This guide covers the one-year LL.M. paper: pattern, marking, dates, eligibility, seats, tie-breakers, and how AILET differs from CLAT PG.

Read this first. Dates, seats and eligibility are re-issued every admission cycle, and third-party sites sometimes disagree with each other. Where something comes from the official NLU Delhi page it is marked Official. Everything else is marked Reported: confirm it in the current notification before you rely on it.

Exam pattern: AILET LL.M.

Question typeMultiple-choice questions only (Official)
Number of questions100, all from different branches of law (Official)
Marks1 mark per question, 100 marks in total (Official)
Duration120 minutes (Official)
Negative marking0.25 mark deducted per wrong answer, so four wrong answers cancel one right answer (Official)
SectionsNone. One paper of 100 questions, which you may attempt in any order (Reported)
Unattempted questionsNo mark, no penalty
A worked example. You attempt 80 questions and get 60 right and 20 wrong. Your score is 60 − (20 × 0.25) = 55. If you had skipped the 8 you were least sure about and 6 of them would have been wrong, you would have avoided −1.5 and given up 2 marks: a net gain of 0.5. Guessing pays only when you can genuinely eliminate options.

How ties are broken

Official: if two or more candidates score the same, merit is decided by (a) senior age, and then (b) a computerised draw of lots.

Eligibility and seats

  • Qualification (Reported): an LL.B. degree (three-year, or five-year integrated) with at least 50% marks, 45% for SC, ST and PwD candidates. Candidates in the final year of the LL.B. have been allowed to apply.
  • Seats (Reported): sources for the 2027 cycle put the regular LL.M. intake at about 70 on merit, plus seats for foreign nationals and OCI/PIO candidates and a supernumerary seat, around 81 in all. This is a very small number against thousands of applicants, which is why the cut-off is high.
  • Confirm both in the admission notification on the NLU Delhi website. Category-wise reservation and fees are listed there.

Applying to both is common. NLU Delhi admits its LL.M. through AILET, so do not assume a CLAT PG score substitutes for it. If you are preparing for both, the CLAT PG section trains passage reading and traps, while this section trains direct recall and accuracy.

What past papers have tested

NLU Delhi publishes previous LL.M. papers, and several coaching sites analyse them. The official scheme names no topics, so this is our reading of those analyses, not an official weightage.

CORE

Constitutional Law is consistently the largest block (one analysis of the 2026 paper counted 23 of 100 questions). Jurisprudence, Criminal Law, Law of Contract and Law of Torts are the next most reliable scorers.

REGULAR

Administrative Law, Family Law, Property Law, Company Law and Public International Law usually contribute a handful of questions each, with Intellectual Property appearing in several recent papers (about five in the same 2026 analysis).

OCCASIONAL

Tax, Environmental and Labour Law, and allied areas such as evidence, procedure, arbitration, competition, consumer law and recent legal developments, show up in smaller numbers and vary year to year.

Recent analyses describe the papers as moderate, mostly conceptual, with some tricky fact-based questions. Open the syllabus page for the topic-level breakdown and tracker.

How to prepare for this paper

  1. Lock in the core five first. Constitutional Law, Jurisprudence, Criminal Law (with the BNS, BNSS and BSA), Contract and Torts carry the bulk of the marks.
  2. Learn the new criminal codes with their old numbers. Questions often hinge on whether you know the new offence, the new section, or the IPC equivalent.
  3. Keep a case file. For each branch, list the leading cases with one line each: the facts that matter and the holding.
  4. Practise untimed, then add pace. Use the Practice Arena to build accuracy and read every explanation. Add speed later: 100 questions in 120 minutes is about 72 seconds each.
  5. Take a mock, then work the notebook. Submit a full paper, read the subject breakdown, then clear your revision notebook before the next one.
  6. Revise current legal developments last. Major judgments, new Acts and committee reports of the past year, checked against official sources.
