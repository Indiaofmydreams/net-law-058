About this question set

This set has 8 multiple-choice questions on Modernism and Post-Modernism from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 1, Jurisprudence. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 8 of the 8 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Jurisprudence notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

8 practice questions with answers

1Post-modernism in law is associated with which of the following movements?

  1. Critical Legal Studies (CLS)
  2. Legal Positivism
  3. Natural Law
  4. Historical School
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Critical Legal Studies (CLS)

Post-modernism in law is expressed through CLS, Feminist Jurisprudence, and Critical Race Theory.

2Michel Foucault's contribution to legal theory is primarily through:

  1. Kelsen's Pure Theory critique
  2. Development of the Grundnorm
  3. Natural rights theory
  4. Analysis of power-knowledge relations and how law disciplines society
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Analysis of power-knowledge relations and how law disciplines society

Foucault analysed how law and punishment are tools of power and discipline in modern society.

Report this question

3'Deconstruction' as applied to law is associated with:

  1. Austin
  2. Rawls
  3. Derrida
  4. Fuller
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Derrida

Jacques Derrida's deconstruction challenges stable meanings in legal texts and reveals internal contradictions.

Report this question

4The 'indeterminacy thesis' in CLS means:

  1. Legal rules are so open-textured that they cannot determine outcomes
  2. Law has clear and determinate answers
  3. Judges always follow precedent
  4. Constitutional law is supreme
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Legal rules are so open-textured that they cannot determine outcomes

CLS's indeterminacy thesis: legal rules and precedents are so flexible that judges decide on ideological grounds.

Report this question

5Feminist jurisprudence primarily critiques law for:

  1. Being too positivist
  2. Ignoring custom
  3. Being gender-biased and reflecting patriarchal power structures
  4. Excessive reliance on natural law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Being gender-biased and reflecting patriarchal power structures

Feminist jurisprudence exposes how law reflects and perpetuates patriarchal power.

Report this question

6Critical Race Theory (CRT) argues that:

  1. Race is a biological fact determinative of legal rights
  2. Law is race-neutral
  3. Racism is embedded in law and legal institutions, not merely individual prejudice
  4. Race has no relevance to legal analysis
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Racism is embedded in law and legal institutions, not merely individual prejudice

CRT: racism is systemic and embedded in legal structures, not just individual acts of prejudice.

Report this question

7'Feminist jurisprudence' challenges which assumption of mainstream jurisprudence?

  1. That law is objective and gender-neutral, when in fact it reflects male experience and perspective
  2. That law is based on natural law
  3. That courts make law
  4. That morality and law are separate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. That law is objective and gender-neutral, when in fact it reflects male experience and perspective

Feminist jurisprudence exposes the male bias in supposedly neutral legal concepts and institutions.

Report this question

8Lyotard defined postmodernism as:

  1. Acceptance of all grand narratives
  2. Revival of natural law
  3. Return to positivism
  4. 'Incredulity towards meta-narratives' — rejection of universal theories and truths
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 'Incredulity towards meta-narratives' — rejection of universal theories and truths

Lyotard: postmodernism = incredulity towards meta-narratives — rejection of universal explanatory systems.
