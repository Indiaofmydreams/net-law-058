1Assertion (A): A geographical indication tag cannot be used as a generic term in trade. Reason (R): Once a GI is registered, it cannot be used to indicate a type or style of product; it must identify the specific geographical origin from which the product derives its qualities.

  1. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are correct but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is correct but R is incorrect
  4. Both A and R are incorrect
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A

A registered GI cannot become generic — the GI Act and TRIPS prevent a GI from being used to designate a variety, type, or style of product without reference to the specific geographical region from which the product originates.

2Which combination correctly represents the THREE pillars of patentability?

  1. Novelty + Originality + Industrial Application
  2. Novelty + Non-obviousness + Commercial Value
  3. Novelty + Inventive Step + Industrial Application
  4. Creativity + Novelty + Utility
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Novelty + Inventive Step + Industrial Application

Under Section 2(1)(j) of the Patents Act, 1970, an 'invention' must satisfy three criteria: (1) Novelty — it must be new; (2) Inventive Step — it must not be obvious (non-obvious); and (3) Industrial Application — it must be capable of being made or used in industry.

3Which of the following is the MOST ACCURATE statement about moral rights under copyright?

  1. Moral rights are economic rights that can be freely assigned
  2. Moral rights survive assignment of copyright and are inalienable under Section 57 of the Copyright Act
  3. Moral rights are only available to the publisher of a work
  4. Moral rights expire with the economic term of copyright
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Moral rights survive assignment of copyright and are inalienable under Section 57 of the Copyright Act

Section 57 of the Copyright Act grants authors the right of paternity (attribution) and the right of integrity. These rights are personal rights of the author, survive the assignment of copyright to another person, are inalienable, and exist even after the author's death.

4In copyright law, what does the term 'neighbouring rights' (or 'related rights') refer to?

  1. Rights of persons who live near the copyright holder
  2. Rights of performers, producers of phonograms, and broadcasting organisations — distinct from but related to copyright
  3. Rights of the heirs and successors of the copyright holder
  4. Rights of co-authors who contributed to a joint work
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Rights of performers, producers of phonograms, and broadcasting organisations — distinct from but related to copyright

Neighbouring rights (related rights) are rights accorded to performers, producers of phonograms, and broadcasting organisations. They are distinct from copyright (which protects authors) but are 'neighbouring' in the sense that they protect persons who disseminate or perform creative works.

5The Biological Diversity Act, 2002 establishes which three-tier structure?

  1. National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) → State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs) → Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs)
  2. National Biodiversity Authority → Central Biodiversity Board → Regional Committees
  3. Ministry of Environment → State Pollution Boards → District Committees
  4. WIPO → National Authority → Local Authorities
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) → State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs) → Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs)

The Biological Diversity Act, 2002 creates a three-tier structure: (1) National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) at the national level; (2) State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs) at the state level; and (3) Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs) at the local level, typically at panchayat/municipality level.

6Which provision of the Patents Act, 1970 deals with 'inventive step' (non-obviousness)?

  1. Section 2(1)(j)
  2. Section 2(1)(ja)
  3. Section 3
  4. Section 48
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Section 2(1)(ja)

Section 2(1)(ja) of the Patents Act defines 'inventive step' as a feature of an invention that involves technical advance as compared to the existing knowledge or having economic significance or both and that makes the invention not obvious to a person skilled in the art.

7What is the correct definition of 'trade mark' under Section 2(zb) of the Trade Marks Act, 1999?

  1. Any symbol used in commerce to identify a product
  2. Only word marks and logos registered with the Trade Marks Registry
  3. A mark capable of being represented graphically and capable of distinguishing the goods or services of one person from those of others
  4. Any distinctive sign used by a manufacturer
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A mark capable of being represented graphically and capable of distinguishing the goods or services of one person from those of others

Section 2(zb) defines 'trade mark' as a mark capable of being represented graphically and which is capable of distinguishing the goods or services of one person from those of others and may include shape of goods, their packaging, and combination of colours.

8Under the IT Amendment Act 2008, Section 66D deals with:

  1. Cyber terrorism
  2. Cheating by impersonation using a computer resource
  3. Identity theft
  4. Publishing obscene material
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Cheating by impersonation using a computer resource

Section 66D punishes whoever, by means of any communication device or computer resource, cheats by personation — i.e., assumes the identity of another person to deceive someone — with imprisonment up to 3 years and/or fine up to ₹1 lakh.

9Which of the following is NOT an absolute ground for refusal of trademark registration under Section 9 of the Trade Marks Act, 1999?

  1. A mark which is similar to an earlier registered trademark for similar goods
  2. A mark which is devoid of distinctive character
  3. A mark which consists exclusively of signs designating quality of the goods
  4. A mark which is contrary to public morality
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A mark which is similar to an earlier registered trademark for similar goods

Similarity to an earlier registered trademark is a relative ground for refusal under Section 11, not an absolute ground under Section 9. Absolute grounds relate to the mark's inherent registrability regardless of other marks.

10In India, which authority administers and controls the Patent Office, Trade Marks Registry, GI Registry, and Designs Wing?

  1. Ministry of Law and Justice
  2. WIPO's India liaison office
  3. National Intellectual Property Commission
  4. Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks (CGPDTM) under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks (CGPDTM) under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry

The Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks (CGPDTM), functioning under the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), Ministry of Commerce and Industry, administers the Patent Office, Trade Marks Registry, GI Registry, and Designs Wing.

11Section 67A of the IT Act, 2000 deals with punishment for:

  1. Tampering with computer source documents
  2. Identity theft
  3. Cyber terrorism
  4. Publishing or transmitting sexually explicit material in electronic form
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Publishing or transmitting sexually explicit material in electronic form

Section 67A, inserted by the IT (Amendment) Act 2008, punishes whoever publishes or transmits material containing sexually explicit act in electronic form with imprisonment up to 5 years and fine up to ₹10 lakh on first conviction.

  1. 1 January 2009

13Which of the following transactions is EXCLUDED from the application of the IT Act, 2000?

  1. Sale of immovable property through electronic record
  2. Online government filings
  3. Electronic contracts between businesses
  4. Digital tax returns
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Sale of immovable property through electronic record

The First Schedule to the IT Act lists exclusions including: negotiable instruments (other than cheques), powers of attorney, trusts, wills, and any contract for the sale or conveyance of immovable property or any interest in such property.

14Section 72A of the IT Act, 2000 deals with:

  1. Breach of confidentiality by government officials
  2. Cyber terrorism
  3. Child pornography
  4. Disclosure of information in breach of a lawful contract
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Disclosure of information in breach of a lawful contract

Section 72A punishes any person including an intermediary who, while providing services under the terms of lawful contract, has secured access to any material containing personal information about another person, and without the consent of the person concerned, discloses such material to any other person.

15Domain name disputes in India relating to well-known trademarks are governed primarily through:

  1. Trade Marks Act, 1999 and the doctrine of passing off
  2. Copyright Act, 1957
  3. IT Act, 2000 exclusively
  4. Geographical Indications Act, 1999
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Trade Marks Act, 1999 and the doctrine of passing off

Domain name disputes in India are primarily resolved through the Trade Marks Act, 1999 (for registered marks) and the common law action of passing off (for unregistered marks with established goodwill), as established in cases like Yahoo Inc. v. Akash Arora.

16Assertion (A): Copyright in India does not require registration for subsistence of protection. Reason (R): The Berne Convention (to which India is a member) establishes the principle that enjoyment and exercise of copyright shall not be subject to any formality.

  1. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are correct but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is correct but R is incorrect
  4. A is incorrect but R is correct
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A

India is a signatory to the Berne Convention. Article 5(2) of the Berne Convention prohibits member states from imposing formalities (registration, deposit, etc.) as a condition for copyright protection — this principle is reflected in the Copyright Act, 1957.

17Which of the following is the correct meaning of 'biopiracy' in the context of intellectual property?

  1. Piracy of biological textbooks
  2. Illegal fishing in international waters
  3. Manufacturing counterfeit pharmaceutical drugs
  4. Appropriation of biological resources or traditional knowledge of indigenous communities without fair compensation or acknowledgment
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Appropriation of biological resources or traditional knowledge of indigenous communities without fair compensation or acknowledgment

Biopiracy refers to the appropriation of the genetic resources and traditional knowledge of indigenous and local communities for commercial purposes without obtaining prior informed consent or sharing benefits equitably with the source communities.

18Under the Patents Act, 1970, who may apply for a patent?

  1. Only Indian citizens
  2. Only the inventor personally
  3. Any person claiming to be the true and first inventor or their assignee, including foreign nationals
  4. Only registered patent agents
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Any person claiming to be the true and first inventor or their assignee, including foreign nationals

Section 6 of the Patents Act provides that an application for a patent may be made by any person claiming to be the true and first inventor of the invention, or the assignee of such person, or the legal representative of a deceased person. There is no restriction on nationality.
