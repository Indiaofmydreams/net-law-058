19 practice questions with answers

1Which scholar defined Administrative Law as 'the law relating to the control of governmental power'?

  1. Dicey
  2. Ivor Jennings
  3. H.W.R. Wade
  4. Garner
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. H.W.R. Wade

H.W.R. Wade defined administrative law as 'the law relating to the control of governmental power.' Garner defined it as 'that branch of public law which is concerned with the composition, powers and duties of administrative authorities.'

Report this question

2A.V. Dicey was opposed to Administrative Law (Droit Administratif). His main objection was:

  1. It was borrowed from French law
  2. It gave too much power to courts
  3. It was not codified
  4. It created a separate system of law for government officials, violating Rule of Law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. It created a separate system of law for government officials, violating Rule of Law

Dicey opposed Droit Administratif (French administrative law) because he believed in the Rule of Law which required every person, including government officials, to be subject to the same ordinary law of the land administered by ordinary courts. Concept note: Dicey's Rule of Law: Three meanings — (1) Supremacy of law; (2) Equality before the law — every man subject to ordinary law; (3) Constitution as a consequence of individual rights. Dicey was later corrected by Jennings and Wade who showed Droit Administratif actually protects citizens from the State.

Report this question

3The 'Droit Administratif' system of France is characterized by:

  1. Complete immunity of the State from suit
  2. Separate administrative courts (Conseil d'État) to deal with disputes involving the State
  3. Courts using stare decisis in administrative decisions
  4. Supremacy of ordinary civil courts
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Separate administrative courts (Conseil d'État) to deal with disputes involving the State

The French Droit Administratif system has a dual system of courts — ordinary courts for private law matters and separate administrative courts (headed by Conseil d'État) for disputes involving the State. France follows civil law tradition, not common law stare decisis.

4Which of the following best describes delegated legislation?

  1. Legislation by constitutional amendment
  2. Judicial precedent that creates law
  3. Legislation made by executive bodies under authority conferred by Parliament
  4. Legislation made by Parliament directly
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Legislation made by executive bodies under authority conferred by Parliament

Delegated (subordinate/subsidiary) legislation is law made by executive bodies (ministers, local authorities, statutory corporations) under specific authority conferred by an Act of Parliament. Concept note: Delegated Legislation: Parliament delegates legislative power to executive bodies to make rules/regulations/bye-laws. Subject to judicial review on grounds of: (1) ultra vires parent Act; (2) violation of FRs; (3) unreasonableness; (4) procedural defects. Key case: Delhi Laws Act Case (1951) — excessive delegation is unconstitutional.

5The principle against 'excessive delegation' means:

  1. Parliament cannot delegate at all
  2. All delegated legislation is void
  3. The executive can override parliamentary enactments
  4. Parliament cannot abdicate itself — it must lay down policy; details only can be delegated
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Parliament cannot abdicate itself — it must lay down policy; details only can be delegated

The doctrine of excessive delegation means Parliament must retain control by laying down the legislative policy and standards. Only the working out of details can be delegated. Leading case: In re Delhi Laws Act, AIR 1951 SC 332.

6'Ombudsman' concept originated in which country?

  1. United States
  2. Sweden
  3. United Kingdom
  4. France
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Sweden

The concept of Ombudsman originated in Sweden in 1809. The Riksdag (Swedish Parliament) appointed the first Ombudsman to oversee the application of laws by officials. India's equivalent is Lokpal (Union) and Lokayukta (State).

7Which Article of the Constitution is the basis for the 'State' being made accountable in administrative law?

  1. Article 21
  2. Article 15
  3. Article 12
  4. Article 13
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Article 12

Article 12 defines 'State' for the purposes of Part III (Fundamental Rights). It includes the Government and Parliament of India, the Government and Legislature of each State, and all local or other authorities within India or under the control of the Government of India.

Report this question

8In Pradeep Kumar Biswas v. Indian Institute of Chemical Biology (2002), the Supreme Court laid down the test for a body to be 'State' under Article 12 as:

  1. Whether the body receives grants
  2. Functional control and financial dependence on government — 'instrumentality or agency of State'
  3. Whether the body is a statutory corporation
  4. Government ownership alone
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Functional control and financial dependence on government — 'instrumentality or agency of State'

In Pradeep Kumar Biswas (2002), a 7-judge bench overruled Sabhajit Tewary v. Union of India and established the 'functional test' — a body is 'State' if it is an instrumentality or agency of the State based on factors like financial dependence, functional control, and whether the functions are of public importance.

9Which Article provides for Administrative Tribunals to deal with service matters?

  1. Article 323
  2. Article 323-A
  3. Article 323-B
  4. Both B and C
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Both B and C

Article 323-A deals with Administrative Tribunals for service matters (inserted by 42nd Amendment, 1976; operationalized by Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985). Article 323-B provides for tribunals for other matters like taxation, elections, industrial disputes, etc.

Report this question

10Under Article 309, the conditions of service of persons serving the Union or States can be regulated by:

  1. The Supreme Court
  2. Only by legislation
  3. An Act of the appropriate legislature; until such Act is made, by executive rules
  4. The President alone
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. An Act of the appropriate legislature; until such Act is made, by executive rules

Article 309 enables the appropriate legislature (Parliament for Union; State Legislature for State) to regulate the recruitment and conditions of service. Until Parliament or the State Legislature makes such a law, the President or Governor may make rules.

Report this question

11The 'Doctrine of Pleasure' — Article 310 — means government servants hold office 'during the pleasure of the President/Governor.' This is subject to:

  1. Article 32 alone
  2. Article 311 — procedural safeguards of inquiry before dismissal/removal/reduction in rank
  3. Cabinet approval
  4. No restriction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Article 311 — procedural safeguards of inquiry before dismissal/removal/reduction in rank

Article 310 establishes the doctrine of pleasure. But Article 311 provides constitutional safeguards: (1) No dismissal by an authority subordinate to the appointing authority; (2) Reasonable opportunity to show cause before dismissal, removal, or reduction in rank.

Report this question

12The Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT) was established under:

  1. Article 323-A and the Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985
  2. Article 323-B
  3. Administrative Tribunals Act, 1976
  4. Service Conduct Rules, 1964
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Article 323-A and the Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985

Article 323-A (inserted by 42nd Amendment, 1976) provides for Administrative Tribunals for adjudication of disputes relating to recruitment and conditions of service of persons appointed to public services. The Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985 operationalized this provision by establishing the CAT.

13In L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India (1997), the Supreme Court held:

  1. Only Supreme Court can review CAT decisions
  2. Administrative Tribunals Act is unconstitutional
  3. Power of High Courts under Articles 226/227 cannot be excluded — it is part of the basic structure
  4. CAT is the final authority on service matters
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Power of High Courts under Articles 226/227 cannot be excluded — it is part of the basic structure

In L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India (1997), a 7-judge Constitutional Bench held that the power of High Courts under Articles 226 and 227 is part of the basic structure of the Constitution and cannot be excluded even by a constitutional provision. CAT decisions are therefore subject to HC Division Bench review. Concept note: L. Chandra Kumar Principle: Judicial review by the High Court is a basic structure feature. Even the Constitution (through Art. 323-A(2)(d)) cannot validly exclude this jurisdiction. CAT functions as a court of first instance; its decisions go to HC Division Bench for judicial review.

14'Lokpal' under the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013 has jurisdiction over:

  1. Only Central government employees below Joint Secretary level
  2. Public servants including PM (with conditions), Ministers, MPs, Group A/B/C/D officials, and certain NGOs
  3. Only Members of Parliament
  4. State government officials only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Public servants including PM (with conditions), Ministers, MPs, Group A/B/C/D officials, and certain NGOs

Lokpal has jurisdiction over the Prime Minister (with significant restrictions — matters relating to external affairs, security, public order excluded), Union Ministers, Members of Parliament, Group A/B/C/D officers of Union, and officers of certain entities receiving government funding.

15The Lokpal is headed by:

  1. A sitting Supreme Court judge
  2. A retired Chief Justice of India compulsorily
  3. A person who is/has been CJI or a SC judge or an eminent person meeting prescribed criteria
  4. The Chief Vigilance Commissioner
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A person who is/has been CJI or a SC judge or an eminent person meeting prescribed criteria

The Lokpal Act, 2013 provides that the Chairperson shall be a person who is or has been the Chief Justice of India or a judge of the Supreme Court OR an eminent person of impeccable integrity and outstanding ability, having special knowledge and expertise of not less than 25 years in public administration.

16Which of the following is NOT a valid ground for challenging delegated legislation in courts?

  1. Procedural ultra vires — failure to follow mandatory consultation/publication
  2. The parent Act itself was passed hastily by Parliament
  3. Ultra vires the parent Act
  4. Violation of Fundamental Rights
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The parent Act itself was passed hastily by Parliament

Delegated legislation can be challenged on grounds of: (1) substantive ultra vires (beyond scope of parent Act); (2) violation of Fundamental Rights; (3) procedural ultra vires (failure to follow mandatory procedures); (4) unreasonableness; (5) sub-delegation without authority. The speed of passing the parent Act is NOT a ground for challenging subordinate legislation.

Report this question

17'Sub-delegation' of legislative power means:

  1. The body which received delegated power further delegates to yet another body
  2. The President delegates to States
  3. The original legislature re-delegates to a lower body
  4. Parliament delegates to the President
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The body which received delegated power further delegates to yet another body

Sub-delegation occurs when a body that has received legislative power by delegation from Parliament further delegates that power to another subordinate body. The maxim 'delegatus non potest delegare' (a delegate cannot further delegate) applies — sub-delegation is valid only if expressly or impliedly permitted by the parent Act.

Report this question

18Parliamentary control over delegated legislation operates through:

  1. Laying before Parliament — affirmative or negative resolution
  2. Committee on Subordinate Legislation
  3. Sunset clauses
  4. Both A and B
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Both A and B

Parliamentary control over delegated legislation operates through: (1) Laying procedure — affirmative resolution (both Houses must approve) or negative resolution (either House can annul within 30/40 days); (2) Committee on Subordinate Legislation in both Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha which scrutinizes delegated legislation.

Report this question

19The doctrine of 'Legitimate Expectation' was held to give only PROCEDURAL rights (not substantive benefit) by the SC in:

  1. Navjyoti Coop. v. Union of India
  2. Food Corporation of India v. Kamdhenu
  3. Union of India v. Hindustan Development Corporation (1993)
  4. M/s Javed Ahmed v. State of Maharashtra
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Union of India v. Hindustan Development Corporation (1993)

In Union of India v. Hindustan Development Corporation (1993), the Supreme Court held that Legitimate Expectation gives a procedural right — the right to be heard before the expectation is defeated — but NOT a substantive right to the promised benefit. The authority can still change policy for public good.
