16 practice questions with answers

1Which of the following best describes the 'Doctrine of Inevitable Disclosure' in trade secret law?

  1. Trade secrets must inevitably be disclosed in patent applications
  2. Trade secrets disclosed in court proceedings lose protection
  3. An employer must inevitably share trade secrets with employees
  4. A former employee will inevitably disclose trade secrets in a new job, justifying a court injunction even without proof of actual misappropriation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A former employee will inevitably disclose trade secrets in a new job, justifying a court injunction even without proof of actual misappropriation

The Doctrine of Inevitable Disclosure (primarily a US concept, also discussed in Indian trade secret contexts) allows an employer to prevent a former employee from working for a competitor if the new job would inevitably require disclosure of the former employer's trade secrets.

2Under TRIPS Article 31, compulsory licences may be granted subject to conditions including:

  1. Prior negotiation with the rights holder on reasonable commercial terms must have been attempted first
  2. The licensee must pay no royalties
  3. Compulsory licences must be granted for all patented inventions simultaneously
  4. No conditions apply in national emergencies
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Prior negotiation with the rights holder on reasonable commercial terms must have been attempted first

TRIPS Article 31 requires that before a compulsory licence is granted, the potential user must have first made efforts to obtain authorisation from the rights holder on reasonable commercial terms, and such efforts have not been successful within a reasonable period. This requirement may be waived in national emergencies.

3In the context of copyright, a 'derivative work' means:

  1. A work that is entirely copied from another
  2. A work made by an employee for an employer
  3. A work that has entered the public domain
  4. A work based upon one or more pre-existing works, such as a translation, musical arrangement, dramatisation, or adaptation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A work based upon one or more pre-existing works, such as a translation, musical arrangement, dramatisation, or adaptation

A derivative work is one that is based upon one or more existing works and incorporates creative expression from those works. Examples include translations, adaptations, dramatisations, abridgements, and motion picture versions of novels. Creating a derivative work requires the permission of the original copyright holder.

4The 'Safe Harbour' provision for Internet Service Providers (ISPs) under the IT Act 2000 is set out in Section 79. What is the corresponding provision under US law?

  1. Section 512 of the Digital Millennium Copyright Act, 1998
  2. Section 230 of the Communications Decency Act, 1996
  3. Section 101 of the Copyright Act, 1976
  4. Section 43(a) of the Lanham Act
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Section 512 of the Digital Millennium Copyright Act, 1998

Section 512 of the US Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA), 1998 provides the 'safe harbour' provisions for ISPs limiting their liability for copyright infringement by users, provided they comply with notice-and-takedown procedures. This is the equivalent of Section 79 of India's IT Act.

5Which of the following IP rights is protected for the LONGEST maximum period in India?

  1. Patent (20 years)
  2. Copyright for literary works (life + 60 years)
  3. Industrial Design (15 years)
  4. Trademark (renewable indefinitely)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Copyright for literary works (life + 60 years)

Copyright for literary works lasts for the life of the author plus 60 years. For a long-lived author, this could easily exceed 100+ years. Trademarks are renewable indefinitely but require periodic renewal. Copyright's duration is the longest fixed-period IP protection.

6The Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules were notified in:

  1. 2019
  2. 2020
  3. 2021
  4. 2022
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 2021

The Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021 were notified on 25 February 2021, replacing the earlier IT (Intermediaries Guidelines) Rules, 2011.

7What is the significance of the 'Mailbox provision' in the context of Indian patent law and TRIPS compliance?

  1. It required India to accept and store (in a 'mailbox') product patent applications for pharmaceuticals and agrochemicals from 1995 to 2005, without examining or granting them
  2. It allowed foreign companies to file patent applications in India via mail
  3. It created a special postal system for IP filings
  4. It was a provision allowing emails to be admitted as patent applications
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It required India to accept and store (in a 'mailbox') product patent applications for pharmaceuticals and agrochemicals from 1995 to 2005, without examining or granting them

Under TRIPS, developing countries not providing product patents had until 2005 to do so, but were required to accept and store applications filed between 1995-2005 in a 'mailbox' without processing them. When India amended its Patents Act in 2005, these mailbox applications were examined under the new law including Section 3(d).

8Section 3(j) of the Patents Act, 1970 excludes from patentability:

  1. Plants and animals in whole or any part thereof other than micro-organisms but including seeds, varieties and species and essentially biological processes for production or propagation of plants and animals
  2. Computer programs
  3. Traditional knowledge
  4. Atomic energy inventions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Plants and animals in whole or any part thereof other than micro-organisms but including seeds, varieties and species and essentially biological processes for production or propagation of plants and animals

Section 3(j) excludes from patentability plants and animals in whole or any part thereof other than micro-organisms, including seeds, varieties, species, and essentially biological processes for the production or propagation of plants and animals. Micro-organisms with technical application may remain patentable.

9Which of the following statements about 'collective works' under the Copyright Act 1957 is CORRECT?

  1. In a collective work, each contributor owns copyright in the whole work jointly
  2. A collective work has no copyright protection
  3. In a collective work, each contributor holds copyright in their individual contribution
  4. The publisher of a collective work holds all copyrights
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. In a collective work, each contributor holds copyright in their individual contribution

A collective work (e.g., an encyclopedia, anthology) is a work in which a number of contributions constitute separate and independent works assembled into a collective whole. Each contributor retains copyright in their own individual contribution, while the editor/compiler may hold copyright in the selection and arrangement.

10Under the GI Act 1999, an 'authorised user' of a geographical indication is:

  1. A producer of goods in respect of which the GI is registered and who is enrolled on the register of authorised users
  2. The GI Registry itself
  3. Any trader who buys goods bearing the GI
  4. The Central Government
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A producer of goods in respect of which the GI is registered and who is enrolled on the register of authorised users

Section 2(b) of the GI Act defines 'authorised user' as a person authorised by a registered proprietor to use a geographical indication. Under Section 17, producers of the goods in respect of which the GI is registered may apply to be registered as authorised users.

11What is 'trade libel' (also called 'injurious falsehood' or 'slander of goods') in IP law?

  1. Defamation of the manufacturer of goods
  2. Trademark infringement through verbal statements
  3. False statements made to third parties about the quality or nature of a competitor's goods or business, causing economic damage
  4. Criminal defamation under IPC Section 499
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. False statements made to third parties about the quality or nature of a competitor's goods or business, causing economic damage

Trade libel/injurious falsehood involves making false statements of fact about a competitor's goods, services, or business to third parties (not the competitor directly) that causes actual economic loss. It differs from personal defamation and is a separate IP/tort remedy.

12The Anton Piller Order (now called 'Search Order' in India) in IP litigation is used to:

  1. Allow a claimant to enter defendant's premises to inspect, photograph, and seize infringing materials without prior warning to prevent destruction of evidence
  2. Arrest a defendant in an IP case
  3. Freeze assets of a defendant
  4. Restrain publication of a newspaper article
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Allow a claimant to enter defendant's premises to inspect, photograph, and seize infringing materials without prior warning to prevent destruction of evidence

An Anton Piller Order (from Anton Piller KG v. Manufacturing Processes Ltd., 1976) is an ex parte civil search order allowing the rights holder to enter and search a defendant's premises to inspect, copy, and seize infringing goods or documents — granted without notice to prevent destruction of evidence.

13The concept of 'Mareva Injunction' (Asset Freezing Order) in IP cases is used to:

  1. Search premises for infringing goods
  2. Prevent publication of an infringing work
  3. Compel disclosure of the identity of anonymous infringers
  4. Prevent defendants from dissipating their assets before judgment to ensure an award of damages can be satisfied
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Prevent defendants from dissipating their assets before judgment to ensure an award of damages can be satisfied

A Mareva injunction (from Mareva Compania Naviera SA v. International Bulkcarriers SA, 1975) freezes a defendant's assets to prevent them from being dissipated or transferred abroad before judgment, ensuring any eventual damages award can be enforced.

14The Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA) of the United States was enacted in:

  1. 1996
  2. 1997
  3. 1998
  4. 2000
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1998

The DMCA was enacted on 28 October 1998. It implemented the WIPO Copyright Treaty (WCT) and WIPO Performances and Phonograms Treaty (WPPT) of 1996 into US law.

15The 'first sale doctrine' as applied to copyright (Section 14 of the Copyright Act 1957 and Section 30 for licences) most closely aligns with which concept in international IP law?

  1. Moral rights
  2. Fair dealing
  3. Exhaustion of rights
  4. Compulsory licensing
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Exhaustion of rights

The first sale doctrine is the copyright expression of the exhaustion of rights principle — once the rights holder sells a copy of a work, their distribution right in that particular copy is exhausted and the purchaser may resell, lend, or otherwise dispose of that copy.

16Section 2(m) of the Copyright Act 1957 defines 'infringing copy' as a copy made or imported in contravention of the provisions of the Act. For a computer programme, an infringing copy means:

  1. A copy made otherwise than in accordance with any licence granted by the owner of copyright
  2. Any copy not purchased from the original developer
  3. A copy stored on any electronic device
  4. Only copies distributed online
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A copy made otherwise than in accordance with any licence granted by the owner of copyright

For a computer programme, an 'infringing copy' under Section 2(m)(ii) means a copy made otherwise than in accordance with the terms of a licence granted by the owner of the copyright in the computer programme.
