UGC NET Law PYQs, shuffled

Every question from five UGC NET Law papers sits in one pool. Each test draws a fresh random set from it, in random order, against a clock of half a minute per question. Answers and explanations come after you submit.

WHY THIS WAY

Why PYQs are shuffled, not listed year by year

A year-wise list is good for reading a paper. It is a weak way to practise for one. Here is the reasoning behind this page.

  • The exam never arrives in order

    On the day, no question tells you which topic is next. A fixed list lets you lean on position and on the memory of a paper you have already seen. Random order removes those crutches, so what you are testing is what you actually know.

  • Mixed topics make you retrieve, not recognise

    When Jurisprudence is followed by Family Law, then a reasoning question, you must work out what each question is asking before you can answer it. Mixing topics like this is harder than blocked practice, and it is the kind of effort that makes recall last.

  • Half-time clock, real pressure

    The real exam averages about 72 seconds a question (150 questions in 3 hours). Here you get 30. It is deliberately tighter: after a test like this, the real pace feels roomy, and you stop spending two minutes on a question you will not crack.

  • A fresh test every time

    Each draw picks questions you have not met on this device first, so repeated tests walk through the whole pool before anything repeats. Choose 20 for a quick sprint, 50 for a serious sitting, 100 for a long stretch.

  • Learn from every miss

    After you submit, you see your answer, the correct one and a short explanation for each question. Filter to just the wrong or skipped ones, and revise those first.

  • Honest about the paper's own flaws

    Published papers sometimes have printing errors or disputed keys. Where we know of one, the review shows a short note on that question, so you are not left doubting yourself.

Please read: about these questions

  • Paper 1 and Law 058 are mixed on purpose. The papers behind this pool contain Paper 1 (general aptitude) questions such as teaching, research, reasoning, data interpretation and ICT, alongside Law 058. They are kept together in one pool, so a shuffled test will contain both.
  • Check against the official key. Questions were typed up from the published papers, and a few were reconstructed where the PDF was garbled. 47 questions carry a short note where the printed paper or key has an error, or the question was dropped. For anything that matters, confirm against the official NTA paper and key.
  • Passage questions are condensed. Where a question refers to a passage, the test shows a one-line summary of it, not the full passage.
  • Not affiliated. NET Law-Verse is an independent study resource and is not affiliated with NTA or UGC. Spotted a mistake? Use the Report button on any question.
