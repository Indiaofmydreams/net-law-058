UGC NET Paper 1 Unit 4: Communication Notes and MCQs

The Communication unit covers how messages are sent and received, how teachers communicate in the classroom and how mass media shape society. Questions often ask about models, types of communication and barriers, and these are easy to learn with a few tables. These notes cover the syllabus and give sample MCQs.

What Unit 4 covers

Communication questions are definition driven, so a clean table of models and barriers covers most of the unit. The official syllabus lists 4 topics for this unit. Each is explained below with the points examiners most often use.

1Communication: meaning, types and characteristics

  • The word comes from the Latin communis, meaning common. Communication is the sharing of meaning between sender and receiver.
  • Elements of the process: sender, encoding, message, channel, receiver, decoding, feedback and noise.
  • Types by form: verbal (oral and written) and non-verbal. Types by direction: downward, upward, horizontal and diagonal. By channel: formal and informal (the grapevine).
  • Characteristics: it is a process, it is continuous, it involves a shared code and it is incomplete without feedback.

2Effective communication: verbal, non-verbal, intercultural and group communication

  • Effective verbal communication is clear, concise, correct and suited to the audience. Active listening is half of communication.
  • Non-verbal communication includes kinesics (body movement), proxemics (space), chronemics (time), paralanguage (tone and pitch) and appearance.
  • Intercultural communication requires awareness of differences in language, values, gestures and norms. Hall described high-context and low-context cultures.
  • Group communication involves more than two people. Common networks are chain, wheel, circle and all-channel.

3Classroom communication and barriers to effective communication

  • Classroom communication should be two-way: the teacher explains, questions and listens, and learners respond and ask.
  • Good classroom practice: clear voice, simple language, examples, eye contact, visual aids and prompt feedback.
  • Barriers are physical (noise, distance), semantic (different meanings of words), psychological (anxiety, prejudice, inattention), cultural and organisational.
  • Barriers are reduced through clear language, feedback, active listening and a supportive environment.

4Mass media and society

  • Mass media reach large and scattered audiences through print, radio, television, film and digital platforms.
  • Functions: inform, educate, entertain, persuade and shape public opinion. Media can also spread misinformation, so media literacy matters.
  • Marshall McLuhan said the medium is the message (Understanding Media, 1964). Agenda-setting theory (McCombs and Shaw, 1972) says media influence what people think about.
  • Indian milestones: Hicky's Bengal Gazette (1780) was the first newspaper, and the Prasar Bharati Act, 1990 created the public broadcaster.

Key terms and facts to remember

Shannon-Weaver model1948

Introduced the idea of noise; a mathematical, one-way model.

Lasswell formula1948

Who says what in which channel to whom with what effect.

Berlo SMCR model1960

Source, Message, Channel, Receiver.

Encoding and decoding

The sender encodes the idea into a message; the receiver decodes it.

Noise

Anything that distorts the message: physical, semantic or psychological.

Feedback

The receiver's response; it makes communication two-way.

Grapevine

Informal communication that spreads quickly.

Kinesics, proxemics, chronemics

Study of body movement, space and time respectively.

McLuhan, the medium is the message1964

From Understanding Media; the form of a medium shapes its effect.

Agenda setting1972

McCombs and Shaw: media shape what the public regards as important.

How to study this unit

  • Learn the models as a table: name, year, one phrase. Matching questions on Shannon-Weaver, Lasswell and Berlo appear often.
  • For barriers, sort each example into physical, semantic or psychological before choosing an answer.
  • Link classroom communication to feedback every time. If an option says one-way only, it is usually wrong.
