About this question set

This set has 20 multiple-choice questions on Law of Crimes from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 4, Law of Crimes. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 20 of the 20 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Law of Crimes notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

20 practice questions with answers

1Mens rea refers to:

  1. Guilty act
  2. Result
  3. Motive only
  4. Guilty mind
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Guilty mind

The mental element of a crime.

Report this question

2Constructive liability means liability for acts done:

  1. By insane persons
  2. By children
  3. Only by oneself
  4. By others in furtherance of common intention or object
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. By others in furtherance of common intention or object

Joint liability principle.

Report this question

3Inchoate offences include:

  1. Rape and robbery
  2. Murder and theft
  3. Attempt, conspiracy and abetment
  4. Hurt and assault
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Attempt, conspiracy and abetment

Incomplete or preparatory offences.

Report this question

4The doctrine of transferred malice applies when:

  1. Intent to harm one results in harm to another
  2. Both are harmed intentionally
  3. Intent is absent
  4. Acts are lawful
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Intent to harm one results in harm to another

Intent transfers to the actual victim.

Report this question

5Assertion (A): Mistake of fact can be a defence.
Reason (R): A person acting by mistake of fact, believing himself bound by law, is protected.

  1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true, but R is false
  4. A is false, but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A

R states the rationale.

Report this question

6Assertion (A): Preparation is generally not punishable.
Reason (R): Preparation involves mere arrangement without direct movement towards the offence.

  1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true, but R is false
  4. A is false, but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A

Attempt begins beyond preparation.

Report this question

7Assertion (A): A child between seven and twelve may be exempt from liability.
Reason (R): Exemption depends on lack of maturity of understanding.

  1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true, but R is false
  4. A is false, but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A

R explains A.

Report this question

8Consider the statements: 1. Compensation to victims may be ordered by the court. 2. Victim compensation is possible only where the trial ends in conviction.

  1. Only statement 1 is correct
  2. Only statement 2 is correct
  3. Both statements are correct
  4. Neither statement is correct
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Only statement 1 is correct

Compensation can be awarded even where trial ends in acquittal in specified cases.

Report this question

9Consider the statements: 1. The death penalty is awarded only in rarest of rare cases. 2. A death sentence can never be commuted.

  1. Only statement 1 is correct
  2. Only statement 2 is correct
  3. Both statements are correct
  4. Neither statement is correct
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Only statement 1 is correct

Clemency and commutation are possible.

Report this question

10Consider the statements: 1. Counterfeiting currency is an offence. 2. Possessing counterfeit notes knowing them to be so can be an offence.

  1. Only statement 1 is correct
  2. Only statement 2 is correct
  3. Both statements are correct
  4. Neither statement is correct
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Both statements are correct

Both are correct.

11Match: A Bachan Singh, B Mithu, C Virsa Singh, D Nanavati with (1) Intentional injury (2) Provocation (3) Rarest of rare (4) Mandatory death

  1. A-3, B-1, C-4, D-2
  2. A-3, B-4, C-1, D-2
  3. A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4
  4. A-4, B-3, C-2, D-1
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A-3, B-4, C-1, D-2

Case-principle mapping.

12Match: A Rioting, B Theft, C Abduction, D Waging war with (1) Property (2) Public tranquillity (3) State (4) Human body

  1. A-2, B-4, C-1, D-3
  2. A-3, B-1, C-2, D-4
  3. A-2, B-1, C-4, D-3
  4. A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4

Answer: C. A-2, B-1, C-4, D-3

Classification of offences.

13Match: A Retributive, B Deterrent, C Reformative, D Preventive with (1) Rehabilitation (2) Eye for an eye (3) Fear of punishment (4) Incapacitation

  1. A-3, B-2, C-4, D-1
  2. A-1, B-4, C-2, D-3
  3. A-2, B-1, C-3, D-4
  4. A-2, B-3, C-1, D-4
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A-2, B-3, C-1, D-4

Theories of punishment.

14A fires at B intending to kill but the bullet kills C. The doctrine applied is:

  1. Transferred malice
  2. Necessity
  3. Strict liability
  4. Provocation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Transferred malice

Intent transfers to C.

Report this question

15A and B agree to rob a bank but are arrested before any act. Offence made out:

  1. Criminal conspiracy
  2. Attempt to rob
  3. Robbery
  4. No offence
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Criminal conspiracy

Agreement is the offence.

Report this question

16To save his own life, A kills an innocent person. Is necessity a defence to murder?

  1. Only for dacoity
  2. No, not for murder
  3. Yes always
  4. Yes if poor
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. No, not for murder

R v. Dudley and Stephens.

Report this question

17Three persons assemble and one commits theft. The others are liable only if:

  1. They had arms
  2. They were present
  3. They shared common intention or object
  4. They are relatives
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. They shared common intention or object

Constructive liability needs a shared intention.

Report this question

18A prior meeting of minds for common intention was stressed in:

  1. Bachan Singh
  2. Virsa Singh
  3. Mahboob Shah v. Emperor
  4. Nanavati
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Mahboob Shah v. Emperor

Common intention requires pre-concert.

Report this question

19Virsa Singh v. State of Punjab is associated with:

  1. Conspiracy
  2. Intentional bodily injury sufficient in ordinary course to cause death
  3. Insanity
  4. Provocation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Intentional bodily injury sufficient in ordinary course to cause death

Test for murder under the injury clause.

Report this question

20K.M. Nanavati v. State of Maharashtra concerned:

  1. Dowry death
  2. Grave and sudden provocation
  3. Attempt
  4. Insanity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Grave and sudden provocation

Led to abolition of jury trials in India.
