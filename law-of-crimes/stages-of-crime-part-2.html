About this question set

This set has 25 multiple-choice questions on Stages of Crime from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 4, Law of Crimes. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key. This set is an answer-key drill: check each answer, then revisit the notes for any you miss.

25 practice questions with answers

1Section 511 IPC provides for punishment of attempt at:

  1. Full punishment
  2. One-fourth punishment
  3. Half the punishment of the completed offence (where no separate provision exists)
  4. Life imprisonment only
Show answer

Answer: C. Half the punishment of the completed offence (where no separate provision exists)

Report this question

2Locus poenitentiae in the context of attempt means:

  1. The place where the crime was committed
  2. The last opportunity to withdraw before the act becomes an attempt
  3. The guilty mind of the accused
  4. The confession of the accused
Show answer

Answer: B. The last opportunity to withdraw before the act becomes an attempt

3In Saju v. State of Kerala (2001), the Supreme Court held regarding common intention (Section 34):

  1. Common intention always requires prior planning of at least 24 hours
  2. Common intention requires a written agreement
  3. Common intention cannot develop during the commission of the act
  4. Common intention can develop on the spot - it need not always be pre-planned; a sudden meeting of minds immediately before the act is sufficient
Show answer

Answer: D. Common intention can develop on the spot - it need not always be pre-planned; a sudden meeting of minds immediately before the act is sufficient

4Under BNS 2023, abetment of an offence outside India which is committed in India is punishable under:

  1. Section 48 BNS
  2. Section 51 BNS
  3. Section 55 BNS
  4. Section 60 BNS
Show answer

Answer: B. Section 51 BNS

5Under BNS 2023, abetment is defined under Section 48. Which of the following is NOT one of the three modes of abetment?

  1. Abetment by instigation
  2. Abetment by conspiracy
  3. Abetment by intimidation
  4. Abetment by intentional aid
Show answer

Answer: C. Abetment by intimidation

Report this question

6Which of the following statements about attempt to commit suicide under BNS 2023 is correct?

  1. BNS 2023 made attempt to commit suicide a more serious offence
  2. Attempt to commit suicide was never an offence under IPC
  3. Section 309 IPC (attempt to commit suicide) has been effectively decriminalised - BNS 2023 does not retain it as a punishable offence
  4. BNS 2023 punishes attempt to commit suicide with 2 years imprisonment
Show answer

Answer: C. Section 309 IPC (attempt to commit suicide) has been effectively decriminalised - BNS 2023 does not retain it as a punishable offence

Report this question

7The equivocality test for distinguishing attempt from preparation requires:

  1. The accused must be caught at the scene
  2. Two witnesses must confirm the attempt
  3. The accused must have admitted the attempt
  4. The act must unequivocally point toward commission of the crime - it must have no innocent explanation
Show answer

Answer: D. The act must unequivocally point toward commission of the crime - it must have no innocent explanation

Report this question

8Under BNS 2023, the punishment for attempt where no specific punishment is provided is:

  1. Half the longest term of imprisonment provided for the offence, or same fine, or both
  2. Full punishment as for the completed offence
  3. One-fourth of the maximum punishment
  4. Minimum 3 years in all cases
Show answer

Answer: A. Half the longest term of imprisonment provided for the offence, or same fine, or both

Report this question

9Withdrawal after a criminal conspiracy is formed:

  1. Always absolves the person of all liability
  2. Is only possible before the agreement is formed
  3. BNS 2023 introduced withdrawal as a complete defence for the first time
  4. Does NOT absolve a conspirator of liability for the conspiracy offence already completed at the agreement stage
Show answer

Answer: D. Does NOT absolve a conspirator of liability for the conspiracy offence already completed at the agreement stage

10Under BNS 2023, making preparation to commit dacoity is covered under:

  1. Section 310 BNS
  2. Section 315 BNS
  3. Section 312 BNS
  4. Section 308 BNS
Show answer

Answer: C. Section 312 BNS

11Which of the following was a NEW category of inchoate offence introduced by BNS 2023 with NO equivalent in IPC?

  1. Abetment
  2. Criminal conspiracy
  3. Attempt
  4. Organised crime (Section 111) and Petty organised crime (Section 112)
Show answer

Answer: D. Organised crime (Section 111) and Petty organised crime (Section 112)

12The case of Kehar Singh v. State (Delhi Administration) (1988) is significant for criminal conspiracy because:

  1. It held that a conspiracy can be inferred from circumstances - direct proof of agreement is not necessary; circumstantial evidence suffices
  2. It required documentary proof of the conspiracy agreement
  3. It held conspiracy requires minimum 5 persons
  4. It limited conspiracy only to offences against the State
Show answer

Answer: A. It held that a conspiracy can be inferred from circumstances - direct proof of agreement is not necessary; circumstantial evidence suffices

13Abetment of an offence that is never actually attempted or committed is punishable under:

  1. Section 107 IPC only
  2. Not punishable at all
  3. Sections 115/116 IPC / Sections 57/58 BNS
  4. Section 120A IPC only
Show answer

Answer: C. Sections 115/116 IPC / Sections 57/58 BNS

14Under BNS 2023, criminal conspiracy is defined in:

15A person who instigates a child below 7 years (doli incapax) to commit an offence is liable as:

  1. Principal offender
  2. Abettor under Section 107 IPC / Section 48 BNS
  3. Not liable at all
  4. Liable only under POCSO

16Locus poenitentiae as a defence is UNAVAILABLE once:

24The correct sequential order of modes of abetment under Section 107 IPC / Section 48 BNS is:

  1. Instigation → Conspiracy → Intentional Aid
  2. Conspiracy → Instigation → Intentional Aid
