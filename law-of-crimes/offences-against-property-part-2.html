6Assertion (A): Dacoity conviction can sustain even if only four out of the originally charged five accused persons are ultimately convicted. Reason (R): The requirement of five or more persons in Section 391 IPC refers to persons who must be PROVED to have conjointly committed robbery.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true
Show answer

Answer: D. A is false but R is true

7Assertion (A): Robbery is an aggravated form of both theft and extortion under IPC/BNS. Reason (R): Robbery becomes dacoity when committed by five or more persons conjointly, making dacoity an aggravated form of robbery.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true

Answer: B. Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A

8Under IPC/BNS, 'house-breaking' (Section 445 IPC/Section 331 BNS) is defined as entering a house or building by:

  1. Any forcible entry
  2. Only by breaking a door or window
  3. Only by using a weapon to enter
  4. Six specific modes including scaling walls, using a key not intended for the lock, through a passage not meant for human entry, or through any passage opened by the offender himself

Answer: D. Six specific modes including scaling walls, using a key not intended for the lock, through a passage not meant for human entry, or through any passage opened by the offender himself

9'Lurking house-trespass by night' (Section 444 IPC) is more serious than ordinary house-trespass because:

  1. It is committed between sunset and sunrise — punishment is up to 3 years RI + fine; house-breaking by night (Section 446) carries up to 14 years
  2. It requires use of weapons
  3. It always involves theft
  4. It requires at least 3 persons
Show answer

Answer: A. It is committed between sunset and sunrise — punishment is up to 3 years RI + fine; house-breaking by night (Section 446) carries up to 14 years

  3. Damage to government property only

  1. Any entry onto another's land
  2. Entry with weapons
  3. Entry at night only
  4. Entry into or remaining on property in possession of another with intent to commit an offence, or to intimidate, insult or annoy the person in possession

13The distinction between 'theft' and 'criminal misappropriation' lies in the fact that in criminal misappropriation:

  1. Criminal misappropriation always involves more valuable property
  2. The property comes into the accused's possession INNOCENTLY (found, received accidentally) and the dishonest intent arises LATER; in theft the dishonest intent exists FROM THE START
  3. Criminal misappropriation requires entrustment
  4. They are identical offences
Show answer

Answer: B. The property comes into the accused's possession INNOCENTLY (found, received accidentally) and the dishonest intent arises LATER; in theft the dishonest intent exists FROM THE START

14Under BNS 2023, 'mischief by fire or explosive substance' destroying a house or vessel (Section 436 IPC equivalent) is punishable under:

  1. Maximum 3 years
  2. Section 326 BNS — imprisonment for life or up to 10 years with fine
  3. Death penalty
  4. Fine only

15The offence of 'receiving stolen property' under IPC/BNS requires the receiver to:

  1. Have stolen the property personally
  2. Dishonestly receive or retain property knowing or having reason to believe it to be stolen — Section 411 IPC/Section 317 BNS
  3. Be the principal offender in the theft
  4. Have a prior criminal conviction
Show answer

Answer: B. Dishonestly receive or retain property knowing or having reason to believe it to be stolen — Section 411 IPC/Section 317 BNS

17In cases of 'cheating' under Section 415 IPC/Section 318 BNS, the deception must occur:

  1. After the delivery of property
  2. Simultaneously with delivery
  3. At any point during the transaction
  4. BEFORE the delivery of property or the act/omission — the victim must be induced BY the deception; deception occurring after delivery does not constitute cheating
Show answer

Answer: D. BEFORE the delivery of property or the act/omission — the victim must be induced BY the deception; deception occurring after delivery does not constitute cheating

18BNS 2023 Section 112 (petty organised crime) covers organised theft and snatching. This is significant because:

  1. It treats systematic gang-based property crime as a separate, more serious category with enhanced punishment (up to 7 years) compared to ordinary theft under Section 303 BNS
  2. It reduces punishment for organised theft
  3. It decriminalises small-scale theft
  4. It only applies to crimes above Rs. 1 lakh in value
Show answer

Answer: A. It treats systematic gang-based property crime as a separate, more serious category with enhanced punishment (up to 7 years) compared to ordinary theft under Section 303 BNS

19Under IPC/BNS, 'extortion' (Section 383 IPC/Section 308 BNS) is complete when:

  1. The person is put in fear of injury AND is thereby dishonestly induced to deliver property — both elements must occur; mere threat without delivery may constitute attempt to extort
  2. Property is physically taken by force
  3. A weapon is used
  4. The threat is in writing only
Show answer

Answer: A. The person is put in fear of injury AND is thereby dishonestly induced to deliver property — both elements must occur; mere threat without delivery may constitute attempt to extort
