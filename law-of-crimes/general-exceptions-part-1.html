About this question set

This set has 21 multiple-choice questions on General Exceptions from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 4, Law of Crimes. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key. This set is an answer-key drill: check each answer, then revisit the notes for any you miss.

  2. M'Naghten Rules

7Intoxication as a defence under IPC Section 85 is available when:

  1. The person voluntarily got intoxicated
  2. The intoxication was administered without his knowledge or against his will
  3. The person was partially drunk
  4. Always available as a defence

12The M'Naghten Rules (1843) require for the insanity defence that:

  1. The accused must be unable to resist impulse
  2. The accused must be suffering from such a defect of reason from disease of mind as not to know the nature of the act, or that it was wrong
  3. The accused must have been intoxicated
  4. The accused must be a minor

13The right of private defence CANNOT be exercised against:

  1. An armed robber
  2. A person committing rape
  3. A public servant acting in good faith

  1. Consent

  3. Necessity
  4. Insanity

15In Deo Narain v. State of UP, the Supreme Court held that in cases of private defence:

  1. The right of private defence must be proved beyond reasonable doubt
  2. The accused need not prove the right beyond reasonable doubt - a preponderance of probability is sufficient
  3. The right does not extend to causing death at all
  4. Section 100 applies only to women
Show answer

Answer: B. The accused need not prove the right beyond reasonable doubt - a preponderance of probability is sufficient

16Section 95 IPC / Section 34 BNS (act causing slight harm) is based on the maxim:

  1. Actus non facit reum nisi mens sit rea
  2. Ignorantia juris non excusat
  3. De minimis non curat lex - the law does not concern itself with trifles
  4. Res ipsa loquitur
Show answer

Answer: C. De minimis non curat lex - the law does not concern itself with trifles

17Section 78 IPC (Section 16 BNS) protects:

  1. Act done under mistake of fact
  2. Act done in pursuance of a judgment/order of court, done in good faith
  3. Act done in private defence
  4. Act done under necessity

18Section 92 IPC / Section 30 BNS protects:

  1. Act done with patient's consent
  2. Act done under court order
  3. Act done in good faith for benefit of a person who is incapable of giving consent
  4. Act done in private defence
