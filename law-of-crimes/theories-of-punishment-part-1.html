About this question set

This set has 19 multiple-choice questions on Theories of Punishment from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 4, Law of Crimes. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key. This set is an answer-key drill: check each answer, then revisit the notes for any you miss.

19 practice questions with answers

1Which theory of punishment focuses on reforming the offender?

  1. Retributive theory
  2. Deterrent theory
  3. Reformative theory
  4. Preventive theory
Show answer

Answer: C. Reformative theory

Report this question

2The 'eye for an eye' concept reflects which theory of punishment?

  1. Deterrent
  2. Retributive
  3. Reformative
  4. Preventive
Show answer

Answer: B. Retributive

Report this question

3Compensation to victims of crime in India is primarily governed by:

  1. Section 357 and 357A CrPC / Section 395 BNSS
  2. IPC Section 302
  3. CPC Order 21
  4. Evidence Act
Show answer

Answer: A. Section 357 and 357A CrPC / Section 395 BNSS

Report this question

4The Supreme Court laid down guidelines on victim compensation in:

  1. Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab
  2. Machhi Singh v. State of Punjab
  3. Kehar Singh v. State
  4. Ankush Shivaji Gaikwad v. State of Maharashtra (2013)
Show answer

Answer: D. Ankush Shivaji Gaikwad v. State of Maharashtra (2013)

Report this question

5Capital punishment in India was upheld as constitutional in:

  1. Machhi Singh v. State of Punjab
  2. Jagmohan Singh v. State of UP
  3. Deena v. Union of India
  4. Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab (1980) - rarest of rare doctrine
Show answer

Answer: D. Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab (1980) - rarest of rare doctrine

Report this question

6Which of the following punishments is NOT prescribed under IPC/BNS for Indian criminal offences?

  1. Banishment (transportation beyond seas - abolished)
  2. Death
  3. Imprisonment for life
  4. Fine
Show answer

Answer: A. Banishment (transportation beyond seas - abolished)

Report this question

7The deterrent theory of punishment aims at:

  1. Reforming the offender
  2. Exacting revenge for the wrong done
  3. Preventing the offender from re-offending by incapacitation
  4. Deterring the offender and society at large from committing crime through fear of punishment
Show answer

Answer: D. Deterring the offender and society at large from committing crime through fear of punishment

Report this question

8The preventive theory of punishment is best illustrated by:

  1. Death penalty
  2. Community service
  3. Probation
  4. Imprisonment that incapacitates the offender during the period of sentence
Show answer

Answer: D. Imprisonment that incapacitates the offender during the period of sentence

Report this question

9Section 357A CrPC introduced:

  1. State-funded victim compensation schemes - every State government must prepare a scheme
  2. Mandatory compensation in all offences
  3. Compensation only for rape victims
  4. Compensation funded by the convict alone
Show answer

Answer: A. State-funded victim compensation schemes - every State government must prepare a scheme

Report this question

10In Ankush Shivaji Gaikwad v. State of Maharashtra (2013), the Supreme Court held:

  1. Courts have no obligation to award compensation
  2. Compensation can be awarded only in cases of acquittal
  3. Courts must mandatorily consider awarding compensation to victims under Section 357 CrPC - non-consideration requires reasons
  4. Compensation is awarded only in civil suits
Show answer

Answer: C. Courts must mandatorily consider awarding compensation to victims under Section 357 CrPC - non-consideration requires reasons

Report this question

11The Probation of Offenders Act 1958 is most closely aligned with which theoretical framework?

  1. Retributive theory
  2. Reformative theory
  3. Deterrent theory
  4. Preventive theory
Show answer

Answer: B. Reformative theory

Report this question

12Community service has been introduced as a form of punishment under BNS 2023. This corresponds to which theory?

  1. Retributive theory
  2. Deterrent theory
  3. Reformative and restorative theory
  4. Preventive theory
Show answer

Answer: C. Reformative and restorative theory

Report this question

13Under BNSS 2023, the victim compensation scheme (equivalent to Section 357A CrPC) is located at:

  1. Section 357 BNSS
  2. Section 400 BNSS
  3. Section 396 BNSS
  4. Section 380 BNSS
Show answer

Answer: C. Section 396 BNSS

Report this question

14Suresh v. State of Haryana (2015) held regarding victim compensation that:

  1. Compensation can only be awarded at trial court level
  2. Compensation under Section 357A CrPC can be awarded even at the appellate stage and even when the accused is acquitted
  3. Compensation requires the accused to be convicted
  4. Only rape victims can claim compensation
Show answer

Answer: B. Compensation under Section 357A CrPC can be awarded even at the appellate stage and even when the accused is acquitted

Report this question

15Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab (1980) specifically disapproved which earlier case on scope of death penalty?

  1. Jagmohan Singh v. State of UP (1973)
  2. Ediga Anamma v. State of AP (1974)
  3. Rajendra Prasad v. State of UP (1979) - which had held death penalty should be limited to threats to national security
  4. Machhi Singh v. State of Punjab (1983)
Show answer

Answer: C. Rajendra Prasad v. State of UP (1979) - which had held death penalty should be limited to threats to national security

Report this question

16Plea bargaining in India (Chapter XXIA CrPC) is most consistent with which theory of punishment?

  1. Retributive theory
  2. Restorative justice
  3. Deterrent theory
  4. Preventive theory
Show answer

Answer: B. Restorative justice

Report this question

17The principle of proportionality of punishment in Indian sentencing law means:

  1. Punishment must be commensurate with the gravity of the offence - neither grossly disproportionate (violating Article 21) nor inadequate
  2. All offences must receive equal punishment
  3. Courts have no discretion in sentencing
  4. Minimum sentences are always constitutionally valid
Show answer

Answer: A. Punishment must be commensurate with the gravity of the offence - neither grossly disproportionate (violating Article 21) nor inadequate

Report this question

18The reformative theory of punishment is best operationalised through which of the following mechanisms in Indian law?

  1. The Probation of Offenders Act 1958 - which allows courts to release first-time and young offenders on probation
  2. Death penalty
  3. Mandatory minimum sentences
  4. Solitary confinement
Show answer

Answer: A. The Probation of Offenders Act 1958 - which allows courts to release first-time and young offenders on probation

Report this question

19Restorative justice as distinct from all four classical punishment theories focuses on:

  1. Deterring future crime through public punishment
  2. Repairing the harm done - involving the victim, offender, and community in reconciliation and reparation
  3. Incapacitating the offender
  4. Exacting retribution proportionate to the crime
Show answer

Answer: B. Repairing the harm done - involving the victim, offender, and community in reconciliation and reparation
