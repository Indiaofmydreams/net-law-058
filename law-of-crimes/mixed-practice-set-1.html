About this question set

This set has 20 multiple-choice questions on Law of Crimes from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 4, Law of Crimes. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 20 of the 20 questions carry a written explanation.

20 practice questions with answers

1'Actus non facit reum nisi mens sit rea' means:

  1. Act is crime
  2. Mind is crime
  3. Intention is not needed
  4. Act alone does not make guilty unless mind is guilty
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Act alone does not make guilty unless mind is guilty

Both a guilty act and a guilty mind are generally required.

Report this question

2In R v. Prince (1875), the court held:

  1. Mistake of age excused the accused
  2. Mens rea was essential
  3. Consent was a defence
  4. Knowledge of age was not required
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Knowledge of age was not required

Strict interpretation of the statutory offence of abduction.

Report this question

3'They also serve who only stand and wait' appears in:

  1. Barendra Kumar Ghosh v. King Emperor
  2. Nanak Chand
  3. Mahboob Shah
  4. Virsa Singh
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Barendra Kumar Ghosh v. King Emperor

A leading case on joint liability and common intention.

Report this question

4Abetment may be by instigation, conspiracy or:

  1. Silence
  2. Intentional aid
  3. Omission only
  4. Possession
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Intentional aid

Intentional aiding is the third mode of abetment.

Report this question

5Criminal conspiracy requires an agreement between:

  1. Two or more persons
  2. Only public servants
  3. At least five persons
  4. Three persons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Two or more persons

Agreement is the essence of the offence.

Report this question

6Abhayanand Mishra v. State of Bihar is associated with:

  1. Defamation
  2. Provocation
  3. Attempt
  4. Insanity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Attempt

The case discusses the stage where preparation becomes attempt.

Report this question

7Right of private defence is not available where:

  1. There is time to approach public authorities
  2. A woman is attacked
  3. Body is attacked
  4. Property is attacked
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. There is time to approach public authorities

The right does not exist where recourse to authorities is possible.

Report this question

8The M'Naghten rules relate to:

  1. Duress
  2. Necessity
  3. Intoxication
  4. Insanity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Insanity

Laid down in 1843 as the test of insanity.

Report this question

9The 'rarest of rare' doctrine for the death penalty comes from:

  1. Jagmohan Singh
  2. Mithu
  3. Machhi Singh
  4. Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab

Bachan Singh (1980) upheld the death penalty in rarest of rare cases.

Report this question

10Mandatory death sentence was struck down in:

  1. Mithu v. State of Punjab
  2. Rajendra Prasad
  3. Bachan Singh
  4. Shatrughan Chauhan
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Mithu v. State of Punjab

Mithu (1983) held a mandatory death sentence unconstitutional.

Report this question

11The reformative theory of punishment emphasises:

  1. Revenge
  2. Deterrence of others
  3. Rehabilitation of the offender
  4. Incapacitation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Rehabilitation of the offender

It aims to reform and reintegrate the offender.

Report this question

12The Dowry Prohibition Act was enacted in:

  1. 1955
  2. 1961
  3. 1986
  4. 1984
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1961

The Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961.

Report this question

13Under POCSO Act 2012, a 'child' is a person below:

  1. 16 years
  2. 21 years
  3. 14 years
  4. 18 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 18 years

POCSO defines a child as anyone below eighteen.

Report this question

14The principal terror law in India is the:

  1. PMLA, 2002
  2. Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 1967
  3. NDPS Act, 1985
  4. Arms Act, 1959
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 1967

UAPA was amended to cover terrorist acts.

Report this question

15The NDPS Act was enacted in:

  1. 1975
  2. 1995
  3. 2005
  4. 1985
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 1985

Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, 1985.

Report this question

16In 2022 the Supreme Court put the sedition provision in abeyance in:

  1. Kedar Nath Singh
  2. Balwant Singh
  3. Shreya Singhal
  4. S.G. Vombatkere v. Union of India
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. S.G. Vombatkere v. Union of India

The Court directed that pending cases be kept in abeyance.

Report this question

17The Criminal Law (Amendment) Act, 2013 followed the report of:

  1. Justice Verma Committee
  2. Law Commission only
  3. Malimath Committee
  4. Padmanabhaiah Committee
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Justice Verma Committee

It followed the Nirbhaya incident of December 2012.

Report this question

18Corporate criminal liability was upheld in:

  1. Salomon v. Salomon
  2. Vishaka
  3. Standard Chartered Bank v. Directorate of Enforcement
  4. Shreya Singhal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Standard Chartered Bank v. Directorate of Enforcement

A company can be prosecuted even where imprisonment is mandatory.

Report this question

19An unlawful assembly requires at least:

  1. Three persons
  2. Five persons
  3. Seven persons
  4. Ten persons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Five persons

Five or more persons with a common unlawful object.

Report this question

20A child below what age is conclusively presumed incapable of crime in India?

  1. Five years
  2. Seven years
  3. Ten years
  4. Twelve years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Seven years

Under both IPC and BNS, a child under seven is exempt.
