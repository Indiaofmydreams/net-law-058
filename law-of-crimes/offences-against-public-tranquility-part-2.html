This set has 16 multiple-choice questions on Offences Against Public Tranquility from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 4, Law of Crimes. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key. This set is an answer-key drill: check each answer, then revisit the notes for any you miss.

16 practice questions with answers

1Under BNS 2023, the rioting provision (Section 146 IPC equivalent) is at:

  1. Section 191 BNS
  2. Section 187 BNS
  3. Section 193 BNS
  4. Section 196 BNS
Show answer

Answer: A. Section 191 BNS

2Section 144 CrPC (now Section 163 BNSS) allows a Magistrate to issue prohibitory orders. Such orders can remain in force for a maximum of:

  1. 2 months in ordinary cases; up to 6 months in cases of danger to human life, health or safety or a likelihood of riot or affray
  2. 1 month only
  3. 6 months always
  4. Unlimited period
Show answer

Answer: A. 2 months in ordinary cases; up to 6 months in cases of danger to human life, health or safety or a likelihood of riot or affray

3The five specific common objects of an unlawful assembly under Section 141 IPC / Section 187 BNS include which of the following?

  1. To commit any offence whatsoever
  2. To overawe by criminal force the Central or any State Government or Parliament or the Legislature of any State or any public servant
  3. To protest against government policies
  4. To organize a trade union strike
Show answer

Answer: B. To overawe by criminal force the Central or any State Government or Parliament or the Legislature of any State or any public servant

4Under IPC/BNS, a member who joins an unlawful assembly AFTER it has been commanded to disperse:

  1. Cannot be held liable as he was not present at formation
  2. Is only guilty if he personally commits violence
  3. Is guilty of an offence under Section 145 IPC / Section 190 BNS, even if the assembly was originally lawful
  4. Is covered under Section 34 IPC only
Show answer

Answer: C. Is guilty of an offence under Section 145 IPC / Section 190 BNS, even if the assembly was originally lawful

5Under BNS 2023, 'wantonly giving provocation with intent to cause riot' (equivalent to Section 153 IPC) is at:

  1. Section 191 BNS
  2. Section 194 BNS
  3. Section 196 BNS
  4. Section 198 BNS
Show answer

Answer: B. Section 194 BNS

6Which of the following is the correct statement about 'communal violence' under Indian criminal law?

  1. There is no single standalone 'communal violence' law currently in force; such acts are prosecuted under IPC/BNS Sections 153A, 295A, 302, etc. The Communal Violence Bill has not been enacted.
  2. The Communal Violence Act 2006 is in force
  3. Communal violence is dealt with exclusively under UAPA
  4. Communal violence is a state subject and states have their own laws only
Show answer

Answer: A. There is no single standalone 'communal violence' law currently in force; such acts are prosecuted under IPC/BNS Sections 153A, 295A, 302, etc. The Communal Violence Bill has not been enacted.

7Section 295A IPC / Section 299 BNS deals with:

  1. Deliberate and malicious acts intended to outrage religious feelings of any class by insulting its religion or religious beliefs — punishable with up to 3 years or fine or both
  2. Promoting enmity between groups
  3. Rioting at a place of worship
  4. Destruction of religious property
Show answer

Answer: A. Deliberate and malicious acts intended to outrage religious feelings of any class by insulting its religion or religious beliefs — punishable with up to 3 years or fine or both

8Under BNS 2023, the equivalent of Section 295A IPC (outraging religious feelings) is at:

  1. Section 196 BNS
  2. Section 302 BNS
  3. Section 299 BNS
  4. Section 197 BNS

9The Supreme Court in Arup Bhuyan v. State of Assam (2011) held regarding membership of a banned organisation under UAPA that:

  1. Mere membership of a banned organisation is NOT sufficient for conviction — there must be proof of active participation in violent activities or incitement to violence
  2. Mere membership is sufficient for conviction
  3. Membership of banned organisations is not an offence at all
  4. The case was overruled by a larger bench immediately
Show answer

Answer: A. Mere membership of a banned organisation is NOT sufficient for conviction — there must be proof of active participation in violent activities or incitement to violence

10Under BNS 2023, 'disobedience to order promulgated by public servant' (equivalent to Section 188 IPC) is at:

  1. Section 187 BNS
  2. Section 195 BNS
  3. Section 223 BNS
  4. Section 200 BNS

11The armed forces can be called to disperse an unlawful assembly under which provision of CrPC/BNSS?

  1. Section 144 CrPC always
  2. Only under the Armed Forces Special Powers Act
  3. Only under the National Security Act
  4. Section 130 CrPC / Section 147 BNSS — when civilian authorities find it impossible to disperse the assembly
Show answer

Answer: D. Section 130 CrPC / Section 147 BNSS — when civilian authorities find it impossible to disperse the assembly

12Under BNS 2023, 'making atmosphere conducive to riot' through wanton provocation (Section 153 IPC equivalent — Section 194 BNS). What is the maximum punishment?

  1. Imprisonment up to 1 year or fine or both; if riot actually ensues — up to 3 years or fine or both
  2. Life imprisonment
  3. Death penalty
  4. Maximum fine of Rs. 500 only
Show answer

Answer: A. Imprisonment up to 1 year or fine or both; if riot actually ensues — up to 3 years or fine or both

13Under IPC/BNS, a person is liable as a member of an unlawful assembly even if:

  1. He did not personally commit the specific offence — constructive liability under Section 149 IPC/Section 190 BNS makes every member liable for acts done in prosecution of the common object
  2. He was merely passing by the scene
  3. He physically opposed the commission of the offence
  4. He withdrew before the offence was committed and informed the police
Show answer

Answer: A. He did not personally commit the specific offence — constructive liability under Section 149 IPC/Section 190 BNS makes every member liable for acts done in prosecution of the common object

14Section 505(2) IPC (statements creating or promoting enmity between classes) differs from Section 153A IPC in that:

  1. They are identical
  2. Section 505(2) requires violence to have occurred
  3. Section 153A applies only to spoken words
  4. Section 505(2) focuses specifically on STATEMENTS made with intent to incite one class against another; Section 153A has broader application to acts, words, and representations
Show answer

Answer: D. Section 505(2) focuses specifically on STATEMENTS made with intent to incite one class against another; Section 153A has broader application to acts, words, and representations

15The Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Amendment Act 2019's most controversial provision allows:

  1. Designation of INDIVIDUALS (not just organisations) as terrorists under Section 35 UAPA — without a trial or conviction
  2. Death penalty for all UAPA offences
  3. Indefinite detention without bail in all UAPA cases
  4. States to designate terrorist organisations independently
Show answer

Answer: A. Designation of INDIVIDUALS (not just organisations) as terrorists under Section 35 UAPA — without a trial or conviction

16The difference between 'riot' (Section 146 IPC/Section 191 BNS) and 'affray' (Section 159 IPC) is:

  1. Riot requires unlawful assembly of 5+ persons using force; affray requires only 2+ persons fighting in a public place — affray has a lower threshold but also lower punishment
  2. They are identical offences with different names
  3. Affray requires 5 persons; riot requires 2
  4. Riot is always non-bailable; affray is always bailable
Show answer

Answer: A. Riot requires unlawful assembly of 5+ persons using force; affray requires only 2+ persons fighting in a public place — affray has a lower threshold but also lower punishment
