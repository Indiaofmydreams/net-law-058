About this question set

This set has 19 multiple-choice questions on General Principles from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 4, Law of Crimes. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key. This set is an answer-key drill: check each answer, then revisit the notes for any you miss.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Law of Crimes notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

19 practice questions with answers

1The Latin term actus reus refers to:

  1. Guilty mind
  2. Guilty act
  3. Criminal intention
  4. Mental element of crime
Show answer

Answer: B. Guilty act

Report this question

2Mens rea literally means:

  1. Physical act
  2. Omission
  3. Guilty mind
  4. Strict liability
Show answer

Answer: C. Guilty mind

Report this question

3Which of the following best describes the doctrine of mens rea?

  1. A person can be punished without any mental element
  2. Criminal liability requires both a wrongful act and a guilty mind
  3. Only the act matters, not the intention
  4. Intention is irrelevant in criminal law
Show answer

Answer: B. Criminal liability requires both a wrongful act and a guilty mind

Report this question

4Under BNS 2023, the term used for culpable homicide requires which mental element?

  1. Negligence only
  2. Intention or knowledge
  3. Motive
  4. Strict liability
Show answer

Answer: B. Intention or knowledge

Report this question

5Constructive liability in criminal law means:

  1. Liability based solely on direct intent
  2. Liability imposed on a person for acts of another in certain circumstances
  3. Liability arising from contract
  4. No liability without proof of mens rea
Show answer

Answer: B. Liability imposed on a person for acts of another in certain circumstances

Report this question

6The principle 'actus non facit reum nisi mens sit rea' means:

  1. An act does not make a man guilty unless the mind is also guilty
  2. Every act is criminal
  3. A guilty mind alone is sufficient for liability
  4. Ignorance of law is no excuse
Show answer

Answer: A. An act does not make a man guilty unless the mind is also guilty

Report this question

7Group liability under IPC is covered under:

  1. Section 76 and 79
  2. Section 34 and 149
  3. Section 300 and 302
  4. Section 120A and 120B
Show answer

Answer: B. Section 34 and 149

Report this question

8Section 34 IPC / BNS deals with:

  1. Criminal conspiracy
  2. Common object
  3. Common intention
  4. Abetment
Show answer

Answer: C. Common intention

Report this question

9The difference between Section 34 (common intention) and Section 149 (common object) IPC is:

  1. Section 34 requires unlawful assembly; Section 149 does not
  2. Section 34 does not require unlawful assembly; Section 149 requires unlawful assembly of 5 or more persons
  3. Both are identical
  4. Section 149 applies to two persons only
Show answer

Answer: B. Section 34 does not require unlawful assembly; Section 149 requires unlawful assembly of 5 or more persons

Report this question

10Vicarious liability in criminal law is:

  1. The rule, not the exception
  2. An exception to the general principle that mens rea must be personal
  3. Applicable in all IPC offences
  4. Applicable only to civil cases
Show answer

Answer: B. An exception to the general principle that mens rea must be personal

Report this question

11Which of the following is NOT a form of mens rea recognised under Indian criminal law?

  1. Intention
  2. Knowledge
  3. Recklessness (as expressly defined)
  4. Motive
Show answer

Answer: D. Motive

Report this question

12Under BNS 2023, Section 34 IPC (common intention) corresponds to:

  1. Section 3(5)
  2. Section 34
  3. Section 60
  4. Section 49
Show answer

Answer: A. Section 3(5)

Report this question

13Knowledge as mens rea differs from intention in that:

  1. They are identical
  2. Knowledge means certainty of consequence; intention means desire of consequence
  3. Knowledge requires higher proof
  4. Intention does not require awareness
Show answer

Answer: B. Knowledge means certainty of consequence; intention means desire of consequence

Report this question

14Which case established that common intention requires prior meeting of minds?

  1. K.M. Nanavati v. State of Maharashtra
  2. State of UP v. Ram Swarup
  3. Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab
  4. Mahbub Shah v. Emperor
Show answer

Answer: D. Mahbub Shah v. Emperor

Report this question

15Strict liability offences in criminal law are those where:

  1. Mens rea must always be proved
  2. Liability arises regardless of guilty mind
  3. Only intention matters
  4. Negligence must be established
Show answer

Answer: B. Liability arises regardless of guilty mind

Report this question

16Which of the following maxims encapsulates the entire philosophy of criminal liability?

  1. Ignorantia juris non excusat
  2. Res ipsa loquitur
  3. Actus non facit reum nisi mens sit rea
  4. Nemo debet bis vexari
Show answer

Answer: C. Actus non facit reum nisi mens sit rea

Report this question

17In which of the following offences is mens rea NOT required under IPC/BNS?

  1. Murder
  2. Theft
  3. Public nuisance (strict liability)
  4. Cheating
Show answer

Answer: C. Public nuisance (strict liability)

Report this question

18Motive and intention in criminal law are:

  1. Identical concepts
  2. Different - motive is the reason behind the act; intention is the desire to bring about a consequence
  3. Motive is required for conviction; intention is not
  4. Both are irrelevant in Indian criminal law
Show answer

Answer: B. Different - motive is the reason behind the act; intention is the desire to bring about a consequence

Report this question

19In Barendra Kumar Ghosh v. King Emperor (1925), the Privy Council held regarding Section 34 IPC that:

  1. Physical presence at the scene is always required
  2. Even a person standing outside can be liable if there is common intention and participation
  3. Common intention requires a prior written agreement
  4. Section 34 applies only to murder
Show answer

Answer: B. Even a person standing outside can be liable if there is common intention and participation
