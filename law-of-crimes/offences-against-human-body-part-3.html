1Under BNS 2023, culpable homicide (Section 299 IPC equivalent) is at:

  1. Section 101 BNS
  2. Section 100 BNS
  3. Section 99 BNS
  4. Section 103 BNS
Show answer

Answer: B. Section 100 BNS

2Assertion (A): Under Section 300 IPC, culpable homicide is murder if the act is done with intention to cause bodily injury sufficient in the ordinary course of nature to cause death. Reason (R): The sufficiency of injury in the ordinary course of nature is determined SUBJECTIVELY based on what the accused believed about the injury.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true
Show answer

Answer: C. A is true but R is false

3The five exceptions to Section 300 IPC that reduce murder to culpable homicide are in correct order:

  1. Grave and sudden provocation → Exceeding private defence → Public servant exceeding powers → Sudden fight → Consent of victim above 18 with risk of death
  2. Consent → Sudden fight → Private defence → Provocation → Public servant
  3. Private defence → Consent → Provocation → Sudden fight → Public servant
  4. Sudden fight → Provocation → Consent → Public servant → Private defence
Show answer

Answer: A. Grave and sudden provocation → Exceeding private defence → Public servant exceeding powers → Sudden fight → Consent of victim above 18 with risk of death

  3. 338, 339, 359, 361
  4. 341, 342, 362, 364

  2. Section 100 BNS
  3. Section 110 BNS
  4. Section 95 BNS

7The Medical Termination of Pregnancy Act 1971 (as amended in 2021) now allows termination up to:

  1. 20 weeks on opinion of one RMP; up to 24 weeks for specific categories (rape survivors, minors, disabled women); beyond 24 weeks by Medical Board in case of foetal abnormalities
  2. 12 weeks only
  3. 16 weeks for all cases
  4. No limit at all

8Under IPC/BNS, which of the following does NOT constitute 'hurt' under Section 319 IPC/Section 114 BNS?

  1. Breaking a bone
  2. Cutting a person's hair without their consent (hair is not a living part of the body under IPC interpretation — no bodily pain, disease or infirmity is caused to the body itself)
  3. Causing disease through infection
  4. Causing temporary infirmity
Show answer

Answer: B. Cutting a person's hair without their consent (hair is not a living part of the body under IPC interpretation — no bodily pain, disease or infirmity is caused to the body itself)

9Under IPC/BNS, 'wrongful restraint' (Section 339 IPC/Section 126 BNS) differs from 'wrongful confinement' (Section 340 IPC/Section 127 BNS) in that:

  1. Wrongful restraint prevents movement in a particular DIRECTION; wrongful confinement prevents movement in ALL directions (total restraint within a bounded area)
  2. They are identical
  3. Wrongful confinement requires physical force; restraint does not
  4. Wrongful restraint requires at least 3 persons
Show answer

Answer: A. Wrongful restraint prevents movement in a particular DIRECTION; wrongful confinement prevents movement in ALL directions (total restraint within a bounded area)

11The Supreme Court in Ramesh Harijan v. State of UP (2012) held on the 'grave and sudden provocation' exception (Exception 1, S.300 IPC) that:

  1. Words alone can never constitute grave and sudden provocation under any circumstances
  2. Provocation must always come from a physical act
  3. Provocation must be witnessed by a third party
  4. Words and gestures alone CAN constitute grave and sudden provocation in exceptional circumstances — the earlier rigid rule that words alone can never provoke was relaxed
Show answer

Answer: D. Words and gestures alone CAN constitute grave and sudden provocation in exceptional circumstances — the earlier rigid rule that words alone can never provoke was relaxed

14The defence of 'consent' in cases of causing hurt (Section 87 IPC/Section 23 BNS) is NOT available when the hurt caused is:

  1. Any hurt whatsoever
  2. Hurt causing a bruise only
  3. Grievous hurt — Section 87 IPC allows consent as defence for causing hurt but NOT for grievous hurt
  4. Hurt in a sporting activity

Answer: C. Grievous hurt — Section 87 IPC allows consent as defence for causing hurt but NOT for grievous hurt

15The transferred malice doctrine (Section 301 IPC/Section 102 BNS) operates on the principle that:

  1. The accused is not guilty if the wrong person is killed
  2. The accused is only guilty of causing death by negligence
  3. The doctrine only applies when the intended victim is also injured
  4. The intention/knowledge in relation to the intended victim transfers to the unintended victim — making the accused as guilty as if the intended victim had been killed
Show answer

Answer: D. The intention/knowledge in relation to the intended victim transfers to the unintended victim — making the accused as guilty as if the intended victim had been killed

16The concept of 'constructive murder' in Indian law means that under the Fourth Clause of Section 300 IPC, the accused:

  1. Need not intend to cause death or even grievous hurt — if the act is so imminently dangerous that it must in all probability cause death and there is no excuse for the risk, it is murder
  2. Must intend to kill a specific person
  3. Must cause the death of multiple persons
  4. Must use a deadly weapon
Show answer

Answer: A. Need not intend to cause death or even grievous hurt — if the act is so imminently dangerous that it must in all probability cause death and there is no excuse for the risk, it is murder

18Section 307 IPC (attempt to murder) differs from Section 308 IPC (attempt to commit culpable homicide) in that:

  1. Section 307 requires the mental state equivalent to murder (Section 300); Section 308 requires only the mental state of culpable homicide not amounting to murder (Section 299)
  2. They are identical
  3. Section 308 is more serious than Section 307
  4. Section 307 requires actual injury; Section 308 does not

Answer: A. Section 307 requires the mental state equivalent to murder (Section 300); Section 308 requires only the mental state of culpable homicide not amounting to murder (Section 299)
