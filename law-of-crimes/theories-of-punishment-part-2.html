1The Supreme Court in Dagdu v. State of Maharashtra (1977) held that in sentencing:

  1. Sentencing hearing is optional
  2. The accused has no right to be heard on sentence
  3. Only the prosecution can address the court on sentence
  4. Courts must give an opportunity of hearing to the ACCUSED on the question of sentence SEPARATELY after conviction
Show answer

Answer: D. Courts must give an opportunity of hearing to the ACCUSED on the question of sentence SEPARATELY after conviction

2BNS 2023 introduced community service as punishment for certain offences including:

  1. Attempted suicide (Section 226 BNS), public intoxication (Section 355 BNS), and certain minor offences
  2. Murder and robbery
  3. All offences punishable with less than 3 years
  4. Only first-time offences
Show answer

Answer: A. Attempted suicide (Section 226 BNS), public intoxication (Section 355 BNS), and certain minor offences

3Incapacitation theory specifically argues for:

  1. Selective incapacitation - identifying and imprisoning the most dangerous, repeat, high-risk offenders for extended periods
  2. Imprisoning all offenders for equal periods
  3. Replacing imprisonment with rehabilitation universally
  4. Abolishing imprisonment entirely
Show answer

Answer: A. Selective incapacitation - identifying and imprisoning the most dangerous, repeat, high-risk offenders for extended periods

4Under BNSS 2023, what is the time limit within which a Special Court must complete trial in cases involving rape of a minor?

  1. 6 months
  2. 1 year
  3. No specific time limit
  4. Two months from the date of filing of the charge sheet
Show answer

Answer: D. Two months from the date of filing of the charge sheet

5Plea bargaining (Chapter XXIA CrPC) CANNOT be used for offences punishable with:

  1. Death, life imprisonment, or offences affecting socio-economic conditions
  2. Only death penalty
  3. All offences above 7 years
  4. All cognizable offences

Answer: A. Death, life imprisonment, or offences affecting socio-economic conditions

6The denunciation/expressive theory of punishment holds that punishment serves to:

  1. Deter potential offenders
  2. Express society's moral condemnation of the criminal act - reinforcing shared moral values
  3. Reform the offender
  4. Prevent the specific offender from reoffending
Show answer

Answer: B. Express society's moral condemnation of the criminal act - reinforcing shared moral values

7Therapeutic jurisprudence as an approach to punishment holds that:

  1. Only medical professionals should decide sentences
  2. All criminal law should be replaced by civil remedies
  3. The law and legal processes should promote psychological and emotional well-being of those involved - focusing on healing rather than purely punitive responses
  4. Punishment must always precede any therapeutic intervention

Answer: C. The law and legal processes should promote psychological and emotional well-being of those involved - focusing on healing rather than purely punitive responses

8The social defence theory (Marc Ancel) holds that:

  1. Society's primary right is to exact retribution
  2. Society must defend itself against crime through scientific study of offenders and treatment/rehabilitation aimed at social reintegration, not retributive punishment
  3. Death penalty is the only effective social defence
  4. Imprisonment must be the default response to all crime
Show answer

Answer: B. Society must defend itself against crime through scientific study of offenders and treatment/rehabilitation aimed at social reintegration, not retributive punishment

9Assertion (A): The rarest of rare doctrine in Bachan Singh makes death penalty mandatory for all murder convictions. Reason (R): The Supreme Court in Bachan Singh held that death penalty should be imposed only when the alternative of life imprisonment is unquestionably foreclosed after weighing aggravating and mitigating circumstances.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true

Answer: D. A is false but R is true

  2. Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true

  2. Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A

  1. Section 395 BNSS
  2. Section 400 BNSS
  3. Section 357 BNSS
  4. Section 396 BNSS

15Jeremy Bentham's 'felicific calculus' is most closely associated with which theory of punishment?

  1. Deterrent theory - punishment must be calibrated to ensure that the pain of punishment exceeds the pleasure of crime by just enough to deter
  2. Retributive theory
  3. Reformative theory
  4. Preventive theory
Show answer

Answer: A. Deterrent theory - punishment must be calibrated to ensure that the pain of punishment exceeds the pleasure of crime by just enough to deter

Report this question

16Kant's retributive theory of punishment holds that:

  1. Punishment is a categorical imperative - it must be imposed because justice demands it, regardless of any future social benefit
  2. Punishment must serve a useful social purpose
  3. Punishment should focus on reforming the offender
  4. Punishment is only justified when it prevents future crime
Show answer

Answer: A. Punishment is a categorical imperative - it must be imposed because justice demands it, regardless of any future social benefit

Report this question

17The general prevention aspect of deterrence theory was most classically articulated by:

  1. Jeremy Bentham in Introduction to Principles of Morals and Legislation
  2. Immanuel Kant in Metaphysics of Morals
  3. Herbert Hart in Punishment and Responsibility
  4. Cesare Beccaria in On Crimes and Punishments (1764) - arguing for certainty and swiftness of punishment over its severity
Show answer

Answer: D. Cesare Beccaria in On Crimes and Punishments (1764) - arguing for certainty and swiftness of punishment over its severity

18Which theorist is associated with the 'positive school' of criminology that attributed crime to biological, psychological, and social factors rather than free will?

  1. Cesare Beccaria
  2. Jeremy Bentham
  3. Cesare Lombroso - his work L'Uomo Delinquente (1876) argued criminals are identifiable by physical characteristics
  4. Immanuel Kant
Show answer

Answer: C. Cesare Lombroso - his work L'Uomo Delinquente (1876) argued criminals are identifiable by physical characteristics

19The 'just deserts' model of sentencing (associated with Andrew von Hirsch) holds that:

  1. Punishment must be whatever effectively deters future crime
  2. Punishment must focus primarily on reforming the offender
  3. Punishment must be proportionate to the seriousness of the offence committed - no more, no less
  4. Punishment must be determined by the victim's preferences

Answer: C. Punishment must be proportionate to the seriousness of the offence committed - no more, no less
