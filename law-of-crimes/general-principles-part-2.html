About this question set

This set has 19 multiple-choice questions on General Principles from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 4, Law of Crimes. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key. This set is an answer-key drill: check each answer, then revisit the notes for any you miss.

19 practice questions with answers

1The doctrine of transferred malice under Indian law means:

  1. Malice can be created by the court
  2. Intention can be transferred when the wrong person is harmed instead of the intended victim
  3. Mental element can be waived in serious crimes
  4. Co-accused carries the malice of the main accused
Show answer

Answer: B. Intention can be transferred when the wrong person is harmed instead of the intended victim

Report this question

2The maxim 'ignorantia juris non excusat' means:

  1. Ignorance of law is no excuse
  2. Ignorance of fact is no excuse
  3. A guilty mind is presumed from the act
  4. Every person is innocent until proven guilty
Show answer

Answer: A. Ignorance of law is no excuse

Report this question

3Fraudulently under IPC Section 25 means:

  1. Doing a thing dishonestly
  2. Doing a thing with intent to defraud but not otherwise
  3. Doing a thing knowingly
  4. Doing a thing negligently
Show answer

Answer: B. Doing a thing with intent to defraud but not otherwise

4The case of Standard Chartered Bank v. Directorate of Enforcement (2005) and Iridium India Telecom Ltd. v. Motorola Inc. (2011) established that:

  1. Only individuals can face criminal liability
  2. Corporate entities can be prosecuted for criminal offences under IPC
  3. Companies are immune from IPC provisions
  4. Only public sector companies face criminal liability
Show answer

Answer: B. Corporate entities can be prosecuted for criminal offences under IPC

5Under BNS 2023, Section 111 deals with:

  1. Murder
  2. Dacoity
  3. Organised crime - punishable with death or life imprisonment if it results in death of any person

  1. Murder

8Which of the following mental states is used in Section 415 IPC (cheating) but NOT in Section 378 IPC (theft)?

10A fires at B intending to kill him. The bullet misses B and kills C. Under which doctrine is A liable for C's murder?

  1. Common intention under Section 34 IPC
  2. Constructive liability under Section 149 IPC
  3. Doctrine of transferred malice - Section 301 IPC / Section 102 BNS
  4. Abetment under Section 107 IPC

Answer: C. Doctrine of transferred malice - Section 301 IPC / Section 102 BNS

11Under BNS 2023, which section defines 'dishonestly'?

  1. Section 2(8) BNS
  2. Section 24 IPC equivalent in BNS Section 25
  3. Section 2(10) BNS
  4. Section 3(2) BNS

12Which of the following statements about omission as actus reus is CORRECT under Indian criminal law?

  1. Omission is always punishable regardless of relationship between parties
  2. Omission is punishable only when there exists a specific legal duty to act, recognised by law
  3. BNS 2023 for the first time introduced omission as actus reus
  4. Omission is punishable only in murder cases

13In Nathulal v. State of Madhya Pradesh, the Supreme Court held that:

  1. In offences where the word 'knowingly' is used, the prosecution must prove actual knowledge - mere negligence is insufficient
  2. Knowledge and intention are identical concepts
  3. Strict liability applies to all food adulteration offences
  4. Motive is an essential ingredient of criminal liability
Show answer

Answer: A. In offences where the word 'knowingly' is used, the prosecution must prove actual knowledge - mere negligence is insufficient

14Under BNS 2023, 'fraudulently' is defined in:

  1. Section 25 IPC equivalent
  2. Section 3(5) BNS
  3. Section 2(10) BNS
  4. Section 2(14) BNS

15In Hari Singh Gond v. State of Madhya Pradesh (2008), the Supreme Court clarified that Section 34 IPC:

  1. Is not a substantive offence - it requires participation in the act, not merely presence
  2. Requires all accused to be physically present at the spot
  3. Can apply even without any common intention if co-accused share the same motive
  4. Always requires a prior written agreement

Answer: A. Is not a substantive offence - it requires participation in the act, not merely presence

18Which of the following correctly describes constructive liability?

  1. Liability that requires proof of individual participation in every act
  2. Criminal responsibility imposed on a person for acts of others by virtue of membership in a group sharing a common object, without requiring personal commission of the specific act
  3. Liability that applies only in civil law
