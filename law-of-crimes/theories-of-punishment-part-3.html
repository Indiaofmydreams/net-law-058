17 practice questions with answers

1H.L.A. Hart in Punishment and Responsibility (1968) distinguished between the 'general justifying aim' of punishment and 'principles of distribution.' This means:

  1. All aspects of punishment must be justified by a single unified theory
  2. Retribution alone can justify punishment
  3. The general aim justifies the institution of punishment; distribution principles constrain who may be punished - the two operate at different levels
  4. Deterrence and retribution are incompatible theories
Show answer

Answer: C. The general aim justifies the institution of punishment; distribution principles constrain who may be punished - the two operate at different levels

2The social defence theory of punishment (associated with Marc Ancel) holds that:

  1. Society's primary right is to exact retribution
  2. Death penalty is the only effective social defence
  3. Imprisonment must be the default response to all crime
  4. Society must defend itself against crime not through retributive punishment but through scientific study of offenders and treatment/rehabilitation aimed at social reintegration
Show answer

Answer: D. Society must defend itself against crime not through retributive punishment but through scientific study of offenders and treatment/rehabilitation aimed at social reintegration

3The concept of 'specific deterrence' differs from 'general deterrence' in that:

  1. They are the same concept
  2. General deterrence is more effective in all cases
  3. Specific deterrence targets the INDIVIDUAL offender - making THIS person not reoffend; general deterrence targets SOCIETY AT LARGE
  4. Specific deterrence requires capital punishment
Show answer

Answer: C. Specific deterrence targets the INDIVIDUAL offender - making THIS person not reoffend; general deterrence targets SOCIETY AT LARGE

4The expressive theory (denunciation theory) of punishment holds that criminal punishment:

  1. Functions purely as a deterrent
  2. Functions to reform the offender
  3. Functions to compensate the victim financially
  4. Functions as society's formal moral condemnation of the criminal act - reinforcing shared values
Show answer

Answer: D. Functions as society's formal moral condemnation of the criminal act - reinforcing shared values

5Restorative justice as a theory of response to crime differs fundamentally from all classical punishment theories because it:

  1. Shifts the focus from State-vs-offender to victim-offender-community triad - seeking repair of harm and restoration of relationships
  2. Focuses on incapacitating the offender
  3. Focuses on maximum deterrence through publicised punishment
  4. Focuses on exacting proportionate retribution

Answer: A. Shifts the focus from State-vs-offender to victim-offender-community triad - seeking repair of harm and restoration of relationships

  1. Biologically determined to commit crime
  2. Products of social environment alone
  3. Mentally ill persons requiring treatment rather than punishment
  4. Rational actors exercising free will who respond to incentives - therefore punishment must make crime unprofitable

8The Supreme Court's direction in Ankush Shivaji Gaikwad v. State of Maharashtra (2013) that courts must mandatorily consider victim compensation most closely operationalises which theory?

  1. Retributive theory
  2. Deterrent theory
  3. Preventive theory
  4. Restorative justice - placing victim's interests and reparation of harm at the centre of the sentencing process

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false

  1. They are identical theories with different names
  2. Incapacitation always requires the death penalty
  3. Preventive theory focuses on rehabilitation; incapacitation focuses on retribution

11The Supreme Court in Mohd. Arif @ Ashfaq v. State (NCT of Delhi) (2011) held in death penalty review that:

  1. Death penalty cannot be reviewed at all
  2. Two judges are sufficient for review
  3. Death penalty review petitions must be heard by a Bench of 3 judges (not 2) of the Supreme Court, and such hearings must be conducted in OPEN COURT — not in chambers
  4. Review petitions in death penalty cases are automatically rejected

14Death penalty is prescribed in BNS 2023 for rape in which specific circumstances?

  1. Death penalty is never prescribed for rape in India
  2. Rape of a minor below 12 years (Section 66 BNS) and gang rape causing death or vegetative state (Section 70(2) BNS)
  3. Death penalty is mandatory for all rape convictions under BNS
  4. India follows Coker doctrine — no death for rape without murder

  2. Expanding death penalty to all murder cases
  3. Retaining death penalty exactly as it is

16Under BNSS 2023, Section 258(1) provides that after conviction the court must hear the accused on the question of sentence. This provision reflects which principle?

  1. Nemo debet bis vexari
  2. Res judicata
  3. In dubio pro reo
  4. Audi alteram partem (hear the other side) — natural justice requires the accused to be heard before a punitive order is passed; the Supreme Court in Dagdu v. State of Maharashtra made this mandatory
