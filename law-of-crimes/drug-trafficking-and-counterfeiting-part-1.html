About this question set

This set has 16 multiple-choice questions on Drug Trafficking and Counterfeiting from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 4, Law of Crimes. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key. This set is an answer-key drill: check each answer, then revisit the notes for any you miss.

16 practice questions with answers

1The primary legislation governing narcotics and drug trafficking in India is:

  1. NDPS Act, 1985
  2. Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940
  3. Prevention of Illicit Traffic in Narcotic Drugs Act
  4. Excise Act
Show answer

Answer: A. NDPS Act, 1985

Report this question

2Counterfeiting of currency is an offence under IPC Section:

  1. 420
  2. 463
  3. 471
  4. 489A
Show answer

Answer: D. 489A

Report this question

3Section 37 of NDPS Act is significant because:

  1. It defines narcotic drugs
  2. It prescribes death penalty
  3. It imposes reverse burden of proof and restricts bail for commercial quantity offences
  4. It defines trafficking
Show answer

Answer: C. It imposes reverse burden of proof and restricts bail for commercial quantity offences

Report this question

4Under NDPS Act 1985, the burden of proof in respect of possession of narcotic substances:

  1. Always lies with prosecution
  2. Is irrelevant
  3. Is governed only by Evidence Act
  4. Shifts to the accused under Section 35
Show answer

Answer: D. Shifts to the accused under Section 35

Report this question

5Under NDPS Act 1985, the offence of financing illicit drug trafficking was added under:

  1. Section 22
  2. Section 27A - punishment of 10-20 years
  3. Section 15
  4. Section 37
Show answer

Answer: B. Section 27A - punishment of 10-20 years

Report this question

6Forgery (Section 463 IPC) is DISTINCT from counterfeiting (489A) in that:

  1. Forgery covers making a false document; counterfeiting specifically covers currency/coin
  2. They are the same offence
  3. Counterfeiting is less serious than forgery
  4. Forgery only applies to government documents
Show answer

Answer: A. Forgery covers making a false document; counterfeiting specifically covers currency/coin

Report this question

7The Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) 2002 is connected to drug trafficking because:

  1. Proceeds of drug offences are scheduled offences under PMLA - drug money is subject to attachment and confiscation
  2. PMLA replaces NDPS Act
  3. PMLA applies only to banks
  4. There is no connection between PMLA and NDPS
Show answer

Answer: A. Proceeds of drug offences are scheduled offences under PMLA - drug money is subject to attachment and confiscation

Report this question

8Under BNS 2023, offences relating to currency notes and bank notes are covered in:

  1. Sections 178-183 BNS
  2. Sections 200-210 BNS
  3. Sections 150-155 BNS
  4. Sections 250-260 BNS
Show answer

Answer: A. Sections 178-183 BNS

Report this question

9Under NDPS Act 1985, Section 35 creates a presumption that:

  1. The accused is innocent until proven guilty
  2. The prosecution must prove mental state beyond doubt
  3. The accused possessed the narcotic drug with culpable mental state - the accused must rebut this presumption
  4. The quantity found is always commercial quantity
Show answer

Answer: C. The accused possessed the narcotic drug with culpable mental state - the accused must rebut this presumption

Report this question

10Under NDPS Act 1985, the minimum punishment for offences involving commercial quantity is:

  1. Simple imprisonment of 5 years
  2. Rigorous imprisonment of 3 years only
  3. Rigorous imprisonment of 10 years extendable to 20 years with fine not less than Rs. 1 lakh
  4. Fine only
Show answer

Answer: C. Rigorous imprisonment of 10 years extendable to 20 years with fine not less than Rs. 1 lakh

Report this question

11Under NDPS Act 1985, Section 27A (financing illicit trafficking) carries punishment of:

  1. 10-20 years RI
  2. 5-10 years RI
  3. 3-7 years RI
  4. Life imprisonment only
Show answer

Answer: A. 10-20 years RI

Report this question

12Forgery (Section 463 IPC / Section 336 BNS) requires as an essential element:

  1. Using a forged document
  2. Receiving a forged document
  3. Destroying a genuine document
  4. Making a false document or electronic record with intent to cause damage, injury, or support a claim - the document must be MADE false
Show answer

Answer: D. Making a false document or electronic record with intent to cause damage, injury, or support a claim - the document must be MADE false

Report this question

13Under BNS 2023, Section 180 deals with:

  1. Using counterfeit currency (Section 489B IPC equivalent)
  2. Counterfeiting currency (Section 489A IPC equivalent)
  3. Possession of counterfeit currency
  4. Making counterfeiting instruments
Show answer

Answer: A. Using counterfeit currency (Section 489B IPC equivalent)

Report this question

14The twin conditions for bail under Section 37 NDPS Act require:

  1. Reasonable grounds to believe not guilty + not likely to commit offence while on bail - BOTH conditions must be simultaneously satisfied
  2. Only one condition needs to be satisfied
  3. Section 37 conditions are merely advisory
  4. Normal bail provisions of CrPC override Section 37
Show answer

Answer: A. Reasonable grounds to believe not guilty + not likely to commit offence while on bail - BOTH conditions must be simultaneously satisfied

Report this question

15Under BNS 2023, Section 178 deals with:

  1. Using counterfeit currency
  2. Possession of counterfeit currency
  3. Counterfeiting currency (Section 489A IPC equivalent)
  4. Making counterfeiting instruments
Show answer

Answer: C. Counterfeiting currency (Section 489A IPC equivalent)

Report this question

16Using a forged document is a SEPARATE offence from making a forged document. Using a forged document is covered under:

  1. Section 471 IPC / Section 344 BNS
  2. Section 463 IPC
  3. Section 489A IPC
  4. Section 467 IPC
Show answer

Answer: A. Section 471 IPC / Section 344 BNS
