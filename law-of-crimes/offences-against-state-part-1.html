About this question set

This set has 17 multiple-choice questions on Offences Against State from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 4, Law of Crimes. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key. This set is an answer-key drill: check each answer, then revisit the notes for any you miss.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Law of Crimes notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

17 practice questions with answers

1Waging war against the Government of India is punishable under IPC:

  1. Section 124A
  2. Section 121
  3. Section 120B
  4. Section 122
Show answer

Answer: B. Section 121

2Sedition under IPC was covered by:

  1. Section 121
  2. Section 124A
  3. Section 153A
  4. Section 505
Show answer

Answer: B. Section 124A

3Under BNS 2023, sedition (Section 124A IPC) has been replaced by:

  1. Section 150
  2. Section 148
  3. Section 152 (Acts endangering sovereignty, unity, and integrity of India)
  4. Section 153

Answer: C. Section 152 (Acts endangering sovereignty, unity, and integrity of India)

  1. POTA 2002
  2. TADA 1987
  3. NSA 1980
  4. UAPA 1967 (as amended)

5Under BNS 2023, waging war against the State is under:

  1. Section 147
  2. Section 148
  3. Section 149
  4. Section 150

6Terrorist act in India is primarily defined under:

  1. Section 302 IPC
  2. Section 121 IPC
  3. Section 15 of UAPA
  4. Section 10 of NSA

7The NIA (National Investigation Agency) was established under:

  1. UAPA 1967
  2. CrPC 1973
  3. NIA Act, 2008
  4. NSA 1980

8The Supreme Court in Kedar Nath Singh v. State of Bihar (1962) upheld Section 124A IPC (sedition) subject to the condition that:

  1. It applies to all criticism of government
  2. It applies only to written material
  3. It was struck down as unconstitutional
  4. It applies only when words have a tendency to incite violence or public disorder
Show answer

Answer: D. It applies only when words have a tendency to incite violence or public disorder

Report this question

9Under UAPA 1967 (as amended in 2019), an individual can be designated as a terrorist by:

  1. The Central Government under Section 35
  2. State Government only
  3. Supreme Court
  4. National Investigation Agency
Show answer

Answer: A. The Central Government under Section 35

Report this question

10BNS 2023 replaced sedition (Section 124A IPC) with a broader provision. The new provision covers:

  1. All criticism of the government
  2. Only armed rebellion
  3. Strikes and protests
  4. Acts that endanger sovereignty, unity, integrity, or threaten armed rebellion - Section 152 BNS
Show answer

Answer: D. Acts that endanger sovereignty, unity, integrity, or threaten armed rebellion - Section 152 BNS

Report this question

11Collecting arms with the intention of waging war against the Government of India is punishable under IPC Section:

  1. 121
  2. 122
  3. 121A
  4. 123
Show answer

Answer: B. 122

12Concealing with intent to facilitate design to wage war is punishable under:

  1. Section 121 IPC
  2. Section 123 IPC / Section 150 BNS
  3. Section 122 IPC
  4. Section 124A IPC
Show answer

Answer: B. Section 123 IPC / Section 150 BNS

Report this question

13Under BNS 2023, terrorist act (Section 113) is defined to include acts that:

  1. Only result in death
  2. Only involve explosives
  3. Only target government buildings
  4. Strike terror in people by using explosives, firearms, lethal weapons, noxious gases - whether or not causing death
Show answer

Answer: D. Strike terror in people by using explosives, firearms, lethal weapons, noxious gases - whether or not causing death

14Under BNS 2023, which section deals with waging war against the Government of India?

  1. Section 147 BNS
  2. Section 148 BNS
  3. Section 150 BNS
  4. Section 152 BNS
Show answer

Answer: A. Section 147 BNS

15Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015) struck down Section 66A of the IT Act. This case is relevant to criminal law because:

  1. It created a new offence of cybercrime
  2. It upheld sedition law in its entirety
  3. It restricted UAPA application
  4. It reinforced that vague and overbroad penal provisions violate Article 19(1)(a) - same principle applies to broadly worded offences against the State
Show answer

Answer: D. It reinforced that vague and overbroad penal provisions violate Article 19(1)(a) - same principle applies to broadly worded offences against the State

16Section 152 BNS (replacement for sedition) differs from old Section 124A IPC in that:

  1. Section 152 BNS explicitly covers digital/electronic communications and financial support to secessionist activities, and removes the word 'sedition' entirely
  2. Section 152 BNS is narrower than Section 124A
  3. Section 152 BNS is identical to Section 124A
  4. Section 152 BNS was struck down by the Supreme Court
Show answer

Answer: A. Section 152 BNS explicitly covers digital/electronic communications and financial support to secessionist activities, and removes the word 'sedition' entirely

17BNS 2023 Section 152 (replacement for sedition) is broader than old Section 124A because it:

  1. Is narrower in scope
  2. Explicitly covers digital/electronic communications and financial support to secessionist activities
  3. Is identical in scope
  4. Only covers physical acts
