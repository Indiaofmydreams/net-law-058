About this question set

This set has 18 multiple-choice questions on Abductive Reasoning from the UGC NET Paper 1 syllabus, unit 5, Mathematical Reasoning and Aptitude. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 18 of the 18 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Mathematical Reasoning notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

18 practice questions with answers

1Match List I with List II:
List I: (a) Deductive (b) Inductive (c) Abductive (d) Analogical
List II: (i) Inference to best explanation (ii) Necessary conclusion (iii) Probable generalisation from instances (iv) Conclusion from similarity

  1. a-ii, b-iii, c-i, d-iv
  2. a-iii, b-ii, c-i, d-iv
  3. a-ii, b-i, c-iii, d-iv
  4. a-iv, b-iii, c-i, d-ii
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. a-ii, b-iii, c-i, d-iv

Each reasoning type is matched with its defining feature.

Report this question

2Another name used by Peirce for abduction is:

  1. Contraposition
  2. Syllogism
  3. Enumeration
  4. Retroduction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Retroduction

Reasoning backward from an observed effect to its probable explanation.

Report this question

3Abduction reasons from:

  1. An observed effect to a probable explanation
  2. Cause to effect with certainty
  3. Sample to population
  4. General to specific
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. An observed effect to a probable explanation

Effect first, then the best explanation.

Report this question

4The usual first step of abductive inference is:

  1. Counting a sample
  2. Observing a surprising fact that needs explanation
  3. Drawing an analogy
  4. Proving a theorem
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Observing a surprising fact that needs explanation

The surprising fact triggers the search for a hypothesis.

Report this question

5Abduction differs from induction in that it:

  1. Compares two cases
  2. Generalises from a sample
  3. Infers an explanation of a particular observation
  4. Deduces consequences
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Infers an explanation of a particular observation

Induction generalises, and abduction explains.

Report this question

6A geologist finds marine fossils on a mountain top and concludes the area was once under the sea. This reasoning is:

  1. Abduction
  2. Perfect induction
  3. Deduction
  4. Analogy
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Abduction

The best explanation of the fossils.

Report this question

7Which criterion reflects "testability" when choosing among explanations?

  1. The hypothesis is old
  2. The hypothesis is accepted by the majority
  3. The hypothesis cannot be checked
  4. The hypothesis yields checkable predictions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The hypothesis yields checkable predictions

A good explanation can be put to the test.

Report this question

8When two hypotheses explain the evidence equally well, abductive practice prefers the one that is:

  1. More complicated
  2. More popular
  3. Simpler and more coherent with background knowledge
  4. Older
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Simpler and more coherent with background knowledge

Simplicity and coherence break the tie.

Report this question

9Which statement best distinguishes abduction from deduction?

  1. Abduction is always invalid
  2. Abduction's conclusion is plausible, while deduction's follows necessarily
  3. Abduction ignores evidence
  4. Both give certain conclusions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Abduction's conclusion is plausible, while deduction's follows necessarily

Plausibility versus necessity.

Report this question

10A technician sees that the power light is off and nothing responds, and concludes the cable is disconnected. This reasoning is:

  1. Abductive
  2. An inductive generalisation
  3. Analogical
  4. Deductive
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Abductive

From symptoms to the likeliest cause.

Report this question

11Which of the following is NOT abductive?

  1. A doctor concludes a patient has anaemia from the symptoms
  2. Police infer a burglary from forced locks
  3. An astronomer infers a hidden planet from orbit disturbances
  4. A mathematician proves that the sum of two even numbers is even
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A mathematician proves that the sum of two even numbers is even

A proof is deductive, and the others infer explanations.

Report this question

12Statement I: Abductive hypotheses may later be confirmed or rejected by further evidence.
Statement II: Once chosen as the best explanation, a hypothesis cannot be revised.

  1. Both true
  2. I false, II true
  3. I true, II false
  4. Both false
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. I true, II false

Abductive conclusions are defeasible.

Report this question

13Assertion (A): A better explanation may replace an earlier abductive conclusion.
Reason (R): Abductive conclusions are defeasible.

  1. Both true, but R is not the correct explanation
  2. Both true, and R is the correct explanation
  3. A true, R false
  4. A false, R true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Both true, and R is the correct explanation

Because the conclusion is revisable, a better rival can replace it.

Report this question

14The detailed account of "inference to the best explanation" in modern epistemology is associated with:

  1. David Hume
  2. Francis Bacon
  3. Aristotle
  4. Gilbert Harman
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Gilbert Harman

Harman and later Peter Lipton developed IBE in the philosophy of science.

Report this question

15A main weakness of abductive reasoning is that:

  1. A better, unconsidered explanation may exist
  2. It uses too many premises
  3. It needs no evidence
  4. Its form is always invalid
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A better, unconsidered explanation may exist

The best available explanation need not be the true one.

Report this question

16Match List I with List II:
List I: (a) Abduction (b) Induction (c) Deduction (d) Analogy
List II: (i) Hypothesis from a surprising fact (ii) Generalisation from instances (iii) Necessary conclusion from premises (iv) Property transfer from a similar case

  1. a-i, b-iii, c-ii, d-iv
  2. a-i, b-ii, c-iii, d-iv
  3. a-iii, b-ii, c-i, d-iv
  4. a-ii, b-i, c-iii, d-iv
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. a-i, b-ii, c-iii, d-iv

Each reasoning type is paired with its characteristic move.

Report this question

17Arrange the steps of abductive inference in order:
1. Observe a surprising fact
2. Generate candidate explanations
3. Compare and evaluate candidates
4. Select the best explanation

  1. 2, 1, 3, 4
  2. 1, 3, 2, 4
  3. 1, 2, 3, 4
  4. 4, 3, 2, 1
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1, 2, 3, 4

Observe, propose, compare, choose.

Report this question

18Archaeologists find stone tools and hearth ash in a cave and infer that prehistoric people lived there. This is:

  1. Abduction
  2. Tautology
  3. Deduction
  4. Perfect induction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Abduction

The occupation hypothesis best explains the finds.
