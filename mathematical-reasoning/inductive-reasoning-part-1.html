About this question set

This set has 25 multiple-choice questions on Inductive Reasoning from the UGC NET Paper 1 syllabus, unit 5, Mathematical Reasoning and Aptitude. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 25 of the 25 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Mathematical Reasoning notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

25 practice questions with answers

1Inductive reasoning moves from:

  1. One analogy to another
  2. Effect to the best explanation
  3. Specific observations to a general conclusion
  4. General principle to a specific case
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Specific observations to a general conclusion

Bottom-up reasoning.

Report this question

2The conclusion of a strong inductive argument is:

  1. Probable
  2. A tautology
  3. Certain
  4. Necessary
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Probable

Induction gives likelihood, not certainty.

Report this question

3Inductive arguments are evaluated as:

  1. Valid or invalid
  2. Sound or unsound
  3. True or false only
  4. Strong or weak
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Strong or weak

Strength and cogency apply to induction, and validity and soundness apply to deduction.

Report this question

4Inductive reasoning is also called:

  1. Top-down reasoning
  2. Bottom-up reasoning
  3. Circular reasoning
  4. Deductive reasoning
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Bottom-up reasoning

It builds from particulars to a generalisation.

Report this question

5"Every crow I have seen is black; therefore all crows are black." This is:

  1. Analogy
  2. Abduction
  3. Enumerative induction
  4. Deduction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Enumerative induction

Enumerate instances, then generalise.

Report this question

6An inductive generalisation is strongest when the sample is:

  1. Large, random and representative
  2. Biased but large
  3. Small and convenient
  4. A single instance
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Large, random and representative

Sample size and representativeness drive strength.

Report this question

7Hasty generalisation is the fallacy of:

  1. Attacking the person
  2. Using ambiguous words
  3. Assuming what is to be proved
  4. Drawing a general conclusion from too few or unrepresentative cases
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Drawing a general conclusion from too few or unrepresentative cases

The sample is too small or biased.

Report this question

8"90% of the students passed. Ravi is a student. So Ravi probably passed." This is:

  1. Deductive and valid
  2. Inductive (statistical syllogism)
  3. A false analogy
  4. Abductive
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Inductive (statistical syllogism)

A statistical premise gives a probable conclusion about an individual.

Report this question

9The Mill's method that identifies the cause as the only common circumstance among different instances of an effect is the Method of:

  1. Agreement
  2. Concomitant Variation
  3. Difference
  4. Residues
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Agreement

Look for what is common to all the instances.

Report this question

10If the effect occurs in one case and not in another, and the cases differ in only one circumstance, that circumstance is the cause. This is the Method of:

  1. Concomitant Variation
  2. Residues
  3. Agreement
  4. Difference
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Difference

A single point of difference identifies the cause.

Report this question

11When a change in one phenomenon is accompanied by a proportional change in another, the method applied is:

  1. Difference
  2. Residues
  3. Concomitant Variation
  4. Agreement
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Concomitant Variation

The two vary together.

Report this question

12Subtracting from a phenomenon the part known to be caused by earlier factors, and attributing the remainder to the remaining factor, is the Method of:

  1. Concomitant Variation
  2. Residues
  3. Agreement
  4. Difference
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Residues

The leftover effect has the leftover cause.

Report this question

13A universal generalisation reached by induction is falsified by:

  1. More confirming instances
  2. A larger sample
  3. A single genuine counterexample
  4. Repeated observations
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A single genuine counterexample

One black swan refutes "all swans are white".

Report this question

14The "problem of induction" is associated with:

  1. David Hume
  2. Descartes
  3. Immanuel Kant
  4. Aristotle
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. David Hume

Hume asked what justifies assuming that the future will resemble the past.

Report this question

15The discovery of black swans refuted "All swans are white". This shows inductive conclusions are:

  1. Always valid
  2. Tautological
  3. Certain
  4. Revisable and uncertain
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Revisable and uncertain

New evidence can overturn an inductive generalisation.

Report this question

16The "post hoc ergo propter hoc" fallacy means:

  1. Arguing from authority
  2. Assuming A caused B merely because B followed A in time
  3. Attacking the speaker
  4. Correlation always implies causation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Assuming A caused B merely because B followed A in time

A time sequence is mistaken for causation.

Report this question

17Ice-cream sales and drowning deaths both rise in summer. Concluding that ice cream causes drowning is:

  1. A correlation-causation fallacy
  2. Strong induction
  3. Sound abduction
  4. Valid deduction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A correlation-causation fallacy

A hidden common cause (hot weather) explains both.

Report this question

18A sample in which every member of the population has an equal chance of selection is called:

  1. Convenience
  2. Quota
  3. Random
  4. Purposive
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Random

Random sampling supports strong induction.

Report this question

19"The first five students I met were tall, so all students of the university are tall." The argument is weak because:

  1. There are too many premises
  2. The conclusion is false
  3. The premises are false
  4. The sample is small and biased
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The sample is small and biased

A weak sample gives a weak generalisation.

Report this question

20Statement I: Inductive reasoning yields probable conclusions.
Statement II: Adding new premises can never change the strength of an inductive argument.

  1. I false, II true
  2. I true, II false
  3. Both false
  4. Both true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. I true, II false

Induction is non-monotonic, so new evidence can strengthen or weaken it.

Report this question

21Scientific laws are mostly formed on the basis of:

  1. Authority
  2. Revelation
  3. Pure deduction
  4. Induction from observations
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Induction from observations

Repeated observation leads to a general law.

Report this question

22Which thinker is most associated with the inductive method in Novum Organum?

  1. Francis Bacon
  2. Aristotle
  3. Hegel
  4. Plato
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Francis Bacon

Bacon proposed the induction-based scientific method.

Report this question

23In the Nyaya system, the pramana corresponding to inference is:

  1. Pratyaksha
  2. Shabda
  3. Anumana
  4. Upamana
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Anumana

Pratyaksha is perception, Upamana is comparison and Shabda is testimony.

Report this question

24The invariable concomitance between the reason (hetu) and the property to be proved (sadhya) in Indian inference is called:

  1. Nigamana
  2. Vyapti
  3. Drishtanta
  4. Paksha
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Vyapti

Vyapti is the universal relation that grounds anumana.

Report this question

25Learning that a sample was heavily biased will usually do what to an inductive argument based on it?

  1. Weaken it
  2. Make it deductively valid
  3. Leave it unchanged
  4. Strengthen it
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Weaken it

Evidence that undermines the sample undermines the strength.
