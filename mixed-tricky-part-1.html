23 practice questions with answers

1The Paris Agreement (2015) on climate change operates on the principle of:

  1. Binding emission targets for all nations uniformly
  2. Differentiated binding targets only for developed nations
  3. Equal emission cuts for all countries
  4. Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) by each country
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) by each country

The Paris Agreement 2015 (COP21) replaced the Kyoto Protocol's top-down approach with bottom-up Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs). It aims to limit global warming to well below 2°C, pursuing 1.5°C. India ratified in October 2016.

2The Aarhus Convention (1998) deals with:

  1. Marine pollution
  2. Hazardous waste management
  3. Access to information, public participation, and access to justice in environmental matters
  4. Climate change financing
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Access to information, public participation, and access to justice in environmental matters

Aarhus Convention (UNECE, 1998) - often called 'the most ambitious venture in environmental democracy' - establishes three pillars: access to environmental information; public participation; access to justice in environmental matters.

3In Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan (1997), the Supreme Court:

  1. Abolished child marriage
  2. Recognized transgender rights
  3. Ordered equal pay for women
  4. Laid down guidelines on sexual harassment at the workplace
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Laid down guidelines on sexual harassment at the workplace

Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan (1997) - SC laid down the 'Vishaka Guidelines' on prevention of sexual harassment at the workplace, in the absence of legislation. These became binding until the POSH Act 2013 was enacted.

Report this question

4The principle of 'Non-Refoulement' is a fundamental principle of:

  1. Refugee law - 1951 Refugee Convention, Article 33
  2. Environmental law
  3. Trade law
  4. International investment law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Refugee law - 1951 Refugee Convention, Article 33

Non-refoulement (Article 33, 1951 Refugee Convention): No state shall expel or return a refugee to territories where his life or freedom would be threatened. It is considered a peremptory norm (jus cogens) of international law.

Report this question

5The 'doctrine of Res Communis' in international law means:

  1. Natural resources like high seas and outer space belong to no state and are freely usable by all
  2. Private property rights over natural resources of nations
  3. Common heritage of mankind with benefit-sharing obligations
  4. Environmental resources managed by international organizations
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Natural resources like high seas and outer space belong to no state and are freely usable by all

Res Communis (common property of all) - no state can claim sovereignty over res communis areas: high seas, outer space, Antarctica. Distinct from Common Heritage of Mankind (CHM) which adds non-appropriation and benefit-sharing.

6India's PIL jurisprudence on environment was pioneered mainly by:

  1. Justice Y.V. Chandrachud
  2. Justice M. Hidayatullah
  3. Justice A.S. Anand
  4. Justice P.N. Bhagwati and Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Justice P.N. Bhagwati and Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer

Justice P.N. Bhagwati and Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer are credited with developing PIL jurisprudence in India in the early 1980s by relaxing locus standi requirements, enabling environmental lawyers like M.C. Mehta to approach the SC.

7The Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act was enacted in:

  1. 1955
  2. 1976
  3. 1989
  4. 1995
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1989

SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act 1989 was enacted to prevent atrocities and hate crimes against SC/ST communities. Amended significantly in 2015 and 2018 (overturning Subhash Kashinath Mahajan SC order).

8Which article of the Indian Constitution prohibits traffic in human beings and forced labour?

  1. Articles 14-18
  2. Articles 23-24
  3. Articles 19-22
  4. Articles 25-28
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Articles 23-24

Article 23 prohibits traffic in human beings, begar (forced labour without payment) and similar forms of forced labour. Article 24 prohibits employment of children below 14 years in hazardous occupation. Together these form the Right Against Exploitation.

9The NHRC was first constituted on:

  1. 8 January 1994
  2. 12 October 1993
  3. 26 January 1993
  4. 1 March 1994
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 12 October 1993

The NHRC was constituted on 12 October 1993. The Protection of Human Rights Act formally received Presidential assent on 8 January 1994. Justice Ranganath Misra was the first Chairperson. The date October 12, 1993 is a frequently tested factual detail.

10'Third Generation Human Rights' includes:

  1. Right to development, right to peace, right to a clean environment
  2. Right to vote, freedom of speech, right to fair trial
  3. Right to work, right to education, right to social security
  4. Right to life, right against torture, right to liberty
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Right to development, right to peace, right to a clean environment

Third generation rights (solidarity/collective rights in Karel Vasak's framework) include: right to development, right to peace, right to a healthy/clean environment, right to the common heritage of mankind. These are held by peoples or groups.

11The Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD) was adopted at:

  1. Rio Earth Summit 1992
  2. Stockholm 1972
  3. Johannesburg 2002
  4. Nagoya 2010
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Rio Earth Summit 1992

The Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD) was adopted at the Rio Earth Summit 1992. Three objectives: conservation of biological diversity; sustainable use of its components; fair and equitable sharing of benefits from use of genetic resources. India ratified in 1994.

12The Cartagena Protocol (2000) is related to:

  1. Marine pollution
  2. Climate change
  3. Protection of traditional knowledge
  4. Biosafety - transboundary movement of living modified organisms (LMOs)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Biosafety - transboundary movement of living modified organisms (LMOs)

The Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety (2000), under the CBD, regulates transboundary movement of Living Modified Organisms (LMOs/GMOs). It applies the precautionary principle to biotechnology. India ratified it in 2003.

Report this question

13The Nagoya Protocol (2010) deals with:

  1. Access to genetic resources and benefit sharing (ABS)
  2. Climate finance
  3. Whaling regulation
  4. Deep seabed mining
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Access to genetic resources and benefit sharing (ABS)

The Nagoya Protocol on Access and Benefit Sharing (2010) supplements the CBD by providing a transparent framework for fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from the utilization of genetic resources. India enacted the Biological Diversity Act 2002 ahead of the Protocol.

14The Sendai Framework (2015-2030) deals with:

  1. Climate change adaptation
  2. Disaster risk reduction
  3. Sustainable development goals
  4. Ocean governance
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Disaster risk reduction

The Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction 2015-2030 was adopted at the Third UN World Conference on Disaster Risk Reduction (Sendai, Japan, 2015). It has four priorities for action and targets to substantially reduce disaster risk and losses by 2030.

15Which case is associated with sustainable development being part of Indian constitutional law?

  1. M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Oleum Gas), 1987
  2. M.C. Mehta v. Kamal Nath, 1997
  3. Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum v. Union of India, 1996
  4. A.P. Pollution Control Board v. Prof. M.V. Nayudu, 1999
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum v. Union of India, 1996

In Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum v. UOI (1996), the SC held that the Precautionary Principle and Polluter Pays Principle are essential features of sustainable development and have been accepted as part of customary international law and hence part of Indian law through Articles 21, 48A, and 51A(g).

16Assertion (A): The right to a healthy environment is a fundamental right in India. Reason (R): Article 21 has been interpreted by the Supreme Court to include the right to a clean and wholesome environment.

  1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A

Both statements are true and directly connected. The Supreme Court through cases beginning with Subhash Kumar v. State of Bihar (1991) expanded Article 21 to include the right to a clean environment. The right exists through judicial interpretation of Article 21.

17Assertion (A): The NHRC cannot inquire into matters relating to the armed forces. Reason (R): Section 19 of the Protection of Human Rights Act 1993 excludes the armed forces from NHRC's direct inquiry jurisdiction.

  1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A

Both the assertion and the reason are correct, and the reason precisely explains the assertion. Section 19 PHR Act 1993 is the specific provision that excludes armed forces from NHRC's direct investigation power, while allowing NHRC to seek reports from Central Government.

18Match: I. Ramsar Convention (1971) - a. Hazardous Wastes; II. Basel Convention (1989) - b. Wetlands; III. CITES (1973) - c. Biodiversity; IV. CBD (1992) - d. Trade in Endangered Species

  1. I-b, II-a, III-d, IV-c
  2. I-a, II-b, III-c, IV-d
  3. I-b, II-d, III-a, IV-c
  4. I-c, II-a, III-b, IV-d
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. I-b, II-a, III-d, IV-c

Ramsar (1971) - Wetlands; Basel (1989) - Transboundary movement of Hazardous Wastes; CITES (1973) - International Trade in Endangered Species; CBD (1992) - Conservation of Biological Diversity. This matching format is very common in UGC NET.

19Match: I. M.C. Mehta v. UOI (1987-Oleum) - a. Public Trust Doctrine; II. M.C. Mehta v. Kamal Nath (1997) - b. Absolute Liability; III. Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum (1996) - c. Sustainable Development; IV. Indian Council Enviro-Legal Action (1996) - d. Polluter Pays Principle

  1. I-b, II-a, III-c, IV-d
  2. I-a, II-b, III-d, IV-c
  3. I-c, II-d, III-a, IV-b
  4. I-d, II-c, III-b, IV-a

Answer: A. I-b, II-a, III-c, IV-d

Oleum Gas (1987) - Absolute Liability; Kamal Nath (1997) - Public Trust Doctrine; Vellore Citizens (1996) - Precautionary Principle and Sustainable Development; Indian Council/Bichhri (1996) - Polluter Pays Principle. This is among the most expected matching questions.

20Match: I. ICCPR - a. CESCR Committee; II. ICESCR - b. CEDAW Committee; III. CEDAW - c. Committee on the Rights of the Child; IV. CRC - d. Human Rights Committee

  1. I-a, II-d, III-c, IV-b
  2. I-b, II-c, III-d, IV-a
  3. I-d, II-a, III-b, IV-c
  4. I-c, II-b, III-a, IV-d
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. I-d, II-a, III-b, IV-c

ICCPR - Human Rights Committee (HRC); ICESCR - Committee on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (CESCR); CEDAW - CEDAW Committee; CRC - Committee on the Rights of the Child. Each core UN human rights treaty has its own treaty monitoring body.

21Which of the following accurately describes the relationship between environmental rights and human rights?

  1. A clean and healthy environment is increasingly recognized as a prerequisite for the enjoyment of human rights, particularly right to life
  2. They are entirely separate and independent streams of law
  3. Human rights law takes precedence over environmental law in all cases
  4. Environmental rights can only be claimed by citizens, not non-citizens
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A clean and healthy environment is increasingly recognized as a prerequisite for the enjoyment of human rights, particularly right to life

The relationship between environmental rights and human rights is one of mutual reinforcement. UNHRC Resolution 48/13 (2021) and UNGA Resolution 76/300 (2022) recognized the right to a clean, healthy and sustainable environment as a human right. Indian SC has long held environmental degradation violates Article 21.

22In which case did the Supreme Court hold that the right to education is a fundamental right under Article 21?

  1. Mohini Jain v. State of Karnataka (1992)
  2. TMA Pai Foundation v. State of Karnataka (2002)
  3. P.A. Inamdar v. State of Maharashtra (2005)
  4. Unni Krishnan v. State of Andhra Pradesh (1993)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Unni Krishnan v. State of Andhra Pradesh (1993)

Unni Krishnan v. State of AP (1993) - the SC held that the right to education is a fundamental right flowing from Article 21 for children up to 14 years. This was subsequently given explicit constitutional basis through Article 21A by the 86th Amendment 2002.

23The doctrine of 'proportionality' in human rights law means:

  1. Equal compensation for all human rights violations
  2. Proportional representation of minorities in Parliament
  3. Equal application of rights to all communities
  4. Any restriction on human rights must be proportionate to the legitimate aim pursued and must not go beyond what is necessary
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Any restriction on human rights must be proportionate to the legitimate aim pursued and must not go beyond what is necessary

Proportionality is a fundamental principle of human rights law - restrictions on rights must be: (i) prescribed by law; (ii) in pursuit of a legitimate aim; (iii) necessary in a democratic society; and (iv) proportionate to the aim. The SC has increasingly applied proportionality analysis in Indian constitutional law.
