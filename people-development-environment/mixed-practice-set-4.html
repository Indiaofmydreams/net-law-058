1The Forest Conservation Act in India was enacted in:

  1. 1972
  2. 1974
  3. 1980
  4. 1986
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1980

The Forest Conservation Act, 1980 restricts the de-reservation of forests or use of forest land for non-forest purposes without prior approval of the Central Government.

Report this question

2Assertion (A): Conserving our soil resources is critical to human survival. Reason (R): Soil is home to many micro-organisms and contains minerals.

  1. Both (A) and (R) are correct and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
  2. Both (A) and (R) are correct but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
  3. (A) is true and (R) is false
  4. (A) is false and (R) is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Both (A) and (R) are correct but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)

Both are true: soil conservation IS critical to survival (for food production, water filtration, etc.), and soil does host micro-organisms and minerals. But the reason (micro-organisms + minerals) is not the complete or correct explanation for why soil conservation is critical to human survival — food security is the primary reason.

3Assertion (A): The problems of environmental sustainability are difficult to resolve. Reason (R): Our understanding of how the environment works and how human choices affect it is inadequate.

  1. Both (A) and (R) are correct and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
  2. Both (A) and (R) are correct but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
  3. (A) is true and (R) is false
  4. (A) is false and (R) is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both (A) and (R) are correct and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)

Both statements are true, and here R correctly explains A. The difficulty in resolving environmental sustainability problems is indeed because our understanding of complex environmental systems and how our choices impact them remains insufficient.

4Assertion (A): People/population control measures do not necessarily help in checking environmental degradation. Reason (R): The relationship between population growth and environmental degradation is rather complex.

  1. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
  2. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
  3. (A) is true but (R) is false
  4. (A) is false but (R) is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)

Both are true and R correctly explains A. Population growth does not have a simple linear relationship with environmental degradation — consumption patterns, technology, and governance matter equally. The complexity of the relationship (R) explains why population control alone doesn't fix environmental issues (A).

5The concept of Sustainable Development was prominently defined in the Brundtland Report as 'development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.' This report was published in:

  1. 1980
  2. 1983
  3. 1987
  4. 1992
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1987

The Brundtland Report ('Our Common Future'), published in 1987 by the World Commission on Environment and Development (WCED), gave the most widely used definition of sustainable development.

Report this question

6Natural vegetation of an area should be preserved mainly for the purpose of:

  1. Providing habitat for birds
  2. Preventing soil erosion
  3. Study by scientists
  4. Providing genetic material for breeding new species
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Providing genetic material for breeding new species

The primary reason for preserving natural vegetation is to maintain genetic diversity, which is essential for breeding new disease-resistant and climate-adaptive crop and plant varieties for future generations.

7The tropical western Ghats biodiversity hotspot in India is situated primarily in:

  1. Punjab
  2. Madhya Pradesh
  3. Rajasthan
  4. Kerala
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Kerala

The Western Ghats, a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the world's biodiversity hotspots, runs through Kerala, Karnataka, Goa, Maharashtra, and Tamil Nadu — with Kerala being the primary state.

8India is planning to raise its installed renewable energy capacity by 2030 to:

  1. 175 GW
  2. 250 GW
  3. 300 GW
  4. 500 GW
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 500 GW

India has updated its renewable energy target to 500 GW of non-fossil fuel-based electricity capacity by 2030, as part of its updated NDC under the Paris Agreement.

9Which of the following is the best definition of an 'anthropogenic' activity?

  1. Activities caused by natural geological processes
  2. Activities caused by volcanic eruptions
  3. Activities resulting from human action or influence
  4. Activities related to animal behaviour
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Activities resulting from human action or influence

Anthropogenic refers to effects or objects produced or caused by human beings. Anthropogenic activities include industrialisation, deforestation, agriculture, urbanisation, and transportation.

Report this question

10Deforestation is primarily responsible for which of the following? (i) Loss of biodiversity (ii) Soil erosion (iii) Increased albedo (iv) Carbon release into the atmosphere

  1. (i) and (ii) only
  2. (i), (ii) and (iv)
  3. (ii), (iii) and (iv)
  4. (i), (ii), (iii) and (iv)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. (i), (ii) and (iv)

Deforestation causes: loss of biodiversity (habitat destruction), soil erosion (no root systems to bind soil), and carbon release (trees are carbon stores). Deforestation actually DECREASES albedo in most cases (forests are darker than cleared land), so increased albedo is incorrect.

11The three main objectives of the Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD) are:

  1. Conservation, Research, and Patenting of genetic resources
  2. Conservation, Sustainable Use, and Fair and Equitable Benefit Sharing
  3. Protection, Restoration, and Commercial Exploitation
  4. Documentation, Conservation, and International Trade control
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Conservation, Sustainable Use, and Fair and Equitable Benefit Sharing

The CBD's three objectives are: (1) Conservation of biological diversity, (2) Sustainable use of its components, and (3) Fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from genetic resources.

12India's biodiversity is protected under the:

  1. Environmental Protection Act, 1986
  2. Wildlife Protection Act, 1972
  3. Biological Diversity Act, 2002
  4. Forest Conservation Act, 1980
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Biological Diversity Act, 2002

India's Biological Diversity Act, 2002 was enacted to implement the obligations under the Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD), providing a framework for conservation, sustainable use, and equitable benefit sharing.

Report this question

13Which of the following refers to the variety of species within a single ecosystem or habitat?

  1. Alpha diversity
  2. Beta diversity
  3. Gamma diversity
  4. Delta diversity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Alpha diversity

Alpha diversity refers to the variety of species within a single ecosystem or habitat (local diversity). Beta diversity measures the change in species composition between different habitats. Gamma diversity refers to the overall diversity of a larger region.

Report this question

14Bioremediation refers to:

  1. Using chemicals to neutralise soil pollutants
  2. Burning contaminated soil to destroy pollutants
  3. Using microorganisms or plants to clean contaminated soil or water
  4. Physical removal and replacement of contaminated soil
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Using microorganisms or plants to clean contaminated soil or water

Bioremediation is a waste management technique that uses living organisms — mainly bacteria, fungi, or plants (phytoremediation) — to neutralise or remove pollutants from contaminated soil or water.

Report this question

15Eutrophication refers to:

  1. Increase in turbidity of water due to suspended particles
  2. Depletion of oxygen in water bodies due to excess nutrient enrichment causing algal blooms
  3. Contamination of water with heavy metals from industries
  4. Evaporation of water bodies due to global warming
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Depletion of oxygen in water bodies due to excess nutrient enrichment causing algal blooms

Eutrophication is the process by which excessive nutrients (mainly nitrogen and phosphorus from agricultural runoff and sewage) lead to algal blooms, depletion of dissolved oxygen, and death of aquatic life.

Report this question

16The Clean Development Mechanism (CDM) was established under:

  1. Montreal Protocol
  2. CBD (Convention on Biological Diversity)
  3. Kyoto Protocol
  4. Paris Agreement
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Kyoto Protocol

The Clean Development Mechanism (CDM) is a flexible mechanism under the Kyoto Protocol that allows developed (Annex I) countries to invest in emission-reduction projects in developing countries and earn carbon credits.
