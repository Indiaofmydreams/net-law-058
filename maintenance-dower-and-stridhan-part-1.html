About this question set

This set has 17 multiple-choice questions on Maintenance, Dower and Stridhan from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 7, Family Law. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 17 of the 17 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Family Law notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

17 practice questions with answers

1Section 125 of the Code of Criminal Procedure (now BNSS) provides for maintenance of:

  1. Wife, children and parents
  2. Wife and children only
  3. Wife only
  4. Divorced wife only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Wife, children and parents

Section 125 CrPC (now BNSS Section 144) provides for maintenance of wife (including divorced wife who has not remarried), legitimate and illegitimate children, and parents.

Report this question

2In the landmark Shah Bano Begum v. Mohd. Ahmed Khan (1985), the Supreme Court held that:

  1. Muslim personal law overrides Section 125 CrPC
  2. Muslim women have no right to maintenance after divorce
  3. A Muslim divorced wife is entitled to maintenance under Section 125 CrPC beyond iddat period
  4. Dower payment extinguishes maintenance obligation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A Muslim divorced wife is entitled to maintenance under Section 125 CrPC beyond iddat period

The Supreme Court held that a divorced Muslim woman is entitled to maintenance under Section 125 CrPC even after the iddat period, as Section 125 is a secular provision applicable to all.

Report this question

3The Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act, 1986 was passed to:

  1. Nullify the effect of the Shah Bano judgment
  2. Grant Muslim women equal divorce rights
  3. Strengthen the Shah Bano judgment
  4. Codify Muslim women's right to maintenance under CrPC
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Nullify the effect of the Shah Bano judgment

The 1986 Act was enacted to nullify the Shah Bano judgment by providing that a Muslim divorced woman's right to maintenance is governed by Muslim Personal Law, not Section 125 CrPC.

Report this question

4In Danial Latifi v. Union of India (2001), the Supreme Court held that under the Muslim Women Act, 1986:

  1. The husband must make a fair and reasonable provision for the wife's entire future life during iddat itself
  2. Only the Waqf Board is responsible for maintenance
  3. Maintenance is only during iddat period
  4. Maintenance beyond iddat is unconstitutional
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The husband must make a fair and reasonable provision for the wife's entire future life during iddat itself

The Supreme Court gave an expansive interpretation — the husband must, within the iddat period, make a reasonable and fair provision and maintenance for the divorced wife's entire future life.

Report this question

5Dower (Mehr) under Muslim Law is:

  1. A gift from husband's parents
  2. A sum of money or property payable by husband to wife as a mark of respect
  3. Property brought by wife to the marriage
  4. Maintenance paid after divorce
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A sum of money or property payable by husband to wife as a mark of respect

Mehr (dower) is a sum of money or property payable by the husband to the wife as a token of respect for entering the marriage contract. It is a debt on the husband.

Report this question

6'Prompt dower' (Mu'ajjal) under Muslim Law means:

  1. Dower fixed by court
  2. Dower payable on dissolution of marriage
  3. Dower payable on demand by the wife before consummation
  4. Dower payable on husband's death
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Dower payable on demand by the wife before consummation

Mu'ajjal (prompt dower) is payable immediately on demand by the wife, even before consummation. The wife can refuse consummation until prompt dower is paid.

Report this question

7'Deferred dower' (Mu'wajjal) under Muslim Law becomes payable:

  1. Immediately after marriage
  2. On dissolution of marriage by death or divorce
  3. On wife's demand at any time
  4. After 7 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. On dissolution of marriage by death or divorce

Mu'wajjal (deferred dower) is payable on dissolution of the marriage, whether by death of the husband or divorce.

Report this question

8'Stridhan' under Hindu Law refers to:

  1. Absolute property of a woman given before or during or after marriage from various sources
  2. Property inherited from father only
  3. Joint family property of wife
  4. Husband's ancestral property
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Absolute property of a woman given before or during or after marriage from various sources

Stridhan is the absolute property of a woman — gifts received before, during, and after marriage from family, husband's family, and others. Section 14 HSA converts any limited interest to absolute ownership.

Report this question

9Under Section 18 of the Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956, a Hindu wife is entitled to separate residence and maintenance if the husband has:

  1. Taken another wife
  2. Been converted to another religion
  3. Treated her with cruelty
  4. All of the above
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. All of the above

Section 18 HAMA provides multiple grounds for a wife to claim separate residence and maintenance including polygamy, conversion, cruelty, desertion, and keeping a concubine.

Report this question

10The minimum quantum of dower (proper dower) under Hanafi law is:

  1. Any amount
  2. 10 dirhams
  3. 100 dirhams
  4. 500 dirhams
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 10 dirhams

Under Hanafi law, the minimum dower is 10 dirhams. Under Maliki law, it is 3 dirhams. If no dower is specified, 'proper dower' (Mahr-ul-misl) is fixed based on the dower of similar women in the family.

Report this question

11Under Section 125 CrPC (now BNSS), the maximum maintenance amount that was earlier fixed has been:

  1. Removed — no cap; court determines based on circumstances
  2. Fixed at Rs. 5000 per month
  3. Fixed at Rs. 25,000 per month
  4. Retained at Rs. 500 per month
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Removed — no cap; court determines based on circumstances

The Rs. 500 cap on maintenance under Section 125 CrPC was removed by the Code of Criminal Procedure (Amendment) Act, 2001. Now courts have discretion to award appropriate maintenance.

Report this question

12In Mohd. Ahmed Khan v. Shah Bano Begum (1985), which Article of the Constitution did the Supreme Court refer to while calling for a Uniform Civil Code?

  1. Article 21
  2. Article 51A
  3. Article 44
  4. Article 25
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Article 44

The Supreme Court in Shah Bano (1985) referred to Article 44 (Directive Principle — Uniform Civil Code) and lamented that it remains a dead letter.

Report this question

13Section 125 CrPC (now BNSS Section 144) provides maintenance to a wife who is unable to maintain herself. 'Wife' for this purpose includes:

  1. A divorced wife who has not remarried
  2. Only a currently married wife
  3. A divorced wife who has remarried
  4. Only a wife married under the Special Marriage Act
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A divorced wife who has not remarried

The expression 'wife' under Section 125 CrPC includes a woman who has been divorced by, or has obtained a divorce from, her husband and has not remarried.

Report this question

14In Iqbal Bano v. State of UP (2007), the Supreme Court held that:

  1. Muslim women cannot claim under Section 125 CrPC
  2. Section 125 CrPC applies to Muslim women unless they have opted for the Muslim Women Act, 1986
  3. The Muslim Women Act, 1986 completely replaces Section 125 CrPC for Muslim women
  4. Muslim women can claim maintenance under both Acts simultaneously
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Section 125 CrPC applies to Muslim women unless they have opted for the Muslim Women Act, 1986

The Supreme Court held that Section 125 CrPC is available to Muslim divorced women unless they have voluntarily chosen to proceed under the Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act, 1986.

Report this question

15Under Section 25 of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, permanent alimony and maintenance can be awarded:

  1. Only to the wife
  2. To either spouse — it is gender-neutral
  3. Only to the husband
  4. Only in cases of mutual consent divorce
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. To either spouse — it is gender-neutral

Section 25 HMA is gender-neutral and allows either the wife or the husband to claim permanent alimony and maintenance from the other.

Report this question

16Section 19 of the Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956 provides that after her husband's death, a Hindu widow is entitled to maintenance from:

  1. Her husband's brothers
  2. Her father-in-law (provided she is unable to maintain herself from her own sources)
  3. Her own parents
  4. The court directly
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Her father-in-law (provided she is unable to maintain herself from her own sources)

Section 19 HAMA provides that a Hindu wife who is unable to maintain herself from her own property or from her husband's estate is entitled to maintenance from her father-in-law. This obligation ceases on her remarriage.

Report this question

17Under Section 20 of the Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956, a Hindu is bound to maintain:

  1. Aged or infirm parents, minor children (legitimate and illegitimate), and unmarried daughters
  2. Parents and children only
  3. All family members
  4. Spouse and children only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Aged or infirm parents, minor children (legitimate and illegitimate), and unmarried daughters

Section 20 HAMA imposes an obligation on a Hindu to maintain aged or infirm parents, legitimate and illegitimate minor children, and unmarried daughters.
