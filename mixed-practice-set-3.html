1Itai-Itai disease is caused by:

  1. Mercury
  2. Arsenic
  3. Cadmium
  4. Lead
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Cadmium

Itai-Itai disease (meaning 'it hurts, it hurts' in Japanese) is caused by cadmium poisoning, leading to severe bone and kidney damage. It was first reported in Japan's Toyama Prefecture.

2Excess fluoride in groundwater causes:

  1. Minamata disease
  2. Fluorosis (dental and skeletal)
  3. Itai-Itai disease
  4. Blue baby syndrome
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Fluorosis (dental and skeletal)

Excess fluoride causes fluorosis — dental fluorosis (mottling of teeth) and skeletal fluorosis (bone damage). It is common in parts of Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh, and Gujarat in India.

3Blue baby syndrome (methemoglobinemia) is caused by excess:

  1. Fluoride in drinking water
  2. Nitrates in drinking water
  3. Arsenic in groundwater
  4. Lead in soil
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Nitrates in drinking water

Blue baby syndrome is caused by excess nitrates in drinking water, commonly from agricultural fertilizer run-off. Nitrates reduce the oxygen-carrying capacity of haemoglobin in infants.

4The permissible noise level (day time) in a residential area as per Indian standards is:

  1. 45 dB
  2. 55 dB
  3. 65 dB
  4. 75 dB
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 55 dB

As per Indian noise standards: Residential areas — Day: 55 dB, Night: 45 dB. Commercial: Day 65 dB, Night 55 dB. Industrial: Day 75 dB, Night 70 dB. Silence zones: Day 50 dB, Night 40 dB.

5What is the permissible noise level in silence zones (hospitals/schools) during daytime?

  1. 40 dB
  2. 45 dB
  3. 50 dB
  4. 55 dB
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 50 dB

Silence zones (hospitals, schools, courts) have the strictest noise limits: Day 50 dB, Night 40 dB, as per Indian noise pollution rules.

6Municipal Solid Waste (MSW) comprises of: (i) Household waste (ii) Sanitation residue (iii) Waste from streets (iv) Construction and demolition debris

  1. (i), (ii) and (iii) only
  2. (i) and (iii) only
  3. (i), (iii) and (iv) only
  4. (i), (ii), (iii) and (iv)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. (i), (ii), (iii) and (iv)

Municipal Solid Waste includes all four: household waste, sanitation residue, waste from streets, and construction & demolition debris — all managed by municipal authorities.

7Biomedical waste in India is regulated under:

  1. Environmental Protection Act, 1986
  2. Biomedical Waste Management Rules, 2016
  3. Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974
  4. Solid Waste Management Rules, 2000
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Biomedical Waste Management Rules, 2016

Biomedical waste (from hospitals, labs, clinics) is regulated under the Biomedical Waste Management Rules, 2016, which replaced the earlier 1998 rules.

8Which type of waste is the fastest growing category globally?

  1. Biomedical waste
  2. Solid municipal waste
  3. Electronic waste (E-waste)
  4. Hazardous industrial waste
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Electronic waste (E-waste)

Electronic waste (e-waste) is the fastest growing waste stream globally, driven by rapid obsolescence of electronic devices and rising consumption of electronics.

9Which greenhouse gas has the highest Global Warming Potential (GWP) per molecule?

  1. Carbon dioxide (CO2)
  2. Methane (CH4)
  3. Nitrous oxide (N2O)
  4. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)

CFCs have the highest GWP among common greenhouse gases. GWP order: CFCs >> N2O (298x CO2) >> CH4 (25x CO2) >> CO2 (baseline = 1). CFCs are also ozone-depleting.

10The annual range of temperature in the interior of continents is high compared to coastal areas because:

  1. Thermal difference between land and water only
  2. Heavy rains in the interior compared to coastal areas
  3. Presence of strong winds in the interior only
  4. Variations in altitude between continents and oceans
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Thermal difference between land and water only

The continentality effect occurs because land heats up and cools down much faster than water. This thermal difference between land and water is the primary reason for the higher annual temperature range in continental interiors.

11Which of the following is NOT a direct effect of climate change?

  1. Sea level rise
  2. Glacier retreat
  3. Ocean acidification
  4. Ozone hole expansion
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Ozone hole expansion

The ozone hole is caused by CFCs destroying ozone molecules — it is separate from climate change (though CFCs are also greenhouse gases). Sea level rise, glacier retreat, and ocean acidification are all direct consequences of global warming.

12NDCs in the context of the Paris Agreement stands for:

  1. National Development Contributions
  2. Nationally Determined Contributions
  3. National Decarbonisation Commitments
  4. Non-Deforestation Clauses
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Nationally Determined Contributions

NDCs (Nationally Determined Contributions) are climate action plans that each country voluntarily pledges under the Paris Agreement. Each country determines its own targets for reducing emissions and adapting to climate impacts.

Report this question

13Acid rain is primarily caused by emissions of:

  1. CO2 and CH4
  2. SO2 and NOx
  3. CFCs and HFCs
  4. CO and particulate matter
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. SO2 and NOx

Acid rain is caused by emissions of sulphur dioxide (SO2) and nitrogen oxides (NOx), mainly from burning fossil fuels and industrial processes. These gases react with water, oxygen, and other chemicals in the atmosphere to form acidic compounds.

14The 'greenhouse effect' naturally keeps Earth's surface about how much warmer than it would otherwise be?

  1. 10°C warmer
  2. 23°C warmer
  3. 33°C warmer
  4. 45°C warmer
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 33°C warmer

The natural greenhouse effect keeps Earth about 33°C warmer than it would otherwise be (average temperature would be -18°C without greenhouse gases, instead of the actual +15°C). The enhanced greenhouse effect from human emissions is causing additional unnatural warming.

15Which of the following statements about the Paris Agreement is INCORRECT?

  1. It applies to all UN member nations, both developed and developing
  2. It requires all countries to submit legally binding emission targets
  3. It aims to limit warming to well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels
  4. It was adopted at COP21 in Paris in 2015
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. It requires all countries to submit legally binding emission targets

The Paris Agreement applies to all countries, but the NDCs (Nationally Determined Contributions) themselves are NOT legally binding — countries determine their own targets voluntarily. Only the obligation to submit and update NDCs is binding, not the targets within them. This is a frequent PYQ distinction.

16The Environmental Protection Act (EPA) of India was enacted in:

  1. 1972
  2. 1980
  3. 1984
  4. 1986
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 1986

The Environmental Protection Act was enacted in 1986, primarily in response to the Bhopal Gas Tragedy of December 1984. It is the umbrella legislation for environmental protection in India.

17The Bhopal Gas Tragedy occurred in:

  1. November 1982
  2. December 1984
  3. January 1986
  4. March 1988
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. December 1984

The Bhopal Gas Tragedy occurred on the night of December 2–3, 1984, at the Union Carbide India Limited pesticide plant. A methyl isocyanate (MIC) gas leak caused one of the world's worst industrial disasters.

18How many National Missions are included in India's National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC)?

  1. 5
  2. 6
  3. 8
  4. 10
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 8

NAPCC (2008) contains 8 National Missions: Solar Mission, Energy Efficiency, Sustainable Habitat, Water, Himalayan Ecosystem, Green India, Sustainable Agriculture, and Strategic Knowledge for Climate Change.

19Which National Mission under NAPCC focuses specifically on afforestation and improving forest cover?

  1. National Water Mission
  2. National Mission for Sustaining the Himalayan Ecosystem
  3. National Mission for a Green India
  4. National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. National Mission for a Green India

The National Mission for a Green India (GIM) under NAPCC aims to protect, restore, and enhance India's forest cover and respond to climate change through a combination of adaptation and mitigation measures.
