21 practice questions with answers

1Article 32 and Article 226 — what is the distinction?

  1. Art. 32 is for SC; Art. 226 is for High Courts; Art. 226 is wider in scope
  2. Art. 32 is for SC; Art. 226 is for High Courts; Art. 32 is wider
  3. Art. 226 can only be used for Fundamental Rights enforcement
  4. Both are identical in scope
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Art. 32 is for SC; Art. 226 is for High Courts; Art. 226 is wider in scope

Article 32 is for Supreme Court — only for Fundamental Rights enforcement. Article 226 is for High Courts — wider, can be used for enforcement of any legal right, not just Fundamental Rights.

2Independence of the judiciary is secured by which provision among others?

  1. Salaries charged to Consolidated Fund of India
  2. Removal only by impeachment
  3. Security of tenure
  4. All of the above
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. All of the above

Judicial independence is secured through multiple mechanisms: fixed salaries charged to Consolidated Fund (not subject to vote), security of tenure until retirement age, removal only through impeachment process.

3The process of appointment of Supreme Court judges — the 'Collegium System' — was evolved through:

  1. All three together established the current system
  2. Second Judges Case (1993)
  3. Third Judges Case (1998)
  4. First Judges Case (1981)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. All three together established the current system

First Judges Case (S.P. Gupta, 1981) — Executive primacy. Second Judges Case (1993) — Collegium system; CJI's opinion gets primacy. Third Judges Case (1998) — Collegium = CJI + 4 senior-most SC judges. NJAC (99th Amendment, 2014) was struck down in 2015. Concept note: Three Judges Cases: The current collegium system where the Chief Justice and 4 senior-most judges recommend appointments gives the judiciary primacy over executive in judicial appointments. The NJAC established by 99th Amendment (2014) was struck down as violative of judicial independence — itself a basic structure element.

4Article 141 provides that the law declared by the Supreme Court shall be binding on:

  1. All High Courts only
  2. All courts in India
  3. All courts in India except the Supreme Court itself
  4. All lower courts only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. All courts in India

Article 141 provides that the law declared by the Supreme Court shall be binding on all courts within the territory of India. The Supreme Court itself follows the principle of stare decisis but is not absolutely bound by its own decisions.

5The Supreme Court's original jurisdiction in disputes between States is provided under:

  1. Article 131
  2. Article 132
  3. Article 136
  4. Article 143
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Article 131

Article 131 gives the Supreme Court exclusive original jurisdiction to adjudicate disputes between the Government of India and one or more States, or between two or more States.

6Article 136 — Special Leave Petition (SLP) — grants the Supreme Court:

  1. Advisory jurisdiction
  2. Original jurisdiction
  3. Revisional jurisdiction
  4. Discretionary appellate jurisdiction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Discretionary appellate jurisdiction

Article 136 grants the Supreme Court wide discretionary power to grant special leave to appeal from any judgment, decree, determination, sentence, or order of any court or tribunal in India.

Report this question

7Public Interest Litigation (PIL) as a concept is an extension of:

  1. Article 131 jurisdiction
  2. Article 136 SLP
  3. Article 32 and Article 226 jurisdiction (epistolary jurisdiction)
  4. Article 143 advisory opinion
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Article 32 and Article 226 jurisdiction (epistolary jurisdiction)

PIL evolved through liberalization of locus standi under Articles 32 and 226. The SC allowed any public-spirited person to write a letter (epistolary jurisdiction) to approach the court on behalf of those unable to access justice. Pioneered by Justices P.N. Bhagwati and V.R. Krishna Iyer.

8Which Article provides for the High Court's power of superintendence over all subordinate courts?

  1. Article 235
  2. Article 228
  3. Article 226
  4. Article 227
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Article 227

Article 227 gives every High Court the power of superintendence over all courts and tribunals throughout the territories in relation to which it exercises jurisdiction.

9The doctrine of 'judicial review' in India covers:

  1. Constitutionality of legislative and executive action only
  2. Only legislative acts
  3. Legislative acts, executive actions, and constitutional amendments (subject to basic structure)
  4. Only executive actions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Legislative acts, executive actions, and constitutional amendments (subject to basic structure)

Judicial review in India covers three areas: (1) legislative acts — struck down if violate Constitution; (2) executive/administrative actions — struck down if ultra vires, unreasonable, or against natural justice; (3) constitutional amendments — struck down if they violate basic structure (post-Kesavananda Bharati).

10The Chief Justice of a High Court is appointed by the President in consultation with:

  1. Chief Justice of India and the Governor of the State — Article 217
  2. Chief Justice of India only
  3. The Governor only
  4. The State Council of Ministers
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Chief Justice of India and the Governor of the State — Article 217

Article 217 provides that the Chief Justice of a High Court shall be appointed by the President after consultation with the Chief Justice of India and the Governor of the State. For other HC judges, the Chief Justice of the concerned HC is also consulted.

11A High Court judge holds office until:

  1. Age of 62 years
  2. The pleasure of the President
  3. Age of 65 years
  4. Age of 60 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Age of 62 years

Article 217(1) provides that a Judge of a High Court shall hold office until he attains the age of 62 years. Supreme Court judges retire at 65 years under Article 124(2).

12The power of a High Court to transfer cases from subordinate courts involving substantial constitutional questions is provided under:

  1. Article 228
  2. Article 226
  3. Article 227
  4. Article 229
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Article 228

Article 228 provides that if the High Court is satisfied that a case pending in a court subordinate to it involves a substantial question of law as to the interpretation of the Constitution, it may withdraw the case and either dispose of it itself or determine the question and return the case.

13Which of the following is CORRECT about High Courts' writ jurisdiction under Article 226?

  1. It is exactly the same as Article 32
  2. It can only be used against State government
  3. It is narrower than SC's writ jurisdiction under Article 32
  4. It is wider than Article 32 — can be used for enforcement of any legal right, not just FRs
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. It is wider than Article 32 — can be used for enforcement of any legal right, not just FRs

Article 226 is wider than Article 32 in two respects: (1) It can be used for enforcement of any legal right, not just Fundamental Rights; (2) High Courts can issue writs against any person or authority within their territorial jurisdiction. However, Article 32 is itself a Fundamental Right, unlike Article 226.

14The concept of 'Territorial Jurisdiction' of High Courts for writ petitions means:

  1. Any HC in India has jurisdiction for Fundamental Rights violations
  2. Only domicile of the petitioner matters
  3. Writs can only be filed in the State where the petitioner resides
  4. The cause of action must arise or the respondent must be located within the HC's territorial jurisdiction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The cause of action must arise or the respondent must be located within the HC's territorial jurisdiction

Under Article 226, the High Court can issue writs to any person or authority within the territories in relation to which it exercises jurisdiction. If any part of the cause of action arises within the HC's territorial jurisdiction, it has jurisdiction. This is especially important for petitions against Central Government.

15Assertion (A): A writ of certiorari under Art. 226 lies against judicial orders of civil courts.
Reason (R): In Radhey Shyam v. Chhabi Nath (2015), the Supreme Court held that orders of civil courts are amenable to supervisory jurisdiction under Art. 227 and not to writ of certiorari.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A is false but R is true

Radhey Shyam overruled Surya Dev Rai; judicial orders of civil courts can be corrected under Art. 227 only.

16Under s.12 of the Contempt of Courts Act, 1971, the maximum punishment for contempt of court is:

  1. Imprisonment up to two years
  2. Imprisonment up to one year or fine up to Rs 5,000
  3. Simple imprisonment up to six months, or fine up to Rs 2,000, or both
  4. Fine up to Rs 10,000 only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Simple imprisonment up to six months, or fine up to Rs 2,000, or both

An apology may be accepted in discharge or remission of punishment.

17Which reference under Art. 143 was returned unanswered by the Supreme Court?

  1. Re Berubari Union
  2. Re Keshav Singh
  3. Re Presidential Poll
  4. Special Reference No. 1 of 1993 (Ayodhya reference)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Special Reference No. 1 of 1993 (Ayodhya reference)

In Dr. M. Ismail Faruqui v. Union of India (1994) the Court declined to answer the reference.

18The first Judge against whom a motion for removal (impeachment) was moved in the Lok Sabha was:

  1. Justice Soumitra Sen
  2. Justice P.D. Dinakaran
  3. Justice V. Ramaswami
  4. Justice S.K. Gangele

19In State of West Bengal v. Nripendra Nath Bagchi (1966), the Supreme Court held that 'control' over subordinate courts under Art. 235 includes:

  1. Only transfers and postings
  2. Only the power to recommend promotions
  3. Powers of the Governor alone
  4. Disciplinary control including the power to dismiss, remove or reduce in rank, vested in the High Court
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Disciplinary control including the power to dismiss, remove or reduce in rank, vested in the High Court

The High Court's control is exclusive of the executive.

20In Prem Chand Garg v. Excise Commissioner, U.P. (1962), the Supreme Court held that:

  1. Art. 142 can override Fundamental Rights
  2. An order under Art. 142 cannot contravene the Fundamental Rights, and Supreme Court rules cannot abridge the free exercise of Art. 32
  3. Art. 145 rules override Art. 32
  4. Security deposits for writs are valid
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. An order under Art. 142 cannot contravene the Fundamental Rights, and Supreme Court rules cannot abridge the free exercise of Art. 32

A rule requiring security deposit for Art. 32 petitions was struck down.
