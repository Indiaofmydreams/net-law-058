About this question set

This set has 8 multiple-choice questions on Landmark Cases from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 2, Constitutional and Administrative Law. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 8 of the 8 questions carry a written explanation.

8 practice questions with answers

1Arrange the following cases on Art. 356 and State Governments in chronological order:
P. S.R. Bommai v. Union of India
Q. State of Rajasthan v. Union of India
R. Nabam Rebia v. Deputy Speaker
S. Rameshwar Prasad v. Union of India

  1. Q-P-S-R
  2. P-Q-S-R
  3. Q-S-P-R
  4. Q-P-R-S
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Q-P-S-R

1977, 1994, 2006, 2016.

Q. Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India

  1. Q-S-R-P
  2. S-Q-R-P
  3. S-R-Q-P
  4. S-Q-P-R

1962, 1978, 1996, 2017.

3Match the case with its holding.
List I:
1. Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India
2. Joseph Shine v. Union of India
3. NALSA v. Union of India
4. K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India
List II:
a. Right to privacy is a Fundamental Right
b. Decriminalised consensual same-sex acts under s.377 IPC
c. Struck down s.497 IPC (adultery)
d. Recognition of transgender persons as a 'third gender'

  1. 1-b, 2-c, 3-a, 4-d
  2. 1-c, 2-b, 3-d, 4-a
  3. 1-b, 2-c, 3-d, 4-a
  4. 1-b, 2-d, 3-c, 4-a
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1-b, 2-c, 3-d, 4-a

Navtej and Joseph Shine are 2018; NALSA 2014; Puttaswamy 2017.

4In Indian Young Lawyers Association v. State of Kerala (Sabarimala, 2018), the Judge who dissented in the 4:1 verdict was:

  1. Justice D.Y. Chandrachud
  2. Justice A.M. Khanwilkar
  3. Justice R.F. Nariman
  4. Justice Indu Malhotra
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Justice Indu Malhotra

Indu Malhotra J held that courts should not interfere in matters of religious faith.

5In Kedar Nath Singh v. State of Bihar (1962), the Supreme Court:

  1. Struck down s.124A IPC
  2. Upheld s.124A IPC but limited it to acts having a tendency or intention to create public disorder or incite violence
  3. Held sedition requires foreign instigation
  4. Held s.124A violates Art. 14
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Upheld s.124A IPC but limited it to acts having a tendency or intention to create public disorder or incite violence

S.G. Vombatkere (2022) kept s.124A in abeyance; BNS s.152 replaced it.

Report this question

6In Association for Democratic Reforms v. Union of India (2024), the Supreme Court struck down the Electoral Bond Scheme because it:

  1. Violated Art. 21A
  2. Violated the voters' right to information under Art. 19(1)(a)
  3. Violated Art. 32
  4. Lacked Presidential assent
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Violated the voters' right to information under Art. 19(1)(a)

The Court held unlimited anonymous corporate funding violates free and fair elections.

Report this question

7In Government of NCT of Delhi v. Union of India (2023), a Constitution Bench held that the Delhi Government has legislative and executive control over 'services' except:

  1. Revenue and finance
  2. Education and health
  3. Public order, police and land
  4. Water and electricity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Public order, police and land

The Union later enacted the GNCTD (Amendment) Act, 2023.

Report this question

8In Anuradha Bhasin v. Union of India (2020), the Supreme Court held that:

  1. Internet access is not protected
  2. Art. 19(2) is inapplicable to the internet
  3. Internet shutdowns need no orders
  4. Freedom of speech and trade through the internet is protected under Arts. 19(1)(a) and 19(1)(g), and indefinite suspension of the internet is impermissible
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Freedom of speech and trade through the internet is protected under Arts. 19(1)(a) and 19(1)(g), and indefinite suspension of the internet is impermissible

Restrictions must satisfy proportionality and be published.
