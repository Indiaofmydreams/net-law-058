UGC NET LAW 058 · 2 WEEKS PATHWAY

You have 2 weeks. Here is how I would approach it.

  • Retrieve, practise and refine

If you have only two weeks, don’t panic. Your strategy simply has to change.

Two weeks is not the time to learn the whole syllabus from scratch. It is the time to retrieve, to become familiar with how questions are framed, and to remove avoidable mistakes.

So I would work in a tight loop: revise a high-yield unit, attempt its MCQs, mark every mistake, fix it, and attempt again within a day or two. By “high-yield” I don’t mean a guess about what will be asked. I mean the units where your own results and the past papers show you are losing marks.

High-yield revision → MCQs → PYQs → Mistakes → Mock tests → Targeted revision

Don’t change your resources now. Use the notes, MCQ explanations and PYQs on this site as one connected system. You are not trying to feel ready. You are trying to be practised.

1What your priority should be

  • Retrieve, don’t relearnYour goal is recall, familiarity, accuracy and exam readiness.
  • Follow your mistakesEach wrong answer tells you exactly what to revise next.
  • Learn how the exam asksPYQs show phrasing, traps and recurring areas.
  • Protect your energyA steady routine and sleep beat one more late-night session.

3Your preparation cycle

Each day, I would keep to this short loop.

  1. LEARNNotes
  2. PRACTISETopic MCQs
  3. RETRIEVERecall, then check
  4. TESTPYQs and mocks
  5. ANALYSEError log
  6. REVISEWeak units
  7. REPEATNext cycle
THE CORE LOOP

Study → Attempt MCQs → Understand mistakes → Revisit the concept → Attempt again

  • FirstRead yesterday’s mistakes before anything new.
  • ThenRevise one unit briefly and attempt its MCQs.
  • ThenFix every mistake from the notes or the explanation.
  • DailyA small Paper 1 set, kept short.
  • EveningStop on time. Rest is part of the plan.

Don’t do this

  • Don’t collect endless study material.
  • Don’t postpone MCQs until you finish the syllabus.
  • Don’t repeatedly read notes without testing recall.
  • Don’t take mocks without analysing your mistakes.
  • Don’t completely change your resources in the final weeks.
  • Don’t try to learn the whole syllabus from scratch.
  • Don’t trade sleep for one more hour of reading.

THE EVIDENCEWhy MCQs sit at the centre of this plan

Spitzer’s (1939) study of schoolchildren found that testing soon after reading slowed forgetting,11 and Roediger and Karpicke (2006) found that tests produced better retention after a week than restudying.2 Roediger and Karpicke also found that restudying can feel more effective at first, which is why it is tempting.

Cepeda and colleagues (2008) found that the best gap between study sessions depends on how long you need to remember.8 My inference is that with a short horizon, re-attempting missed questions within a day or two is sensible. For classroom-oriented guidance, see Agarwal and Bain.14

What this does and doesn’t show. These studies are mostly laboratory and classroom experiments on memory. None of them tested a UGC NET strategy, and none can promise a result in the exam. This pathway is informed by evidence on retrieval practice, practice testing and distributed learning. It is guidance, not a guarantee.
