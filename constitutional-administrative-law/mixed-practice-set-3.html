20 practice questions with answers

1The word 'SOCIALIST' was inserted into the Preamble of the Indian Constitution by which Amendment?

  1. 46th Amendment
  2. 43rd Amendment
  3. 44th Amendment
  4. 42nd Amendment
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 42nd Amendment

The 42nd Amendment (1976) inserted 'Socialist', 'Secular', and 'Integrity' into the Preamble.

Report this question

2The Preamble of the Indian Constitution declares India to be a:

  1. Sovereign Secular Federal Republic
  2. Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic
  3. Unitary Socialist Republic
  4. Federal Democratic Republic
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic

The Preamble reads: 'We, the people of India, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic...'

Report this question

3In which landmark case did the Supreme Court hold that the Preamble is a part of the Constitution and can be used to interpret its provisions?

  1. Berubari Union Case
  2. Golaknath v. State of Punjab
  3. Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala
  4. Minerva Mills v. Union of India
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala

In Kesavananda Bharati (1973), the Supreme Court by a 7:6 majority held that the Preamble is a part of the Constitution. Earlier in Berubari Case (1960), the SC held the Preamble was not part of the Constitution. Concept note: Basic Structure Doctrine: In Kesavananda Bharati (1973), the Supreme Court held that Parliament can amend any part of the Constitution but CANNOT destroy its 'basic structure.' The Preamble reflects the basic structure. Key elements include: supremacy of Constitution, republican and democratic form, secular character, separation of powers, federal character, and judicial review.

Report this question

4Directive Principles of State Policy are contained in which Part of the Constitution?

  1. Part III
  2. Part IV
  3. Part IVA
  4. Part XIV
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Part IV

Part III contains Fundamental Rights (Articles 12–35). Part IV contains Directive Principles (Articles 36–51). Part IVA contains Fundamental Duties (Article 51-A).

Report this question

5Directive Principles are NOT enforceable in court. Which Article makes this explicit?

  1. Article 38
  2. Article 40
  3. Article 36
  4. Article 37
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Article 37

Article 37 states that the provisions in Part IV shall not be enforceable by any court, but the principles are nevertheless fundamental in the governance of the country.

Report this question

6In the conflict between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles, which prevails according to Minerva Mills v. Union of India (1980)?

  1. Directive Principles always prevail
  2. Fundamental Rights always prevail
  3. There is a harmonious balance; neither can destroy the other
  4. Parliament can decide case by case
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. There is a harmonious balance; neither can destroy the other

In Minerva Mills (1980), the Court struck down provisions of the 42nd Amendment which gave absolute primacy to DPSPs, holding that harmony between Parts III and IV is itself a basic feature of the Constitution. Concept note: FR vs DPSP Evolution: State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan (1951) — FRs prevailed; Golaknath (1967) — FRs unamendable; Kesavananda Bharati (1973) — both can coexist; Minerva Mills (1980) — harmony between Parts III and IV is basic structure.

7Which Directive Principle directs the State to organize village panchayats?

  1. Article 43
  2. Article 41
  3. Article 39
  4. Article 40
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Article 40

Article 40 directs the State to take steps to organise village panchayats and endow them with such powers and authority as may be necessary to enable them to function as units of self-government.

Report this question

8Assertion (A): DPSPs are non-justiciable. Reason (R): They are fundamental in governance and it is the duty of the State to apply them in making laws.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A

Both A (Article 37 — non-justiciable) and R (Article 37 — fundamental in governance) are true. However, R does not explain WHY they are non-justiciable. They are non-justiciable because of a deliberate constitutional design separating justiciable rights from non-justiciable policy guidelines.

Report this question

9Fundamental Duties are contained in which Article of the Constitution?

  1. Article 50A
  2. Article 51-A
  3. Article 52-A
  4. Article 51
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Article 51-A

Article 51-A, inserted by the 42nd Amendment, 1976, contains the Fundamental Duties. The 11th duty was added by the 86th Amendment, 2002.

Report this question

10How many Fundamental Duties are currently in Article 51-A?

  1. 12
  2. 9
  3. 10
  4. 11
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 11

Originally 10 Fundamental Duties were inserted by the 42nd Amendment (1976). The 11th duty — to provide opportunities for education to children between 6-14 years — was added by the 86th Amendment (2002).

11The Governor of a State is appointed by:

  1. The President of India
  2. The State Legislature
  3. The Chief Minister
  4. The Prime Minister
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The President of India

Article 155 provides that the Governor of each State shall be appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal.

Report this question

12Article 200 deals with:

  1. Money Bills in State Legislature
  2. Joint sitting of Parliament
  3. Assent to Bills by the President
  4. Assent to Bills by the Governor
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Assent to Bills by the Governor

Article 200 provides that when a Bill has been passed by the State Legislature, it shall be presented to the Governor who shall declare his assent, withhold assent, return (non-money bill) for reconsideration, or reserve it for the President.

Report this question

13The special provisions for certain States are contained in which Part of the Constitution?

  1. Part XX
  2. Part XXII
  3. Part XXI
  4. Part XIX
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Part XXI

Part XXI of the Constitution (Articles 369-392) contains temporary, transitional and special provisions including special provisions for certain States under Articles 370-371J.

Report this question

14Article 370, which granted special status to Jammu & Kashmir, was:

  1. Struck down by Supreme Court
  2. Amended by 99th Amendment
  3. Its operative provisions abrogated by Presidential Order in August 2019
  4. Repealed by Parliament
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Its operative provisions abrogated by Presidential Order in August 2019

Presidential Order CO 272 (2019) effectively abrogated Article 370's special provisions. J&K was reorganized into two Union Territories — J&K and Ladakh. The Supreme Court upheld the abrogation in Dr. Farooq Abdullah v. Union of India (2023).

Report this question

15Article 371 relates to special provisions for which State?

  1. Assam
  2. Nagaland
  3. Mizoram
  4. Maharashtra and Gujarat
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Maharashtra and Gujarat

Article 371 — Maharashtra and Gujarat. Article 371-A — Nagaland. Article 371-B — Assam. Article 371-C — Manipur. Article 371-D — Andhra Pradesh. Article 371-F — Sikkim. Article 371-G — Mizoram. Article 371-H — Arunachal Pradesh.

Report this question

16Which Article allows Parliament to provide for the establishment of local legislatures or Councils of Ministers for Union Territories?

  1. Article 239
  2. Article 239-A
  3. Article 240
  4. Article 241
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Article 239-A

Article 239-A was inserted by the 14th Amendment, 1962. It allows Parliament to create a body to function as a Legislature or a Council of Ministers or both for certain Union Territories.

Report this question

17The Election Commission of India is established under which Article of the Constitution?

  1. Article 326
  2. Article 324
  3. Article 328
  4. Article 323
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Article 324

Article 324 vests the superintendence, direction, and control of the preparation of electoral rolls and the conduct of all elections to Parliament and State Legislatures in the Election Commission of India.

Report this question

18The Chief Election Commissioner can be removed from office:

  1. Only in the same manner as a Supreme Court judge
  2. By a resolution of Parliament
  3. By the Prime Minister
  4. By the President at will
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Only in the same manner as a Supreme Court judge

The CEC is removed from office in the same manner and on the same grounds as a Supreme Court judge — by an address of each House of Parliament supported by a majority of total membership and not less than two-thirds of members present and voting.

Report this question

19The Election Commission becomes multi-member when:

  1. There are more than 3 candidates in any election
  2. The President so appoints other Election Commissioners
  3. Parliament passes a law for it
  4. During general elections only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The President so appoints other Election Commissioners

Article 324(2) provides that the Election Commission shall consist of the CEC and such number of other Election Commissioners as the President may from time to time fix.

Report this question

20The Model Code of Conduct is:

  1. An executive order under Article 77
  2. A constitutional provision
  3. A set of guidelines evolved by the Election Commission — not statutory, but binding in practice
  4. A statutory document under Representation of People Act
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A set of guidelines evolved by the Election Commission — not statutory, but binding in practice

The Model Code of Conduct is not a statutory document. It is a set of guidelines evolved by the Election Commission to regulate the conduct of political parties and candidates during elections. It derives force from Article 324.
