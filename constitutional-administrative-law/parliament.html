19 practice questions with answers

1The maximum strength of the Lok Sabha after the 104th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2020 is:

  1. 552 (530 + 20 + 2 Anglo-Indians)
  2. 543 elected members only
  3. 550 members
  4. 545 members
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 543 elected members only

The 104th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2020 abolished the provision for nomination of Anglo-Indian members to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies. The maximum strength of Lok Sabha is now 543 elected members.

2The maximum strength of the Rajya Sabha is:

  1. 238 elected + 12 nominated = 250
  2. 250 elected + 12 nominated = 262
  3. 200 + 12 = 212
  4. 233 elected + 12 nominated = 245
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 238 elected + 12 nominated = 250

Article 80 provides that the Council of States (Rajya Sabha) shall consist of not more than 238 representatives of States and Union Territories (elected) and 12 nominated by the President for their expertise in literature, science, art, and social service. Maximum = 250.

3Under Article 110, a Bill is a Money Bill if it contains ONLY provisions dealing with:

  1. Imposition, abolition, remission, alteration, or regulation of any tax
  2. Appropriation of moneys from the Consolidated Fund
  3. Borrowing of money by the Government
  4. All of the above among other specified matters in Art. 110(1)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. All of the above among other specified matters in Art. 110(1)

Article 110(1) defines a Money Bill as a Bill containing ONLY provisions dealing with matters specified in clauses (a) to (g), including: (a) imposition/abolition/remission/alteration/regulation of tax; (b) regulation of borrowing; (c) custody of Consolidated Fund; (d) appropriation from Consolidated Fund; (e) supplementary expenditure; (f) audit accounts; (g) any incidental matter.

4A Money Bill can be introduced only in:

  1. A joint sitting of Parliament
  2. Either House
  3. Rajya Sabha
  4. Lok Sabha — Article 109
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Lok Sabha — Article 109

Article 109 provides that a Money Bill shall not be introduced in the Council of States (Rajya Sabha). It must be introduced only in the Lok Sabha. The Rajya Sabha can only make recommendations on a Money Bill within 14 days — Lok Sabha may accept or reject them.

5Parliamentary privilege under Article 105 includes:

  1. Freedom of speech in Parliament
  2. Right not to be arrested in civil cases during session and 40 days before/after
  3. Exemption from jury service
  4. Both A and B
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Both A and B

Article 105 privileges include: (1) Freedom of speech in Parliament — MPs cannot be sued for anything said in Parliament; (2) No arrest in civil cases during session + 40 days before and after. Key case: Sita Soren v. Union of India (2024) — 7-judge bench held that bribe-taking MPs have NO immunity under Article 105, overruling P.V. Narasimha Rao (1998). Concept note: Parliamentary Privileges: Article 105(2) — no Member liable for anything said or vote given in Parliament. The court cannot question Parliamentary proceedings. Sita Soren v. Union of India (2024) is the latest landmark — MPs who take bribes to vote are NOT protected.

6A joint sitting of both Houses under Article 108 CANNOT be convened for which Bills?

  1. Money Bills and Constitutional Amendment Bills
  2. Only Constitutional Amendment Bills
  3. Financial Bills
  4. Ordinary Bills only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Money Bills and Constitutional Amendment Bills

Joint sitting under Article 108 is only for ordinary Bills on which there is a deadlock. Money Bills cannot go to joint sitting (they are exclusively Lok Sabha's domain). Constitutional Amendment Bills under Article 368 must be passed by each House separately by special majority — no joint sitting.

Report this question

7The 'Anti-Defection Law' is contained in which Schedule of the Constitution?

  1. Eleventh Schedule
  2. Ninth Schedule
  3. Eighth Schedule
  4. Tenth Schedule
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Tenth Schedule

The Tenth Schedule was inserted by the 52nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1985. It provides for disqualification of members on grounds of defection. The 91st Amendment (2003) removed the 'split' exception, retaining only 'merger' (2/3 members joining another party).

8Under the Tenth Schedule, a Member of the Legislature is disqualified for defection if:

  1. He voluntarily gives up membership of his political party OR votes contrary to party direction without prior permission and without being condoned within 15 days
  2. He is absent during any vote
  3. He votes against his party's direction on any vote
  4. He criticizes his party leader publicly
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. He voluntarily gives up membership of his political party OR votes contrary to party direction without prior permission and without being condoned within 15 days

Under the Tenth Schedule, a member is disqualified if: (1) he voluntarily gives up membership of his political party; (2) he votes or abstains in contravention of any direction issued by his party without prior permission and the party has not condoned it within 15 days.

9The Ninth Schedule of the Constitution was introduced by which Amendment and for what purpose?

  1. 44th Amendment, 1978
  2. First Constitutional Amendment, 1951 — to protect land reform laws from Fundamental Rights challenge
  3. Original Constitution — to protect land reform laws
  4. 42nd Amendment, 1976
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. First Constitutional Amendment, 1951 — to protect land reform laws from Fundamental Rights challenge

The Ninth Schedule was inserted by the Constitution (First Amendment) Act, 1951 to protect certain land reform laws from being challenged on the ground of violation of Fundamental Rights. Key case: I.R. Coelho v. State of Tamil Nadu (2007) — laws in 9th Schedule after April 24, 1973 (Kesavananda) can be challenged if they violate basic structure.

10Under Article 368, which constitutional provisions require ratification by not less than half of the State Legislatures?

  1. Federal provisions — Art. 54, 55, 73, 162, Seventh Schedule, representation of States in Parliament, Art. 368 itself
  2. All constitutional amendments
  3. Directive Principles provisions
  4. Fundamental Rights provisions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Federal provisions — Art. 54, 55, 73, 162, Seventh Schedule, representation of States in Parliament, Art. 368 itself

Article 368 provides three categories of amendment: (1) Simple majority — matters like creation of new States; (2) Special majority (2/3 + majority of total) — most provisions; (3) Special majority + ratification by not less than half of State Legislatures — federal provisions affecting Centre-State relations.

11Which of the following about Parliamentary privileges is/are CORRECT? (i) Freedom of speech in Parliament is absolute; (ii) A Member can be arrested for a criminal offence even during session; (iii) Parliamentary proceedings cannot be questioned in any court; (iv) Strangers can be excluded from galleries

  1. (i) and (iii) only
  2. (ii), (iii), and (iv) only
  3. (i), (ii), and (iii) only
  4. All of the above
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. (ii), (iii), and (iv) only

(i) is INCORRECT — freedom of speech in Parliament is subject to rules/orders of Parliament, not absolute; (ii) CORRECT — no immunity from criminal arrest (only civil arrest immunity); (iii) CORRECT — Article 105(2) courts cannot question proceedings; (iv) CORRECT — both Houses can exclude strangers from galleries during session.

12Arrange these Constitutional Amendments in chronological order (earliest first): (i) Insertion of Fundamental Duties (51-A); (ii) Right to Property ceased to be Fundamental Right; (iii) 'Socialist' and 'Secular' in Preamble; (iv) 10th Schedule (Anti-Defection)

  1. (i), (iii), (ii), (iv)
  2. (iii), (i), (ii), (iv)
  3. (ii), (i), (iii), (iv)
  4. (iii) and (i) together (1976), then (ii) (1978), then (iv) (1985)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. (iii) and (i) together (1976), then (ii) (1978), then (iv) (1985)

42nd Amendment, 1976 — inserted both (iii) 'Socialist' and 'Secular' in Preamble AND (i) Fundamental Duties (Art. 51-A) in the same amendment. 44th Amendment, 1978 — removed (ii) right to property as FR. 52nd Amendment, 1985 — inserted (iv) Tenth Schedule (Anti-Defection Law).

13Which of the following Schedules relates to the languages recognized by the Constitution?

  1. Ninth Schedule
  2. Eighth Schedule
  3. Tenth Schedule
  4. Seventh Schedule
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Eighth Schedule

The Eighth Schedule contains the list of languages officially recognized by the Constitution. Originally 14 languages at inception; now 22 languages after various amendments. The 8th Schedule is also relevant for Official Language purposes under Articles 344 and 351.

14Which of the following Bills was NOT enacted at a joint sitting of the two Houses under Art. 108?

  1. Dowry Prohibition Bill, 1960
  2. Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill, 1977
  3. Prevention of Terrorism Bill, 2002
  4. Citizenship (Amendment) Bill, 2019
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Citizenship (Amendment) Bill, 2019

Joint sittings have been held only three times.

15In the Aadhaar case (Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Retd.) v. Union of India, 2018), the Supreme Court by majority:

  1. Held the Aadhaar Act could not be certified as a Money Bill
  2. Held the Aadhaar Act was validly passed as a Money Bill under Art. 110(1)(a)
  3. Declined to examine the Speaker's certification
  4. Referred the Money Bill question to a seven-judge Bench
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Held the Aadhaar Act was validly passed as a Money Bill under Art. 110(1)(a)

Chandrachud J dissented, calling the Money Bill route a fraud on the Constitution.

16Consider a Money Bill:
1. It can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha.
2. It can be introduced only on the recommendation of the President.
3. The Rajya Sabha may only make recommendations, to be returned within 14 days.
4. It can be introduced in the Rajya Sabha with the Speaker's certificate.

  1. 1, 2 and 3 only
  2. 1, 3 and 4 only
  3. 2, 3 and 4 only
  4. 1, 2 and 4 only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 1, 2 and 3 only

Art. 109; Art. 117(1).

17In Tej Kiran Jain v. N. Sanjiva Reddy (1970), the Supreme Court held that:

  1. Art. 105(2) immunity extends only to relevant speeches
  2. Immunity does not extend to members' written submissions
  3. Courts may review the content of speeches
  4. Art. 105(2) gives absolute immunity for anything said in Parliament, even if defamatory
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Art. 105(2) gives absolute immunity for anything said in Parliament, even if defamatory

The words 'anything said' were given their full meaning.

18In Keisham Meghachandra Singh v. Speaker, Manipur Legislative Assembly (2020), the Supreme Court:

  1. Held the Speaker's disqualification power is non-justiciable
  2. Held that a Speaker cannot belong to a political party
  3. Struck down the Tenth Schedule
  4. Directed that the Speaker should ordinarily decide Tenth Schedule petitions within three months and suggested Parliament consider an independent tribunal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Directed that the Speaker should ordinarily decide Tenth Schedule petitions within three months and suggested Parliament consider an independent tribunal

The Court urged Parliament to reconsider vesting adjudication in the Speaker.

19In Kuldip Nayar v. Union of India (2006), the Supreme Court:

  1. Upheld the amendment removing the domicile requirement and introducing open ballot for Rajya Sabha elections
  2. Struck down open ballot in Rajya Sabha elections
  3. Held that Rajya Sabha elections must be by direct vote
  4. Declared that federalism bars non-resident members
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Upheld the amendment removing the domicile requirement and introducing open ballot for Rajya Sabha elections

The right to vote in Rajya Sabha elections is statutory, not fundamental.
