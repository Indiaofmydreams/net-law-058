About this question set

This set has 16 multiple-choice questions on Emergency Provisions from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 2, Constitutional and Administrative Law. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 16 of the 16 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Constitutional & Administrative Law notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

16 practice questions with answers

1Which of the following Fundamental Rights can be suspended during a National Emergency declared under Article 352?

  1. Articles 14 and 15 only
  2. Articles 20 and 21 only
  3. All Fundamental Rights except Articles 20 and 21
  4. All Fundamental Rights
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. All Fundamental Rights except Articles 20 and 21

The 44th Amendment, 1978 made Articles 20 and 21 non-suspendable even during a National Emergency. All other Fundamental Rights can be suspended by Presidential Order under Article 359.

Report this question

2Article 352 deals with which type of Emergency?

  1. Internal Disturbance Emergency
  2. Financial Emergency
  3. President's Rule
  4. National Emergency
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. National Emergency

Article 352 deals with National Emergency on grounds of war, external aggression, or armed rebellion. Article 356 is President's Rule. Article 360 is Financial Emergency.

Report this question

3During a National Emergency, Article 358 suspends which rights automatically?

  1. Rights under Articles 20 and 21
  2. All Fundamental Rights
  3. All rights under Article 19
  4. Only Article 19(1)(a)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. All rights under Article 19

Article 358 provides for automatic suspension of Article 19 freedoms when a National Emergency is proclaimed on grounds of war or external aggression. Article 359 requires a Presidential Order for suspending other FRs (but NOT Articles 20 & 21).

Report this question

4National Emergency under Article 352 can be proclaimed by the President on the written advice of:

  1. Chief Justice of India
  2. Cabinet (Council of Ministers)
  3. Parliament
  4. Prime Minister alone
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Cabinet (Council of Ministers)

After the 44th Amendment (1978), the proclamation of National Emergency requires the written advice of the Cabinet (Council of Ministers). The Prime Minister alone cannot advise such proclamation — it must be a Cabinet decision in writing.

Report this question

5President's Rule under Article 356 can be imposed when:

  1. The State government violates Fundamental Rights
  2. The constitutional machinery of the State has failed
  3. The Governor recommends only
  4. There is financial mismanagement in the State
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The constitutional machinery of the State has failed

Article 356 can be invoked when the President is satisfied on receipt of a report from the Governor or otherwise that the government of the State cannot be carried on in accordance with the provisions of the Constitution. Concept note: S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994): 9-judge bench held Art. 356 is subject to judicial review. Floor of the House is the only place to test majority. Secularism is a basic feature — a State government acting against secularism can be dismissed under Art. 356.

Report this question

6The maximum period for which Financial Emergency under Article 360 can operate continuously without Parliament's approval:

  1. Indefinitely with Parliament's approval every 2 months initially
  2. 2 months
  3. 1 year
  4. 6 months
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Indefinitely with Parliament's approval every 2 months initially

Unlike National Emergency and President's Rule, Financial Emergency under Article 360 has NO maximum period. Once proclaimed and approved by Parliament, it continues until the President revokes it. Initial approval must be within 2 months.

Report this question

7For continuation of National Emergency after one month, approval must be by:

  1. Simple majority of Parliament
  2. Both Houses separately by simple majority
  3. Special majority of Parliament (2/3 of total membership + majority present and voting)
  4. Joint sitting of Parliament
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Special majority of Parliament (2/3 of total membership + majority present and voting)

National Emergency requires approval by special majority: not less than two-thirds of the members of each House of Parliament present and voting AND a majority of the total membership of each House.

Report this question

8The 44th Amendment, 1978 made which key change regarding Emergency provisions?

  1. Created Financial Emergency
  2. Changed 'internal disturbance' to 'armed rebellion' and required Cabinet's written advice
  3. Added Article 352
  4. Abolished President's Rule
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Changed 'internal disturbance' to 'armed rebellion' and required Cabinet's written advice

The 44th Amendment (1978) made key changes: (1) substituted 'armed rebellion' for 'internal disturbance'; (2) required Cabinet's written advice before proclamation; (3) made Articles 20 and 21 non-suspendable; (4) raised the majority required for approval.

Report this question

9Rajasthan v. Union of India (1977) was significant because:

  1. It interpreted the Preamble
  2. It held that President's Rule proclamation under Article 356 was entirely non-justiciable
  3. It first established the collegium system
  4. It invalidated imposition of President's Rule
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. It held that President's Rule proclamation under Article 356 was entirely non-justiciable

In Rajasthan v. Union of India (1977), the Supreme Court held that the proclamation under Article 356 was non-justiciable. This position was later overruled by the landmark S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994).

Report this question

10Match the Articles with their subjects: (i) Art.249 (ii) Art.352 (iii) Art.356 (iv) Art.360 — (a) Financial Emergency (b) Parliament legislates on State List in national interest (c) National Emergency (d) Failure of constitutional machinery in State

  1. i-b, ii-c, iii-d, iv-a
  2. i-c, ii-b, iii-d, iv-a
  3. i-a, ii-d, iii-c, iv-b
  4. i-b, ii-d, iii-c, iv-a
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. i-b, ii-c, iii-d, iv-a

Article 249 (b) — Parliament legislates on State List in national interest via Rajya Sabha resolution. Article 352 (c) — National Emergency. Article 356 (d) — Failure of constitutional machinery/President's Rule. Article 360 (a) — Financial Emergency.

Report this question

11Under the original Constitution, a National Emergency could be proclaimed on the ground of 'internal disturbance.' Which Amendment replaced this with 'armed rebellion'?

  1. 42nd Amendment, 1976
  2. 45th Amendment, 1980
  3. 43rd Amendment, 1977
  4. 44th Amendment, 1978
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 44th Amendment, 1978

The 44th Amendment (1978) substituted 'armed rebellion' for 'internal disturbance' in Article 352. This made it harder to impose emergency for mere civil unrest — a direct response to the misuse during the 1975 Emergency.

Report this question

12The National Emergency of 1975 was proclaimed on the ground of:

  1. External aggression by Pakistan
  2. Threat of war with China
  3. Armed rebellion in Punjab
  4. Internal disturbance — the first and only such proclamation on internal grounds
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Internal disturbance — the first and only such proclamation on internal grounds

The National Emergency proclaimed on June 25, 1975 was the only emergency declared on the ground of internal disturbance (now 'armed rebellion'). Earlier emergencies (1962, 1965, 1971) were on grounds of external aggression/war.

Report this question

13Under Article 352(3), a proclamation of National Emergency must be approved by each House of Parliament by:

  1. Absolute majority of total membership of each House
  2. Simple majority
  3. Unanimous vote
  4. Special majority — not less than 2/3 of members present and voting AND a majority of total membership
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Special majority — not less than 2/3 of members present and voting AND a majority of total membership

Article 352(6) requires special majority for approval of National Emergency proclamation: not less than two-thirds of members of each House present and voting, which must also be a majority of the total membership of each House.

Report this question

14The maximum initial period for which a National Emergency can operate without Parliamentary approval is:

  1. Two months
  2. Six months
  3. 15 days
  4. One month
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. One month

Under Article 352(4), the proclamation of National Emergency must be laid before each House of Parliament and ceases to operate at the expiration of one month unless approved before that. After approval, it continues for 6 months.

Report this question

15Once approved, a National Emergency continues for what period and how is it renewed?

  1. 3 months at a time
  2. One year without renewal
  3. 6 months — must be renewed every 6 months by special majority
  4. Until revoked, with no renewal requirement
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 6 months — must be renewed every 6 months by special majority

After initial Parliamentary approval, the National Emergency continues for 6 months. For each subsequent 6-month period, fresh approval by special majority is required.

Report this question

16A National Emergency can be revoked by the President:

  1. On his own initiative OR on a resolution passed by Lok Sabha by simple majority — Article 352(7) & (8)
  2. By the Supreme Court order
  3. Only with Parliamentary approval
  4. Only by Cabinet recommendation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. On his own initiative OR on a resolution passed by Lok Sabha by simple majority — Article 352(7) & (8)

Under Article 352(8), if a notice is signed by not less than 1/10 of total members of Lok Sabha, a special sitting must be held within 14 days. If the House passes a resolution by simple majority disapproving the Emergency, the President must revoke it. This is the only instance where Lok Sabha alone can act constitutionally. Concept note: Lok Sabha Revocation: Article 352(8) gives the Lok Sabha alone the unique power to disapprove a National Emergency by simple majority — this is an exception to the general rule that both Houses must act together on constitutional matters.
