1In T.N. Seshan v. Union of India (1995), the Supreme Court held:

  1. Election Commission cannot have multiple members
  2. President's supervision over Election Commission is valid
  3. Model Code of Conduct is legally binding
  4. The CEC and other Election Commissioners have equal powers; CEC has no veto
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The CEC and other Election Commissioners have equal powers; CEC has no veto

The Supreme Court in T.N. Seshan (1995) held that all Election Commissioners have equal powers and the CEC does not have a veto over the other Commissioners. Decisions are taken by majority.

2Assertion (A): The doctrine of eclipse applies to pre-constitutional laws that are inconsistent with Part III.
Reason (R): Such laws are void ab initio under Art. 13(1) and cannot be revived.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A is true but R is false

Pre-constitutional laws are only overshadowed (dormant), not dead (Bhikaji Narain Dhakras); Deep Chand/Mahendra Lal Jaini treat post-constitutional laws as void ab initio.

3Basheshar Nath v. CIT (1959) is associated with the proposition that:

  1. Only Art. 19 rights may be waived
  2. A citizen can waive any fundamental right by contract
  3. Fundamental rights cannot be waived by a citizen, as they are a constitutional policy
  4. Waiver is possible only before a court
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Fundamental rights cannot be waived by a citizen, as they are a constitutional policy

The majority held Art. 14 rights cannot be waived.

4In Sri Venkataramana Devaru v. State of Mysore (1958), the Supreme Court resolved a conflict between Art. 25(2)(b) and Art. 26(b) by:

  1. Holding Art. 26(b) absolute
  2. Applying the rule of harmonious construction, holding that the denominational right under Art. 26(b) is subject to the right of all Hindus to enter temples under Art. 25(2)(b)
  3. Holding Art. 25(2)(b) inapplicable to temples
  4. Striking down the impugned Act
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Applying the rule of harmonious construction, holding that the denominational right under Art. 26(b) is subject to the right of all Hindus to enter temples under Art. 25(2)(b)

Harmonious construction gives effect to both provisions as far as possible.

5Under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 (implementing Art. 39A), an award of a Lok Adalat is:

  1. Deemed a decree of a civil court, final and binding, with no appeal against it
  2. Appealable to the High Court
  3. A mere recommendation requiring court confirmation
  4. Enforceable only through arbitration
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Deemed a decree of a civil court, final and binding, with no appeal against it

Section 21 of the Act.

6In Sarla Mudgal v. Union of India (1995), the Supreme Court:

  1. Held that a Hindu husband converting to Islam to remarry commits bigamy under s.494 IPC, and urged the State to implement Art. 44
  2. Upheld Muslim personal law on polygamy for all communities
  3. Struck down s.494 IPC
  4. Held that Art. 44 is a fundamental right
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Held that a Hindu husband converting to Islam to remarry commits bigamy under s.494 IPC, and urged the State to implement Art. 44

The Court said conversion does not dissolve the first marriage and called for a Uniform Civil Code.

7The fundamental duty 'to develop the scientific temper, humanism and the spirit of inquiry and reform' is contained in Art. 51A:

  1. Clause (g)
  2. Clause (h)
  3. Clause (i)
  4. Clause (j)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Clause (h)

(g) environment; (i) public property and violence; (j) individual and collective excellence.

8Match the Directive Principle with its Article.
List I:
1. Art. 40
2. Art. 43A
3. Art. 49
4. Art. 50
List II:
a. Separation of judiciary from the executive
b. Organisation of village panchayats
c. Participation of workers in management
d. Protection of monuments of national importance

  1. 1-c, 2-b, 3-d, 4-a
  2. 1-b, 2-d, 3-c, 4-a
  3. 1-b, 2-c, 3-d, 4-a
  4. 1-b, 2-c, 3-a, 4-d

Answer: C. 1-b, 2-c, 3-d, 4-a

9In Property Owners Association v. State of Maharashtra (2024), a nine-judge Bench held on Art. 39(b) that:

  1. Not every privately owned resource is a material resource of the community; some may qualify depending on factors such as nature, scarcity and impact on the community, and the earlier wide view of Krishna Iyer J. was not accepted
  2. Every privately owned resource is a 'material resource of the community'
  3. Art. 39(b) applies only to natural resources
  4. Art. 31C is no longer valid
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Not every privately owned resource is a material resource of the community; some may qualify depending on factors such as nature, scarcity and impact on the community, and the earlier wide view of Krishna Iyer J. was not accepted

The majority rejected the wide 'socialist' reading from Ranganatha Reddy / Sanjeev Coke.

10Under Art. 171, the Legislative Council of a State shall have not more than ___ of the Assembly's total membership and not less than ___ members (except J&K earlier).

  1. One-third; 60
  2. One-half; 40
  3. One-third; 40
  4. One-fourth; 30
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. One-third; 40

Art. 169 allows creation/abolition by Parliament on the Assembly's special majority resolution; it is not an Art. 368 amendment.

11The repeated re-promulgation of ordinances without placing them before the Legislature was held to be a 'fraud on the Constitution' in:

  1. T.N. Seshan v. Union of India
  2. Rameshwar Prasad v. Union of India
  3. A.K. Roy v. Union of India
  4. D.C. Wadhwa v. State of Bihar
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. D.C. Wadhwa v. State of Bihar

The Bihar Governor had re-promulgated ordinances for years; Krishna Kumar Singh (2017) affirmed the principle.

12In State of Punjab v. Principal Secretary to the Governor of Punjab (2023), the Supreme Court held that a Governor:

  1. Can withhold assent indefinitely
  2. Cannot sit on Bills; if he withholds assent under Art. 200 he must return the Bill with a message 'as soon as possible'
  3. Can veto Money Bills
  4. Has absolute discretion in reserving Bills
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Cannot sit on Bills; if he withholds assent under Art. 200 he must return the Bill with a message 'as soon as possible'

Indefinite inaction defeats the legislative process and parliamentary democracy.

13In Subhash Desai v. Principal Secretary, Governor of Maharashtra (2023), the Supreme Court:

  1. Held the Governor's call for a floor test was unjustified for lack of objective material, but did not restore the status quo ante because the Chief Minister had resigned without facing it
  2. Reinstated the resigned Chief Minister
  3. Upheld the Speaker's choice of Whip
  4. Held the floor test is mandatory in all cases
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Held the Governor's call for a floor test was unjustified for lack of objective material, but did not restore the status quo ante because the Chief Minister had resigned without facing it

The Court also referred the Nabam Rebia decision to a larger Bench.

14In Union of India v. Tulsiram Patel (1985), the Constitution Bench held that:

  1. The provisos to Art. 311(2) are unconstitutional
  2. Pleasure doctrine is absolute
  3. The exceptions in the second proviso to Art. 311(2) are valid, but reasons for dispensing with the inquiry must be recorded in writing and are subject to judicial review
  4. Art. 311 applies to the armed forces fully
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The exceptions in the second proviso to Art. 311(2) are valid, but reasons for dispensing with the inquiry must be recorded in writing and are subject to judicial review

Natural justice may be excluded where holding an inquiry is not reasonably practicable.

15In B.P. Singhal v. Union of India (2010), the Supreme Court held that the President's pleasure to remove a Governor:

  1. Is not unfettered; removal needs compelling reasons, and cannot be for a change of Government
  2. Is unfettered
  3. Requires a Parliamentary resolution
  4. Requires CJI consultation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Is not unfettered; removal needs compelling reasons, and cannot be for a change of Government

Reasons need not be disclosed unless prima facie mala fides is shown.

16Under Art. 312(3), the All-India Judicial Service shall not include any post inferior to:

  1. Civil Judge (Junior Division)
  2. Additional District Judge
  3. District Judge
  4. Chief Judicial Magistrate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. District Judge

Art. 312(1) requires a Rajya Sabha resolution by two-thirds present and voting.
