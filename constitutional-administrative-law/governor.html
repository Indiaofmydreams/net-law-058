About this question set

17 practice questions with answers

1The Governor is appointed by the President under Article 155. The Governor's term of office is:

  1. 5 years — fixed and irremovable
  2. 6 years
  3. Co-terminus with the State government
  4. 5 years or during the pleasure of the President — whichever is earlier
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 5 years or during the pleasure of the President — whichever is earlier

Article 156(1) provides that the Governor shall hold office during the pleasure of the President. Article 156(3) provides that the Governor shall hold office for a term of 5 years from the date of entering upon office — subject to the pleasure of the President.

2Which of the following is a discretionary power of the Governor NOT requiring Council of Ministers' advice?

  1. Appointment of Chief Minister when no party has clear majority
  2. Reserving a Bill for the consideration of the President — Article 200
  3. Giving assent to a Bill
  4. Summoning the State Legislature
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Appointment of Chief Minister when no party has clear majority

The Governor's discretionary powers include: (1) Appointment of CM when no clear majority; (2) Dismissal of CoM which has lost majority but refuses to resign; (3) Dissolution of State Assembly in certain situations; (4) Reservation of Bills; (5) Seeking information from CM under Article 167. All others require CoM advice. Concept note: Governor's Discretionary Powers (Article 163(2)): These are exercised without CoM advice: Appointment of CM when no clear majority, Dismissal of CoM that has lost majority, Dissolution of Assembly (contested), Reservation of Bills for Presidential consideration, Seeking information from CM (Art. 167), Sending Art. 356 report.

Report this question

3Under Article 167, the Chief Minister is under a duty to:

  1. Report Cabinet decisions to Parliament
  2. Report all Cabinet decisions to the President
  3. Communicate to the Governor all decisions of the Council of Ministers and proposals for legislation
  4. Seek Governor's prior approval for all Cabinet decisions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Communicate to the Governor all decisions of the Council of Ministers and proposals for legislation

Article 167 imposes three duties on the Chief Minister: (1) to communicate to the Governor all decisions of CoM relating to administration of the State; (2) to furnish information relating to administration of the State and proposals for legislation as the Governor may call for; (3) if the Governor requires, to submit for consideration of CoM any matter on which a decision has been taken by a Minister.

Report this question

4Under Article 200, can the Governor give assent, withhold assent, or return a Bill passed by the State Legislature?

  1. Only Money Bills can be returned
  2. Yes — Governor may assent, withhold, or return (non-money bill) for reconsideration; if re-passed, must give assent
  3. No — must always give assent
  4. Yes — Governor can withhold assent permanently
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Yes — Governor may assent, withhold, or return (non-money bill) for reconsideration; if re-passed, must give assent

Article 200 gives the Governor four options: (1) give assent; (2) withhold assent; (3) return (non-Money Bill) for reconsideration; (4) reserve for President's consideration. Crucially, if the State Legislature re-passes the Bill with or without amendments, the Governor MUST give assent.

Report this question

5When a Governor reserves a Bill for the President's consideration under Article 200, the President under Article 201 may:

  1. Give or withhold assent only
  2. Give assent, withhold assent, or direct the Governor to return it for reconsideration
  3. Return it; if re-passed, President must assent
  4. Both B and C — President has wider discretion than Governor
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Both B and C — President has wider discretion than Governor

Under Article 201, the President has full discretion — he may give assent, withhold assent, or direct the Governor to return the Bill for reconsideration. Unlike the Governor (who MUST assent if a Bill is re-passed), the President is NOT bound to assent even if the State Legislature re-passes it.

6Under Article 213, the Governor can promulgate Ordinances when:

  1. The State Legislature is not in session and immediate action is necessary
  2. State Legislature is in recess only
  3. There is a law and order situation only
  4. The Governor deems it fit at any time
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The State Legislature is not in session and immediate action is necessary

Article 213 empowers the Governor to promulgate Ordinances when the State Legislature is not in session (or both Houses of a bicameral legislature) and the Governor is satisfied that circumstances exist that render immediate action necessary.

7An Ordinance promulgated by the Governor ceases to operate if:

  1. Not approved by the State Legislature within 6 weeks of its reassembly
  2. A resolution disapproving it is passed by either House
  3. Both A and B, or if the Governor revokes it
  4. Only if the Governor revokes it
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Both A and B, or if the Governor revokes it

Under Article 213(2), an Ordinance ceases to operate: (1) at the expiry of 6 weeks after reassembly of the Legislature; (2) if before that, a resolution disapproving it is passed; (3) if the Governor withdraws it at any time.

8The Governor's pardoning power under Article 161 versus the President's power under Article 72 — which statement is CORRECT?

  1. Governor's power extends to sentences of death awarded under State laws
  2. Governor CANNOT pardon a death sentence — that is exclusively President's power
  3. Governor pardons all offences including military offences
  4. Both have identical pardoning powers
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Governor CANNOT pardon a death sentence — that is exclusively President's power

Article 161 gives the Governor power to grant pardons for offences against any law relating to a matter to which the executive power of the State extends. Article 72 gives the President the power to pardon death sentences, court martial offences, and offences against Union laws. Governor has NO power over death sentences.

Report this question

9In Shamsher Singh v. State of Punjab (1974), the Supreme Court held on the role of the Governor that:

  1. The Governor can appoint and dismiss Ministers independently
  2. The Governor, like the President, is a constitutional head who acts on CoM advice in all matters except where Constitution expressly provides for discretion
  3. The Governor has overriding power over the State Council of Ministers
  4. The Governor acts entirely on personal discretion in all matters
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The Governor, like the President, is a constitutional head who acts on CoM advice in all matters except where Constitution expressly provides for discretion

Shamsher Singh v. State of Punjab (1974) — 7-judge bench — held that both the President and Governor are constitutional heads bound by the advice of the Council of Ministers except where the Constitution expressly confers discretionary powers. This is the foundational case on constitutional conventions regarding executive heads.

10The Governor's report recommending imposition of President's Rule under Article 356 is:

  1. Subject to Rajya Sabha approval first
  2. Binding on the President
  3. Final and conclusive
  4. Not binding — President acts on Union Cabinet's advice, not solely on Governor's report
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Not binding — President acts on Union Cabinet's advice, not solely on Governor's report

The Governor's report is the 'occasion' for invoking Article 356, but the decision is made by the Union Cabinet and the President acts on Cabinet's advice. The President is not bound by the Governor's report. S.R. Bommai held that the courts can examine the material on which President was satisfied.

11A person to be appointed as Governor must satisfy which qualifications?

  1. Be nominated by the concerned State Legislature
  2. Be a retired judge of the Supreme Court
  3. Be a citizen of India aged not less than 30 years
  4. Be a citizen of India aged not less than 35 years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Be a citizen of India aged not less than 35 years

Article 157 prescribes that a person shall not be eligible for appointment as Governor unless he is a citizen of India and has completed the age of 35 years. Article 158 adds that he must not be a member of either House of Parliament or State Legislature and must not hold any office of profit.

12The Governor enjoys personal immunity from legal proceedings under Article 361. This immunity covers:

  1. Only official acts done in exercise of powers and duties
  2. Only criminal proceedings
  3. For 2 years after leaving office
  4. Official acts AND personal acts during tenure — complete immunity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Official acts AND personal acts during tenure — complete immunity

Article 361 provides: (1) The President/Governor shall not be answerable to any court for the exercise of his powers; (2) No criminal proceedings whatsoever shall be instituted against them during their tenure; (3) No civil proceedings for acts done in personal capacity without 2 months' notice.

13In Rameshwar Prasad v. Union of India (2006), the Supreme Court:

  1. Held the Governor's action was valid but ordered fresh elections
  2. Held that the Governor can dissolve Assembly without any reason
  3. Struck down the premature dissolution of Bihar Assembly by the Governor as mala fide
  4. Upheld the dissolution of Bihar Assembly by the Governor
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Struck down the premature dissolution of Bihar Assembly by the Governor as mala fide

In Rameshwar Prasad v. Union of India (2006), the SC struck down the dissolution of the Bihar Legislative Assembly before its first session as unconstitutional and mala fide. The Governor had acted on incorrect advice and in a politically motivated manner.

14In B.P. Singhal v. Union of India (2010), the Supreme Court held regarding removal of Governors:

  1. The power to remove a Governor cannot be exercised arbitrarily, capriciously, or for extraneous reasons — Governor must be given opportunity to be heard
  2. The President can remove a Governor at will for any reason
  3. A Governor cannot be removed before 5 years
  4. Removal of Governor requires Parliamentary approval
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The power to remove a Governor cannot be exercised arbitrarily, capriciously, or for extraneous reasons — Governor must be given opportunity to be heard

In B.P. Singhal v. Union of India (2010), the SC held that while the President can remove a Governor under Article 156(1) (pleasure doctrine), this power cannot be exercised arbitrarily, capriciously or for political reasons. The Governor must be given an opportunity to present their case.

15Assertion (A): The Governor cannot be sued in a personal capacity during tenure. Reason (R): Article 361 confers complete immunity from criminal proceedings and civil proceedings during tenure.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation

Article 361 provides complete immunity to the President and Governor: no criminal proceedings during tenure, and for civil proceedings for personal acts, 2 months' notice is required. R correctly and completely explains A.

Report this question

16Assertion (A): Article 200 empowers Governor to withhold assent to a Bill passed by State Legislature. Reason (R): If Governor withholds assent and State Legislature re-passes the Bill, Governor must give assent.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false — Governor can again withhold
  4. A is false but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A

A is true — Article 200 does empower the Governor to withhold assent. R is also true — if the State Legislature re-passes a Bill, the Governor must give assent. But R is not the EXPLANATION of A — it is a separate rule about the consequence of re-passing. The two propositions are related but R does not explain A.

17In Nabam Rebia v. Deputy Speaker, Arunachal Pradesh (2016), the Supreme Court held:

  1. The Speaker can be removed by the Governor
  2. The Governor has absolute discretion in all Assembly matters
  3. The Governor's power to advance the session was unconstitutional — he cannot act contrary to advice of Council of Ministers on summoning sessions
  4. The Governor can summon a session of the Assembly at any time on his own
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The Governor's power to advance the session was unconstitutional — he cannot act contrary to advice of Council of Ministers on summoning sessions

In Nabam Rebia v. Deputy Speaker (2016), the SC held that summoning, proroguing, and dissolving the State Legislative Assembly are matters on which the Governor must act on the advice of the Council of Ministers. The Governor cannot advance the session on his own to destabilize an elected government.
