About this question set

This set has 15 multiple-choice questions on Union Executive from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 2, Constitutional and Administrative Law. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 15 of the 15 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Constitutional & Administrative Law notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

15 practice questions with answers

1The executive power of the Union is vested in:

  1. The Prime Minister
  2. The President of India
  3. The Parliament
  4. The Council of Ministers
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The President of India

Article 53 vests the executive power of the Union in the President. However, the President is a constitutional head who acts on the advice of the Council of Ministers (Article 74).

2The President of India is elected by:

  1. Direct election by citizens
  2. Elected members of Parliament only
  3. Elected members of Parliament + Elected members of State Legislative Assemblies
  4. All members of Parliament + all members of State Legislatures
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Elected members of Parliament + Elected members of State Legislative Assemblies

The electoral college consists of elected members of both Houses of Parliament and elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of States (including Delhi and Puducherry). Nominated members and members of State Legislative Councils are excluded.

Report this question

3Article 74(1) provides that the Council of Ministers headed by the PM shall 'aid and advise' the President. After the 44th Amendment, the President:

  1. Can refer it back once but must act on revised advice
  2. Can reject the advice
  3. Must consult the Supreme Court before acting
  4. Shall act in accordance with such advice
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Can refer it back once but must act on revised advice

The 44th Amendment (1978) added a proviso to Article 74(1): The President may return the advice for reconsideration, but if re-tendered, the President shall act accordingly.

4Assertion (A): The President can promulgate an Ordinance under Article 123 only when both Houses of Parliament are not in session. Reason (R): An Ordinance ceases at the expiration of six weeks from the reassembly of Parliament.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation

A is true — Ordinance can be promulgated when at least one House of Parliament is not in session (not necessarily both). R is also true — 6 weeks maximum after reassembly. But R is the duration rule, not the explanation for when it can be issued.

Report this question

5Under Article 111, if the President returns a Bill to Parliament and Parliament re-passes it with or without amendment, the President:

  1. Shall NOT withhold assent — must sign the Bill
  2. Can withhold assent again
  3. Can reserve it for the Supreme Court's opinion
  4. Can dissolve Parliament
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Shall NOT withhold assent — must sign the Bill

Article 111 provides that if the Bill is re-passed by both Houses with or without amendments, the President shall not withhold assent. This provision makes the President's veto a suspensive veto, not an absolute veto.

Report this question

6The Vice President of India is elected by:

  1. Same electoral college as President
  2. Elected members of both Houses of Parliament
  3. Members of both Houses of Parliament — elected AND nominated
  4. Elected members of Rajya Sabha only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Members of both Houses of Parliament — elected AND nominated

Under Article 66, the Vice President is elected by the members of an electoral college consisting of members of both Houses of Parliament — both elected AND nominated members. This is broader than the President's electoral college which includes only elected members.

Report this question

7The Vice President acts as the ex-officio Chairman of which House?

  1. Lok Sabha
  2. Rajya Sabha
  3. Both Houses on alternate days
  4. Neither — only when President is absent
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Rajya Sabha

Article 64 provides that the Vice-President shall be ex-officio Chairman of the Council of States (Rajya Sabha). The Speaker is the presiding officer of the Lok Sabha.

Report this question

8Under Article 65, the Vice President acts as President when:

  1. A vacancy arises in the office of the President or the President is unable to discharge functions
  2. Parliament passes a resolution
  3. The Prime Minister is absent
  4. The Speaker so directs
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A vacancy arises in the office of the President or the President is unable to discharge functions

Article 65(1) — VP discharges functions of President during casual vacancies. Article 65(2) — VP acts as President during absence/inability of the President. The VP can act only until the President resumes duty or a new President is elected.

Report this question

9The President of India can seek the advisory opinion of the Supreme Court under:

  1. Article 143
  2. Article 141
  3. Article 136
  4. Article 131
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Article 143

Article 143 empowers the President to refer a question of law or fact of public importance to the Supreme Court for its advisory opinion. The SC's opinion is advisory and not binding on the President, but has great persuasive value.

Report this question

10Under Article 72, the President's pardoning power extends to:

  1. Only cases where appeal is pending
  2. All cases of conviction including State law offences
  3. Only Supreme Court convicted persons
  4. Cases involving offences against Union laws and death sentences
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Cases involving offences against Union laws and death sentences

Article 72 gives the President power to grant pardon, reprieve, respite, remission, or commutation of punishment to persons convicted of: (1) offences against Union laws; (2) offences by court martial; (3) all sentences of death. The Governor's pardoning power (Art. 161) covers State law offences but NOT death sentences or court martial.

Report this question

11Match: (i) Article 123 (ii) Article 213 (iii) Article 72 (iv) Article 161 with (a) Ordinance by Governor (b) Pardoning power of President (c) Ordinance by President (d) Pardoning power of Governor

  1. i-c, ii-a, iii-b, iv-d
  2. i-a, ii-c, iii-d, iv-b
  3. i-c, ii-a, iii-d, iv-b
  4. i-b, ii-a, iii-c, iv-d
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. i-c, ii-a, iii-b, iv-d

Article 123 (c) — President's Ordinance-making power. Article 213 (a) — Governor's Ordinance-making power. Article 72 (b) — President's pardoning power including death sentences. Article 161 (d) — Governor's pardoning power (State law offences, NOT death sentences).

12In S.R. Chaudhuri v. State of Punjab (2001), the Supreme Court held that:

  1. A non-member can be repeatedly appointed Minister for successive six-month terms
  2. Ministers need not be members of the legislature at all
  3. A person who is not a member of the legislature cannot be appointed Minister again for a second consecutive term without being elected
  4. Art. 164(4) does not apply to Chief Ministers
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A person who is not a member of the legislature cannot be appointed Minister again for a second consecutive term without being elected

Repeated appointment without election defeats the purpose of Art. 164(4).

13Consider the Attorney General of India:
1. He is appointed by the President.
2. He must be a person qualified to be appointed a Judge of the Supreme Court.
3. He can be removed only by impeachment like a Judge.
4. He has the right of audience in all courts in the territory of India.

  1. 1, 2 and 4 only
  2. 1, 2 and 3 only
  3. 2, 3 and 4 only
  4. 1, 3 and 4 only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 1, 2 and 4 only

He holds office during the pleasure of the President (Art. 76).

Report this question

14Assertion (A): The Comptroller and Auditor General can be removed only in the manner and on the grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court.
Reason (R): This safeguard protects the independence of the CAG from the executive.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

Art. 148(1): removal like a Supreme Court Judge secures independence.

Report this question

15The Vice-President of India can be removed by:

  1. A resolution of the Rajya Sabha passed by a majority of all the then members and agreed to by the Lok Sabha, after 14 days' notice
  2. A two-thirds majority in both Houses in joint sitting
  3. Impeachment by Parliament as for the President
  4. A resolution of the Rajya Sabha by two-thirds of members present and voting
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A resolution of the Rajya Sabha passed by a majority of all the then members and agreed to by the Lok Sabha, after 14 days' notice

Art. 67(b) - 'effective majority' in Rajya Sabha and simple majority in Lok Sabha.
