14 practice questions with answers

1The Bangkok Declaration 1993 represents:

  1. Universal standards of human rights binding on all
  2. An African charter on human rights
  3. Asian governments' perspective emphasizing sovereignty and cultural context
  4. European approach to rights enforcement
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Asian governments' perspective emphasizing sovereignty and cultural context

The Bangkok Declaration 1993 was adopted by Asian governments before the Vienna World Conference. It emphasized sovereignty, non-interference, and that human rights must be considered in the context of national and regional particularities - a cultural relativist position.

2The Vienna Declaration 1993 resolved the universalism-relativism debate by:

  1. Fully accepting cultural relativism
  2. Affirming universality while acknowledging cultural particularities in implementation
  3. Rejecting all cultural considerations
  4. Leaving it entirely to each state to decide
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Affirming universality while acknowledging cultural particularities in implementation

Vienna Declaration 1993 Para 5: While significance of national and regional particularities must be borne in mind, it is the duty of States to promote and protect all human rights and fundamental freedoms. This is the standard resolution of the debate.

3Jack Donnelly's position on universalism vs. cultural relativism is:

  1. Strong cultural relativism
  2. Strong universalism - all rights apply uniformly everywhere without variation
  3. Relative universality - universal core but cultural variation in implementation
  4. Indifference - both are equally valid
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Relative universality - universal core but cultural variation in implementation

Jack Donnelly argues for 'relative universality' - human rights are universal in their substance (the core) but there is legitimate space for cultural variation in interpretation and implementation. He firmly rejects strong relativism.

4Which best represents 'cultural relativism' in human rights?

  1. Human rights are the same everywhere because of shared humanity
  2. Economic rights take precedence over civil rights
  3. Standards of right and wrong are culturally determined and cannot be universally applied
  4. State sovereignty overrides all individual rights
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Standards of right and wrong are culturally determined and cannot be universally applied

Cultural relativism holds that moral judgments including human rights standards are relative to cultural context. Strong cultural relativists argue that universal human rights impose Western values on non-Western societies.

Report this question

5The African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights (Banjul Charter, 1981) is distinctive because:

  1. It includes group/peoples' rights and duties alongside individual rights
  2. It recognizes only civil and political rights
  3. It established the African Human Rights Court as first court globally
  4. It allows no derogation from any rights
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It includes group/peoples' rights and duties alongside individual rights

The African Charter 1981 is distinctive for including peoples'/group rights (right to self-determination, development, clean environment); imposing duties on individuals toward family, society and state; and use of 'clawback clauses'.

6Abdullahi An-Na'im's approach to the universalism-relativism debate advocates:

  1. Full cultural relativism and rejection of UDHR
  2. Complete acceptance of Western human rights standards
  3. Internal cultural discourse - reform from within each culture through cross-cultural dialogue
  4. State-centered approach where states define rights
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Internal cultural discourse - reform from within each culture through cross-cultural dialogue

An-Na'im advocates 'internal cultural discourse' - transformation should come from within each culture through dialogue rather than external imposition. He applied this to Islamic law, arguing human rights values can be derived from within the Islamic tradition.

7Amartya Sen's argument about universal human rights holds that:

  1. Ideas of freedom, tolerance and rights have roots in many cultures, not only the West
  2. Human rights are purely a Western invention
  3. Asian values are incompatible with human rights
  4. Economic development must precede civil rights
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Ideas of freedom, tolerance and rights have roots in many cultures, not only the West

Sen counters the 'Asian Values' argument (Lee Kuan Yew, Mahathir Mohamed) by showing that ideas of freedom, tolerance and governance have historical roots in Asian, African and other cultures - not exclusively Western.

8The Inter-American Court of Human Rights operates under which instrument?

  1. UN Charter
  2. American Convention on Human Rights (Pact of San Jose, 1969)
  3. OAS Charter 1948
  4. Rio Declaration 1992
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. American Convention on Human Rights (Pact of San Jose, 1969)

The Inter-American Court of Human Rights was established under the American Convention on Human Rights (Pact of San Jose) 1969. It is the regional human rights court for the Americas, based in San Jose, Costa Rica.

9The European Convention on Human Rights (1950) established:

  1. The EU Court of Justice
  2. The European Commission only
  3. A peer review mechanism with no court
  4. The European Court of Human Rights at Strasbourg
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The European Court of Human Rights at Strasbourg

The European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR) 1950 established the European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) based in Strasbourg, France. After Protocol 11 (1998), individuals can directly petition the ECtHR against member states after exhausting domestic remedies.

10The concept of 'margin of appreciation' in human rights law means:

  1. The degree of flexibility allowed to states in implementing human rights obligations
  2. The standard of compensation for human rights violations
  3. The jurisdiction of international courts over domestic matters
  4. The principle of proportionality in human rights
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The degree of flexibility allowed to states in implementing human rights obligations

The 'margin of appreciation' doctrine (developed by the ECtHR) recognizes that national authorities are better placed than international judges to assess what is necessary in their specific context. It gives states a degree of discretion in restricting certain rights.

11'Jus Cogens' norms in human rights law are:

  1. Peremptory norms of international law from which no derogation is permitted
  2. Norms that apply only to developing countries
  3. Norms created by the UN Security Council
  4. Customary norms that can be overridden by treaty
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Peremptory norms of international law from which no derogation is permitted

Jus cogens (compelling law) are peremptory norms of international law from which no derogation is permitted even by treaty (Article 53, Vienna Convention on Law of Treaties 1969). Human rights jus cogens include: prohibition of genocide, slavery, torture, crimes against humanity.

12The 'Asian Values' debate in human rights was primarily associated with which leaders?

  1. Mahatma Gandhi and Jawaharlal Nehru
  2. Nelson Mandela and Desmond Tutu
  3. Lee Kuan Yew (Singapore) and Mahathir Mohamed (Malaysia)
  4. Deng Xiaoping and Mao Zedong
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Lee Kuan Yew (Singapore) and Mahathir Mohamed (Malaysia)

The 'Asian Values' argument was most prominently advanced by Lee Kuan Yew of Singapore and Mahathir Mohamed of Malaysia in the 1990s - holding that Asian cultures prioritize community over individual and economic development over political freedoms. Amartya Sen critiqued this position fundamentally.

13Which of the following best describes the 'interdependence' of human rights?

  1. All rights must be enforced at the same time
  2. Economic rights depend on political stability
  3. Rights of one generation depend on rights of the previous generation
  4. Realization of one right facilitates and is linked to the realization of other rights
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Realization of one right facilitates and is linked to the realization of other rights

Interdependence means the realization of one right is connected to the realization of others - the right to education facilitates the right to work; the right to health depends on the right to food and water; civil-political rights enable people to claim socioeconomic rights.

14The concept of 'human rights defenders' was formally recognized by:

  1. Vienna Declaration 1993
  2. UN Declaration on Human Rights Defenders 1998
  3. ICCPR Optional Protocol
  4. Durban Declaration 2001
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. UN Declaration on Human Rights Defenders 1998

The UN Declaration on the Right and Responsibility of Individuals, Groups and Organs of Society to Promote and Protect Universally Recognized Human Rights and Fundamental Freedoms ('Declaration on Human Rights Defenders') was adopted by UNGA in 1998.
