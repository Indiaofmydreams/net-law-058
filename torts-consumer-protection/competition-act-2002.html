About this question set

This set has 23 multiple-choice questions on Competition Act 2002 from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 5, Law of Torts and Consumer Protection. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 23 of the 23 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Torts & Consumer Protection notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

23 practice questions with answers

1The Competition Act, 2002 replaced:

  1. Consumer Protection Act, 1986
  2. MRTP Act, 1969
  3. Companies Act, 1956
  4. FEMA, 1999
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. MRTP Act, 1969

The Competition Act 2002 replaced the Monopolies and Restrictive Trade Practices Act 1969.

Report this question

2The Competition Commission of India (CCI) was established under:

  1. Article 39 of the Constitution
  2. MRTP Act
  3. Companies Act
  4. Section 7 of the Competition Act, 2002
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Section 7 of the Competition Act, 2002

Section 7 of the Competition Act provides for the establishment of CCI.

Report this question

3The Competition Commission of India (CCI) was operationalised in:

  1. 2003
  2. 2007
  3. 2009
  4. 2002
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 2009

Though the Act was passed in 2002, CCI became fully operational in 2009.

Report this question

4Under Section 3 of the Competition Act 2002, which agreements are prohibited?

  1. All commercial agreements
  2. Government contracts
  3. International trade agreements
  4. Anti-competitive agreements — horizontal and vertical
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Anti-competitive agreements — horizontal and vertical

Section 3 prohibits both horizontal (per se) and vertical (rule of reason) anti-competitive agreements.

Report this question

5Horizontal agreements presumed to be anti-competitive (per se rule) include:

  1. Exclusive supply agreements
  2. Price fixing, bid rigging, market sharing, output limitation
  3. Tie-in arrangements
  4. Resale price maintenance
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Price fixing, bid rigging, market sharing, output limitation

Horizontal agreements among competitors are presumed to have AAEC — per se void.

Report this question

6Section 4 of the Competition Act 2002 deals with:

  1. Abuse of dominant position
  2. Anti-competitive agreements
  3. Regulation of combinations
  4. CCI composition
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Abuse of dominant position

Section 4 prohibits abuse of dominant position.

Report this question

7'Dominant position' under the Competition Act 2002 is determined with respect to:

  1. Relevant market (relevant product market + relevant geographic market)
  2. National market only
  3. Global market
  4. As determined by the government
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Relevant market (relevant product market + relevant geographic market)

Dominance is assessed in the context of the relevant market.

Report this question

8'Combinations' under the Competition Act 2002 (Sections 5 & 6) refer to:

  1. Any business partnership
  2. Government contracts
  3. All joint ventures
  4. Mergers, amalgamations, and acquisitions beyond prescribed thresholds requiring CCI approval
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Mergers, amalgamations, and acquisitions beyond prescribed thresholds requiring CCI approval

Combinations above prescribed asset/turnover thresholds require mandatory CCI approval.

Report this question

9The per se rule vs the rule of reason in competition law:

  1. Per se — certain agreements are inherently anti-competitive; Rule of reason — effects are assessed
  2. Both are the same
  3. Per se applies to vertical agreements; Rule of reason to horizontal
  4. Rule of reason applies only in criminal competition matters
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Per se — certain agreements are inherently anti-competitive; Rule of reason — effects are assessed

Horizontal agreements (price fixing etc.) are per se; vertical agreements assessed under rule of reason.

Report this question

10CCI v. Steel Authority of India Ltd. (2010) — the Supreme Court held:

  1. CCI has no interim powers
  2. CCI can impose criminal penalties
  3. CCI has power to grant interim relief under Section 33 after forming a prima facie opinion
  4. CCI has jurisdiction only over private companies
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. CCI has power to grant interim relief under Section 33 after forming a prima facie opinion

CCI can grant interim orders under Section 33 upon forming a prima facie view.

Report this question

11The 'relevant product market' under the Competition Act 2002 is defined under:

  1. Section 2(t)
  2. Section 4
  3. Section 19(7)
  4. Section 5
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Section 19(7)

Section 19(7) defines relevant product market based on demand-side substitutability.

Report this question

12In Belaire Owner's Association v. DLF Ltd. (2011), CCI found DLF guilty of:

  1. Abuse of dominant position — imposition of one-sided terms on homebuyers in Gurgaon
  2. Anti-competitive agreement
  3. Predatory pricing
  4. Cartelisation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Abuse of dominant position — imposition of one-sided terms on homebuyers in Gurgaon

DLF imposed unfair and one-sided terms on buyers — abuse of dominant position under Section 4.

Report this question

13A 'cartel' under the Competition Act means:

  1. An association of producers, sellers or distributors who by agreement fix prices, limit production, or share markets
  2. Any trade association
  3. A government-approved combination
  4. A joint venture
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. An association of producers, sellers or distributors who by agreement fix prices, limit production, or share markets

Cartels are the most serious form of anti-competitive conduct under the Competition Act.

Report this question

14The leniency programme under the Competition Act allows:

  1. A cartel member who discloses the cartel to CCI to receive reduction in penalty
  2. Any company to avoid all penalties
  3. Government enterprises to avoid competition law
  4. Small enterprises to be exempt from Competition Act
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A cartel member who discloses the cartel to CCI to receive reduction in penalty

Leniency (lesser penalty) is available to cartel members who cooperate with CCI investigations.

Report this question

15'Predatory pricing' under Section 4 of the Competition Act means:

  1. High pricing
  2. Sale of goods or services at a price below cost with intent to eliminate competitors or reduce competition
  3. Price discrimination
  4. Price fixing with competitors
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Sale of goods or services at a price below cost with intent to eliminate competitors or reduce competition

Predatory pricing is a form of abuse of dominance under Section 4.

Report this question

16The Competition (Amendment) Act, 2023 introduced:

  1. Complete overhaul of the Act
  2. Criminal liability for cartels
  3. Merger of CCI with SEBI
  4. Deal value threshold for combinations, settlement and commitment mechanism, reduced timelines for CCI approval
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Deal value threshold for combinations, settlement and commitment mechanism, reduced timelines for CCI approval

The 2023 Amendment modernised the combination regime and added settlement mechanisms.

Report this question

17Under Section 6(2) of the Competition Act 2002, parties to a combination must notify CCI within:

  1. 7 days of approval of merger by board/shareholders
  2. 30 days
  3. 60 days
  4. 90 days
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 30 days

Notification must be made within 30 days of the triggering event (board/shareholder approval).

Report this question

18FICCI-Multiplex Association of India v. United Producers/Distributors Forum (2011) — CCI found that:

  1. Coordinated refusal by film producers to supply films to multiplex owners constituted an anti-competitive agreement under Section 3
  2. Film distribution is outside Competition Act
  3. Multiplex owners abused dominant position
  4. Vertical agreements were per se void
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Coordinated refusal by film producers to supply films to multiplex owners constituted an anti-competitive agreement under Section 3

Collective refusal to deal is an anti-competitive horizontal agreement under Section 3.

Report this question

19Section 19(3) of the Competition Act 2002 lists factors for determining AAEC. Which is NOT one of them?

  1. Revenue of the enterprise
  2. Creation of barriers to entry
  3. Foreclosure of competition
  4. Accrual of benefits to consumers
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Revenue of the enterprise

Revenue of the enterprise is not a factor under Section 19(3) for AAEC determination.

Report this question

20Under Section 27 of the Competition Act 2002, CCI can pass which orders?

  1. Imprisonment
  2. Cease and desist order, modification of agreement, penalty up to 10% of average turnover
  3. Winding up of enterprise
  4. Criminal prosecution directly
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Cease and desist order, modification of agreement, penalty up to 10% of average turnover

Section 27 empowers CCI to order cessation, modification, and impose financial penalties.

Report this question

21Excel Crop Care Ltd. v. CCI (2017) — the Supreme Court held:

  1. Exchange of price information between competitors can constitute an anti-competitive agreement under Section 3
  2. Price information exchange is always lawful
  3. Only written agreements are covered
  4. Cartel requires a formal agreement document
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Exchange of price information between competitors can constitute an anti-competitive agreement under Section 3

Even circumstantial evidence of coordination can establish a cartel under Section 3.

Report this question

22A 'horizontal agreement' under Section 3 of the Competition Act 2002 is one between:

  1. Enterprises at different stages (supplier-distributor)
  2. Government and private enterprises
  3. Parent and subsidiary companies
  4. Enterprises at the same stage of production/distribution — competitors
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Enterprises at the same stage of production/distribution — competitors

Horizontal agreements are between competitors at the same level of the supply chain.

Report this question

23The Director General investigates cases referred by CCI under which section of the Competition Act?

  1. Section 41
  2. Section 33
  3. Section 26
  4. Section 19
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Section 26

Section 26 provides for investigation by the Director General on CCI's direction.
