11 practice questions with answers

1In India, the liability of the State in tort is governed by:

  1. Article 300 of the Constitution read with Section 65 of the Government of India Act, 1858
  2. Article 12 only
  3. IPC
  4. Consumer Protection Act
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Article 300 of the Constitution read with Section 65 of the Government of India Act, 1858

Article 300 allows the Union and States to sue and be sued.

2The distinction between 'sovereign' and 'non-sovereign' functions for State tortious liability was first drawn in India in:

  1. Kasturilal v. State of U.P. (1965)
  2. M.C. Mehta v. Union of India
  3. Nilabati Behera v. State of Orissa
  4. P.&O. Steam Navigation Co. v. Secretary of State (1861)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. P.&O. Steam Navigation Co. v. Secretary of State (1861)

The P&O case first drew the sovereign/non-sovereign distinction in India.

Report this question

3Kasturilal Ralia Ram Jain v. State of U.P. (1965) held:

  1. Loss of property while in police custody — State not liable as it was a sovereign function
  2. State is absolutely liable
  3. State liable for tort committed by its servants
  4. State has no immunity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Loss of property while in police custody — State not liable as it was a sovereign function

Police custody was held to be a sovereign function — State immune from liability.

Report this question

4Nilabati Behera v. State of Orissa (1993) is significant because:

  1. State immunity was upheld
  2. Consumer protection applied
  3. Supreme Court awarded compensation under Article 32 for custodial death — public law remedy distinct from private tort law
  4. Sovereign immunity was expanded
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Supreme Court awarded compensation under Article 32 for custodial death — public law remedy distinct from private tort law

This case established constitutional tort as a distinct public law remedy.

Report this question

5Rudal Shah v. State of Bihar (1983) is significant because:

  1. State immunity was upheld
  2. Rylands v. Fletcher was applied
  3. First case where Supreme Court awarded monetary compensation under Article 32 for wrongful detention of 14 years
  4. Consumer protection applied to State
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. First case where Supreme Court awarded monetary compensation under Article 32 for wrongful detention of 14 years

Rudal Shah was the first case awarding compensation under Article 32 for illegal detention.

Report this question

6State of Rajasthan v. Vidyawati (1962) held:

  1. State liable for negligent driving by a government employee while on official duty — jeep accident case
  2. State immune — sovereign function
  3. No liability for State vehicles
  4. Driver personally liable only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. State liable for negligent driving by a government employee while on official duty — jeep accident case

Driving a jeep is a non-sovereign/commercial function — State liable.

Report this question

7D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal (1997) affirmed:

  1. State immunity from tort
  2. Absolute liability for private parties
  3. Consumer protection for prisoners
  4. Monetary compensation as a public law remedy for custodial torture and death under Article 21
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Monetary compensation as a public law remedy for custodial torture and death under Article 21

D.K. Basu laid down guidelines for arrest and affirmed compensation for custodial violations.

Report this question

8Chairman, Railway Board v. Chandrima Das (2000) held:

  1. Railways are immune from tort
  2. Only private law tort remedy available
  3. Railway is liable for gang rape of a woman in railway premises — public law remedy under Article 226 available
  4. Contributory negligence reduces compensation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Railway is liable for gang rape of a woman in railway premises — public law remedy under Article 226 available

The State's fundamental duty to protect Article 21 rights extends to railway premises.

Report this question

9Sebastian M. Hongray v. Union of India (1984) involved:

  1. Mandamus for compensation
  2. Habeas corpus and directed payment of exemplary costs for custodial disappearance
  3. Certiorari against the State
  4. Prohibition
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Habeas corpus and directed payment of exemplary costs for custodial disappearance

The Supreme Court directed exemplary costs for unexplained custodial disappearances.

Report this question

10The trend of post-1993 Supreme Court decisions regarding State liability is:

  1. Eroding the sovereign/non-sovereign distinction and awarding compensation for constitutional tort
  2. Expanding sovereign immunity
  3. Restricting compensation
  4. Codifying State liability
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Eroding the sovereign/non-sovereign distinction and awarding compensation for constitutional tort

Post-Nilabati Behera, the Court has progressively expanded State liability through constitutional tort.

Report this question

11The concept of 'constitutional tort' refers to:

  1. A tort defined in the Constitution
  2. Violation of fundamental rights by the State giving rise to compensatory remedy under Articles 32/226
  3. State's absolute liability
  4. Rylands v. Fletcher applied to State
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Violation of fundamental rights by the State giving rise to compensatory remedy under Articles 32/226

Constitutional tort is the remedy in public law for State violations of fundamental rights.
