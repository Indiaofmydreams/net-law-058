About this question set

This set has 13 multiple-choice questions on Trespass from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 5, Law of Torts and Consumer Protection. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 13 of the 13 questions carry a written explanation.

13 practice questions with answers

1Trespass to land requires:

  1. Damage to land
  2. Direct and intentional interference with land in another's possession — actionable per se
  3. Malicious intent
  4. Permission of owner
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Direct and intentional interference with land in another's possession — actionable per se

Trespass to land is actionable per se — no proof of damage needed.

Report this question

2'Trespass ab initio' means:

  1. One who enters lawfully but misuses the authority becomes a trespasser from the beginning
  2. Trespass from the start
  3. Permission makes the entry lawful permanently
  4. No remedy for trespass
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. One who enters lawfully but misuses the authority becomes a trespasser from the beginning

The doctrine treats the original entry as unlawful if authority is subsequently abused.

Report this question

3Six Carpenters' Case (1610) is the leading case on:

  1. Nuisance
  2. Trespass ab initio
  3. Defamation
  4. Negligence
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Trespass ab initio

The Six Carpenters' Case established the doctrine of trespass ab initio.

Report this question

4Trespass to the person includes:

  1. Negligence and nuisance
  2. Defamation and malicious prosecution
  3. Assault, battery, false imprisonment
  4. Strict liability and nuisance
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Assault, battery, false imprisonment

The three forms of trespass to person are assault, battery, and false imprisonment.

Report this question

5In battery, the contact must be:

  1. Direct and intentional — even if not hostile
  2. Hostile and intentional
  3. Negligent
  4. Consequential
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Direct and intentional — even if not hostile

Battery does not require hostility — direct intentional contact suffices.

Report this question

6The cuius est solum doctrine in trespass to land was modified by:

  1. Bernstein v. Skyviews & General Ltd. (1978) — landowner's rights in airspace limited to height necessary for ordinary use
  2. Donoghue v. Stevenson
  3. Rylands v. Fletcher
  4. Wagon Mound No. 1
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Bernstein v. Skyviews & General Ltd. (1978) — landowner's rights in airspace limited to height necessary for ordinary use

Airspace rights are limited to what is necessary for the reasonable use and enjoyment of the land.

Report this question

7Assault in tort law requires:

  1. Actual physical contact
  2. An act creating reasonable apprehension of immediate unlawful force — no physical contact needed
  3. Intent to injure
  4. Use of a weapon
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. An act creating reasonable apprehension of immediate unlawful force — no physical contact needed

Assault is the apprehension of immediate unlawful force — not the force itself.

Report this question

8False imprisonment requires:

  1. Imprisonment in a jail
  2. Use of force
  3. Imprisonment for a criminal offence
  4. Total restraint of plaintiff's freedom of movement — even without physical contact
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Total restraint of plaintiff's freedom of movement — even without physical contact

Any total restraint on movement constitutes false imprisonment.

Report this question

9Meering v. Grahame-White Aviation Co. (1920) established that:

  1. Knowledge of imprisonment is essential
  2. False imprisonment requires physical restraint only
  3. The plaintiff need not be aware of the false imprisonment at the time for the tort to be committed
  4. Consent is always a defence
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The plaintiff need not be aware of the false imprisonment at the time for the tort to be committed

False imprisonment can occur even if the plaintiff is unaware of the restraint.

Report this question

10Bird v. Jones (1845) held:

  1. Partial obstruction of a public bridge was not false imprisonment as plaintiff had means of egress
  2. Partial obstruction constitutes false imprisonment
  3. Complete restraint is not required
  4. Trespass to land occurred
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Partial obstruction of a public bridge was not false imprisonment as plaintiff had means of egress

False imprisonment requires total restraint — partial obstruction with escape route available is insufficient.

Report this question

11Scott v. Shepherd (1773) is the classic case on:

  1. Nuisance
  2. Negligence
  3. Trespass — throwing a lighted squib through a market; direct and immediate force — trespass established
  4. Defamation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Trespass — throwing a lighted squib through a market; direct and immediate force — trespass established

The lighted squib case established that direct force constitutes trespass.

Report this question

12Fowler v. Lanning (1959) held that in an action for trespass to person:

  1. Plaintiff must prove either intention or negligence — trespass is no longer actionable without fault
  2. Trespass is actionable per se without proof of fault
  3. Strict liability applies to trespass
  4. No action lies without actual damage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Plaintiff must prove either intention or negligence — trespass is no longer actionable without fault

The modern view requires proof of intention or negligence for trespass to person.

Report this question

13Letang v. Cooper (1965) held that where the act causing injury is unintentional:

  1. The correct cause of action is negligence, not trespass to the person
  2. Trespass applies to all physical contact
  3. Both trespass and negligence can be pleaded
  4. Intentional acts cannot be trespass
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The correct cause of action is negligence, not trespass to the person

Unintentional acts causing personal injury should be sued in negligence, not trespass.
