About this question set

This set has 16 multiple-choice questions on General Defences from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 5, Law of Torts and Consumer Protection. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 16 of the 16 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Torts & Consumer Protection notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

16 practice questions with answers

1'Volenti non fit injuria' means:

  1. No injury to a willing person
  2. Voluntary acceptance of risk is a complete defence
  3. Both A and B
  4. Strict liability defence
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Both A and B

Both the meaning and legal effect of the maxim are correct.

Report this question

2For 'volenti non fit injuria' to apply, the plaintiff must have:

  1. Knowledge of risk only
  2. Consented to the negligence
  3. Been paid compensation
  4. Knowledge + voluntary acceptance of the risk
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Knowledge + voluntary acceptance of the risk

Both scienter (knowledge) and volens (acceptance) are required.

Report this question

3In Hall v. Brooklands Auto Racing Club (1932):

  1. Strict liability was applied
  2. Defamation was established
  3. Volenti non fit injuria was upheld for a spectator at a motor race
  4. Trespass was discussed
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Volenti non fit injuria was upheld for a spectator at a motor race

A spectator at a motor race accepted the inherent risk of the sport.

Report this question

4The defence of 'ex turpi causa non oritur actio' means:

  1. No action arises from an illegal act of plaintiff
  2. Contributory negligence
  3. Act of God
  4. Statutory authority
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. No action arises from an illegal act of plaintiff

A claimant cannot succeed if their claim arises from their own illegal conduct.

Report this question

5Contributory negligence as a complete defence was converted to partial defence in England by:

  1. Law Reform Act 1934
  2. Law Reform (Contributory Negligence) Act 1945
  3. Limitation Act 1939
  4. Occupiers' Liability Act 1957
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Law Reform (Contributory Negligence) Act 1945

The 1945 Act introduced apportionment of damages instead of complete bar.

Report this question

6'Act of God' (vis major) as a defence requires:

  1. Any natural event
  2. Human negligence
  3. Statutory immunity
  4. An extraordinary natural event that could not be anticipated or guarded against
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. An extraordinary natural event that could not be anticipated or guarded against

The event must be extraordinary and beyond reasonable anticipation.

Report this question

7In Nichols v. Marsland (1876), which defence was successfully pleaded?

  1. Volenti non fit injuria
  2. Contributory negligence
  3. Statutory authority
  4. Act of God
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Act of God

An extraordinary flood — act of God — was a successful defence to Rylands v. Fletcher.

Report this question

8The defence of statutory authority means:

  1. Acts done under express or implied authority of a statute are protected from tortious liability
  2. Any government act is protected
  3. Acts done by ministers are immune
  4. Acts done by police are immune
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Acts done under express or implied authority of a statute are protected from tortious liability

Statutory authority must be express or implied — not a general government act.

Report this question

9Private defence is available in tort when:

  1. Force used is proportionate to the threat
  2. There is an imminent threat
  3. No more force than necessary is used
  4. All of the above
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. All of the above

All three conditions must be met for private defence to succeed.

Report this question

10Necessity as a defence in tort:

  1. Is the same as volenti
  2. Justifies intentional harm to prevent greater harm
  3. Is available only in criminal law
  4. Requires consent of the plaintiff
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Justifies intentional harm to prevent greater harm

Necessity involves choosing the lesser of two evils.

Report this question

11Leigh v. Gladstone (1909) is related to which defence?

  1. Act of God
  2. Necessity
  3. Volenti
  4. Statutory authority
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Necessity

Force-feeding of a prisoner was held justified under necessity.

Report this question

12Which of the following is NOT a general defence in tort?

  1. Volenti non fit injuria
  2. Mistake of law
  3. Act of God
  4. Private defence
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Mistake of law

Mistake of law is not a defence in tort.

Report this question

13In Dann v. Hamilton (1939), the court held regarding 'volenti non fit injuria':

  1. Plaintiff who knowingly travelled with a drunk driver was held to have consented
  2. Strict liability applied
  3. Contributory negligence was the only defence
  4. Mere knowledge of risk does not necessarily amount to consent — volenti not applied
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Mere knowledge of risk does not necessarily amount to consent — volenti not applied

Knowledge of risk alone does not establish voluntary acceptance — both are required.

Report this question

14The defence of volenti is NOT available when:

  1. Plaintiff was genuinely free to choose
  2. Plaintiff had no real choice — economic or social compulsion (Smith v. Baker, 1891)
  3. Plaintiff was a spectator at a sporting event
  4. Plaintiff signed a waiver
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Plaintiff had no real choice — economic or social compulsion (Smith v. Baker, 1891)

Where there is no real freedom to choose, volenti cannot apply.

Report this question

15Haynes v. Harwood (1935) is a leading case on:

  1. Rescue cases — rescuer injured while saving third party from defendant's negligence — defendant liable
  2. Strict liability
  3. Nuisance
  4. Volenti non fit injuria
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Rescue cases — rescuer injured while saving third party from defendant's negligence — defendant liable

Volenti does not apply to rescuers acting in an emergency.

Report this question

16Stanley v. Powell (1891) is a leading case on:

  1. Trespass
  2. Nuisance
  3. Inevitable accident — pellet deflected by tree and injured plaintiff; defendant not liable
  4. Defamation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Inevitable accident — pellet deflected by tree and injured plaintiff; defendant not liable

An unforeseeable deflection of a pellet constituted an inevitable accident.
