About this question set

This set has 22 multiple-choice questions on General Principles of Tortious Liability from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 5, Law of Torts and Consumer Protection. 22 practice questions with answers

1The maxim 'damnum sine injuria' means:

  1. Injury without damage
  2. Legal damage with legal injury
  3. Damage without legal injury
  4. No damage, no suit
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Damage without legal injury

Damage without legal injury — no action lies.

Report this question

2The maxim 'injuria sine damno' means:

  1. Damage without legal injury
  2. No liability without fault
  3. Legal injury without actual damage — yet actionable
  4. Contributory negligence
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Legal injury without actual damage — yet actionable

Legal injury without actual damage is actionable per se.

Report this question

3In Ashby v. White (1703), the principle illustrated was:

  1. Damnum sine injuria
  2. Volenti non fit injuria
  3. Strict liability
  4. Injuria sine damno
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Injuria sine damno

Plaintiff's right to vote was wrongfully denied; no actual loss but still actionable.

Report this question

4In Gloucester Grammar School case, the principle illustrated was:

  1. Injuria sine damno
  2. Strict liability
  3. Absolute liability
  4. Damnum sine injuria
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Damnum sine injuria

Opening a rival school caused damage but no legal wrong.

Report this question

5The concept of 'Malice in law' in tort means:

  1. Personal hatred
  2. Motive to harm
  3. Intentional commission of a wrongful act without just cause or excuse
  4. Criminal intent
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Intentional commission of a wrongful act without just cause or excuse

Malice in law does not require personal spite — it is intent to do a wrongful act.

Report this question

6'Malice in fact' in tort law refers to:

  1. Any intentional act
  2. Strict liability situations
  3. Actual ill-will or spite
  4. Negligence
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Actual ill-will or spite

Malice in fact means actual ill-will or spite toward the plaintiff.

Report this question

7Which of the following is NOT an essential element of a tort?

  1. A wrongful act
  2. Legal damage
  3. Criminal intention
  4. Legal remedy
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Criminal intention

Criminal intention is not required for a tort.

Report this question

8The principle of 'respondeat superior' means:

  1. Let the master answer / employer liable for employee's tort
  2. Let the principal decide
  3. Superior courts have jurisdiction
  4. Strict liability of the state
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Let the master answer / employer liable for employee's tort

The master is vicariously liable for servant's torts in course of employment.

Report this question

9Vicarious liability requires which relationship?

  1. Master-servant
  2. Principal-agent
  3. Partners
  4. All of the above
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. All of the above

Vicarious liability arises in master-servant, principal-agent and partnership relationships.

Report this question

10For vicarious liability of master, the tort must be committed:

  1. With master's knowledge
  2. With master's consent
  3. At the master's premises only
  4. During the course of employment
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. During the course of employment

The tort must occur in the course of employment.

Report this question

11Limpus v. London General Omnibus Co. (1862) is related to:

  1. Defamation
  2. Vicarious liability — act within course of employment even if prohibited
  3. Nuisance
  4. Strict liability
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Vicarious liability — act within course of employment even if prohibited

Prohibited act done in unauthorised way still within course of employment.

Report this question

12The doctrine of 'common employment' (fellow servant rule) was abolished in England by:

  1. Law Reform Act, 1945
  2. Occupiers' Liability Act, 1957
  3. Limitation Act, 1939
  4. Law Reform (Personal Injuries) Act, 1948
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Law Reform (Personal Injuries) Act, 1948

The Law Reform (Personal Injuries) Act 1948 abolished the fellow servant rule.

Report this question

13Which of the following best represents the objective standard of care in negligence?

  1. Standard of a reasonable man (bonus pater familias)
  2. Standard of the defendant personally
  3. Standard of an expert
  4. Standard prescribed by the court
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Standard of a reasonable man (bonus pater familias)

The reasonable man standard is the objective test for negligence.

Report this question

14Donoghue v. Stevenson (1932) established:

  1. Strict liability
  2. Neighbour principle and duty of care in negligence
  3. Absolute liability
  4. Defamation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Neighbour principle and duty of care in negligence

Lord Atkin's neighbour principle — a manufacturer owes duty of care to the ultimate consumer.

Report this question

15The doctrine of 'qui facit per alium, facit per se' means:

  1. Let the master answer
  2. He who acts through another acts himself — basis of vicarious liability
  3. No liability without fault
  4. Absolute liability
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. He who acts through another acts himself — basis of vicarious liability

This maxim forms the philosophical basis of vicarious liability.

Report this question

16Independent contractors are generally NOT covered under vicarious liability EXCEPT when:

  1. The work is extra-hazardous
  2. Non-delegable duty exists
  3. Employer authorised the tort
  4. All of the above
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. All of the above

In all three situations, the employer may be vicariously liable even for independent contractors.

Report this question

17Mersey Docks & Harbour Board v. Coggins & Griffiths (1947) is the leading case on:

  1. Defamation
  2. Strict liability
  3. Borrowed/loaned servant — determining who is the 'employer' for vicarious liability
  4. Nuisance
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Borrowed/loaned servant — determining who is the 'employer' for vicarious liability

The case determines which employer bears vicarious liability for a borrowed servant.

Report this question

18The test to determine master-servant relationship (control test) was extended in:

  1. Donoghue v. Stevenson
  2. Mersey Docks case
  3. Cassidy v. Ministry of Health (1951)
  4. Limpus v. LGOC
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Cassidy v. Ministry of Health (1951)

Cassidy extended the control test — hospitals liable for negligence of doctors.

Report this question

19Lister v. Hesley Hall Ltd. (2001) extended vicarious liability to:

  1. Acts of independent contractors
  2. Government employees
  3. Medical negligence
  4. Sexual abuse by an employee — 'close connection' test replacing the Salmond test
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Sexual abuse by an employee — 'close connection' test replacing the Salmond test

The close connection test replaced the narrower Salmond test for vicarious liability.

Report this question

20The 'frolic of his own' doctrine in vicarious liability means:

  1. When an employee deviates completely from his duties — employer not liable
  2. Employee's act within scope of employment
  3. Employer's personal liability
  4. Borrowed servant doctrine
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. When an employee deviates completely from his duties — employer not liable

A complete deviation from duty takes the employee outside course of employment.

Report this question

21Joel v. Morrison (1834) established the principle of:

  1. Strict liability
  2. Absolute liability
  3. Nuisance
  4. Deviation from employment route — still within course of employment if minor
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Deviation from employment route — still within course of employment if minor

A minor detour (deviation) does not take an employee outside course of employment.

Report this question

22Baker v. Bolton (1808) established the common law rule that:

  1. No action lies for death of another — later reversed by Fatal Accidents Act
  2. Damages are awarded for death
  3. Dependants can sue
  4. State is liable for death
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. No action lies for death of another — later reversed by Fatal Accidents Act

At common law, a personal action dies with the person. India's Fatal Accidents Act 1855 provides a remedy.
