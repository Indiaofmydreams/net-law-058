About this question set

This set has 20 multiple-choice questions on Torts & Consumer Protection from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 5, Law of Torts and Consumer Protection. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 20 of the 20 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Torts & Consumer Protection notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

20 practice questions with answers

1Strict liability for escape of dangerous things was laid down in:

  1. Bolam
  2. Wagon Mound
  3. Rylands v. Fletcher
  4. Donoghue v. Stevenson
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Rylands v. Fletcher

Rylands v. Fletcher (1868).

Report this question

2Absolute liability in India was laid down in:

  1. M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (1987)
  2. Bhopal Gas Leak Act
  3. Municipal Corporation of Delhi v. Subhagwanti
  4. Vishaka
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (1987)

The Oleum gas leak case removed exceptions to strict liability.

Report this question

3The neighbour principle was formulated in:

  1. Rylands v. Fletcher
  2. Bolton v. Stone
  3. Hedley Byrne
  4. Donoghue v. Stevenson
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Donoghue v. Stevenson

Lord Atkin, 1932.

Report this question

4The Wagon Mound (No. 1) laid down the test of:

  1. Reasonable foreseeability of damage
  2. Contributory negligence
  3. Strict liability
  4. Directness
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Reasonable foreseeability of damage

Overruled the directness test of Re Polemis.

Report this question

5'Volenti non fit injuria' means:

  1. To a willing person no injury is done
  2. Damage without injury
  3. Injury without damage
  4. The thing speaks for itself
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. To a willing person no injury is done

Consent is a defence to a tort.

Report this question

6Criminal defamation was upheld as constitutional in:

  1. Shreya Singhal
  2. Subramanian Swamy v. Union of India
  3. Puttaswamy
  4. R. Rajagopal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Subramanian Swamy v. Union of India

Decided in 2016.

Report this question

7Compensation for illegal detention of an MLA was granted in:

  1. Bhim Singh v. State of J&K
  2. D.K. Basu
  3. Rudul Sah
  4. Nilabati Behera
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Bhim Singh v. State of J&K

A public-law remedy for violation of Article 21.

Report this question

8State liability for non-sovereign functions was recognised in:

  1. Nagendra Rao
  2. P&O Steam Navigation
  3. State of Rajasthan v. Vidyawati
  4. Kasturilal Ralia Ram Jain
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. State of Rajasthan v. Vidyawati

Vidyawati (1962) rejected immunity for non-sovereign acts.

Report this question

9The doctrine 'res ipsa loquitur' means:

  1. No harm no foul
  2. The thing speaks for itself
  3. Damage without injury
  4. Let the master answer
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The thing speaks for itself

Negligence may be inferred from the occurrence itself.

Report this question

10Private nuisance is interference with:

  1. Reputation
  2. Personal liberty
  3. Contract rights
  4. Use and enjoyment of land
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Use and enjoyment of land

It is an unlawful interference with enjoyment of land.

Report this question

11The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 replaced the Act of:

  1. 1991
  2. 2002
  3. 1976
  4. 1986
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 1986

It came into force in July 2020.

Report this question

12The authority created under the 2019 Act to regulate unfair trade practices is the:

  1. CCPA
  2. TRAI
  3. CCI
  4. SEBI
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. CCPA

Central Consumer Protection Authority.

Report this question

13Medical services were held to be 'service' under consumer law in:

  1. Jacob Mathew
  2. Bolam
  3. Indian Medical Association v. V.P. Shantha
  4. Samira Kohli
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Indian Medical Association v. V.P. Shantha

Decided in 1995.

Report this question

14For criminal medical negligence, the standard is:

  1. Absolute liability
  2. Gross negligence
  3. Slight negligence
  4. Strict liability
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Gross negligence

Jacob Mathew v. State of Punjab (2005).

Report this question

15No-fault liability under the Motor Vehicles Act means compensation:

  1. Only for property
  2. Only on proof of negligence
  3. Irrespective of fault
  4. Only to drivers
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Irrespective of fault

Statutory fixed compensation without proving fault.

Report this question

16The Competition Act, 2002 replaced the:

  1. SEBI Act
  2. MRTP Act, 1969
  3. Companies Act 1956
  4. FERA
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. MRTP Act, 1969

It created the Competition Commission of India.

Report this question

17Contributory negligence results in:

  1. Reduced damages
  2. Higher damages
  3. Punitive damages
  4. No liability
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Reduced damages

Claimant's own fault reduces recovery.

Report this question

18'Damnum sine injuria' means:

  1. Legal injury without damage
  2. Damage with injury
  3. Injury to property
  4. Damage without legal injury
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Damage without legal injury

Illustrated by Gloucester Grammar School case.

Report this question

19'Injuria sine damno' was illustrated in:

  1. Donoghue
  2. Mogul Steamship
  3. Bradford v. Pickles
  4. Ashby v. White
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Ashby v. White

Violation of a legal right is actionable even without loss.

Report this question

20Employer's liability for an employee's tort in course of employment is:

  1. Strict liability
  2. Absolute liability
  3. Vicarious liability
  4. Contributory liability
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Vicarious liability

Rooted in respondeat superior.

Report this question
