About this question set

This set has 17 multiple-choice questions on Miscellaneous Torts from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 5, Law of Torts and Consumer Protection. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 17 of the 17 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Torts & Consumer Protection notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

17 practice questions with answers

1Fault liability is based on the principle:

  1. No liability without fault
  2. Strict liability regardless of fault
  3. Absolute liability
  4. Statutory liability
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. No liability without fault

Fault liability requires proof of intentional or negligent wrongdoing.

Report this question

2The test for nuisance is:

  1. Malicious use
  2. Negligent use only
  3. Intentional harm only
  4. Reasonable user of land
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Reasonable user of land

The reasonable user test balances the defendant's use against the plaintiff's right to enjoyment.

Report this question

3Which of the following is a defence to private nuisance?

  1. Statutory authority
  2. Prescriptive right (20 years)
  3. Both A and B
  4. Malice of defendant
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Both A and B

Both statutory authority and a prescriptive right (long use) are defences to private nuisance.

Report this question

4The absolute liability principle applies in India to:

  1. All enterprises
  2. Only government enterprises
  3. Only MNCs
  4. Enterprises engaged in hazardous or inherently dangerous industry
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Enterprises engaged in hazardous or inherently dangerous industry

Absolute liability under M.C. Mehta applies to industries dealing in hazardous/dangerous activities.

Report this question

5Parental authority as a defence in tort covers:

  1. Any act by a parent
  2. Reasonable and moderate acts done in good faith for child's benefit
  3. Corporal punishment always
  4. None of the above
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Reasonable and moderate acts done in good faith for child's benefit

Only reasonable acts done in good faith for the child's benefit are protected.

Report this question

6Inevitable accident means:

  1. Any accident
  2. Act of God only
  3. An accident not avoidable by any precaution a reasonable man could be expected to take
  4. Negligent accident
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. An accident not avoidable by any precaution a reasonable man could be expected to take

Inevitable accident is distinct from Act of God — it covers human-caused but unpreventable events.

Report this question

7Necessity as a defence in tort:

  1. Justifies intentional harm to prevent greater harm
  2. Is the same as volenti
  3. Is available only in criminal law
  4. Requires consent of the plaintiff
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Justifies intentional harm to prevent greater harm

Necessity permits an intentional act that would otherwise be a tort in order to prevent greater harm.

Report this question

8Which section of the Motor Vehicles Act 1988 deals with compulsory third-party insurance?

  1. Section 140
  2. Section 146
  3. Section 165
  4. Section 163A
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Section 146

Section 146 makes third-party insurance mandatory for all motor vehicles.

Report this question

9Under Section 166(3) of the Motor Vehicles Act 1988, where an accident involves death, an application for compensation may be made by:

  1. Only legal heirs
  2. Only the insurer
  3. Legal representatives of deceased, any agent authorised by them, or the insurer
  4. Only the police
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Legal representatives of deceased, any agent authorised by them, or the insurer

Section 166(3) allows legal representatives and their agents to file compensation applications.

Report this question

10Unfair trade practice under the Consumer Protection Act includes:

  1. Reasonable pricing only
  2. False representation of goods, misleading advertisement, offering gifts or prizes with intent to deceive
  3. Refusal to sell
  4. Import of goods
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. False representation of goods, misleading advertisement, offering gifts or prizes with intent to deceive

Unfair trade practices include false representations, misleading ads, and deceptive prize offers.

Report this question

11Anns v. Merton LBC (1978) established which test for duty of care?

  1. Three-part Caparo test
  2. Bolam test
  3. Neighbour principle only
  4. Two-stage test — proximity/foreseeability + policy reasons to limit duty
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Two-stage test — proximity/foreseeability + policy reasons to limit duty

Lord Wilberforce's two-stage test was later modified and replaced by the Caparo three-part test.

Report this question

12The Gloucester Grammar School case (1410) is the earliest illustration of:

  1. Injuria sine damno
  2. Strict liability
  3. Nuisance
  4. Damnum sine injuria
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Damnum sine injuria

Opening a rival school caused financial loss but no legal wrong — damnum sine injuria.

Report this question

13Halsey v. Esso Petroleum (1961) is a case establishing liability for:

  1. Both public and private nuisance simultaneously — smell, acid smuts, noise
  2. Strict liability only
  3. Trespass to land only
  4. Rylands v. Fletcher only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both public and private nuisance simultaneously — smell, acid smuts, noise

The case is unique in covering both public and private nuisance arising from the same defendant's activities.

Report this question

14The Wilkinson v. Downton (1897) rule has been absorbed under which modern tort category?

  1. Negligence
  2. Strict liability
  3. Nuisance
  4. Intentional infliction of emotional distress / harassment
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Intentional infliction of emotional distress / harassment

Wilkinson v. Downton now falls under intentional infliction of emotional distress as an independent tort.

Report this question

15Under the Consumer Protection Act 2019, e-commerce platforms are:

  1. Specifically regulated — must provide seller information, cannot indulge in unfair trade practices
  2. Completely exempt
  3. Only liable for defective goods
  4. Regulated only by IT Act
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Specifically regulated — must provide seller information, cannot indulge in unfair trade practices

The 2019 Act specifically includes e-commerce regulation — a major departure from the 1986 Act.

Report this question

16Under the Competition Act 2002, maximum penalty for cartelisation is:

  1. Rs. 10 crore fixed
  2. 5% of turnover
  3. Imprisonment only
  4. Up to 3 times profit per year of continuation of cartel OR 10% of average turnover, whichever is higher
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Up to 3 times profit per year of continuation of cartel OR 10% of average turnover, whichever is higher

The cartel penalty is the most severe under the Competition Act — up to 3× profit per year.

Report this question

17Penalty for misleading advertisement under the Consumer Protection Act 2019 is:

  1. Rs. 1 lakh fixed penalty
  2. Only injunction, no fine
  3. Criminal imprisonment only
  4. Up to Rs. 10 lakh (first offence), Rs. 50 lakh (subsequent offence); endorsers also liable
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Up to Rs. 10 lakh (first offence), Rs. 50 lakh (subsequent offence); endorsers also liable

The 2019 Act introduced significant penalties for misleading ads and made endorsers liable.
