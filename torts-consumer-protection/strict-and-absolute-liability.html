18 practice questions with answers

1The rule in Rylands v. Fletcher (1868) states:

  1. Absolute liability without any exception
  2. Strict liability only for nuisance
  3. Negligence liability for dangerous things
  4. A person who brings on his land something likely to do mischief if it escapes must keep it at his peril
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A person who brings on his land something likely to do mischief if it escapes must keep it at his peril

The rule imposes strict liability for dangerous things that escape from land.

2Which court formulated the rule in Rylands v. Fletcher?

  1. Court of Appeal by Lord Denning
  2. House of Lords only
  3. Exchequer Chamber by Blackburn J., affirmed by House of Lords
  4. Privy Council
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Exchequer Chamber by Blackburn J., affirmed by House of Lords

Blackburn J. formulated the rule; the House of Lords added the non-natural use requirement.

Report this question

3Which of the following is NOT a defence under Rylands v. Fletcher?

  1. Act of God
  2. Remoteness of damage
  3. Consent of plaintiff
  4. Act of stranger
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Remoteness of damage

Remoteness of damage is not a recognised defence under Rylands v. Fletcher.

Report this question

4The requirement of 'non-natural use' under Rylands v. Fletcher was elaborated in:

  1. Donoghue v. Stevenson
  2. Wagon Mound
  3. Cambridge Water Co. v. Eastern Counties Leather (1994)
  4. Richards v. Lothian (1913)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Richards v. Lothian (1913)

Richards v. Lothian clarified that non-natural use must be an extraordinary or unusual use of land.

Report this question

5In M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (1987) — Oleum Gas leak case — the Supreme Court of India:

  1. Applied Rylands v. Fletcher
  2. Evolved the doctrine of absolute liability — no exceptions for industries engaged in hazardous/inherently dangerous activities
  3. Applied contributory negligence
  4. Applied Consumer Protection Act
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Evolved the doctrine of absolute liability — no exceptions for industries engaged in hazardous/inherently dangerous activities

Justice Bhagwati evolved absolute liability — more stringent than Rylands v. Fletcher.

Report this question

6The absolute liability doctrine in India differs from Rylands v. Fletcher because:

  1. Absolute liability allows act of God as defence
  2. Absolute liability has no exceptions and compensation is proportionate to the capacity of enterprise
  3. Absolute liability requires non-natural use
  4. Absolute liability is codified
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Absolute liability has no exceptions and compensation is proportionate to the capacity of enterprise

M.C. Mehta doctrine allows no exceptions and scales compensation to enterprise wealth.

Report this question

7In Rylands v. Fletcher (1868), the facts were:

  1. Gas leaked from defendant's factory
  2. Chemicals contaminated a river
  3. Fire spread from defendant's land
  4. Defendant stored water in a reservoir; water burst through old mine shafts into plaintiff's mines
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Defendant stored water in a reservoir; water burst through old mine shafts into plaintiff's mines

The case arose from water escaping through abandoned mine shafts to flood the plaintiff's mines.

Report this question

8Transco plc v. Stockport Metropolitan Borough Council (2003) — the House of Lords held:

  1. Rylands is to be abolished
  2. Absolute liability is adopted in England
  3. Foreseeability is irrelevant
  4. Rylands v. Fletcher is a sub-species of private nuisance; non-natural use requires extraordinary/exceptional use
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Rylands v. Fletcher is a sub-species of private nuisance; non-natural use requires extraordinary/exceptional use

Transco confirmed Rylands as a sub-species of nuisance with a strict non-natural use requirement.

9Cambridge Water Co. v. Eastern Counties Leather plc (1994) held that for Rylands v. Fletcher:

  1. Foreseeability of damage of the relevant type IS required
  2. Foreseeability of damage is NOT required
  3. Absolute liability applies in England
  4. Act of God is no defence
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Foreseeability of damage of the relevant type IS required

Foreseeability of the type of damage is a prerequisite for Rylands v. Fletcher in England.

10The Bhopal Gas Leak (December 2-3, 1984) was caused by leakage of which gas?

  1. Methyl Isocyanate (MIC)
  2. Chlorine
  3. Phosgene
  4. Hydrogen Cyanide
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Methyl Isocyanate (MIC)

Methyl Isocyanate (MIC) leaked from Union Carbide's plant in Bhopal.

Report this question

11In the Oleum Gas Leak case (M.C. Mehta v. Union of India, 1987), which factory leaked oleum gas?

  1. Union Carbide, Bhopal
  2. ONGC
  3. Shriram Food and Fertilisers Industries, Delhi
  4. BPCL
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Shriram Food and Fertilisers Industries, Delhi

Shriram Food and Fertilisers Industries in Delhi leaked oleum gas, prompting the absolute liability doctrine.

Report this question

12The Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991 was enacted as a consequence of:

  1. Absolute liability principle — to provide immediate relief to victims of hazardous substance accidents
  2. Strict liability under Rylands v. Fletcher
  3. Consumer Protection Act
  4. Competition Act
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Absolute liability principle — to provide immediate relief to victims of hazardous substance accidents

The Act requires owners of hazardous substances to maintain insurance for immediate victim relief.

Report this question

13Charan Lal Sahu v. Union of India (1990) related to:

  1. Nuisance
  2. Motor Vehicles Act
  3. Bhopal Gas Leak and constitutional validity of the Bhopal Gas Leak Disaster (Processing of Claims) Act 1985
  4. Consumer Protection Act
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Bhopal Gas Leak and constitutional validity of the Bhopal Gas Leak Disaster (Processing of Claims) Act 1985

The Supreme Court upheld the validity of the Bhopal Act providing for government to represent victims.

Report this question

14The strict liability for dangerous animals (scienter rule) requires:

  1. Negligence in keeping the animal
  2. Absolute liability regardless of knowledge
  3. Knowledge of the animal's vicious propensity
  4. Statutory notification
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Knowledge of the animal's vicious propensity

The owner must have knowledge (scienter) of the animal's dangerous propensity.

Report this question

15Sochacki v. Sas (1947) — fire lit in a fireplace spread. The court held:

  1. Rylands v. Fletcher applies — strict liability
  2. Fire in a domestic fireplace is natural use — no strict liability under Rylands
  3. Absolute liability applies
  4. Contributory negligence reduces damages
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Fire in a domestic fireplace is natural use — no strict liability under Rylands

Domestic use of a fireplace is natural use of land — Rylands does not apply.

Report this question

16Bolton v. Stone (1951) — a cricket ball hit outside the ground injured the plaintiff. Held:

  1. Strict liability applied to cricket grounds
  2. The risk was so small and unlikely that no reasonable man would have guarded against it — no negligence
  3. Nuisance was established
  4. Contributory negligence fully applied
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The risk was so small and unlikely that no reasonable man would have guarded against it — no negligence

The extremely small probability of harm meant no negligence — risk was minimal.

Report this question

17Miller v. Jackson (1977) — Lord Denning held regarding a cricket ground causing nuisance:

  1. Public interest in cricket may outweigh individual right — injunction refused though damages granted
  2. Injunction was granted stopping cricket
  3. Strict liability applied
  4. Plaintiff had come to nuisance — no remedy
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Public interest in cricket may outweigh individual right — injunction refused though damages granted

Lord Denning balanced public interest against private nuisance — injunction refused, damages awarded.

Report this question

18State of Punjab v. Modern Cultivators (1965) held:

  1. State is immune for canal management
  2. Sovereign immunity covers canal operations
  3. State is liable when water from a government canal embankment flooded plaintiff's fields — Rylands v. Fletcher applied to State in non-sovereign capacity
  4. Absolute liability applied
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. State is liable when water from a government canal embankment flooded plaintiff's fields — Rylands v. Fletcher applied to State in non-sovereign capacity

Canal management is a non-sovereign function — State liable under Rylands v. Fletcher.
