About this question set

This set has 25 multiple-choice questions on Consumer Protection Act from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 5, Law of Torts and Consumer Protection. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 25 of the 25 questions carry a written explanation.

25 practice questions with answers

1The Consumer Protection Act, 1986 was replaced by:

  1. Consumer Protection Act, 2015
  2. Consumer Protection Act, 2019
  3. Competition Act, 2019
  4. It has not been replaced
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Consumer Protection Act, 2019

The Consumer Protection Act 2019 replaced the 1986 Act.

Report this question

2Under the Consumer Protection Act 1986, 'consumer' means a person who:

  1. Buys goods/avails services for consideration, for personal use, not for commercial purpose
  2. Buys goods/avails services for commercial resale
  3. Any person who uses goods
  4. Only the original purchaser
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Buys goods/avails services for consideration, for personal use, not for commercial purpose

Commercial purpose excludes a person from being a 'consumer' under COPRA.

Report this question

3The three-tier redressal system under the Consumer Protection Act 1986 consists of:

  1. District Forum, State Commission, National Commission
  2. Lok Adalat, State Commission, Supreme Court
  3. District Court, High Court, Supreme Court
  4. Arbitration, Mediation, Conciliation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. District Forum, State Commission, National Commission

The three tiers are District Forum, State Commission, and National Commission.

Report this question

4Pecuniary jurisdiction of the District Consumer Forum (1986 Act) was:

  1. Up to Rs. 1 crore
  2. Up to Rs. 5 lakhs
  3. Up to Rs. 20 lakhs
  4. Unlimited
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Up to Rs. 20 lakhs

Under 1986 Act: up to Rs. 20 lakhs. Under 2019 Act: up to Rs. 1 crore.

Report this question

5'Deficiency in service' under the Consumer Protection Act means:

  1. Any fault in goods
  2. Breach of contract only
  3. Criminal negligence
  4. Any fault, imperfection, shortcoming or inadequacy in quality, nature and manner of performance of service
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Any fault, imperfection, shortcoming or inadequacy in quality, nature and manner of performance of service

Deficiency in service is broadly defined to cover any shortcoming in quality of service.

Report this question

6Which of the following is NOT a right of consumers under the Consumer Protection Act?

  1. Right to safety
  2. Right to unlimited compensation
  3. Right to be heard
  4. Right to redressal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Right to unlimited compensation

There is no right to unlimited compensation under COPRA.

Report this question

7Indian Medical Association v. V.P. Shantha (1995) held:

  1. Medical profession is outside Consumer Protection Act
  2. Consumer Protection Act applies only to goods
  3. Medical negligence is not a consumer dispute
  4. Medical services fall within the purview of Consumer Protection Act 1986 — doctors and hospitals are covered
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Medical services fall within the purview of Consumer Protection Act 1986 — doctors and hospitals are covered

This landmark case brought medical services under the ambit of COPRA.

Report this question

8A complaint under the Consumer Protection Act 1986 must be filed within:

  1. 1 year
  2. 2 years from the date cause of action arose
  3. 3 years
  4. No limitation period
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 2 years from the date cause of action arose

The limitation period is 2 years from the date of cause of action.

Report this question

9Under Section 17 of the Consumer Protection Act 1986, the State Commission has original jurisdiction for complaints exceeding:

  1. Rs. 5 lakhs
  2. Rs. 20 lakhs
  3. Rs. 1 crore
  4. Rs. 50 lakhs
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Rs. 20 lakhs

State Commission original jurisdiction: Rs. 20 lakhs to Rs. 1 crore (1986 Act).

Report this question

10Spring Meadows Hospital v. Harjol Ahluwalia (1998) held:

  1. Parents of a child who became a vegetable due to medical negligence — both parents and child are consumers
  2. Only the child is a consumer
  3. Medical negligence not a consumer dispute
  4. Parents have no locus standi
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Parents of a child who became a vegetable due to medical negligence — both parents and child are consumers

Both the patient and parents are consumers under COPRA in medical negligence cases.

Report this question

11Under the Consumer Protection Act 1986, a 'complaint' can be filed by:

  1. Consumer, registered consumer association, Central or State Government, one or more consumers on behalf of others
  2. Only the individual consumer
  3. Only NGOs
  4. Only the government
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Consumer, registered consumer association, Central or State Government, one or more consumers on behalf of others

COPRA allows class complaints and government complaints in addition to individual consumer complaints.

Report this question

12Which of the following services is NOT covered under the Consumer Protection Act 1986?

  1. Banking, insurance, medical
  2. Transport and communication
  3. Free services / services rendered free of charge
  4. Housing and construction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Free services / services rendered free of charge

Services rendered free of charge are excluded from the definition of 'service' under COPRA.

Report this question

13Ghaziabad Development Authority v. Balbir Singh (2004) — Supreme Court held:

  1. Housing authorities are immune
  2. Compensation for mental agony and harassment by a housing authority is awardable under Consumer Protection Act
  3. Only statutory damages apply
  4. Consumer forum has no jurisdiction over housing authorities
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Compensation for mental agony and harassment by a housing authority is awardable under Consumer Protection Act

Mental agony and harassment are compensable under COPRA against housing authorities.

Report this question

14Under Section 14(1)(d) of the Consumer Protection Act 1986, the District Forum can award:

  1. Compensation for loss or injury suffered due to negligence of opposite party
  2. Imprisonment
  3. Criminal fine
  4. Injunction only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Compensation for loss or injury suffered due to negligence of opposite party

Section 14(1)(d) empowers the District Forum to award compensation for loss or injury.

Report this question

15Kishore Lal v. Chairman, ESI Corporation (2007) — Supreme Court held that Consumer Forums jurisdiction:

  1. Is excluded wherever special tribunals exist
  2. Cannot entertain disputes involving government bodies
  3. Is barred by limitation always
  4. Is concurrent with other remedies unless expressly excluded by a special statute
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Is concurrent with other remedies unless expressly excluded by a special statute

Consumer Forum jurisdiction is concurrent — not ousted merely by existence of other forums.

Report this question

16Nizam Institute of Medical Sciences v. Prasanth S. Dhananka (2009) — the Supreme Court awarded Rs. 1 crore for medical negligence. The principle affirmed was:

  1. Fixed tariff applies to medical negligence
  2. Compensation must be 'just' — covering future medical expenses, loss of earnings, pain and suffering comprehensively
  3. No compensation beyond Rs. 10 lakhs
  4. COPRA excludes government hospitals
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Compensation must be 'just' — covering future medical expenses, loss of earnings, pain and suffering comprehensively

Just compensation must be comprehensive and not merely symbolic.

Report this question

17Under the Consumer Protection Act 2019, complaints can now be filed:

  1. Only in the district where opposite party resides
  2. Only at the national level
  3. In the district where complainant resides or works for gain — major shift from 1986 Act
  4. Only through advocates
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. In the district where complainant resides or works for gain — major shift from 1986 Act

The 2019 Act is more consumer-friendly — filing can be at complainant's location.

Report this question

18Under the Consumer Protection Act 2019, the pecuniary jurisdiction of the National Commission is:

  1. Above Rs. 1 crore
  2. Above Rs. 10 crore
  3. Above Rs. 20 crore
  4. Unlimited
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Above Rs. 10 crore

Under 2019 Act: National Commission jurisdiction is above Rs. 10 crore.

Report this question

19Lucknow Development Authority v. M.K. Gupta (1994) established:

  1. Government is immune from consumer disputes
  2. Motor vehicles are consumer goods
  3. Housing is a service under Consumer Protection Act — public authorities are covered
  4. Competition law applies to housing
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Housing is a service under Consumer Protection Act — public authorities are covered

Public authorities offering housing services are covered under COPRA.

Report this question

20Section 2(o) of the Consumer Protection Act 1986 — which is specifically EXCLUDED from 'service'?

  1. Services under a contract of personal service (employment contract)
  2. Medical services
  3. Banking services
  4. Insurance services
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Services under a contract of personal service (employment contract)

Employment/personal service contracts are excluded from the definition of 'service' under COPRA.

Report this question

21V. Kishan Rao v. Nikhil Super Speciality Hospital (2010) overruled the earlier position that:

  1. Doctors are not covered under COPRA
  2. COPRA excludes government hospitals
  3. Limitation of 2 years applies to medical cases
  4. Medical negligence cases under COPRA must always be referred to expert bodies — consumer forums can adjudicate without expert evidence in all cases
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Medical negligence cases under COPRA must always be referred to expert bodies — consumer forums can adjudicate without expert evidence in all cases

Consumer forums need not always refer medical negligence cases to expert committees.

Report this question

22Ravneet Singh Bagga v. KLM Royal Dutch Airlines (1999) — National Commission held:

  1. Airlines are outside COPRA jurisdiction
  2. Overbooking of confirmed tickets by an airline constitutes deficiency in service under COPRA
  3. International airlines are immune
  4. Overbooking is a standard trade practice
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Overbooking of confirmed tickets by an airline constitutes deficiency in service under COPRA

Overbooking a confirmed ticket is a deficiency in service under COPRA.

Report this question

23Maruti Udyog Ltd. v. Susheel Kumar Gabgotra (2006) — the Supreme Court held:

  1. Consumer's complaint alone is sufficient proof
  2. Manufacturer is strictly liable without proof
  3. Manufacturing defect must be proved by expert evidence; mere opinion of consumer is insufficient
  4. COPRA does not cover vehicles
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Manufacturing defect must be proved by expert evidence; mere opinion of consumer is insufficient

Expert evidence is required to establish manufacturing defects in consumer disputes.

Report this question

24Product liability under the Consumer Protection Act 2019 (Chapter VI) imposes liability on:

  1. Only manufacturers
  2. Product manufacturer, product service provider, and product seller
  3. Only importers
  4. Only retailers
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Product manufacturer, product service provider, and product seller

Chapter VI of the 2019 Act creates a three-party product liability framework.

Report this question

25Under the Consumer Protection Act 2019, the Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) has power to:

  1. Promote, protect and enforce consumer rights; investigate violations; recall unsafe goods; impose penalties for misleading advertisements
  2. Replace the National Commission
  3. Adjudicate all consumer disputes
  4. Regulate only e-commerce
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Promote, protect and enforce consumer rights; investigate violations; recall unsafe goods; impose penalties for misleading advertisements

CCPA is a new regulatory body under the 2019 Act with wide investigative and enforcement powers.
