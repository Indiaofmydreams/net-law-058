19 practice questions with answers

1The Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 provides for 'no-fault liability' under:

  1. Section 166 only
  2. Section 165 only
  3. Section 146
  4. Sections 140 and 163A
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Sections 140 and 163A

Both Section 140 and Section 163A provide no-fault liability mechanisms.

Report this question

2Under Section 140 of the Motor Vehicles Act 1988 (no-fault liability), compensation for death is:

  1. Rs. 25,000
  2. Rs. 50,000
  3. Rs. 1,00,000
  4. Unlimited
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Rs. 50,000

Section 140: Rs. 50,000 for death; Rs. 25,000 for grievous hurt (original 1988 amounts).

Report this question

3'No fault liability' under the Motor Vehicles Act means:

  1. Compensation is payable regardless of fault or negligence of the driver
  2. Claimant must prove negligence
  3. Insurance company is not liable
  4. Owner is not liable
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Compensation is payable regardless of fault or negligence of the driver

No fault liability does not require proof of negligence — it is a strict liability mechanism.

Report this question

4The Claims Tribunal under the Motor Vehicles Act 1988 is constituted under:

  1. Section 165
  2. Section 140
  3. Section 163A
  4. Section 110
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Section 165

Section 165 provides for the constitution of Motor Accidents Claims Tribunals (MACT).

Report this question

5Third-party insurance under the Motor Vehicles Act 1988 is:

  1. Optional
  2. Required only for commercial vehicles
  3. Compulsory
  4. Required only in metropolitan cities
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Compulsory

Section 146 makes third-party insurance compulsory for all motor vehicles.

Report this question

6Sarla Verma v. Delhi Transport Corporation (2009) is a landmark Supreme Court case on:

  1. No-fault liability under MV Act
  2. Standardization of the multiplier method for calculating compensation in motor accident claims
  3. Third party insurance
  4. Claims tribunal jurisdiction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Standardization of the multiplier method for calculating compensation in motor accident claims

Sarla Verma standardized the multiplier method and income assessment for motor accident compensation.

Report this question

7National Insurance Co. Ltd. v. Pranay Sethi (2017) — the Constitution Bench held:

  1. No fault liability
  2. Addition of future prospects while computing compensation for loss of dependency
  3. Third party insurance mandatory
  4. Compulsory insurance
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Addition of future prospects while computing compensation for loss of dependency

Future prospects must be added — 40% for employed below 40 years, 25% for 40-50 years, 10% above 50.

Report this question

8Section 36 of the Motor Vehicles Act 1988 requires every driver of a transport vehicle to hold:

  1. An ordinary driving licence
  2. A commercial permit
  3. A transport vehicle licence (badge)
  4. Central government authorisation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A transport vehicle licence (badge)

Transport vehicle drivers must hold a special transport licence/badge under Section 36.

Report this question

9Nagappa v. Gurudayal Singh (2003) — the Supreme Court held:

  1. MACT cannot award compensation beyond what is claimed in the petition
  2. Only insurance companies can be respondents
  3. Death claims have a fixed ceiling
  4. MACT can award compensation exceeding the amount claimed — tribunal is not bound by amount in application
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. MACT can award compensation exceeding the amount claimed — tribunal is not bound by amount in application

MACT has wide powers and is not limited by the amount claimed in the application.

Report this question

10United India Insurance Co. v. Lehru (2003) — Supreme Court held:

  1. A person travelling on the roof of a vehicle is a third party entitled to compensation
  2. Roof passengers are not third parties
  3. Insurance company is not liable for roof passengers
  4. No fault liability does not apply to roof passengers
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A person travelling on the roof of a vehicle is a third party entitled to compensation

Roof passengers are third parties — insurance company cannot deny compensation on that ground.

11Section 149(2) of the Motor Vehicles Act 1988 — which is a valid ground for insurer to defend a claim?

  1. Vehicle involved in an accident on a private road
  2. Vehicle used without a valid permit
  3. Claim exceeds policy amount
  4. Claimant is a relative of the deceased
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Vehicle used without a valid permit

Use without a valid permit is a ground under Section 149(2) for insurer's defence.

Report this question

12Oriental Insurance Co. v. Nanjappan (2004) — the principle laid down is:

  1. Even where the driver had no valid licence, insurer must pay to third party and then recover from insured — 'pay and recover' rule
  2. Insurer is completely absolved when licence is invalid
  3. MACT has no jurisdiction in licence disputes
  4. Criminal liability replaces civil liability
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Even where the driver had no valid licence, insurer must pay to third party and then recover from insured — 'pay and recover' rule

The pay-and-recover principle protects innocent third parties from insurer defences.

Report this question

13The Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act 2019 enhanced no-fault compensation under Section 164 to:

  1. Rs. 1 lakh for death; Rs. 50,000 for grievous hurt
  2. Rs. 5 lakhs for death; Rs. 2.5 lakhs for grievous hurt
  3. Rs. 10 lakhs for death; Rs. 5 lakhs for grievous hurt
  4. Unlimited compensation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Rs. 5 lakhs for death; Rs. 2.5 lakhs for grievous hurt

The 2019 Amendment significantly enhanced no-fault compensation amounts.

14National Insurance Co. Ltd. v. Swaran Singh (2004) — Constitution Bench laid down that:

  1. Invalid licence always absolves insurer
  2. Insurer has no defence against third parties
  3. Disqualification of driver does not automatically absolve insurer from third party liability — insurer must prove breach was fundamental
  4. Third party claims are absolute
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Disqualification of driver does not automatically absolve insurer from third party liability — insurer must prove breach was fundamental

Merely proving no valid licence is not enough — insurer must show fundamental breach.

Report this question

15The structured formula for compensation under Section 163A of the MV Act is based on:

  1. Second Schedule — structured formula based on age and income
  2. Multiplier method
  3. Judicial discretion
  4. Actuarial calculation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Second Schedule — structured formula based on age and income

Section 163A uses the Second Schedule structured formula as an alternative to the fault-based claim.

Report this question

16The multiplier method for computing compensation considers:

  1. Actual loss of earnings only
  2. Fixed sum per accident
  3. Actuarial tables only
  4. Annual dependency multiplied by multiplier based on age from the Second Schedule
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Annual dependency multiplied by multiplier based on age from the Second Schedule

The multiplier reflects the number of years the dependency would have continued.

Report this question

17Pushpabai Purshottam Udeshi v. Ranjit Ginning and Pressing Co. (1977) is a leading case on:

  1. Res ipsa loquitur in motor accident cases — owner is prima facie negligent when employed driver causes accident
  2. No fault liability
  3. Compulsory insurance
  4. Claims tribunal jurisdiction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Res ipsa loquitur in motor accident cases — owner is prima facie negligent when employed driver causes accident

The owner of the vehicle bears prima facie liability when their employed driver causes an accident.

Report this question

18General Manager, Kerala SRTC v. Susamma Thomas (1994) is significant for:

  1. No fault liability
  2. Laying down the basis for the multiplier method in motor accident compensation before Sarla Verma
  3. Third party insurance
  4. Claims tribunal procedure
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Laying down the basis for the multiplier method in motor accident compensation before Sarla Verma

Susamma Thomas established the multiplier method framework later refined in Sarla Verma.

Report this question

19Under the Motor Vehicles Act 1988, an insurer can avoid liability to a third party claimant on which grounds?

  1. Vehicle involved in accident on a state highway
  2. Claimant is a minor
  3. Accident occurred at night
  4. Vehicle driven without a valid licence, policy obtained by misrepresentation, vehicle used beyond permitted purpose — all subject to pay and recover
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Vehicle driven without a valid licence, policy obtained by misrepresentation, vehicle used beyond permitted purpose — all subject to pay and recover

All listed grounds allow insurer to recover from insured after paying third party under pay-and-recover.
