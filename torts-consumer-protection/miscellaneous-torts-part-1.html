About this question set

This set has 18 multiple-choice questions on Miscellaneous Torts from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 5, Law of Torts and Consumer Protection. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 18 of the 18 questions carry a written explanation.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Torts & Consumer Protection notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

18 practice questions with answers

1The rule in Wilkinson v. Downton (1897) relates to:

  1. Strict liability
  2. Nuisance
  3. Intentional infliction of nervous shock as a tort
  4. Defamation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Intentional infliction of nervous shock as a tort

A false statement intentionally causing psychiatric harm is an independent tort.

Report this question

2The principle of 'actio personalis moritur cum persona' was modified in India by:

  1. The Limitation Act
  2. The Code of Civil Procedure
  3. The Consumer Protection Act
  4. The Legal Representatives Suits Act, 1855 — allowing legal representatives to sue/be sued in tort
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The Legal Representatives Suits Act, 1855 — allowing legal representatives to sue/be sued in tort

The Legal Representatives Suits Act 1855 allows causes of action to survive death.

Report this question

3Under the Fatal Accidents Act 1855 (India), who can sue for compensation upon death caused by a tort?

  1. Only spouse
  2. Legal representatives/heirs of the deceased
  3. Only children
  4. Only parents
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Legal representatives/heirs of the deceased

The Fatal Accidents Act 1855 allows legal representatives to sue for wrongful death.

Report this question

4The correct chronological order of key legislation is:

  1. Motor Vehicles Act → Consumer Protection Act → Competition Act → Fatal Accidents Act
  2. Consumer Protection Act → Motor Vehicles Act → Competition Act → Fatal Accidents Act
  3. Competition Act → Motor Vehicles Act → Consumer Protection Act → Fatal Accidents Act
  4. Fatal Accidents Act 1855 → Consumer Protection Act 1986 → Motor Vehicles Act 1988 → Competition Act 2002
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Fatal Accidents Act 1855 → Consumer Protection Act 1986 → Motor Vehicles Act 1988 → Competition Act 2002

Fatal Accidents Act 1855 is the oldest; Competition Act 2002 is the most recent among these.

Report this question

5Absolute privileges in defamation (no malice can defeat them) include:

  1. Parliamentary proceedings, judicial proceedings, State communications between high officials
  2. Media reports, fair comment, justification
  3. Academic publications, trade union communications, employer references
  4. All of the above
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Parliamentary proceedings, judicial proceedings, State communications between high officials

Only parliamentary, judicial, and certain State communications enjoy absolute privilege.

Report this question

6Glasgow Corporation v. Muir (1943) is related to:

  1. Strict liability
  2. Nuisance
  3. Objective standard of reasonable foreseeability in negligence
  4. Defamation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Objective standard of reasonable foreseeability in negligence

Lord Macmillan confirmed that foreseeability is judged objectively — what the reasonable man would foresee.

Report this question

7The 'polluter pays' principle in Indian environmental tort law was significantly advanced in:

  1. M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Ganga Pollution case, 1988)
  2. Kasturilal v. U.P.
  3. Sarla Verma case
  4. Nilabati Behera case
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Ganga Pollution case, 1988)

The Ganga Pollution case applied the polluter pays principle in Indian environmental law.

Report this question

8Subhash Kumar v. State of Bihar (1991) — the Supreme Court recognised:

  1. Absolute liability of industries
  2. Right to pollution-free water and air as part of the right to life under Article 21
  3. Consumer rights
  4. Motor vehicle compensation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Right to pollution-free water and air as part of the right to life under Article 21

The right to a clean environment is part of the right to life under Article 21.

Report this question

9The Environment Protection Act, 1986 was enacted largely in response to:

  1. Oleum Gas Leak
  2. Floods in Assam
  3. Nuclear tests in Rajasthan
  4. Bhopal Gas Tragedy, December 1984
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Bhopal Gas Tragedy, December 1984

The Bhopal Gas Tragedy directly prompted the enactment of the Environment Protection Act 1986.

Report this question

10Halsey v. Esso Petroleum (1961) covered both public and private nuisance in relation to:

  1. Water contamination
  2. Strict liability for oil
  3. Smell, acid smuts, and noise from an oil depot — nuisance upheld
  4. Trespass to land
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Smell, acid smuts, and noise from an oil depot — nuisance upheld

The case involved multiple forms of interference — all constituting nuisance.

Report this question

11In contributory negligence under Indian law:

  1. It is a complete defence
  2. It bars the claim entirely
  3. It results in apportionment of damages
  4. It is not recognised
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. It results in apportionment of damages

Indian courts apply the apportionment principle analogous to the 1945 UK Act.

Report this question

12The principle 'joint tortfeasors' means:

  1. Each tortfeasor is jointly and severally liable for the whole damage
  2. Each tortfeasor pays proportional damages only
  3. Only the principal tortfeasor is liable
  4. Damages are divided equally
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Each tortfeasor is jointly and severally liable for the whole damage

Each joint tortfeasor is fully liable — the plaintiff can claim the full amount from any one of them.

Report this question

13Automobiles Dealers Association v. Global Automobiles (2012) — CCI held that exclusive territorial arrangements by automobile companies:

  1. Are always exempt
  2. Amount to per se violations
  3. Are outside CCI's jurisdiction
  4. Could constitute vertical anti-competitive agreements under Section 3(4) — examined under rule of reason
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Could constitute vertical anti-competitive agreements under Section 3(4) — examined under rule of reason

Vertical agreements in automobile distribution are assessed under the rule of reason.

Report this question

14Charan Singh v. Healing Touch Hospital (2000) — the National Commission held regarding compensation:

  1. Compensation should be exemplary always
  2. No compensation for medical services
  3. Contributory negligence fully bars compensation
  4. Compensation should be fair and not a windfall — proportionate to loss suffered
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Compensation should be fair and not a windfall — proportionate to loss suffered

Compensation under COPRA should be fair and commensurate — not a windfall.

Report this question

15Under 'restrictive trade practice' under the Consumer Protection Act 1986, which is correct?

  1. Any restrictive business agreement
  2. A trade practice which tends to bring about manipulation of price or conditions of delivery to affect flow of supplies creating unjustified costs/restrictions on consumers
  3. Price fixation only
  4. Monopolistic practice only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A trade practice which tends to bring about manipulation of price or conditions of delivery to affect flow of supplies creating unjustified costs/restrictions on consumers

Restrictive trade practice involves manipulation affecting supply flow and imposing unjustified costs.

Report this question

16The de minimis exemption in competition law means:

  1. All small enterprises are exempt from Competition Act
  2. Only MNCs are covered
  3. Enterprises below certain turnover/asset thresholds are exempt from provisions relating to combinations
  4. Section 3 does not apply to small traders
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Enterprises below certain turnover/asset thresholds are exempt from provisions relating to combinations

Small combinations below prescribed thresholds are exempt from mandatory CCI notification.

Report this question

17Raj Rani v. Oriental Insurance (2009) — regarding future prospects in motor accident compensation:

  1. Future prospects are not considered
  2. Only actual income at time of death counts
  3. Future prospects must be added to income — 50% addition for those under 40 years
  4. Future prospects added only for government employees
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Future prospects must be added to income — 50% addition for those under 40 years

Future prospects add 50% for under 40, 30% for 40-50, 15% for above 50 (later refined in Pranay Sethi).

Report this question

18Tort law is distinguished from criminal law primarily because:

  1. Tort is a public wrong; crime is a private wrong
  2. Tort is a private wrong; the remedy is compensation to the aggrieved party
  3. Both are the same
  4. Tort requires imprisonment
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Tort is a private wrong; the remedy is compensation to the aggrieved party

Tort is a private wrong — the remedy (unliquidated damages) goes to the injured party, not the State.
