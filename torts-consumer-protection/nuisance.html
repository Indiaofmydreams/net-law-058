9 practice questions with answers

1The essential difference between public and private nuisance is:

  1. Public nuisance affects community at large; private nuisance affects individual's use/enjoyment of land
  2. Public nuisance is civil; private nuisance is criminal
  3. Public nuisance requires malice; private nuisance does not
  4. Both are identical
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Public nuisance affects community at large; private nuisance affects individual's use/enjoyment of land

The distinction is between interference with community rights vs individual land rights.

Report this question

2Public nuisance is a:

  1. Tort only
  2. Crime only
  3. Both a crime and a tort (when special damage is caused to an individual)
  4. Neither crime nor tort
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Both a crime and a tort (when special damage is caused to an individual)

Public nuisance is a crime; an individual can sue in tort only if they suffer special damage.

Report this question

3Sturges v. Bridgman (1879) established which principle in nuisance?

  1. Coming to a nuisance is no defence
  2. Act of God
  3. Volenti
  4. Statutory authority
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Coming to a nuisance is no defence

The fact that the plaintiff came to the nuisance does not bar their claim.

Report this question

4Radhey Shyam v. Gur Prasad (1978) — the Allahabad High Court held:

  1. Noise from a flour mill amounted to private nuisance affecting plaintiff's health and comfort
  2. Noise from a flour mill was not nuisance
  3. Nuisance requires physical damage
  4. Coming to nuisance is a valid defence
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Noise from a flour mill amounted to private nuisance affecting plaintiff's health and comfort

Noise interference with comfort and health constitutes private nuisance.

Report this question

5Attorney General v. PYA Quarries Ltd. (1957) defined public nuisance as:

  1. Any nuisance in a public place
  2. Government-caused nuisance
  3. Nuisance on public roads only
  4. Nuisance affecting a section of the public sufficiently large that it is not reasonable to expect one person to bring an action
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Nuisance affecting a section of the public sufficiently large that it is not reasonable to expect one person to bring an action

The class of persons affected must be so large that individual action is unreasonable.

Report this question

6Hollywood Silver Fox Farm v. Emmett (1936) established that in private nuisance:

  1. Malice of the defendant is relevant — firing guns near plaintiff's farm to scare foxes was nuisance even though same act without malice would be lawful
  2. Malice is irrelevant
  3. Strict liability applies
  4. Statutory authority is a complete defence
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Malice of the defendant is relevant — firing guns near plaintiff's farm to scare foxes was nuisance even though same act without malice would be lawful

Malice can convert an otherwise lawful act into actionable nuisance.

Report this question

7Christie v. Davey (1893) illustrates:

  1. Rylands v. Fletcher
  2. Defamation
  3. Malice converts lawful act into nuisance — defendant banged on walls and shouted to disturb plaintiff's music lessons
  4. Trespass
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Malice converts lawful act into nuisance — defendant banged on walls and shouted to disturb plaintiff's music lessons

Deliberate interference motivated by malice constituted nuisance.

Report this question

8The locality/character of neighbourhood rule in nuisance was stated in:

  1. Hollywood Silver Fox Farm
  2. Rylands v. Fletcher
  3. Sturges v. Bridgman (1879) — 'what would be a nuisance in Belgrave Square would not necessarily be so in Bermondsey'
  4. Donoghue v. Stevenson
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Sturges v. Bridgman (1879) — 'what would be a nuisance in Belgrave Square would not necessarily be so in Bermondsey'

The character of the neighbourhood is relevant in assessing private nuisance.

Report this question

9Under which section of the Code of Civil Procedure can a private individual sue against public nuisance?

  1. Section 91 CPC — with leave of the court or Advocate General's consent
  2. Section 9 CPC
  3. Order 1 Rule 8 CPC
  4. Section 80 CPC
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Section 91 CPC — with leave of the court or Advocate General's consent

Section 91 CPC allows private individuals to sue for public nuisance with required consent.
