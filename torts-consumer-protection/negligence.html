About this question set

22 practice questions with answers

1The three essential elements of negligence laid down in Donoghue v. Stevenson (1932) are:

  1. Malice, intent, damage
  2. Strict liability, breach, consent
  3. Fault, damage, injury
  4. Duty, breach, damage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Duty, breach, damage

The three elements of negligence are duty of care, breach of that duty, and resulting damage.

Report this question

2The 'neighbour principle' was propounded by:

  1. Lord Denning
  2. Lord Atkin in Donoghue v. Stevenson
  3. Lord Diplock
  4. Lord Wilberforce
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Lord Atkin in Donoghue v. Stevenson

Lord Atkin's neighbour principle defines who owes a duty of care to whom.

Report this question

3'Res ipsa loquitur' means:

  1. Strict liability
  2. The thing speaks for itself — raises presumption of negligence
  3. Absolute liability
  4. Contributory negligence
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The thing speaks for itself — raises presumption of negligence

Res ipsa loquitur raises a presumption of negligence from the facts themselves.

Report this question

4Byrne v. Boadle (1863) is the leading case on:

  1. Defamation
  2. Nuisance
  3. Trespass
  4. Res ipsa loquitur
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Res ipsa loquitur

A barrel falling from a warehouse established res ipsa loquitur.

Report this question

5The Bolam test (1957) applies to the standard of care for:

  1. Ordinary persons
  2. Employers
  3. Professionals / medical practitioners
  4. Minors
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Professionals / medical practitioners

The Bolam test applies the standard of the ordinary skilled professional.

Report this question

6Caparo Industries v. Dickman (1990) established a three-part test for duty of care:

  1. Damage, proximity, malice
  2. Foreseeability, proximity, fairness/justice/reasonableness
  3. Intent, damage, reasonableness
  4. Duty, breach, causation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Foreseeability, proximity, fairness/justice/reasonableness

The Caparo three-part test is the current test for duty of care in England.

Report this question

7In Jacob Mathew v. State of Punjab (2005), the Supreme Court of India held:

  1. Doctors are strictly liable
  2. Negligence in medical cases requires proof of departure from accepted standard of care
  3. Res ipsa loquitur always applies to medical negligence
  4. Hospitals have absolute liability
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Negligence in medical cases requires proof of departure from accepted standard of care

Medical negligence requires proof of departure from accepted professional standards.

Report this question

8The 'but for' test in negligence determines:

  1. Standard of care
  2. Remoteness
  3. Causation — but for defendant's breach, would damage have occurred?
  4. Duty of care
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Causation — but for defendant's breach, would damage have occurred?

The but-for test establishes factual causation in negligence.

Report this question

9The 'reasonable man' standard in negligence was classically described in:

  1. Donoghue v. Stevenson
  2. Caparo Industries
  3. Blyth v. Birmingham Waterworks Co. (1856)
  4. Bolam case
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Blyth v. Birmingham Waterworks Co. (1856)

Negligence is doing something a prudent man would not do, or omitting what a prudent man would do.

Report this question

10Paris v. Stepney Borough Council (1951) established that:

  1. Strict liability applies to employers
  2. Standard of care is higher when the potential harm is more serious
  3. Medical negligence standard applies to all
  4. Res ipsa loquitur applies
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Standard of care is higher when the potential harm is more serious

One-eyed worker — employer should have provided goggles given higher potential harm.

Report this question

11The 'learned hand formula' (B < PL) in negligence was propounded by:

  1. Lord Atkin
  2. Lord Denning
  3. Judge Learned Hand in United States v. Carroll Towing (1947)
  4. Justice Bhagwati
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Judge Learned Hand in United States v. Carroll Towing (1947)

B = burden of precaution; P = probability; L = magnitude of loss. If B < PL, defendant is negligent.

Report this question

12The distinction between 'primary' and 'secondary' victims in nervous shock was established in:

  1. Donoghue v. Stevenson
  2. Page v. Smith (1996) and Alcock v. Chief Constable of South Yorkshire (1991)
  3. Wilkinson v. Downton
  4. McLoughlin v. O'Brian
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Page v. Smith (1996) and Alcock v. Chief Constable of South Yorkshire (1991)

Page v. Smith covers primary victims; Alcock covers secondary victims (Hillsborough disaster).

Report this question

13In Alcock v. Chief Constable of South Yorkshire Police (1991), secondary victims must prove:

  1. Physical presence only
  2. Proximity of relationship, proximity of time/space, and direct perception
  3. Any relationship with the deceased
  4. Mental illness prior to incident
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Proximity of relationship, proximity of time/space, and direct perception

The three requirements for secondary victim claims in psychiatric injury.

Report this question

14Spring v. Guardian Assurance (1994) held:

  1. Strict liability applies to employers
  2. Absolute liability in employment
  3. Employers can be liable in negligence for negligent references causing economic loss
  4. No duty of care for economic loss
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Employers can be liable in negligence for negligent references causing economic loss

A special relationship exists between employer and employee for references.

Report this question

15Hedley Byrne & Co. v. Heller (1964) established liability for:

  1. Product liability
  2. Medical negligence
  3. Strict liability for financial institutions
  4. Negligent misstatement causing pure economic loss — where there is a 'special relationship'
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Negligent misstatement causing pure economic loss — where there is a 'special relationship'

The special relationship test for negligent misstatement.

Report this question

16Barnett v. Chelsea & Kensington Hospital (1969) is a leading case on:

  1. Medical negligence standard
  2. Causation — 'but for' test — even if doctor had been careful, patient would have died; no liability
  3. Res ipsa loquitur
  4. Bolam test
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Causation — 'but for' test — even if doctor had been careful, patient would have died; no liability

The but-for test was not satisfied — arsenic poisoning was fatal regardless of treatment.

Report this question

17The three requirements for res ipsa loquitur (Scott v. London & St. Katherine Docks, 1865) are:

  1. Foreseeability, proximity, fairness
  2. Duty, breach, damage
  3. Thing under defendant's control; accident would not ordinarily happen without negligence; no explanation given
  4. Negligence, intention, damage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Thing under defendant's control; accident would not ordinarily happen without negligence; no explanation given

All three conditions must be satisfied for res ipsa loquitur to apply.

Report this question

18Donoghue v. Stevenson (1932) — in the facts, the plaintiff found:

  1. A dead mouse in a tin of food
  2. Chemicals in a bottle of water
  3. A foreign object in a can of beer
  4. A decomposed snail in a bottle of ginger beer
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A decomposed snail in a bottle of ginger beer

The decomposed snail in the opaque ginger beer bottle is the iconic fact of the case.

Report this question

19Grant v. Australian Knitting Mills (1936) extended Donoghue v. Stevenson to hold that:

  1. Retailers are primarily liable
  2. Manufacturers are liable for defective products even when sold through retailers without any possibility of intermediate examination
  3. Strict liability applies to clothing
  4. Contract with retailer bars tort claim
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Manufacturers are liable for defective products even when sold through retailers without any possibility of intermediate examination

The principle of manufacturer's duty extends to all products without intermediate examination.

Report this question

20Bourhill v. Young (1943) — a pregnant woman suffered nervous shock. The House of Lords held:

  1. She was within the area of foreseeable danger — duty of care established
  2. Strict liability applied
  3. Volenti non fit injuria applied
  4. She was outside the area of foreseeable danger — no duty of care
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. She was outside the area of foreseeable danger — no duty of care

The plaintiff was not within the foreseeable area of danger created by the defendant.

Report this question

21Home Office v. Dorset Yacht Co. (1970) held:

  1. Home Office owed a duty of care for failure to prevent Borstal boys from escaping and damaging plaintiff's yacht
  2. State is immune from negligence
  3. Third parties have no duty
  4. Strict liability applied
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Home Office owed a duty of care for failure to prevent Borstal boys from escaping and damaging plaintiff's yacht

The Home Office was held liable for foreseeable damage caused by escaping Borstal boys.

Report this question

22Municipal Corporation of Delhi v. Subhagwanti (1966) — a clock tower in Chandni Chowk collapsed. The Supreme Court applied:

  1. Rylands v. Fletcher
  2. Absolute liability
  3. Res ipsa loquitur — MCD liable for negligence in maintaining the structure
  4. Volenti non fit injuria
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Res ipsa loquitur — MCD liable for negligence in maintaining the structure

Res ipsa loquitur applied — the collapse itself spoke to negligence in maintenance.
