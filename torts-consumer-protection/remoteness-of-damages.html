About this question set

This set has 12 multiple-choice questions on Remoteness of Damages from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 5, Law of Torts and Consumer Protection. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 12 of the 12 questions carry a written explanation.

12 practice questions with answers

1The two leading tests for remoteness of damage in tort are:

  1. Donoghue test and Caparo test
  2. Re Polemis test and Wagon Mound test
  3. Bolam test and but-for test
  4. Rylands test and Donoghue test
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Re Polemis test and Wagon Mound test

Re Polemis (directness) and Wagon Mound (foreseeability) are the two leading tests.

Report this question

2The Re Polemis (1921) test for remoteness is:

  1. Reasonable foreseeability
  2. Proximity
  3. Proportionality
  4. Directness — defendant liable for all direct consequences
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Directness — defendant liable for all direct consequences

Re Polemis held the defendant liable for all direct consequences of their negligent act.

Report this question

3The Wagon Mound (No. 1) (1961) test for remoteness is:

  1. Reasonable foreseeability of the type of damage
  2. Directness
  3. Proximity
  4. Neighbourhood principle
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Reasonable foreseeability of the type of damage

Wagon Mound No. 1 replaced Re Polemis with the foreseeability test.

Report this question

4The 'thin skull' or 'egg-shell skull' rule means:

  1. Defendant not liable for unforeseeable type of injury
  2. Damages are to be reduced proportionately
  3. Remoteness applies strictly
  4. Defendant takes victim as he finds him — liable even if injury is more severe due to pre-existing condition
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Defendant takes victim as he finds him — liable even if injury is more severe due to pre-existing condition

Once type of harm is foreseeable, defendant is liable for the full extent even if greater than expected.

Report this question

5Smith v. Leech Brain & Co. (1962) is the leading case on:

  1. Wagon Mound test
  2. Contributory negligence
  3. Strict liability
  4. Thin skull / egg-shell skull rule
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Thin skull / egg-shell skull rule

Burn on the lip triggered cancer due to pre-existing condition — defendant fully liable.

Report this question

6In Wagon Mound (No. 2) (1967), the Privy Council held:

  1. There was no liability
  2. Re Polemis was restored
  3. Directness was the test
  4. Liability was upheld because the risk was foreseeable even if small
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Liability was upheld because the risk was foreseeable even if small

Even a small foreseeable risk of fire from oil is sufficient — defendant liable.

Report this question

7The principle of 'novus actus interveniens' means:

  1. Original defendant remains liable
  2. A new intervening act breaks the chain of causation
  3. Contributory negligence applies
  4. Thin skull rule applies
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A new intervening act breaks the chain of causation

A sufficiently independent intervening act breaks the chain of causation.

Report this question

8McKew v. Holland (1969) is related to:

  1. Thin skull rule
  2. Remoteness — Wagon Mound test
  3. Novus actus interveniens — plaintiff's unreasonable conduct broke the chain
  4. Defamation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Novus actus interveniens — plaintiff's unreasonable conduct broke the chain

Plaintiff's unreasonable act of descending a steep staircase without support broke the chain.

Report this question

9Bradford v. Robinson Rentals (1967) is a leading case on:

  1. Wagon Mound test
  2. Res ipsa loquitur
  3. Thin skull rule — frostbite was a foreseeable type of injury even if extent was extreme
  4. Contributory negligence
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Thin skull rule — frostbite was a foreseeable type of injury even if extent was extreme

Cold injury was a foreseeable type — thin skull rule applied to the extreme frostbite.

Report this question

10Doughty v. Turner Manufacturing Co. (1964) — asbestos lid fell into molten liquid and an unforeseeable eruption occurred. Held:

  1. Thin skull rule applied
  2. Strict liability for factories
  3. Res ipsa loquitur applied
  4. No liability — the type of damage (chemical eruption) was not foreseeable
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. No liability — the type of damage (chemical eruption) was not foreseeable

The type of damage (chemical eruption) was unforeseeable — distinguished from thin skull.

Report this question

11Overseas Tankship (UK) Ltd. v. Morts Dock is the full name of:

  1. Wagon Mound No. 1 (1961)
  2. Wagon Mound No. 2
  3. Re Polemis
  4. Cambridge Water Co. case
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Wagon Mound No. 1 (1961)

The Wagon Mound No. 1 case replaced Re Polemis with the reasonable foreseeability test.

Report this question

12Overseas Tankship (UK) Ltd. v. Miller Steamship Co. is popularly known as:

  1. Wagon Mound No. 1
  2. Re Polemis
  3. Wagon Mound No. 2 (1967)
  4. Cambridge Water Co. case
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Wagon Mound No. 2 (1967)

Wagon Mound No. 2 — different plaintiffs, same fire, liability upheld as risk was foreseeable.
