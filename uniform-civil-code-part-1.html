16 practice questions with answers

1The Uniform Civil Code is enshrined in which Article of the Indian Constitution?

  1. Article 40
  2. Article 51A
  3. Article 44
  4. Article 37
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Article 44

Article 44 of the Constitution (Part IV — Directive Principles of State Policy) provides that the State shall endeavour to secure a Uniform Civil Code for all citizens.

2Goa is unique in India because:

  1. It follows the Special Marriage Act exclusively
  2. It has its own Constitution
  3. It follows Muslim Personal Law for all residents
  4. It has a Uniform Civil Code applicable to all citizens based on the Portuguese Civil Code, 1867
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. It has a Uniform Civil Code applicable to all citizens based on the Portuguese Civil Code, 1867

Goa has been following the Portuguese Civil Code of 1867 since its liberation in 1961, making it the only Indian state with a Uniform Civil Code applicable to all residents regardless of religion.

Report this question

3In Sarla Mudgal v. Union of India (1995), the Supreme Court addressed the issue of:

  1. Muslim women's maintenance rights
  2. Christian divorce law reform
  3. Inter-religion adoption
  4. Hindu men converting to Islam to contract a second marriage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Hindu men converting to Islam to contract a second marriage

The Supreme Court held that a Hindu husband who converts to Islam to contract a second marriage commits bigamy under Section 494 IPC, as the first Hindu marriage is not dissolved by conversion.

4The Uttarakhand Uniform Civil Code, 2024 was passed on:

  1. 5th January 2024
  2. 7th February 2024
  3. 15th March 2024
  4. 26th November 2023
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 7th February 2024

The Uttarakhand UCC was passed by the Uttarakhand Legislative Assembly on 7th February 2024, making it the first new UCC legislation in independent India.

5Which of the following correctly describes the nature of Article 44 of the Constitution?

  1. Constitutional amendment provision
  2. Fundamental Duty — enforceable against citizens
  3. Directive Principle — not directly enforceable but fundamental in governance
  4. Fundamental Right — justiciable
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Directive Principle — not directly enforceable but fundamental in governance

Article 44 is a Directive Principle of State Policy under Part IV. It is not directly enforceable by courts but is fundamental in governance (Article 37).

6Which Law Commission Report addressed Reform of Family Law in 2003?

  1. 71st Report (1978)
  2. 185th Report (2003)
  3. 242nd Report (2012)
  4. Both (a) and (b)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 185th Report (2003)

The 185th Report of the Law Commission (2003) dealt with Reform of Family Law. The 21st Law Commission in 2018 stated UCC is 'neither necessary nor desirable' at this stage.

7The Jose Paulo Coutinho v. Maria Luiza Valentina Pereira (2019) case is associated with:

  1. Christian divorce law
  2. Praising Goa as a shining example of a Uniform Civil Code
  3. Muslim women's maintenance
  4. Inter-country adoption
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Praising Goa as a shining example of a Uniform Civil Code

In this case, the Supreme Court praised Goa as a shining example of a Uniform Civil Code functioning successfully for all communities.

8Which case is associated with a Hindu husband's second marriage after conversion to Islam being held as bigamy, entitling the first wife to maintenance?

  1. Danial Latifi (2001)
  2. Sarla Mudgal (1995)
  3. Maneka Gandhi (1978)
  4. Shah Bano (1985)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Sarla Mudgal (1995)

In Sarla Mudgal (1995), the Supreme Court held that a Hindu husband converting to Islam to contract a second marriage commits bigamy and the first wife retains all rights including maintenance.

9The 21st Law Commission of India (2018) on the Uniform Civil Code concluded:

  1. UCC should cover only adoption and succession
  2. UCC should be enacted immediately
  3. UCC should apply only to marriage and divorce
  4. UCC is 'neither necessary nor desirable at this stage'; diversity should be protected
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. UCC is 'neither necessary nor desirable at this stage'; diversity should be protected

The 21st Law Commission (Consultation Paper, 2018) concluded that a UCC is 'neither necessary nor desirable at this stage' and recommended working towards equality within personal laws while respecting diversity.

10In Sarla Mudgal v. Union of India (1995), the Supreme Court specifically held that a Hindu husband converting to Islam to contract a second marriage:

  1. Validly dissolves the first Hindu marriage
  2. Does NOT dissolve the first marriage; the second marriage is void and amounts to bigamy under Section 494 IPC
  3. Is valid if the husband pays mehr to the second wife
  4. Requires the first wife's consent to be valid
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Does NOT dissolve the first marriage; the second marriage is void and amounts to bigamy under Section 494 IPC

Sarla Mudgal (1995) — conversion to Islam does not dissolve a Hindu marriage. The second marriage contracted after conversion constitutes bigamy under Section 494 IPC (now BNS Section 82).

11Article 44 of the Constitution uses the phrase 'shall endeavour to secure.' This implies:

  1. An obligation enforceable through writ petition
  2. A time-bound obligation to be completed within 10 years
  3. A mandatory obligation on the State to enact UCC immediately
  4. A directory obligation — the State must make sincere efforts but it is not justiciable by courts
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A directory obligation — the State must make sincere efforts but it is not justiciable by courts

Article 44 is a DPSP — aspirational in nature. 'Shall endeavour' is an obligation of effort, not result; it is not directly enforceable under Article 37.

Report this question

12Which Article declares that DPSP provisions 'shall not be enforceable by any court, but are fundamental in the governance of the country'?

  1. Article 12
  2. Article 51A
  3. Article 44
  4. Article 37
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Article 37

Article 37 — not enforceable by courts but fundamental in governance; this general Article covers all DPSPs including Article 44.

Report this question

13The tension between Article 44 (UCC) and Article 25 (freedom of religion) was addressed in Sarla Mudgal (1995) where the Court observed:

  1. There is no connection between religion and personal law in a secular state
  2. UCC would violate Article 25
  3. UCC should be enacted within one year
  4. UCC is already in force through CrPC
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. There is no connection between religion and personal law in a secular state

Sarla Mudgal (1995) — Justice Kuldip Singh observed that there is no necessary connection between religion and personal law in a secular state; personal laws are not essentially religious.

14In Shah Bano (1985), the Supreme Court's observation on Article 44 was:

  1. It is a matter of regret that Article 44 has remained a dead letter; a common civil code will help national integration
  2. UCC should be enacted within one year
  3. UCC would violate Article 25
  4. UCC is already in force
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It is a matter of regret that Article 44 has remained a dead letter; a common civil code will help national integration

Shah Bano (1985) — CJ Y.V. Chandrachud observed that Article 44 remains a 'dead letter' and a common civil code will help the cause of national integration — one of the earliest judicial observations on UCC.

Report this question

15The Portuguese Civil Code applicable in Goa was originally enacted in:

  1. 1867
  2. 1890
  3. 1910
  4. 1860
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 1867

The Portuguese Civil Code of 1867 was enacted during Portuguese rule and has continued to apply in Goa after its liberation in 1961.

Report this question

16Under the Goa Civil Code, the matrimonial property system followed is:

  1. Separate property system — each spouse retains their own property
  2. Community property (communion of assets) — property acquired before and during marriage is jointly owned by both spouses
  3. Property belongs to the husband entirely
  4. Property management is by the wife
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Community property (communion of assets) — property acquired before and during marriage is jointly owned by both spouses

Goa follows the community property (communion of assets) system — all property is jointly owned by both spouses from the date of marriage; each is entitled to half on divorce or death.
