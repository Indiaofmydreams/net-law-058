10 practice questions with answers

1The Rome Statute establishing the ICC was adopted in:

  1. 1993
  2. 1996
  3. 1998
  4. 2002
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1998

Rome Statute ADOPTED 1998, ENTERED INTO FORCE 17 July 2002. ICTY = 1993. ICTR = 1994. Chronological order: ICTY (1993) → ICTR (1994) → Rome Statute adopted (1998) → ICC operational (2002).

2The ICC has jurisdiction over which crimes under Article 5 of the Rome Statute?

  1. Terrorism and drug trafficking
  2. Genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes, crime of aggression
  3. All crimes committed by states
  4. Crimes committed against UN peacekeepers only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes, crime of aggression

Four crimes under Article 5 Rome Statute. The crime of aggression was added but only activated from 17 July 2018 via Kampala Amendments (2010). Terrorism and drug trafficking are NOT ICC crimes.

3The principle of complementarity under the Rome Statute means:

  1. ICC automatically prosecutes all war crimes
  2. ICC acts only when states are genuinely unable or unwilling to prosecute — ICC is a court of last resort
  3. ICC and national courts have equal and concurrent jurisdiction
  4. Only states that ratified the Rome Statute are subject to ICC
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. ICC acts only when states are genuinely unable or unwilling to prosecute — ICC is a court of last resort

Complementarity = ICC defers to genuinely functioning national systems. It is NOT a court of first resort. This is the fundamental structural principle of the ICC.

Report this question

4The Responsibility to Protect (R2P) doctrine was endorsed at:

  1. Rome Conference, 1998
  2. 2005 UN World Summit
  3. 1993 Vienna Human Rights Conference
  4. 2001 Durban Conference
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 2005 UN World Summit

R2P endorsed at 2005 UN World Summit Outcome Document. NOT the 1998 Rome Conference. Covers four crimes: genocide, war crimes, ethnic cleansing, crimes against humanity. Political commitment — NOT a binding treaty.

Report this question

5Universal Jurisdiction allows states to prosecute:

  1. Only their own nationals for crimes committed abroad
  2. Any person for certain international crimes regardless of where committed or nationality
  3. Only crimes committed in their territory
  4. Only crimes authorized by the UN Security Council
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Any person for certain international crimes regardless of where committed or nationality

Universal Jurisdiction applies to: grave breaches of Geneva Conventions, genocide, torture, piracy. Distinguished from ICC jurisdiction: Universal jurisdiction = state courts acting. ICC jurisdiction = The Hague court with complementarity.

Report this question

6The Kampala Amendments (2010) to the Rome Statute activated the ICC's jurisdiction over:

  1. Terrorism
  2. Drug trafficking
  3. Crime of Aggression
  4. Cyber warfare crimes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Crime of Aggression

Kampala Review Conference 2010 — Crime of Aggression activated from 17 July 2018. NOT part of Rome Statute's original 1998 framework. When an option says 'Rome Statute originally included crime of aggression with full jurisdiction' — that is wrong.

Report this question

7Command Responsibility under Article 28 of the Rome Statute means:

  1. Only those who personally commit war crimes can be prosecuted
  2. Military commanders are criminally responsible for crimes of subordinates if they knew or should have known and failed to prevent or punish
  3. Political leaders are immune from ICC prosecution
  4. Command responsibility only applies to civilian leaders, not military commanders
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Military commanders are criminally responsible for crimes of subordinates if they knew or should have known and failed to prevent or punish

Article 28 Rome Statute — applies to both military commanders and civilian superiors (different standards). Historical authority: Yamashita Case (1946). Both commanders AND civilians can bear responsibility.

8Which of the following is the primary contemporary challenge to IHL implementation?

  1. Lack of customary IHL rules
  2. Over-regulation by too many overlapping treaties
  3. Convergence of challenges: non-state armed groups, asymmetric warfare, terrorism, cyber operations, and lack of political will to prosecute
  4. ICJ lacks jurisdiction over IHL violations
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Convergence of challenges: non-state armed groups, asymmetric warfare, terrorism, cyber operations, and lack of political will to prosecute

IHL implementation challenges are multi-dimensional in contemporary conflicts. Non-state armed groups not bound by IHL treaties is the single most cited challenge.

Report this question

9The Genocide Convention was adopted in:

  1. 1945
  2. 1948
  3. 1951
  4. 1966
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1948

Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of the Crime of Genocide — 1948. The term 'genocide' was coined by Raphael Lemkin (Polish-Jewish lawyer) in 1944.

Report this question

10The ICTY (International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia) was established in:

  1. 1991
  2. 1993
  3. 1995
  4. 1998
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1993

ICTY established 1993 by SC Resolution 827. ICTR established 1994. Both under Chapter VII. ICTY was the first international tribunal since Nuremberg (1945).
