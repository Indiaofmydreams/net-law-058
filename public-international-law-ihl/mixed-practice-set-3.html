21 practice questions with answers

1De Facto recognition differs from De Jure recognition in that:

  1. De Facto is permanent while De Jure is temporary
  2. De Facto is provisional and based on effective control
  3. De Facto confers full diplomatic relations
  4. De Jure does not imply legal recognition
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. De Facto is provisional and based on effective control

De Facto = provisional, tentative, based on effective control. De Jure = full, legal, permanent. De Facto can be withdrawn; De Jure generally cannot.

2The Tobar Doctrine, in contrast to the Estrada Doctrine, requires:

  1. Immediate recognition of all new governments
  2. Non-interference in the internal affairs of any government
  3. Governments arising from unconstitutional means must be confirmed by popular election before recognition
  4. Recognition only by the UN Security Council
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Governments arising from unconstitutional means must be confirmed by popular election before recognition

Tobar Doctrine (Ecuador) — requires democratic confirmation. Estrada Doctrine (Mexico) — no opinion on legitimacy. They are philosophically opposite.

3The first modern international arbitration is considered to be:

  1. Corfu Channel Case
  2. Alabama Claims Arbitration (1872)
  3. Trail Smelter Arbitration (1941)
  4. Lotus Case (1927)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Alabama Claims Arbitration (1872)

Alabama Claims Arbitration (1872) — USA v. Great Britain. First modern international arbitration. Corfu Channel (1949) is a judicial settlement, NOT arbitration. Do not confuse the two.

Report this question

4Advisory opinions of the ICJ are:

  1. Binding on all UN members
  2. Binding on the requesting organ
  3. Not legally binding but carry great authoritative weight
  4. Binding only on P5 members
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Not legally binding but carry great authoritative weight

Advisory opinions are NOT legally binding — this is a direct contrast with contentious jurisdiction judgments which ARE binding on the parties.

Report this question

5The Permanent Court of Arbitration (PCA) was established by the:

  1. UN Charter, 1945
  2. Statute of the ICJ, 1945
  3. Hague Convention for the Pacific Settlement of Disputes, 1899
  4. Vienna Convention, 1969
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Hague Convention for the Pacific Settlement of Disputes, 1899

PCA established 1899 — it is NOT a court but a roster of arbitrators. Do not confuse PCA (1899, arbitration) with PCIJ (1920, League of Nations court) or ICJ (1945, UN court).

Report this question

6The method of dispute settlement where a third party only facilitates communication without proposing solutions is:

  1. Conciliation
  2. Mediation
  3. Good Offices
  4. Arbitration
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Good Offices

Good Offices = facilitates communication only, does not propose solutions. Mediation = suggests terms. Conciliation = investigates and proposes. Arbitration = binding decision.

Report this question

7The Trail Smelter Arbitration (1941) between USA and Canada established:

  1. State Immunity from jurisdiction
  2. No state may allow its territory to be used in a manner that causes injury in the territory of another state
  3. Diplomatic protection principles
  4. Principle of non-intervention
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. No state may allow its territory to be used in a manner that causes injury in the territory of another state

Trail Smelter (1941) = no-harm rule / foundational principle in international environmental law. Do NOT confuse with Corfu Channel (duty to notify danger) or Nicaragua (non-intervention).

8In Nicaragua v. USA (1986), the ICJ found that the USA:

  1. Had lawfully exercised the right of self-defense
  2. Violated customary international law and UN Charter by supporting Contras and mining Nicaraguan harbors
  3. Was immune from ICJ jurisdiction
  4. Had lawfully acted under Security Council authorization
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Violated customary international law and UN Charter by supporting Contras and mining Nicaraguan harbors

Nicaragua Case (1986) — non-intervention principle AND threshold for customary law formation. USA lost on merits but blocked enforcement via SC veto. Do NOT confuse with Corfu Channel or Barcelona Traction.

Report this question

9Retorsion as a method of self-help refers to:

  1. Acts of war
  2. Countermeasures that are lawful but unfriendly
  3. Countermeasures that are otherwise illegal
  4. Diplomatic severance as a last resort
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Countermeasures that are lawful but unfriendly

Retorsion = lawful but unfriendly acts (e.g., withdrawing trade benefits). Reprisals = otherwise illegal acts used as countermeasures. This distinction is directly tested.

10The Corfu Channel Case (ICJ, 1949) established which principle?

  1. Fishing rights in territorial waters
  2. Duty to notify other states of known dangers in international straits
  3. Diplomatic asylum rights
  4. Right of recognition of new states
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Duty to notify other states of known dangers in international straits

Corfu Channel Case (UK v. Albania, 1949) — mines in the Corfu Channel caused damage to British warships. ICJ held Albania responsible for failing to notify of the danger.

11The principle of non-intervention under international law (as affirmed in the Nicaragua Case 1986) means:

  1. States should avoid participating in UN operations
  2. No state may coercively intervene in the internal or external affairs of another state
  3. States cannot interfere in trade of other states
  4. Courts should not intervene in political questions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. No state may coercively intervene in the internal or external affairs of another state

Non-intervention = Article 2(7) UN Charter; reaffirmed in Nicaragua v. USA (1986). The Nicaragua Case is the authority on BOTH the non-intervention principle AND formation of customary law.

12Which document is described as a 'constitution for the oceans'?

  1. Hague Convention 1907
  2. UNCLOS (UN Convention on the Law of the Sea, 1982)
  3. Vienna Convention 1969
  4. UN Charter 1945
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. UNCLOS (UN Convention on the Law of the Sea, 1982)

UNCLOS 1982 — entered into force 1994. Governs territorial sea (12 nm), EEZ (200 nm), continental shelf, high seas. Called the 'constitution for the oceans'.

Report this question

13The International Law Commission (ILC) was established by the UN General Assembly in:

  1. 1945
  2. 1947
  3. 1950
  4. 1956
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1947

ILC established 1947 — tasked with codification and progressive development of international law. The ILC's 2001 Articles on State Responsibility are the authoritative text on state responsibility.

Report this question

14Under which provision does the Vienna Convention on Consular Relations (1963) govern consular protection?

  1. Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations 1961
  2. Vienna Convention on Consular Relations 1963
  3. Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties 1969
  4. UN Charter Article 105
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Vienna Convention on Consular Relations 1963

Three Vienna Conventions: Diplomatic Relations (1961), Consular Relations (1963), Law of Treaties (1969). These are used as distractors for each other in every few cycles.

Report this question

15Which of the following correctly states the hierarchical relationship in international law?

  1. Treaty > Custom > Jus Cogens
  2. Jus Cogens > Treaty > Custom (roughly co-equal)
  3. Custom > Treaty > Jus Cogens
  4. All sources are always equal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Jus Cogens > Treaty > Custom (roughly co-equal)

Jus Cogens prevails over all — overrides both treaties and custom. Within ordinary sources, treaties and custom are largely co-equal (later treaty can override earlier custom and vice versa).

16The 'concept of responsibility of states for internationally wrongful acts' was codified in ILC Articles in:

  1. 1969
  2. 1986
  3. 2001
  4. 2005
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 2001

ILC Articles on State Responsibility — 2001. Key concepts: internationally wrongful act = breach of international obligation attributable to a state; circumstances precluding wrongfulness; countermeasures.

17Assertion (A): The Security Council can take binding decisions on all matters. Reason (R): Under Chapter VII of the UN Charter, the Security Council can take enforcement action that is binding on all member states.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is false but R is true
  4. A is true but R is false
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A is false but R is true

A is FALSE — SC decisions under Chapter VI are recommendatory. Only Chapter VII decisions are binding. R correctly states the Chapter VII position. The word 'all matters' in A makes it wrong.

18Assertion (A): The ICC exercises universal jurisdiction over international crimes. Reason (R): The ICC's jurisdiction is based on territorial or nationality link, or SC referral — not universal jurisdiction.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is false but R is true
  4. A is true but R is false
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A is false but R is true

ICC does NOT have universal jurisdiction. ICC jurisdiction: crime in State Party territory, accused is national of State Party, or UNSC refers the situation. R correctly explains why A is false.

19Assertion (A): Common Article 3 of the Geneva Conventions is sometimes called a 'mini-convention'. Reason (R): It provides minimum humanitarian standards applicable in non-international armed conflicts across all four Geneva Conventions.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

Both correct — Common Article 3 is called a mini-convention precisely because it packs core IHL protections into one article applicable across all four GCs for NIAC situations.

20Assertion (A): Non-refoulement under the 1951 Convention is an absolute and non-derogable right with no exceptions. Reason (R): Article 33(2) of the 1951 Convention allows refoulement of a refugee who is a danger to national security or has been convicted of a particularly serious crime.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is false but R is true
  4. A is true but R is false
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A is false but R is true

A is FALSE — non-refoulement has exceptions under Article 33(2) for national security threats or convicted serious criminals. R correctly states the exceptions. Non-refoulement under UNCAT is closer to absolute but under the 1951 Convention it is not.

21Assertion (A): The Nottebohm Case established that diplomatic protection can be exercised based on any formal grant of nationality. Reason (R): The ICJ held that nationality must reflect a genuine connection — a social fact of attachment, interests, and sentiments.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. A is false but R is true
  3. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  4. A is true but R is false
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A is false but R is true

A is FALSE — Nottebohm rejected the notion that any formal grant suffices. The ICJ required genuine connection. R correctly states the ICJ's actual holding in Nottebohm (1955).
