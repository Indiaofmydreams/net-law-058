12 practice questions with answers

1The rule that an extradited person can only be tried for the crime specified in the extradition request is known as:

  1. Double Criminality Rule
  2. Principle of Specialty
  3. Political Offence Exception
  4. Non Bis in Idem
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Principle of Specialty

Specialty = what you can be tried FOR. Double Criminality = where the act must be a crime in BOTH states. These two principles are tested in the same question with swapped definitions.

2The Double Criminality Rule in extradition means:

  1. The person must have committed two crimes
  2. The act must be a crime in both the requesting and requested states
  3. Extradition requires two treaties
  4. The sentence must be doubled if extradited
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The act must be a crime in both the requesting and requested states

Double Criminality = the act must constitute a crime under the laws of BOTH states. This is a basic threshold requirement for extradition.

Report this question

3The Asylum Case (Colombia v. Peru) was decided by the ICJ in:

  1. 1948
  2. 1950
  3. 1955
  4. 1960
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1950

Asylum Case 1950 — ICJ held Colombia had NO right to unilaterally characterize the offence as political to grant diplomatic asylum. No customary right of diplomatic asylum established.

Report this question

4The principle of aut dedere aut judicare means:

  1. Extradite or prosecute
  2. Grant asylum or extradite
  3. Double punishment is forbidden
  4. Specialty governs extradition
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Extradite or prosecute

Aut dedere aut judicare — the obligation is ALTERNATIVE, not cumulative. States must either surrender the accused OR prosecute themselves. Found in Torture Convention Art. 7, Hague Hijacking Convention, Montreal Convention.

5The Soering Case (1989) decided by the European Court of Human Rights established that:

  1. Extradition can never be granted for capital offences
  2. Extradition may violate Article 3 ECHR if the person would face inhuman treatment — 'death row phenomenon'
  3. UK must extradite all murder suspects without conditions
  4. The political offence exception applies to murder
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Extradition may violate Article 3 ECHR if the person would face inhuman treatment — 'death row phenomenon'

Soering v. UK (1989) — decided by the EUROPEAN COURT OF HUMAN RIGHTS, NOT the ICJ. The examiner places ICJ as a distractor. Prospective violation of Article 3 ECHR bars extradition.

6Which of the following cases is NOT related to extradition?

  1. Savarkar case
  2. Abu Salem case
  3. Meunier Re case
  4. Paquete Habana case
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Paquete Habana case

Paquete Habana is about customary international law and laws of naval warfare — NOT extradition. Asked in June 2025: when Paquete Habana appears in an extradition list, eliminate it immediately.

Report this question

7The Castioni Re case (1891, UK) is significant because it:

  1. Established the double criminality rule
  2. Established that political offenders are not extraditable
  3. Required exhaustion of local remedies
  4. Held that mercenaries cannot claim political offence exception
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Established that political offenders are not extraditable

Castioni Re (1891) = political offence exception established. Meunier Re (1894) = exception narrowed (anarchists who target general public excluded). Both cases appear together in UGC NET extradition questions.

Report this question

8The modern political offence exception does NOT apply to:

  1. Sedition against a foreign government
  2. Espionage
  3. Terrorist acts causing mass civilian casualties
  4. Publishing anti-government pamphlets
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Terrorist acts causing mass civilian casualties

Modern treaties and conventions (UN Convention Against Terrorism, etc.) exclude terrorist acts from political offence exception. The Meunier Re principle extended this further.

Report this question

9The distinction between extradition and deportation is:

  1. There is no legal distinction
  2. Extradition is surrender to a specifically requesting state for trial/punishment; deportation is removal from a state not necessarily to the requesting state
  3. Deportation is lawful; extradition is unlawful
  4. Only states can be deported; extradition applies to individuals
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Extradition is surrender to a specifically requesting state for trial/punishment; deportation is removal from a state not necessarily to the requesting state

Key legal distinction: Extradition = surrender to requesting state for criminal proceedings. Deportation = immigration/public order removal. Rendition = informal/illegal transfer.

10Under the Draft Convention on Territorial Asylum adopted by UNGA in 1974, the grant of asylum is recognized as a sovereign right of a state under which Article?

  1. Article 1
  2. Article 2
  3. Article 3
  4. Article 5
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Article 1

Article 1 of the 1974 Draft Convention on Territorial Asylum — sovereign right of state to grant asylum. Asked directly in December 2025 with Articles 2, 3, and 5 as distractors.

Report this question

11What is the correct chronological order of these extradition cases: United States v. Rauscher, Soering v. UK, Kindler v. Canada?

  1. Soering, Rauscher, Kindler
  2. Rauscher, Soering, Kindler
  3. Kindler, Soering, Rauscher
  4. Rauscher, Kindler, Soering
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Rauscher, Soering, Kindler

US v. Rauscher (1886) → Soering v. UK (1989) → Kindler v. Canada (1991). Rauscher is the oldest extradition case in the standard list. December 2025 tested this sequence directly.

12The Haya de la Torre Case (Colombia v. Peru, 1951) — follow-up to the Asylum Case — held that:

  1. Colombia must surrender Haya de la Torre to Peru
  2. Haya de la Torre must leave the Colombian Embassy immediately
  3. The ICJ declined to prescribe the specific manner in which Colombia's obligation to terminate the asylum should be carried out
  4. Peru must grant safe conduct to Haya de la Torre
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The ICJ declined to prescribe the specific manner in which Colombia's obligation to terminate the asylum should be carried out

ICJ found asylum was irregularly granted but declined to order the specific manner of termination — left it to the parties to negotiate.
