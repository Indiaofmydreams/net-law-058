About this question set

This set has 19 multiple-choice questions on Sources of International Law from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 3, Public International Law and IHL. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 19 of the 19 questions carry a written explanation.

19 practice questions with answers

1Which Article of the ICJ Statute enumerates the sources of International Law?

  1. Article 36
  2. Article 38
  3. Article 40
  4. Article 59
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Article 38

Article 38 lists sources of IL. Article 36 deals with compulsory jurisdiction (optional clause). This swap is the single most repeated trap in UGC NET PIL questions.

Report this question

2The two essential elements of Customary International Law are:

  1. Treaty and Opinio Juris
  2. State Practice and Opinio Juris
  3. State Consent and Judicial Decision
  4. General Principles and State Practice
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. State Practice and Opinio Juris

State Practice is the objective element; Opinio Juris (belief that the practice is legally obligatory) is the subjective element.

Report this question

3Opinio Juris sive necessitatis refers to:

  1. The written text of a treaty
  2. The belief that a practice is legally obligatory
  3. A resolution of the UN General Assembly
  4. A judgment of the ICJ
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The belief that a practice is legally obligatory

Opinio Juris is the SUBJECTIVE element of custom. State Practice is the OBJECTIVE element. UGC NET has repeatedly asked which is which.

Report this question

4A treaty conflicting with a peremptory norm (jus cogens) is:

  1. Voidable
  2. Void
  3. Merely unenforceable
  4. Subject to renegotiation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Void

Under Article 53 of the VCLT 1969, a treaty conflicting with jus cogens is VOID ab initio — not voidable, not suspended. 'Voidable' as an answer is always wrong here.

Report this question

5The Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties was adopted in:

  1. 1945
  2. 1961
  3. 1969
  4. 1974
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1969

VCLT adopted 1969, entered into force 1980. When asked 'adopted' — 1969. When asked 'entered into force' — 1980. This distinction is set as a trap.

Report this question

6Under Article 59 of the ICJ Statute, judicial decisions are:

  1. Binding on all states
  2. Binding only between the parties to the dispute
  3. Persuasive authority only
  4. Binding on all UN member states
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Binding only between the parties to the dispute

ICJ decisions are not precedent in a strict sense — they bind only the parties to that specific case.

Report this question

7Erga Omnes obligations were recognized in which landmark ICJ case?

  1. Corfu Channel Case (1949)
  2. Barcelona Traction Case (1970)
  3. Nicaragua v. USA (1986)
  4. North Sea Continental Shelf Case (1969)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Barcelona Traction Case (1970)

Barcelona Traction (Belgium v. Spain) 1970 — erga omnes = obligations owed to the international community as a whole. Do NOT confuse with Nottebohm (nationality) or Nicaragua (non-intervention).

Report this question

8Which of the following is NOT listed as a source of international law under Article 38(1) of the ICJ Statute?

  1. International Conventions
  2. International Custom
  3. UN General Assembly Resolutions
  4. General Principles of Law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. UN General Assembly Resolutions

UNGA Resolutions are 'soft law' — not formally listed in Article 38(1), though they can contribute to opinio juris and crystallize into custom.

Report this question

9The North Sea Continental Shelf Cases (1969) are most associated with which principle?

  1. Recognition of States
  2. Formation of Customary International Law
  3. Asylum and Extradition
  4. UN Charter principles
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Formation of Customary International Law

ICJ clarified that state practice must be extensive, representative, and consistent — not necessarily universal. Do NOT confuse with the Lotus Case (freedom of states).

Report this question

10The PCIJ in the Lotus Case (1927) held that:

  1. States have absolute freedom to act unless a permissive rule exists
  2. International law restricts state sovereignty at every step
  3. Whatever is not expressly permitted is forbidden
  4. Turkey had no jurisdiction over the French officer
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. States have absolute freedom to act unless a permissive rule exists

Lotus Principle: 'restrictions upon the independence of States cannot be presumed' — what is not prohibited is permitted. The case was before the PCIJ, NOT the ICJ.

Report this question

11Under VCLT, Article 32 — travaux préparatoires are used as:

  1. Primary means of treaty interpretation
  2. Supplementary means of interpretation to confirm meaning or resolve ambiguity
  3. Binding interpretation only if all parties consent
  4. A tool for treaty amendment
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Supplementary means of interpretation to confirm meaning or resolve ambiguity

Article 31 VCLT = primary rule (good faith, ordinary meaning, context). Article 32 = supplementary (travaux préparatoires). Do not elevate travaux to primary status.

Report this question

12Which of the following correctly identifies the subsidiary sources of international law under Article 38(1)(d) of the ICJ Statute?

  1. UN Security Council Resolutions
  2. Decisions of national courts only
  3. Judicial decisions and teachings of the most highly qualified publicists
  4. General Assembly Declarations
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Judicial decisions and teachings of the most highly qualified publicists

Article 38(1)(d) — BOTH judicial decisions AND teachings of publicists together. The examiner sometimes lists only one. The correct answer must include both.

Report this question

13A state that persistently objects to an emerging rule of customary international law from its inception is:

  1. Still bound once the rule crystallizes as custom
  2. Bound after 10 years of consistent state practice by others
  3. Not bound by that rule — persistent objector doctrine
  4. Required to take the matter to the ICJ
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Not bound by that rule — persistent objector doctrine

Persistent Objector Rule — the state must have objected FROM THE BEGINNING and CONSISTENTLY. A state that objects after custom forms remains bound. Authority: Anglo-Norwegian Fisheries Case (1951).

Report this question

14Jus cogens norms can only be modified by:

  1. A UN Security Council Resolution
  2. A subsequent norm of general international law having the same character
  3. A treaty signed by a majority of states
  4. An ICJ advisory opinion
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A subsequent norm of general international law having the same character

Article 53 VCLT — jus cogens can only be displaced by a subsequent peremptory norm of the same character.

Report this question

15The principle ex injuria jus non oritur means:

  1. Rights arise from lawful acts only; illegal acts cannot generate legal rights
  2. Law arises from injury suffered
  3. Treaties override custom in all situations
  4. Sovereignty is absolute
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Rights arise from lawful acts only; illegal acts cannot generate legal rights

A state cannot acquire legal rights through an illegal act. Also called the Stimson Doctrine in context of non-recognition of territory acquired by force.

Report this question

16A state may invoke fundamental change of circumstances (rebus sic stantibus) to terminate a treaty ONLY if:

  1. The change makes performance more difficult economically
  2. The changed circumstances were the essential basis of consent AND the change radically transforms obligations still to be performed
  3. The government of that state changes
  4. A new head of state refuses to be bound
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The changed circumstances were the essential basis of consent AND the change radically transforms obligations still to be performed

Article 62 VCLT — rebus sic stantibus is strictly limited. Economic hardship alone is insufficient. Both conditions must be met simultaneously.

Report this question

17The Advisory Opinion on Reservations to the Genocide Convention (ICJ, 1951) laid down which test for permissibility of reservations?

  1. Unanimity of all parties to the treaty
  2. Compatibility with the object and purpose of the treaty
  3. Submission within 90 days of signing
  4. Endorsement by the UN Secretary-General
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Compatibility with the object and purpose of the treaty

ICJ's flexible test — departed from the traditional unanimity rule. This opened the modern law of reservations.

Report this question

18Under VCLT, a treaty between State A and State B creates rights for State C (a third party) only if:

  1. State C is a member of the UN
  2. The treaty expressly provides a right and State C assents — Article 36 VCLT
  3. Automatically, as all treaties bind all states
  4. The UN General Assembly endorses the treaty
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The treaty expressly provides a right and State C assents — Article 36 VCLT

Pacta tertiis nec nocent nec prosunt — a treaty binds only its parties. Article 36 creates a limited exception for third-party rights with their assent.

Report this question

19Which of the following statements about Grotius and Vattel is correct?

  1. Both were positivists
  2. Grotius was a naturalist; Vattel was a positivist
  3. Both were naturalists
  4. Vattel was a naturalist; Grotius was a positivist
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Both were naturalists

Grotius and Vattel were NATURALISTS — NOT positivists. This appeared as a false statement in December 2025. Positivists: Bentham, Austin, Triepel, Anzilotti.
