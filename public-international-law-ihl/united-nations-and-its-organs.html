18 practice questions with answers

1The UN Charter was signed on:

  1. 24 October 1945
  2. 26 June 1945
  3. 1 January 1942
  4. 10 December 1948
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 26 June 1945

UN Charter SIGNED 26 June 1945 at San Francisco. ENTERED INTO FORCE 24 October 1945 (UN Day). 10 December is Human Rights Day (UDHR). Never confuse these dates.

Report this question

2On which date did the United Nations Organisation come into existence?

  1. 20 October 1945
  2. 24 October 1945
  3. 10 December 1948
  4. 10 December 1945
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 24 October 1945

24 October 1945 = UN established (entry into force of Charter). Asked directly in June 2025. The distractor 10 December 1948 is UDHR adoption date. Extremely high frequency across 2020, 2022, 2024, June 2025.

Report this question

3How many principal organs does the UN have under Article 7 of the Charter?

  1. 4
  2. 5
  3. 6
  4. 7
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 6

Six principal organs: General Assembly, Security Council, ECOSOC, Trusteeship Council, ICJ, Secretariat. WHO, ILO, UNESCO are specialized agencies — NOT principal organs.

Report this question

4The Security Council has how many members in total?

  1. 10
  2. 11
  3. 15
  4. 20
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 15

15 members: 5 permanent (P5: USA, UK, France, Russia, China) + 10 non-permanent elected for 2-year terms. Non-permanent members serve 2 years, not 5.

Report this question

5Which Chapter of the UN Charter deals with enforcement action binding on all member states?

  1. Chapter VI
  2. Chapter VII
  3. Chapter VIII
  4. Chapter XIV
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Chapter VII

Chapter VI = peaceful settlement = RECOMMENDATORY. Chapter VII = enforcement action = BINDING. This distinction is fundamental. When the examiner says SC resolutions are always binding — that is wrong; only Chapter VII decisions are binding.

Report this question

6The 'Uniting for Peace' Resolution 377A was adopted by:

  1. UN Security Council in 1945
  2. UN General Assembly in 1950
  3. UN General Assembly in 1956
  4. UN Security Council in 1950
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. UN General Assembly in 1950

Resolution 377A — adopted by the GENERAL ASSEMBLY in 1950 (NOT Security Council). Enables GA to convene Emergency Special Session when SC is paralyzed by veto. First invoked during Korean War.

Report this question

7An abstention by a permanent member of the Security Council on a non-procedural (substantive) vote:

  1. Counts as a veto
  2. Does not constitute a veto — established practice since 1946
  3. Requires referral to the General Assembly
  4. Nullifies the resolution automatically
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Does not constitute a veto — established practice since 1946

Abstention ≠ veto — established by consistent SC practice since founding of the UN; confirmed in Namibia Advisory Opinion (1971). The veto requires an AFFIRMATIVE vote to block, not abstention.

Report this question

8The ICJ is located at:

  1. New York
  2. Geneva
  3. The Hague
  4. Vienna
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The Hague

ICJ seat = The Hague, Netherlands. WTO = Geneva. UN main seat = New York. Frequently tested as part of international institution geography questions.

Report this question

9ICJ judges hold office for a term of:

  1. 5 years
  2. 6 years
  3. 9 years
  4. Life
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 9 years

15 judges, elected for 9-year terms, by BOTH General Assembly and Security Council simultaneously. No two judges may be of the same nationality. Re-election is possible.

Report this question

10The UN Secretary-General is appointed by:

  1. General Assembly alone
  2. Security Council alone
  3. General Assembly on recommendation of the Security Council
  4. ICJ
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. General Assembly on recommendation of the Security Council

Article 97 UN Charter — appointed by GA on SC recommendation. The SC recommendation is subject to veto. This two-step process is a direct exam question.

Report this question

11Article 99 of the UN Charter empowers the UN Secretary-General to:

  1. Veto Security Council resolutions
  2. Bring to the attention of the Security Council any matter which may threaten international peace and security
  3. Convene emergency sessions of the General Assembly
  4. Deploy UN peacekeeping forces unilaterally
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Bring to the attention of the Security Council any matter which may threaten international peace and security

Article 99 = the SG's independent political power. When asked which organ or official can bring matters to the SC's attention — answer is Secretary-General under Article 99.

Report this question

12Article 2(7) of the UN Charter prohibits the UN from:

  1. Admitting new member states
  2. Intervening in matters essentially within the domestic jurisdiction of states, subject to Chapter VII enforcement measures
  3. Holding Security Council sessions without all P5 present
  4. Imposing economic sanctions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Intervening in matters essentially within the domestic jurisdiction of states, subject to Chapter VII enforcement measures

Domestic Jurisdiction Clause — Article 2(7). Importantly: the Chapter VII exception overrides this non-intervention principle when there is a threat to peace, breach of peace, or act of aggression.

13The Trusteeship Council has:

  1. Been formally abolished by UNGA resolution
  2. Been dissolved because it completed its mandate — all trust territories became independent
  3. Suspended operations in 1994 but still formally exists under the UN Charter
  4. Been merged with ECOSOC
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Suspended operations in 1994 but still formally exists under the UN Charter

The Trusteeship Council has NOT been abolished. It suspended operations in 1994 when Palau (the last trust territory) became independent. It still formally exists under the Charter.

Report this question

14Which UN organ 'addresses global justice concerns' among the six principal organs?

  1. Trusteeship Council
  2. General Assembly
  3. International Court of Justice
  4. Security Council
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. International Court of Justice

ICJ = global justice/judicial settlement. GA = political/legislative. SC = peace and security. Trusteeship Council = colonial territories. Asked directly in June 2025.

Report this question

15The correct order in which peaceful settlement methods appear in Article 33 of the UN Charter is:

  1. Mediation, Negotiation, Arbitration, Enquiry, Conciliation
  2. Negotiation, Enquiry, Mediation, Conciliation, Arbitration
  3. Arbitration, Conciliation, Mediation, Enquiry, Negotiation
  4. Enquiry, Negotiation, Conciliation, Mediation, Arbitration
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Negotiation, Enquiry, Mediation, Conciliation, Arbitration

Article 33 UN Charter order: Negotiation → Enquiry → Mediation → Conciliation → Arbitration → Judicial Settlement → Regional Agencies. Enquiry coming SECOND (before Mediation) is the most commonly misplaced step. Tested in December 2025.

Report this question

16The three principles of UN peacekeeping operations established by Dag Hammarskjöld are:

  1. Force, Neutrality, Authorization
  2. Consent of the host state, Impartiality, Non-use of force except in self-defence
  3. Chapter VII, P5 approval, Regional coordination
  4. Sovereignty, Non-intervention, Collective security
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Consent of the host state, Impartiality, Non-use of force except in self-defence

Classical UN peacekeeping (Chapter VI½) — three principles. Compulsory enforcement under Chapter VII is NOT a peacekeeping principle but a separate enforcement mechanism.

Report this question

17The Security Council established both ICTY (1993) and ICTR (1994) under which Chapter of the UN Charter?

  1. Chapter VI
  2. Chapter VII
  3. Chapter VIII
  4. Chapter XIV
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Chapter VII

Both tribunals were established by SC Resolutions under Chapter VII as subsidiary organs of the Security Council. This gave them enforcement authority and obligated all UN member states to cooperate.

Report this question

18Under Article 94(2) of the UN Charter, if a state fails to comply with an ICJ judgment, the other party may:

  1. Directly impose sanctions
  2. Refer the matter to the Security Council, which may make recommendations or take measures
  3. Approach the General Assembly for enforcement
  4. Automatically invoke Chapter VII action
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Refer the matter to the Security Council, which may make recommendations or take measures

Article 94(2) — referral to SC; but SC has discretion and P5 veto can block enforcement. This is why ICJ judgments can be defied — as demonstrated in Nicaragua v. USA (1986).
