This set has 22 multiple-choice questions on Public International Law & IHL from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 3, Public International Law and IHL. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 22 of the 22 questions carry a written explanation.

1State responsibility arises from:

  1. An internationally wrongful act attributable to a State
  2. Recognition
  3. Any treaty
  4. Any dispute
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. An internationally wrongful act attributable to a State

Breach plus attribution.

2Restrictive sovereign immunity protects:

  1. Only military acts
  2. Acts jure imperii, not acts jure gestionis
  3. Only commercial acts
  4. All State acts
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Acts jure imperii, not acts jure gestionis

Commercial acts are not immune.

3Assertion (A): Treaties bind only consenting States.
Reason (R): Pacta sunt servanda requires treaties in force to be performed in good faith.

  1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true, but R is false
  4. A is false, but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A

Both are true but R explains performance, not the consent requirement.

18The Corfu Channel case (1949) involved:

  1. France and Spain
  2. Peru and Colombia
  3. India and Pakistan
  4. United Kingdom and Albania
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. United Kingdom and Albania

It concerned mines and innocent passage.

19Which theory holds that recognition by other states is constitutive of international legal personality?

  1. Declaratory Theory
  2. Constitutive Theory
  3. Estrada Doctrine
  4. Tobar Doctrine
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Constitutive Theory

Constitutive Theory (Oppenheim) — recognition CREATES the state's international personality. Declaratory Theory — recognition merely acknowledges an already existing fact.

Report this question

20The Montevideo Convention on Rights and Duties of States was signed in:

  1. 1928
  2. 1933
  3. 1945
  4. 1961
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1933

Montevideo Convention 1933 — four criteria of statehood: permanent population, defined territory, effective government, capacity to enter into relations with other states.

Report this question

21Which of the following is NOT a criterion for statehood under the Montevideo Convention, 1933?

  1. Permanent population
  2. Defined territory
  3. Effective government
  4. Recognition by the United Nations
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Recognition by the United Nations

The four Montevideo criteria do not include UN recognition. Recognition by other states is a separate question — and under Declaratory Theory, not even required for statehood.

Report this question

22The Estrada Doctrine was propounded by which country?

  1. USA
  2. Mexico
  3. France
  4. Argentina
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Mexico

Estrada Doctrine — Mexican Foreign Minister Genaro Estrada, 1930. States should not comment on legitimacy of other governments. Asked directly in June 2025 — Mexico is the one-word answer.
