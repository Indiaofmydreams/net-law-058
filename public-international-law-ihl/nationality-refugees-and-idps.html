17 practice questions with answers

1Which Article of the UDHR 1948 provides that 'everyone has the right to a nationality'?

  1. Article 10
  2. Article 15
  3. Article 7
  4. Article 21
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Article 15

Article 15 UDHR = right to nationality. Article 14 UDHR = right to seek and enjoy asylum. These two adjacent articles are the most swapped in UGC NET. Asked directly in December 2025.

2The principle of non-refoulement is enshrined in:

  1. UN Charter, 1945
  2. Article 33 of the 1951 Refugee Convention
  3. Article 14 of UDHR 1948
  4. Vienna Convention on Consular Relations, 1963
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Article 33 of the 1951 Refugee Convention

Non-refoulement = Article 33 of the 1951 Convention. Not Article 14 UDHR (that is right to seek asylum). Not UN Charter. This specific article number is tested every few cycles.

Report this question

3The 1951 Refugee Convention defines a refugee as someone who has a well-founded fear of persecution based on:

  1. Economic hardship
  2. War or natural disaster
  3. Race, religion, nationality, membership of a particular social group, or political opinion
  4. Any violation of human rights
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Race, religion, nationality, membership of a particular social group, or political opinion

Five grounds only — Article 1A(2). Economic hardship, climate change, and natural disasters are NOT included. When these appear as options, eliminate them immediately.

Report this question

4Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) differ from refugees because:

  1. IDPs have not fled persecution
  2. IDPs remain within their own country's borders
  3. IDPs are covered by the 1951 Convention
  4. IDPs are only found in Africa
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. IDPs remain within their own country's borders

IDPs cross NO international border — they remain in their own country. Refugees cross an international border. IDPs are NOT covered by the 1951 Convention — this is a fundamental distinction.

Report this question

5The Nottebohm Case (ICJ, 1955) is a landmark case on the importance of:

  1. Asylum
  2. Nationality and genuine connection
  3. Extradition
  4. Immunity of states
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Nationality and genuine connection

ICJ held Liechtenstein could not exercise diplomatic protection for Nottebohm as his nationality lacked 'genuine connection'. Asked directly in June 2025. Do NOT confuse with Barcelona Traction (erga omnes).

Report this question

6UNHCR was established in:

  1. 1945
  2. 1948
  3. 1950
  4. 1951
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1950

UNHCR established 1950, operational 1 January 1951. The 1951 Refugee Convention came AFTER UNHCR. UNHCR won Nobel Peace Prize in 1954 (first) and 1981 (second). The 1981 date appeared in December 2025.

Report this question

7The UN Guiding Principles on Internal Displacement (1998) are also known as:

  1. Nansen Principles
  2. Deng Principles
  3. Cartagena Principles
  4. Bangkok Principles
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Deng Principles

Named after Dr. Francis Deng, Special Representative of the UN Secretary-General. These are NON-BINDING but reflect existing IHL, IHRL, and refugee law as applicable to IDPs.

Report this question

8The 1967 Protocol to the Refugee Convention was important because:

  1. It created UNHCR
  2. It removed the geographic and temporal limitations of the 1951 Convention
  3. It codified non-refoulement for the first time
  4. It established refugee camps
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. It removed the geographic and temporal limitations of the 1951 Convention

The 1951 Convention originally applied only to European refugees and events before 1 January 1951. The 1967 Protocol made it universal by removing both the geographic and temporal limitations.

Report this question

9Under Article 1F of the 1951 Refugee Convention, a person is excluded from refugee status if they have committed:

  1. A traffic offence in the host country
  2. A crime against peace, a war crime, or a crime against humanity
  3. Any criminal act whatsoever
  4. An offence against a UN agency
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A crime against peace, a war crime, or a crime against humanity

Exclusion Clauses (Article 1F) — who NEVER qualifies from the start. Cessation Clauses (Article 1C) — when refugee status ENDS. Do not swap these two concepts.

Report this question

10The Nansen Passport was a historic travel document for stateless persons issued under the authority of:

  1. UNHCR
  2. League of Nations through Fridtjof Nansen as High Commissioner for Refugees (1922)
  3. International Red Cross
  4. League of Arab States
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. League of Nations through Fridtjof Nansen as High Commissioner for Refugees (1922)

Nansen Passport (1922) — world's first internationally recognized refugee travel document. Issued under the League of Nations — NOT UNHCR which did not exist until 1950.

Report this question

11The Convention Relating to the Status of Stateless Persons was adopted in:

  1. 1951
  2. 1954
  3. 1961
  4. 1967
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1954

1954 Convention — defines a stateless person as one 'not considered a national by any state under the operation of its law'. Separate from the 1951 Refugee Convention and the 1961 Convention on Reduction of Statelessness.

Report this question

12The Cartagena Declaration (1984) is significant because it:

  1. Created UNHCR
  2. Expanded the refugee definition for Latin America to include persons fleeing generalized violence, civil war, and massive human rights violations
  3. Applied to Asian states
  4. Made non-refoulement legally binding
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Expanded the refugee definition for Latin America to include persons fleeing generalized violence, civil war, and massive human rights violations

Cartagena Declaration (1984) = Latin America. OAU Convention (1969) = Africa. Bangkok Principles (AALCO, 1966) = Asia-Pacific. Know which regional instrument belongs to which region.

Report this question

13Jus soli as the basis of nationality means:

  1. Nationality follows the father's nationality
  2. Nationality is acquired by birth on the territory of a state
  3. Nationality is acquired by marriage
  4. Nationality is conferred by legislative act only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Nationality is acquired by birth on the territory of a state

Jus soli = right of the soil (USA, Canada). Jus sanguinis = right of blood/descent (Germany, Japan, India primarily). Do not invert these.

Report this question

14Before exercising diplomatic protection, which condition must normally be satisfied?

  1. The national must have resided in that state for 10 years
  2. The local remedies available in the respondent state must first be exhausted
  3. The UN Security Council must authorize the claim
  4. The national must be a permanent resident
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The local remedies available in the respondent state must first be exhausted

Exhaustion of Local Remedies Rule — a fundamental procedural prerequisite. PLUS the Nottebohm genuine link requirement on nationality. Both conditions must be met.

Report this question

15Under Article 14 of the UDHR 1948, the right to asylum is:

  1. An absolute right to be granted asylum in any state
  2. A right to seek and enjoy asylum — not a right to demand it be granted
  3. Only available to persons from Communist states
  4. Limited to persons recognized as refugees
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A right to seek and enjoy asylum — not a right to demand it be granted

Article 14 UDHR says 'right to seek and enjoy' — not to receive or be granted. States have no binding obligation to grant asylum under UDHR alone.

Report this question

16Match the correct entries — Entry into force of the 1951 Refugee Convention occurred in which year?

  1. 1951
  2. 1952
  3. 1954
  4. 1967
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1954

1951 Convention entered into force in 1954. The 1967 Protocol entered into force in 1967. UN Commission on Refugees formed in 1950. This exact matching appeared in December 2025.

Report this question

17Climate/environmental migrants who flee rising sea levels are currently classified under international law as:

  1. Refugees under the 1951 Convention
  2. IDPs
  3. Stateless persons
  4. Environmental migrants — not covered by the 1951 Convention refugee definition
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Environmental migrants — not covered by the 1951 Convention refugee definition

The 1951 Convention's five grounds do not include environmental causes. This is a significant contemporary gap in international refugee law — a high-probability topic for June 2026.
