About this question set

This set has 18 multiple-choice questions on IHL — Conventions and Protocols from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 3, Public International Law and IHL. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key and read the explanation. 18 of the 18 questions carry a written explanation.

18 practice questions with answers

1How many Geneva Conventions were adopted in 1949?

  1. 2
  2. 3
  3. 4
  4. 5
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 4

Four Geneva Conventions adopted in 1949 — NOT 1864 (that was only the First GC for wounded soldiers). 1949 = all four. 1977 = Additional Protocols I and II. 2005 = AP III (Red Crystal).

Report this question

2The Third Geneva Convention (GC III) of 1949 specifically protects:

  1. Wounded soldiers on land
  2. Prisoners of War
  3. Civilians
  4. Wounded sailors at sea
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Prisoners of War

GC I = wounded/sick on land. GC II = wounded/sick/shipwrecked at sea. GC III = POWs. GC IV = Civilians. This sequence must be automatic — tested every cycle in identification or matching format.

Report this question

3Which Article of the Geneva Conventions of 1949 is called the 'mini convention within the conventions'?

  1. Article 2
  2. Article 3
  3. Article 4
  4. Article 5
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Article 3

Common Article 3 = 'mini convention' = applies to Non-International Armed Conflicts. The exact phrase 'mini convention' was asked directly in June 2025. Article 2 = application to international armed conflicts.

Report this question

4Common Article 3 of the Geneva Conventions applies to:

  1. International armed conflicts only
  2. Non-international armed conflicts (civil wars) only
  3. Both international and non-international armed conflicts equally
  4. Armed conflicts involving UN forces only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Non-international armed conflicts (civil wars) only

Common Article 3 = NIAC only. Article 2 = IAC. AP I = IAC. AP II = NIAC (higher threshold than CA3). CA3 is the minimum floor for NIAC; AP II adds more protections for qualifying conflicts.

Report this question

5Additional Protocol I (1977) to the Geneva Conventions applies to:

  1. Non-international armed conflicts
  2. International armed conflicts
  3. Both categories equally
  4. Armed conflicts involving WMDs only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. International armed conflicts

AP I (1977) = International Armed Conflicts (IAC). AP II (1977) = Non-International Armed Conflicts (NIAC). AP III (2005) = Red Crystal emblem. Know all three APs.

Report this question

6The Martens Clause was originally introduced in the:

  1. Geneva Convention 1949
  2. Hague Convention 1899
  3. UN Charter 1945
  4. Rome Statute 1998
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Hague Convention 1899

Martens Clause (1899) — named after Friedrich Martens, Russian diplomat. States that civilians and combatants remain under protection of principles of law of nations even in cases not covered by specific treaty rules.

Report this question

7The ICRC (International Committee of the Red Cross) was founded in:

  1. 1859
  2. 1863
  3. 1864
  4. 1949
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1863

ICRC founded 1863 by Henry Dunant. First Geneva Convention was 1864. ICRC (1863) came BEFORE the First GC (1864) — not the other way around. When asked which is older, ICRC wins by one year.

Report this question

8The principle of distinction in IHL requires:

  1. Distinguishing between combatants and civilians at all times
  2. Distinguishing between international and non-international conflicts
  3. Distinguishing between war crimes and crimes against humanity
  4. Only officers can be targeted
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Distinguishing between combatants and civilians at all times

Distinction = one of the four core IHL principles: Distinction, Proportionality, Military Necessity, Humanity. Distinction requires always separating combatants from civilians — attacks may only target combatants.

Report this question

9The principle of proportionality in IHL means:

  1. Equal numbers of forces on both sides
  2. Anticipated civilian casualties must not be excessive in relation to expected military advantage
  3. States must use force equal to the attack received
  4. Weapons must be proportionate to the target's size
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Anticipated civilian casualties must not be excessive in relation to expected military advantage

Proportionality — Article 51(5)(b) AP I. Military Necessity permits measures necessary to achieve a legitimate military objective but cannot override specific IHL prohibitions.

Report this question

10The Hague Conventions primarily deal with:

  1. Protection of victims of war
  2. Conduct of hostilities — means and methods of warfare
  3. Rights of refugees
  4. Diplomatic immunity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Conduct of hostilities — means and methods of warfare

Hague Law = conduct of hostilities (means and methods of warfare). Geneva Law = protection of victims (wounded, sick, POWs, civilians). These two streams are the foundational structure of IHL.

Report this question

11A person hors de combat means:

  1. A spy
  2. A civilian
  3. A combatant who is wounded, sick, shipwrecked, or has surrendered
  4. A war criminal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A combatant who is wounded, sick, shipwrecked, or has surrendered

Hors de combat = 'out of the fight'. Such persons must be protected and cannot be attacked — core IHL protection.

Report this question

12The Additional Protocol III (2005) introduced which emblem?

  1. Red Cross
  2. Red Crescent
  3. Red Lion
  4. Red Crystal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Red Crystal

AP III (2005) = Red Crystal — a neutral emblem for states that could not use the cross or crescent for religious/political reasons. The three emblems are: Red Cross, Red Crescent, Red Crystal.

Report this question

13The correct chronological order of these weapons conventions entering into force is:

  1. Chemical Weapons Convention, Biological Weapons Convention, Ottawa Landmines, Conventional Weapons Convention
  2. Biological Weapons Convention, Conventional Weapons Convention, Chemical Weapons Convention, Ottawa Landmines
  3. Ottawa Landmines, Chemical Weapons Convention, Biological Weapons Convention, Conventional Weapons Convention
  4. Conventional Weapons Convention, Ottawa Landmines, Chemical Weapons Convention, Biological Weapons Convention
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Biological Weapons Convention, Conventional Weapons Convention, Chemical Weapons Convention, Ottawa Landmines

BWC (1975) → CCW (1983) → CWC (1997) → Ottawa Landmines (1999). The trap is assuming CWC came before CCW — it did not. Asked directly in June 2025.

Report this question

14Perfidy under IHL refers to:

  1. Legitimate deception such as camouflage and decoys
  2. Acts inviting enemy confidence that they are protected under IHL, then exploiting that protection to kill or capture
  3. Propaganda against the enemy
  4. Attacking under cover of darkness
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Acts inviting enemy confidence that they are protected under IHL, then exploiting that protection to kill or capture

Perfidy = PROHIBITED (Article 37 AP I). Ruse of war = PERMITTED. Perfidy exploits protected status (e.g., feigning surrender, falsely using Red Cross emblem). Ruse = legitimate deception (camouflage, decoys).

Report this question

15Under GC III (POW Convention), prisoners of war are required to provide only:

  1. Full intelligence about their military unit
  2. Name, rank, date of birth, and service number (the 'Big Four')
  3. Their home address and family details
  4. The location of their military base
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Name, rank, date of birth, and service number (the 'Big Four')

Article 17 GC III — POWs cannot be compelled to give more than the Big Four. Any coercive interrogation violates GC III. 'Unit location' or 'military operations details' in the options = always wrong.

Report this question

16Under Article 47 of Additional Protocol I, which of the following is a requirement for mercenary status?

  1. Being a national of a party to the conflict
  2. Not being a member of the armed forces of a party to the conflict
  3. Having served in the military before
  4. Operating under UN authorization
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Not being a member of the armed forces of a party to the conflict

If a person IS a national of a party to the conflict, they CANNOT be a mercenary by definition. All six Article 47 criteria must be met. Nationality of a party = automatic disqualification from mercenary status.

Report this question

17The Chemical Weapons Convention (CWC) was adopted in:

  1. 1972
  2. 1980
  3. 1993
  4. 1998
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1993

CWC adopted 1993, entered into force 1997. BWC adopted 1972, in force 1975. These years are tested in arrangement questions — BWC always comes before CWC.

Report this question

18IHL Assertion-Reason: Assertion (A): IHL regulates the conduct of war (jus in bello), protecting persons not participating in hostilities and restricting means and methods of warfare. Reason (R): IHL is inspired by considerations of humanity and mitigation of human suffering. Select the correct option:

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

This assertion-reason has appeared in nearly identical form in 2021, 2022, and 2023. Both are true AND the Reason genuinely explains the Assertion. Answer is always option (1).
