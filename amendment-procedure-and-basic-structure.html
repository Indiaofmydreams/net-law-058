About this question set

This set has 13 multiple-choice questions on Amendment Procedure & Basic Structure from the UGC NET Law (058) syllabus, unit 2, Constitutional and Administrative Law. 13 practice questions with answers

1Which of the following requires a special majority AND ratification by at least half of the State legislatures under Art. 368(2)?

  1. Amendment of the Citizenship provisions (Arts. 5-11)
  2. Formation of new States under Art. 3
  3. Election of the President (Arts. 54-55)
  4. Amendment of the Fifth Schedule
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Election of the President (Arts. 54-55)

Arts. 2, 3, 4, Fifth and Sixth Schedules and citizenship can be changed by simple majority outside Art. 368. Election of the President is in the proviso to Art. 368(2) requiring State ratification.

2Shankari Prasad Singh Deo v. Union of India (1951) upheld the validity of which Amendment?

  1. Fourth Amendment
  2. Seventeenth Amendment
  3. Twenty-fourth Amendment
  4. First Amendment
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. First Amendment

Held that the power to amend under Art. 368 includes power to amend Fundamental Rights; 'law' in Art. 13 does not include constitutional amendments.

Report this question

3Sajjan Singh v. State of Rajasthan (1965) concerned the validity of which Amendment?

  1. First Amendment
  2. Seventeenth Amendment
  3. Forty-second Amendment
  4. Twenty-fifth Amendment
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Seventeenth Amendment

Upheld the 17th Amendment (1964) following Shankari Prasad, though two judges (Hidayatullah, Mudholkar) expressed doubts on basic features.

4I.C. Golak Nath v. State of Punjab (1967) was decided by a bench of:

  1. 7 judges, unanimously, upholding the Ninth Schedule
  2. 13 judges, by 7:6, propounding the basic structure doctrine
  3. 11 judges, by 6:5, holding Parliament cannot amend Fundamental Rights
  4. 9 judges, by 5:4, upholding Parliament's power to amend Fundamental Rights
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 11 judges, by 6:5, holding Parliament cannot amend Fundamental Rights

Golak Nath overruled Shankari Prasad and Sajjan Singh, applying the doctrine of prospective overruling.

Report this question

5Which Amendment was enacted to nullify the effect of Golak Nath and inserted Art. 13(4) and Art. 368(3)?

  1. Thirty-ninth Amendment (1975)
  2. Twenty-fourth Amendment (1971)
  3. Twenty-fifth Amendment (1971)
  4. Forty-second Amendment (1976)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Twenty-fourth Amendment (1971)

24th Amendment declared Parliament's power to amend any provision incl. Part III, made Presidential assent compulsory and amended the marginal note of Art. 368.

6Article 31C (Directive Principles in Art. 39(b) and (c) to prevail over Arts. 14, 19 and 31) was inserted by:

  1. Forty-second Amendment, 1976
  2. Forty-fourth Amendment, 1978
  3. Twenty-fifth Amendment, 1971
  4. Twenty-fourth Amendment, 1971
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Twenty-fifth Amendment, 1971

25th Amendment also replaced 'compensation' by 'amount' in Art. 31(2).

Report this question

7Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973) was decided by a bench of ____ judges with a majority of ____.

  1. 11 judges; 6:5
  2. 13 judges; 8:5
  3. 9 judges; 5:4
  4. 13 judges; 7:6
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 13 judges; 7:6

The Court held Parliament can amend any provision but not alter the 'basic structure'. It struck down the 2nd part of Art. 31C (excluding judicial review) inserted by the 25th Amendment.

Report this question

8Which provisions inserted by the 42nd Amendment were struck down in Minerva Mills v. Union of India (1980)?

  1. Words 'Socialist' and 'Secular' in the Preamble
  2. Clauses (4) and (5) of Article 368
  3. Article 323A
  4. Article 51A (Fundamental Duties)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Clauses (4) and (5) of Article 368

Minerva Mills held limited amending power is itself a basic feature and that harmony between Part III and Part IV is part of the basic structure; it also struck down the extended Art. 31C.

Report this question

9In Waman Rao v. Union of India (1981), the Supreme Court fixed which date for testing Ninth Schedule laws against the basic structure?

  1. 26 January 1950
  2. 18 June 1951
  3. 24 April 1973
  4. 3 January 1977
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 24 April 1973

Ninth Schedule laws inserted before 24 April 1973 (the date of Kesavananda) were saved; those after are open to review. Reaffirmed in I.R. Coelho (2007).

Report this question

10In I.R. Coelho v. State of Tamil Nadu (2007), a nine-judge bench held that:

  1. Ninth Schedule laws enjoy absolute immunity
  2. Art. 31A is a basic feature that cannot be amended
  3. Art. 31B is unconstitutional
  4. Ninth Schedule laws enacted after 24 April 1973 are open to judicial review on the touchstone of the basic structure
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Ninth Schedule laws enacted after 24 April 1973 are open to judicial review on the touchstone of the basic structure

Art. 31B immunity is not absolute; laws violating basic structure/Art. 21 and Art. 14 can be struck down.

Report this question

11I. The 24th Amendment made it obligatory for the President to give assent to Constitution Amendment Bills.
II. Art. 368(3) declares that Art. 13 shall not apply to amendments made under Art. 368.

  1. Both I and II are correct
  2. Only I is correct
  3. Only II is correct
  4. Neither I nor II is correct
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both I and II are correct

Both true. 24th Amendment (1971) inserted Art. 13(4) and 368(3) and made Presidential assent compulsory.

Report this question

12Assertion (A): Judicial review of constitutional amendments is possible in India.
Reason (R): Kesavananda Bharati held that amendments violating the basic structure are void.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

Basic structure review is the judicial check on Art. 368 power.

Report this question

13Assertion (A): Art. 368(4) and (5) were struck down in Minerva Mills.
Reason (R): The Court held that a limited amending power is itself a basic feature of the Constitution.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

Parliament cannot enlarge its own amending power into absolute power.
