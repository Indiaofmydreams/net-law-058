14 practice questions with answers

1The Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act was enacted in:

  1. 2019
  2. 2014 (following NALSA judgment)
  3. 2016
  4. 2021
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 2019

Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act 2019 provides for recognition of identity of transgender persons, prohibition of discrimination, right to education and employment, and establishment of a National Council for Transgender Persons. It follows the NALSA v. UOI (2014) judgment.

2The NALSA v. Union of India (2014) judgment recognized:

  1. Rights of bonded labourers
  2. Rights of forest dwelling tribes
  3. Rights of persons with mental illness
  4. Rights of transgender persons as third gender with full constitutional rights
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Rights of transgender persons as third gender with full constitutional rights

NALSA v. Union of India (2014) - the SC declared transgender persons the 'third gender,' holding they have full constitutional rights under Articles 14, 15, 16, 19, and 21. The Court directed the government to treat them as socially and educationally backward classes and provide reservations.

3The Mental Healthcare Act 2017 replaced:

  1. Persons with Disabilities Act 1995
  2. Indian Lunacy Act 1912
  3. Epidemic Diseases Act 1897
  4. Mental Health Act 1987
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Mental Health Act 1987

Mental Healthcare Act 2017 replaced the Mental Health Act 1987. It recognizes the right of persons with mental illness to access mental healthcare, gives them the right to make advance directives, prohibits ECT without anaesthesia, and decriminalizes attempted suicide.

4The Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act 2015 provides for trying children in conflict with law aged 16-18 years as adults for:

  1. Heinous offences only, after assessment by the Juvenile Justice Board
  2. All offences
  3. Offences carrying more than 3 years imprisonment
  4. Repeat offences only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Heinous offences only, after assessment by the Juvenile Justice Board

JJ Act 2015 created a new category: children between 16-18 years who commit heinous offences (carrying minimum 7 years imprisonment) may be tried as adults after assessment by the Juvenile Justice Board (JJB) as to their mental and physical capacity.

5The National Food Security Act 2013 provides for:

  1. Prohibition on food adulteration
  2. Legal entitlement to subsidised food grains to up to 75% of rural and 50% of urban population
  3. Food safety standards for all food products
  4. Right to free meals for all citizens
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Legal entitlement to subsidised food grains to up to 75% of rural and 50% of urban population

National Food Security Act 2013 provides legal entitlement to subsidised food grains (5 kg per person per month at Rs. 1-3/kg) to up to 75% of rural population and 50% of urban population. Also covers pregnant women, lactating mothers, and children under 14 years.

6The Persons with Disabilities Act 1995 was replaced by which legislation?

  1. National Trust Act 1999
  2. Mental Healthcare Act 2017
  3. Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act 2016
  4. Rehabilitation Council of India Act 1992
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act 2016

The Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act 2016 replaced the Persons with Disabilities (Equal Opportunities, Protection of Rights and Full Participation) Act 1995. The 2016 Act aligns Indian law with CRPD 2006, increases specified disabilities from 7 to 21, mandates 5% reservation.

7The Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act 2009 operationalises which constitutional provision?

  1. Article 21 - Right to Life
  2. Article 45 - Directive Principle on education
  3. Article 51A(k) - Fundamental Duty of parents
  4. Article 21A - Right to Education (86th Constitutional Amendment)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Article 21A - Right to Education (86th Constitutional Amendment)

Article 21A was inserted by the 86th Constitutional Amendment Act 2002, making free and compulsory education a fundamental right for children aged 6-14 years. The RTE Act 2009 gives legislative effect to Article 21A. It mandates 25% reservation for EWS in private unaided schools.

8The principle of 'best interests of the child' under the CRC applies to:

  1. Only judicial proceedings involving children
  2. Only to children below 12 years
  3. Only in custody disputes
  4. All actions concerning children whether by public or private social welfare institutions, courts, administrative authorities or legislative bodies
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. All actions concerning children whether by public or private social welfare institutions, courts, administrative authorities or legislative bodies

CRC Article 3(1): In all actions concerning children, whether undertaken by public or private social welfare institutions, courts of law, administrative authorities or legislative bodies, the best interests of the child shall be a primary consideration. 'All actions' is comprehensive.

9CEDAW defines discrimination against women as:

  1. Only intentional acts of discrimination by public authorities
  2. Any distinction, exclusion or restriction made on the basis of sex which results in impairing the exercise of human rights by women in any field
  3. Discrimination only in employment and education
  4. Any act that injures women physically
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Any distinction, exclusion or restriction made on the basis of sex which results in impairing the exercise of human rights by women in any field

CEDAW Article 1: 'Discrimination against women' means any distinction, exclusion or restriction made on the basis of sex which has the effect or purpose of impairing or nullifying the recognition, enjoyment or exercise by women of human rights. The 'effect or purpose' formulation covers both intentional and unintentional discrimination.

10The 'social model of disability' (which the CRPD 2006 is based on) holds that:

  1. Disability is a medical condition that needs to be cured
  2. Disabled persons need charity and welfare, not rights
  3. Disability is a natural condition requiring no special legal framework
  4. Disability results from the interaction between persons with impairments and attitudinal and environmental barriers that hinder their full participation in society
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Disability results from the interaction between persons with impairments and attitudinal and environmental barriers that hinder their full participation in society

The social model of disability shifts the focus from the individual's impairment (medical model) to the societal barriers that disable people with impairments. CRPD 2006 is squarely based on the social model - it requires removing barriers and making society inclusive.

11The Right to Education Act 2009 mandates that private unaided schools must reserve what percentage of seats for economically weaker sections (EWS)?

  1. 10%
  2. 15%
  3. 25%
  4. 33%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 25%

Section 12(1)(c) of the RTE Act 2009: Every private unaided school shall admit in Class I to the extent of at least 25% of the strength of that class, children belonging to the weaker section and disadvantaged group in the neighbourhood. The State Government reimburses these schools for EWS students.

12The UN Principles for Older Persons (1991) cover which areas?

  1. Education, health, shelter, food
  2. Independence, participation, care, self-fulfillment, and dignity
  3. Rights only in residential care facilities
  4. Economic rights of elderly persons only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Independence, participation, care, self-fulfillment, and dignity

The UN Principles for Older Persons 1991 (UNGA Resolution 46/91) - while non-binding - covers five areas: independence; participation; care; self-fulfillment; and dignity. There is no binding treaty specifically on older persons' rights.

13The Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act 2015 defines 'child in conflict with law' as:

  1. Any child who is involved in criminal activity regardless of age
  2. Only children below 14 years who commit offences
  3. Children between 16-18 years who commit heinous offences
  4. A child who is alleged or found to have committed an offence and who has not completed 18 years of age on the date of commission of such offence
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A child who is alleged or found to have committed an offence and who has not completed 18 years of age on the date of commission of such offence

JJ Act 2015, Section 2(13): 'Child in conflict with law' means a child who is alleged or found to have committed an offence and who has not completed eighteen years of age on the date of commission of such offence. The special provision for 16-18 year olds in heinous offences is a sub-category.

14The Women's Reservation Bill (Constitution 106th Amendment Act, 2023) provides for reservation of seats for women in:

  1. Lok Sabha only
  2. Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies (and Delhi Assembly)
  3. Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha and all State Legislatures
  4. Only State Legislative Assemblies
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies (and Delhi Assembly)

The Constitution (106th Amendment) Act 2023 (Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam) inserts Articles 330A and 332A to reserve one-third of seats for women in Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies (and NCT of Delhi). Reservation will be effective after delimitation following the next Census.
