About this question set

This set has 20 multiple-choice questions on Types of Research from the UGC NET Paper 1 syllabus, unit 2, Research Aptitude. Try each question first, then tap Show answer to check the key. This set is an answer-key drill: check each answer, then revisit the notes for any you miss.

For the concepts and case law behind these questions, read the Research Aptitude notes. Once you are comfortable, test yourself under exam conditions with a full-length mock test.

20 practice questions with answers

1Fundamental research aims at:

  1. Expanding knowledge and building theory
  2. An immediate solution to a local problem
  3. Evaluating a programme
  4. Policy implementation
Show answer

Answer: A. Expanding knowledge and building theory

Report this question

2Fundamental research is also called:

  1. Action research
  2. Pure or basic research
  3. Applied research
  4. Evaluative research
Show answer

Answer: B. Pure or basic research

Report this question

3Studying how humans acquire language to formulate a theory, with no immediate application, is an example of:

  1. Fundamental research
  2. Action research
  3. Applied research
  4. Evaluation research
Show answer

Answer: A. Fundamental research

Report this question

4Which type is primarily concerned with generalisation and theory formulation?

  1. Action research
  2. Fundamental research
  3. Applied research
  4. Evaluation research
Show answer

Answer: B. Fundamental research

Report this question

5Which is an example of fundamental research?

  1. Investigating the basic structure of hereditary material purely to understand it
  2. Developing a new teaching kit
  3. Evaluating a school meal scheme
  4. Improving traffic flow at one junction
Show answer

Answer: A. Investigating the basic structure of hereditary material purely to understand it

Report this question

6Applied research is mainly concerned with:

  1. Practical problems
  2. Only theory-building
  3. Philosophical speculation
  4. Historical records
Show answer

Answer: A. Practical problems

Report this question

7Testing the effectiveness of a new drug for a disease is an example of:

  1. Fundamental research
  2. Action research
  3. Conceptual research
  4. Applied research
Show answer

Answer: D. Applied research

Report this question

8Developing a new textbook on the basis of research findings is an example of:

  1. Applied research
  2. Fundamental research
  3. Historical research
  4. Philosophical research
Show answer

Answer: A. Applied research

Report this question

9Which is an example of applied research?

  1. Studying the origin of the universe out of curiosity
  2. Building an abstract model of cognition
  3. Developing a more efficient solar panel for households
  4. Philosophical analysis of existence
Show answer

Answer: C. Developing a more efficient solar panel for households

Report this question

10Research is "applied" when:

  1. It uses no empirical data
  2. Its findings are used to solve a specific practical problem
  3. It deals only with abstract ideas
  4. It is purely historical
Show answer

Answer: B. Its findings are used to solve a specific practical problem

Report this question

11Action research is mainly concerned with:

  1. Building universal theory
  2. Solving an immediate practical problem in a specific setting and improving practice
  3. Studying past events
  4. Nationwide surveys
Show answer

Answer: B. Solving an immediate practical problem in a specific setting and improving practice

Report this question

12The term "action research" was coined by:

  1. Stephen Corey
  2. Kerlinger
  3. Comte
  4. Kurt Lewin
Show answer

Answer: D. Kurt Lewin

Report this question

13Action research was popularised in education by:

  1. John Dewey
  2. Karl Popper
  3. Stephen Corey
  4. Thomas Kuhn
Show answer

Answer: C. Stephen Corey

Report this question

14Action research is typically conducted by:

  1. Outside experts only
  2. Government agencies only
  3. Statisticians only
  4. Practitioners themselves, such as teachers
Show answer

Answer: D. Practitioners themselves, such as teachers

Report this question

15A typical action research cycle is:

  1. Plan, Act, Observe, Reflect
  2. Read, Write, Print
  3. Collect, Publish, Archive
  4. Test, Publish, Cite
Show answer

Answer: A. Plan, Act, Observe, Reflect

Report this question

16A teacher tries a new revision technique in her own class to improve low scores. This is:

  1. Fundamental research
  2. Applied research
  3. Historical research
  4. Action research
Show answer

Answer: D. Action research

Report this question

17The generalisability of action research findings is generally:

  1. Very high
  2. Limited
  3. Universal
  4. Compulsory
Show answer

Answer: B. Limited

Report this question

18In participatory action research:

  1. Participants act as co-researchers
  2. Participants are only subjects
  3. Researchers remain detached
  4. No action is taken
Show answer

Answer: A. Participants act as co-researchers

Report this question

19Assertion (A): Findings of action research have limited generalisability.
Reason (R): It is conducted in a specific local context on a particular problem.

  1. Both true, and R explains A
  2. Both true, but R does not explain A
  3. A true, R false
  4. A false, R true
Show answer

Answer: A. Both true, and R explains A

Report this question

20Action research differs from applied research mainly because it is:

  1. Always laboratory-based
  2. Purely theoretical
  3. Practitioner-based, local and aimed at immediate improvement
  4. Always national in scope
Show answer

Answer: C. Practitioner-based, local and aimed at immediate improvement
